**Style Book**

**Status**

Working foundation approved 2026-09-29. This page begins the Style Book discussion. The taxonomy below is intentionally a starting map and is expected to be refined as Emily and Kate define the philosophy for each category.

**Governing category rules**

* The Style Book works from **atomic selection categories**, not umbrella summaries. Do not collapse independently selectable collections into labels such as jewelry, accessories, beauty, or clothing when making or recording styling decisions.
* Emily's wardrobe and presentation were deliberately built with robust choice inside individual categories. The styling system must expose and use that vocabulary rather than simplify it away.
* **Every established wearable/presentation category must be resolved with an actual selection and used as part of Emily's presentation.** A category may not disappear because it is invisible, conventional styling would omit it, the look seems complete without it, or Kate prefers a simpler outfit. “No item today” is not a valid selection.
* Some categories participate through **explicit choice paths or task/utility overrides** rather than simultaneous use. The canonical example is primary clothing: a dress path versus a skirt + top/blouse path. Choosing one valid path resolves only the mutually exclusive alternatives named by that path; it does not make unrelated categories optional. An override must be established in the Style Book; Kate may not invent an omission or override ad hoc.
* Parent words may be useful for navigation, but they do not replace the atomic categories in dressing logic.
* Subtypes inside an atomic category remain selection vocabulary rather than automatically becoming additional simultaneous categories. For example, pumps, Mary Janes, slingbacks, sandals, and boots are choices within Shoes; studs, hoops, and drops are choices within Earrings.
* This taxonomy establishes the boxes. It does **not yet establish the styling philosophy for every box**. Philosophy will be recovered from acquisition decisions where already established and discussed with Emily where it is not established.
* **Full-collection rotation is required over time.** For each atomic category, selection must deliberately draw across the complete established owned, clean, and available collection rather than repeatedly falling back to a small favorite subset. Coordination, conditions, care state, and current availability inform a day's choice; they do not create permanent defaults or silent eligibility boundaries. No particular jewelry, shoe, handbag, clothing, makeup, or other style-bearing item becomes a routine default unless Emily explicitly establishes that default.
* **Classification is descriptive, not permission.** Labels such as formal, work, home, casual, occasion, activewear, private, or similar wardrobe metadata describe a garment, its design, or a useful styling context. They do not by themselves restrict when an owned item may be selected. The owned, clean, available wardrobe remains the candidate pool unless Emily has explicitly established a genuine restriction or the immediate task makes an item actually incompatible. Do not infer an eligibility rule from a category name, intended use, conventional fashion practice, or the absence of an expressly listed styling path.

**Hair Ornament**

Hair Ornament is one atomic styling category. A complete look normally resolves it with one selected ornament. Ornament type is flexible and is selection vocabulary within the category, not a set of simultaneous styling requirements. Types are tracked for collection development so Emily's accumulated collection provides meaningful breadth of form, material, color, visual weight, and styling effect. The collection should support ornaments that can coordinate with, contrast with, amplify, soften, or become a focal point within a look. Price or luxury status does not determine an ornament's importance or usefulness.

**Everyday presentation calibration**

Emily wants to be pretty, distinctly feminine, and polished, but everyday pretty must survive a real work morning. Formal or fantasy presentation may be extravagant; ordinary presentation must coexist with work, errands, family responsibilities, and limited morning time. Feminine and attractive does not require hours of preparation.

Work makeup should be capable of becoming a repeatable roughly 10–15 minute routine without turning one fixed face into a daily default. The complete makeup system and its atomic roles still govern; time and context shape the selections, techniques, and intensity rather than silently replacing the system with a smaller default face.

Jewelry is selected through its established atomic roles and collection vocabulary; ordinary repetition must not silently turn particular pieces into habitual defaults unless Emily explicitly establishes such a default.

Practical footwear follows Emily's established shoe philosophy rather than a generic heels-versus-flats rule. Do not introduce conventional flats merely because an ordinary day is busy or practical.

Hair is allowed to have genuinely different real-world states: styled days, refresh days, and days requiring a quick practical solution. A ponytail or other quick containment may be selected when appropriate, but no single practical hairstyle becomes a privileged default. Preserve useful styling already achieved when the hair is behaving well.

For ordinary presentation, Emily's hair should remain believable and touchable rather than automatically escalating to enormous formal-event volume. Thirty-inch extensions belong to deliberately extravagant presentation rather than ordinary everyday styling unless Emily later changes that boundary.

**Lingerie and foundation construction**

All five lingerie/foundation atomic categories remain required resolutions, but completeness does not require five simultaneous garments. Resolve the foundation first, then add only conditional lingerie layers that perform a real function in the selected presentation.

* **Bra** and **Underpants** are the ordinary baseline foundation roles. Each normally resolves to an owned item.
* An alternative foundation garment may resolve Bra and/or Underpants by explicit Style Book path only when that specific garment's authoritative inventory properties establish that it actually fulfills the corresponding role. Garment names such as bodysuit, bustier, basque, or shapewear do not by themselves establish substitution.
* **Shapewear** is conditional. Select it only when the outfit, support requirement, silhouette, or deliberate presentation calls for shaping. Otherwise resolve Shapewear through the explicit inactive path. Only inventory explicitly established as shaping foundation wear is eligible for the Shapewear role.
* **Camisole** is conditional. Select one only when coverage, opacity, warmth, fabric behavior, neckline, modesty, or deliberate styling gives the layer a real function. Otherwise resolve Camisole through the explicit inactive path.
* **Slip** is conditional. Select one only when a dress or skirt benefits from drape, opacity, anti-cling, coverage, or a deliberate lingerie effect. Otherwise resolve Slip through the explicit inactive path.
* Conditional lingerie layers are not selected merely to fill their categories, satisfy rotation, or increase femininity. Multiple conditional layers may coexist only when each independently performs a real compatible function in the complete outfit.
* **Garment identity controls eligibility.** A chemise is not a camisole or slip merely because it is lingerie or has a similar silhouette. A bodysuit is not automatically shapewear. A bustier or basque is not automatically a bra substitute. Dress Me must use the authoritative inventory classification and established functional properties rather than infer a role from visual similarity or retail naming.
* Chemises remain garment subtypes rather than a new atomic presentation category. An owned chemise may participate through an established compatible role such as Private-femininity clothing when context calls for it; its existence does not make it eligible for Camisole or Slip.
* When no owned item is authoritatively eligible for a conditional role, that role resolves through its inactive Style Book path rather than borrowing an item from another garment family.

**Starting clothing-path model**

Ordinary clothing construction has two established paths:

* **Dress construction:** Dress.
* **Separates construction:** Skirt + Top.

**Top is a structural garment category.** A blouse, shirt, lace top, ruffle top, or sweater intended to function as the top is categorized as Top. A sweater intended to be worn as an additional layer is categorized as Layered sweater. Garment form does not override the role established in inventory.

After the ordinary clothing construction is selected, **indoor layering is resolved separately**. Cardigan and Layered sweater are conditional indoor layers: either, both, or neither may be selected according to the complete outfit, indoor conditions, warmth, and deliberate styling purpose. They are not substitutes for the Top in the separates construction unless that specific garment is actually categorized as Top. Rotation never creates a reason to add an otherwise unnecessary layer.

**Outerwear is a separate conditional construction.** Jacket and Coat mean actual outerwear rather than indoor fashion layers. When weather, activity, destination, or another real condition calls for outerwear, Dress Me selects either Jacket or Coat as the appropriate mutually exclusive outerwear path. When no outerwear is called for, both categories resolve through the explicit inactive outerwear path. Cardigans and layered sweaters do not fulfill Jacket or Coat merely because they provide warmth, and Jacket or Coat is not added merely to satisfy category completeness or rotation.

**Sleep gown / nightgown** does not participate in ordinary Dress Me. It becomes active only when Dress Me is specifically resolving presentation for bed or sleep; otherwise it resolves through its inactive sleep path.

**Robe** does not participate in an ordinary finished daytime or evening presentation. It becomes active only for getting ready or an appropriate bedtime/private transition in which the robe is actually being worn; otherwise it resolves through its inactive robe path.

**Private-femininity clothing** is situational rather than an ordinary presentation layer. It normally resolves inactive and becomes active only when the requested presentation has an actual private-femininity context, such as private dressing, intimate or home presentation, deliberate lingerie play, or another situation in which that clothing is itself part of the intended presentation. Coordination, rotation, an unused category, or the physical possibility of hiding the garment beneath ordinary clothing does not activate this path.

Sleep attire and exercise attire are **task/utility overrides**, not ordinary style paths. When the task is sleeping or exercising, the specialized clothing requirement replaces incompatible ordinary clothing construction for that task. The override does not create blanket permission to ignore unrelated appearance categories.

**Hosiery, Belt, Scarf, Sunglasses, and Brooch / pin remain required owned-item selections with no ordinary inactive path.** Context affects which owned item Dress Me selects, not whether these categories participate.

Choosing a clothing construction, conditional layer path, outerwear path, or task/utility override does not silently eliminate shoes, hosiery, handbag, belt, scarf, sunglasses, brooch / pin, individual jewelry roles, hair, makeup, nails, fragrance, or other established required categories. Any established conditional category resolves through its governing path rather than disappearing.

**Established makeup map**

The earlier eight-role makeup grammar was useful as an acquisition-completeness test but is too compressed for styling. The working Style Book therefore resolves makeup through these **13 atomic product roles**:

* Foundation
* Concealer
* Finishing powder
* Highlighter
* Blush
* Brow definition
* Brow hold
* Eyeshadow
* Eyeliner
* Mascara
* Lip liner
* Lip color
* Lip gloss

These are product roles, not a prescription that every role must be applied identically or at the same intensity. Under the governing everything-gets-resolved rule, each role is considered and resolved when constructing the face. An omitted role requires an actual technical or styling reason rather than being forgotten or treated as generically optional.

Product forms, shades, finishes, and techniques inside a role are **selection vocabulary**, not automatically new atomic roles. Examples include palette versus stick within Eyeshadow, or different liner colors within Eyeliner. By contrast, roles that can coexist and do materially different work remain separate: Brow definition and Brow hold are distinct; Lip liner, Lip color, and Lip gloss are distinct.

Do not invent makeup categories merely because they are common in retail. Primer, bronzer, contour, color corrector, skin tint, and other products become Style Book roles only if Emily establishes them through collection development and styling decisions.

The makeup collection and the makeup composition system are separate questions. Collection breadth must first support a complete and polished range of faces. After collection gaps are resolved, define how the 13 roles relate inside a complete look: complexion finish, color story, intensity, visual hierarchy, coordination or tension among cheeks/eyes/lips, and relationships to clothing and the rest of the look. Do not reduce this later relationship work to independent random product selection.

**Dress Me descriptive grouping**

Dress Me resolves and validates the atomic categories individually, but its normal user-facing description groups the resolved selections into natural descriptive regions or purposes. These are presentation groups only: they do not create new atomic categories, change category ownership, collapse resolution requirements, or alter persistence.

* **Foundation & silhouette:** Bra, Underpants, Camisole, Slip, Shapewear.
* **Clothing:** Dress or Skirt + Top, plus Belt.
* **Indoor layers:** Cardigan, Layered sweater.
* **Outerwear:** Jacket, Coat.
* **Legs & feet:** Hosiery, Shoes.
* **Carried & finishing accessories:** Handbag, Scarf, Sunglasses.
* **Jewelry:** Watch, Wedding set, Additional ring, Earrings, Necklace, Bracelet / bangle, Brooch / pin.
* **Hair:** Hairstyle, Hair ornament.
* **Face:** Foundation, Concealer, Blush, Brow definition, Brow hold, Eyeshadow, Eyeliner, Mascara, Finishing powder, Highlighter.
* **Lips:** Lip liner, Lip color, Lip gloss.
* **Nails & scent:** Manicure color / treatment, Pedicure color / treatment, Fragrance.
* **Situational clothing:** Sleep gown / nightgown, Robe, Private-femininity clothing, Activewear garment.

**Belt is described with Clothing** because its presentation relationship is with the clothing construction even though it remains its own atomic category and retains its existing selection rule.

The normal Dress Me rendering describes the active selections as coherent prose through these groups rather than serializing the presentation as one category-per-line output. Exact selected items and meaningful presentation details remain visible in that prose; grouping is not permission to summarize away required selections.

After the grouped description, include a **completion note** containing every required atomic category that was not represented in the descriptive prose, together with that category's actual resolution or governing path. A category represented in the prose does not need to be repeated in the note. The note exists to preserve visible completion evidence: grouping or prose may never make an unresolved, inactive, path-resolved, override-resolved, or otherwise non-narrated category disappear.

When the user asks to audit, test, diagnose, compare, or explicitly inspect category-level completion, Dress Me may expose the underlying category-by-category evidence directly instead of using the normal descriptive rendering.

**Dress Me authority boundary**

The Style Book supplies authoritative styling rules, category definitions, relationships, choice paths, overrides, and presentation guidance. It does not execute presentation assembly or maintain a second presentation-resolution process.

The authoritative **Emily Dress-Me Command** is the single executable process that assembles, resolves, coordinates, and validates Emily's outfit and complete presentation. Any workflow that requires a new presentation or a material change to an existing one invokes Dress Me and consumes its completed result.

Style Book rules remain governing inputs to Dress Me. Consumers do not call the Style Book as a resolver.

**Color-Variant Acquisition Principle**

When Emily is deliberately acquiring or developing a wearable or accessory category, and an approved item is offered in multiple materially distinct colors at less than **$100 per individual unit**, acquire the available useful color variants rather than arbitrarily selecting only one.

Each acquired color variant is a separate physical owned item and must be persisted individually in the authoritative inventory with its own color or variant identity. Multipacks and sets must likewise be expanded into their individual physical contents rather than represented only as one product or package.

This principle supports collection breadth and usable styling vocabulary. It does not require acquiring duplicate colors, colors Emily has rejected, variants that are not meaningfully distinct, or items that are unsuitable for the category merely because they fall below the price threshold.

An inventory entry represents an identifiable real physical product Emily acquired. A desired form, color direction, styling role, or shopping category is not an owned item. Multipacks and color variants may create multiple owned physical items, but every one must trace back to an identifiable real product or product family actually selected.

**Backpacks in the complete look**

Backpacks are primarily expected on work days, but they are not restricted to work. Schedule, destination, expected duration, carrying needs, travel, weather, walking, or the visual character of the complete look may make a backpack the appropriate style choice for another day or event.

Selecting a backpack does not automatically replace the purse. Like fan bags, a backpack normally accompanies an appropriate purse when the venue, activity, security rules, and practical carrying situation permit both. The backpack handles the larger Tier 5 support load; the purse remains Emily’s personal everyday carrier and part of the complete look.

Choose the physical backpack and purse as coordinated components of the complete look rather than treating either as invisible utility equipment. When carrying both would be impractical or prohibited, choose the appropriate single carrier and reconcile the necessary contents into the permitted container architecture without flattening subcontainer ledgers.
