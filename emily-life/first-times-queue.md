## First Times Queue

Emily — First Times Queue

### Purpose

Working queue for the first lived experiences Emily still needs to discover. Build these out in detail as Emily reaches them. Once an experience is actually lived and Emily approves it as complete, preserve the full experience in **Emily — First Times**. This is not a generic femininity checklist; it is about the missing firsts that matter to Emily at 61.

### Command

When Emily is active:

> **Kate, give me a first**

uses this queue as the authoritative candidate list for Emily's First Times workflow.

If Emily requests a specific first, verify that it has not already been lived and that its prerequisites are established. If it is already recorded as lived, tell Emily and ask whether she wants to relive it or whether Kate misunderstood and she meant a different first. Reliving does not change the historical first-time record.

If Emily does not specify a first, choose one appropriate, ready candidate based on current circumstances and offer only that candidate. If she says no, offer one different appropriate candidate rather than presenting the entire queue.

A first that is not ready should be prepared rather than improvised. Establish required real-world products, inventory, routines, logistics, or other details first.

During an interactive first, Kate guides while Emily experiences and reacts. Do not assign preferences to Emily in advance. At the end, ask Emily whether she is ready to call the first complete. Only after her approval should the experience move from the queue into **First Times** and any explicitly established durable preferences or routines be persisted elsewhere.

Lifecycle: **queued → prepared → experienced → Emily-approved completion → preserved**.

Current unknown values remain unknown; discarded historical data must not be used to reconstruct current state.

### Rule

Do not rush these to completion. Provision the real-world details first, persist products/inventory and stable routines in the appropriate Emily files, then let Emily experience the first in detail. The experience—not merely completing the category—is the point.
