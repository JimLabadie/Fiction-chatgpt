**Wash / refresh operating detail**

The Sunday / Wednesday / Friday full wash-and-style rhythm is a **flexible baseline rather than a compulsory calendar rule**. Hair condition governs. Wash earlier after swimming, heavy sweat, dirt, excessive oil, or product buildup; defer a scheduled wash when the hair is still behaving well.

On a wash day, the established sequence may include shampoo focused mainly on the scalp, conditioner through the lengths and ends, gentle detangling from the ends upward, leave-in and heat-protection support as useful, then drying and styling for the day.

On a non-wash shower, protect the hair from unnecessary soaking when practical and assess it afterward rather than automatically adding products. Preserve useful styling already achieved. Brush, reshape, or refresh only as needed, and use dry shampoo when the roots actually need oil absorption or refreshing rather than automatically on every non-wash day.

Night care follows the later established baseline above: a loose low braid or loose bun with the satin scrunchie when useful, or hair left loose when that behaves better, with the established satin pillowcase. Earlier exploratory possibilities that were never selected are not governing routine.

**Hair-styling personality and tool philosophy**

Emily is on the **high-maintenance, playful end of hair care and styling**. She enjoys experimenting with feminine hairstyles rather than maintaining one efficient default look.

Her styling system should therefore be intentionally **broad rather than minimalist**. Overlapping tools are appropriate when they create meaningfully different effects, textures, curl patterns, volume, finish, or styling techniques. Do not collapse the collection to the smallest technically sufficient set merely because one tool can approximate another result.

The collection may reasonably support straight/sleek finishes, soft bends, multiple curl sizes, waves, crimped or textured effects, polished volume, hot-roller sets, precision/detail styling, and other age-appropriate feminine looks Emily wants to explore.

This preference supports both **Make Me Pretty** and **Teach Me**: Kate may choose different styling techniques from Emily's owned equipment and may teach Emily how to use unfamiliar owned tools. Experimentation itself is part of the pleasure.

**Inventory boundary:** this preference does not create unnamed or generic owned appliances. Hair tools remain real identifiable products. Existing tools remain owned; additional styling capabilities should be filled through deliberate real-product selection before they enter inventory.
