## Purse Capability Lookup

This page records the physical architecture and demonstrated functional capability of **every owned purse** in Emily Life. It supplements the Purse inventory and carry-system rules; it does not replace either.

Every owned purse is evaluated using the same process regardless of tier, intended style role, size, or previous use. Do not exclude a purse from evaluation because a function appears unusual for its tier.

Tier describes a purse's general carrying class. It does not establish the purse's capacity, required contents, organizational method, or suitability for a particular activity.

Each purse is evaluated individually using established product specifications where available, including exterior dimensions, usable opening, depth/gusset, closure, internal compartments and pockets, built-in card organization, structure, carrying method, and other features that materially affect usable capacity.

### Physical capability guidance

Functional capability is contextual. A purse does not need to carry the entire day's load to be suitable for an activity. Other established active carriers may provide complementary capacity. For example, a Tier 1 purse may be suitable for a workday when an Active Backpack carries the work equipment and other support items.

Module use is determined by actual fit and usefulness. A purse may carry all, some, or none of the established modules. Built-in organization may perform a module's function without requiring the physical module.

Loose carry is not a failure state. When a purse's architecture or available space makes loose organization more practical, appropriate items may be carried loose or in built-in pockets.

Functional capability is established from the purse's actual architecture and the requirements of the complete carry configuration. Do not infer capability solely from tier, manufacturer size labels, appearance, or prior categorization.

Unknown physical specifications remain unknown rather than being inferred.

### Style and use guidance

Each purse records five descriptive characteristics that help evaluate it in combination with the day's outfit, activities, other active carriers, and circumstances. These characteristics are guidance rather than permissions or prohibitions.

**Formality** — Describes where the purse generally falls from casual through polished to formal based on its materials, construction, silhouette, finish, and design.

**Visual Presence** — Describes how strongly the purse participates in the overall look, from quiet/supporting through noticeable to statement/spectacular.

**Personality** — Describes the character the purse contributes, such as restrained, classic, feminine, playful, whimsical, ironic, artistic, dramatic, or other useful descriptors. Personality is not a formality judgment.

**Practicality** — Describes how forgiving the purse is in actual use, including delicacy, durability, closure security, weather sensitivity, ease of access, and other handling considerations. Practicality does not determine where a purse may be carried.

**Carry Behavior** — Records how the purse is actually carried and how that affects use, including hand-carry, clutch, wristlet, shoulder, crossbody, removable straps, hands-free capability, and other relevant characteristics.

These characteristics do not create occasion bans. Selection considers them together with physical capacity, the complete carry configuration, the actual outfit, the activity, and Emily's preferences. An unusual choice may be particularly successful because of the contrast or personality it contributes.
