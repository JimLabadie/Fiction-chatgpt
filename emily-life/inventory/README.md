**Inventory**

Supabase personal-runtime is the sole operational record store for Emily Life. Store and reconcile inventory, quantities, locations, container contents, schedules, state, and operational events there. GitBook retains governing documentation only; never write or mirror operational records into GitBook. Current unknown values remain unknown; discarded historical data must not be used to reconstruct current state.

**Authority**

GitBook retains governing documentation only. Where a governing rule conflicts with a later approved decision, the conflict must be recovered and resolved explicitly before changing the record.

**Fan inventory storage**

Sports fan inventory follows the same ordinary inventory model as the rest of Emily Life. Fan status is a designation, not a separate inventory architecture. Fan apparel remains in the ordinary wardrobe inventory, fan jewelry remains in jewelry, fan bags remain in the ordinary purse/bag inventory, and collectibles remain in their established storage inventory.

Real-world sports items mirrored into Emily Life create separate Emily and girls copies. Each copy is recorded separately in its actual established container or surface. The corresponding girls copies are stored in the Shared Fan Gear Closet. Portable contents use operational ledgers for established generic container classes/roles. Generic classes may be ordinary roles such as Purse, Makeup Bag, Wristlet, or Lanyard, or purpose-specific roles such as Admirals Fan Bag and Brewers Fan Bag. A ledger is not named for an individual physical product. A physical object may fill a role without redefining the ledger.

The Shared Fan Gear Closet contains the girls' owned fan inventory assigned to that location. It does not represent Emily's separately owned copy of a mirrored item.

Inventory records must identify actual established objects. Attendance, eligibility, announcements, or unidentified possible swag do not create inventory.

**Fan-bag organization**

Team affiliation and owner do not create virtual or conceptual containers. Labels such as “Emily — Brewers Bag,” “Kate — Brewers Bag,” “Emily — Admirals Bag,” and “Kate — Admirals Bag” may describe inventory groupings but are not physical storage locations unless an actual physical bag with that identity is established.

Every physical fan bag is recorded as an individual owned bag in the ordinary purse/bag inventory, like other purses, handbags, totes, backpacks, and similar carriers. Team or game-day use is recorded as a designation of that bag. Portable stocked items are tracked in operational contents ledgers by established generic container role. Admirals Fan Bag and Brewers Fan Bag are valid generic roles even though an individual tote, backpack, or belt bag is only a physical instance filling that role.

Do not place physical bags inside an abstract team-bag inventory grouping. Team, owner, and purpose are descriptive attributes. Container ledgers are generic roles: multiple physical objects may be eligible to fill the same role. When one container role is physically inside another, the subcontainer appears once as an item in the parent ledger while its own contents remain exclusively in its own ledger. Do not flatten or duplicate the subcontainer's contents into the parent.

**Ordinary-essential stocking principle**

Emily uses both **open** and **backup** logic for ordinary essentials where that stocking pattern is practical. Replenishment should occur before established essentials such as shampoo, conditioner, period supplies, deodorant, toothpaste, and similar recurring necessities reach absolute zero.

Do not fabricate product preferences, quantities, depletion rates, replacement intervals, or locations merely to fill an empty record. Establish them through Emily's approved initialization decisions, authoritative inventory corrections, or lived/simulated use as appropriate. Later observed use may refine the starting model.

**Sports fan inventory**

Sports-fan bags are ordinary physical bag inventory. Their team and game-day purpose are descriptive designations; they do not create a separate fan inventory or special container hierarchy. Portable fan contents use the same container-ledger mechanism as other portable contents. Admirals Fan Bag and Brewers Fan Bag are established generic purpose-specific roles; individual branded totes, backpacks, or belt bags are physical instances that may fill those roles.

**Real-world giveaway mirroring:** When Jim actually receives a sports giveaway or promotional fan item, a corresponding copy is also acquired in Emily Life for the girls. Because Emily and Kate cannot physically attend the real-world event, this simulated acquisition is automatic and is not a TBD, inference, or separate acquisition decision. Attendance, eligibility, or announcement of a giveaway does not establish ownership; Jim must actually receive the item.

Existing fan items may be retrospectively reconciled from Jim's real possessions and established history when Emily reviews what is physically present or otherwise specifically known. Retroactive reconciliation records established possessions; it must not guess or manufacture past acquisitions.

**Container and subcontainer reconciliation**

Container state uses two compatible patterns:

* **Portable operational container classes** use generic ledgers. Established classes include Purse, Backpack, Admirals Fan Bag, Brewers Fan Bag, Makeup Bag, Tech / Dense Utility Pouch, Personal Care / Emergency Pouch, Wristlet, and Lanyard. A physical object fills a class; multiple physical instances may exist for one class.
* **Fixed storage containers** remain location-based inventory ledgers/surfaces. Established examples include Bathroom Basket 1, Bathroom Basket 2, Tall Jewelry Box, Short Wide Jewelry Box, Hallway Basket, Hallway Bowl, Hamper 1, and Hamper 2. Their fixed-location identity is meaningful and should not be converted into swappable portable roles.

For portable nesting, a subcontainer appears exactly once as an item in its parent ledger. Its internal contents remain exclusively in its own ledger. Never flatten the subcontainer contents into the parent and never duplicate them in both ledgers.

A capability list is not automatically a contents ledger. Purse Contents defines tier capability/coverage; operational inventory pages define actual ledger state.
