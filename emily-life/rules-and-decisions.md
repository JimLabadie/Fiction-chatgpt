**Rules & Decisions**

**Verification**

Check live sources before making claims about current inventory, targets, or state. Discovery comes before content inspection when checking an external repository or Drive location.

**No silent reconciliation**

If two sources disagree, report the conflict and recover the later approved decision. Do not choose whichever source is convenient.

**Approval**

Proposals are not inventory. An item or rule becomes governing only after explicit approval and persistence.

**Privacy**

Emily-specific information stays inside Emily contexts unless explicitly requested elsewhere.

**Simplicity**

Prefer the simplest structure that preserves truth and continuity. Do not build extra systems merely because they are possible.

**Utility infrastructure vs. personal expression**

For Emily's purse and backpack system, ordinary utilitarian infrastructure may be selected and persisted without item-by-item approval when the function has already been approved. Every selected item must still be a real, identifiable product with enough model, size, count, or SKU information to support later restocking or replacement.

Items that materially express personal style, affect fit or comfort, depend on device/health compatibility, or create meaningful recurring cost require Emily's input before becoming the standard. This includes the bags themselves and normally includes wallets, padfolios, umbrellas, water bottles, pouches, cosmetics, fragrances, and similar visible choices. Cosmetics and fragrance are selected in their dedicated later beauty/fragrance work and then referenced by the purse system rather than independently re-selected for the purse.

Automatic utility selection must never turn search results into canon merely because they appeared in research; the chosen concrete product must be deliberately selected and persisted.

**Consumable inventory accounting**

For consumables carried away from their home storage, a transfer between locations is not usage. Record consumption only when the item is actually used, discarded as spent, lost, or otherwise leaves usable inventory. Purse replenishment therefore moves stock from its home location into the purse without reducing system-wide quantity. Location-level counts and system-wide counts must reconcile.

**Persistence verification**

Supabase personal-runtime is the sole operational record store for Emily Life. Store and reconcile inventory, quantities, locations, container contents, schedules, state, and operational events there. GitBook retains governing documentation only; never write or mirror operational records into GitBook. Current unknown values remain unknown; discarded historical data must not be used to reconstruct current state.

**Cross-calendar mirror boundary**

Emily's calendar is the writable calendar for Emily-private simulated life. Jim's primary Google Calendar and the **Labadie Family Calendar** are approved source calendars for schedule context.

Relevant source events may be mirrored one-way into Emily's calendar so Emily has a unified view of obligations affecting the day.

* Direction is strictly **Jim primary + Labadie Family → Emily**.
* Never create, edit, delete, or otherwise maintain Emily-private state on Jim's primary calendar or the Labadie Family Calendar.
* Mirrored Emily-calendar copies must be identifiable as mirrors, private where supported, transparent/non-blocking, and carry **no reminders/alarms**.
* Mirroring is reconciliation, not blind duplication. If a source event changes or is removed, update or remove its Emily mirror.
* A mirrored event remains source-owned context, not an Emily-originated appointment.
* The Morning Briefing reconciles configured mirrors before using the day's calendar state.

**Natural activation phrases**

Emily Life commands may have memorable, playful activation phrases. These are aliases for capabilities, not brittle passwords. Natural-language equivalents remain valid.

Current playful activators:

* **Kate, I need my Yoda girl!** → Teach Me Something.
* **Kate, I feel the need, the need for silk.** → Retail Therapy.
* **Kate, we need to play dressup! Period!** → Play in the Closet.

Existing situational command **Kate, I want to do this as Emily** remains governing. **Kate, what would Emily do?** is a separate perspective/decision lens.

**Pinned Style Book follow-up — makeup**

**Makeup acquisition-gap audit**

Before treating any makeup role as an acquisition gap, retrieve the current authoritative Product Ledger and the live Style Book makeup map.

Rules & Decisions does not maintain current makeup-product counts, current collection-completeness status, or copied inventory summaries.

Determine breadth and capability from the actual currently owned products each time the audit is performed. A previous audit conclusion may explain a historical decision but does not establish the current collection.

**Situational packing does not redefine inventory**

An item selected for a particular outing does not become part of that outing’s specialized inventory merely because it is carried there.

**Ownership, designation, selection, and physical containment remain separate concepts.** Ordinary Emily inventory may be selected whenever current conditions make it useful. For example, an umbrella is ordinary all-purpose inventory and may accompany work, errands, a fan outing, travel, or another activity when weather warrants it. Carrying the umbrella in a fan bag for one outing does not make it fan gear or a standing component of that fan bag.

Activity-specific inventory retains its established designation. For example, fan-specific ponchos may be selected for an appropriate fan outing without becoming general-purpose weather inventory.

When preparing for an outing:

1. establish the destination, activity, schedule, weather, venue restrictions, and other relevant conditions;
2. select useful items from both ordinary inventory and any applicable activity-specific inventory;
3. determine which selected items are worn or carried;
4. place carried items into the appropriate permitted container or subcontainer for that outing.

Temporary physical containment does not change an item’s inventory identity, designation, or normal home. After the outing, situational items return to their established state unless Emily explicitly establishes a durable change.

Specialized container ledgers therefore record their established stocked contents and current physical containment where relevant; they must not be used as ownership categories for every situational item that happens to travel inside them.
