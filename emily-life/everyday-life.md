**Consumable Usage Model**

This model governs ordinary simulated depletion for Emily's established consumables. It exists so elapsed time can advance mundane inventory without requiring Emily to manually log every ordinary use.

**Governing rules**

* **Owned does not mean active.** A product may be owned and provisioned while its corresponding routine or first experience has not yet been established. Such an item is **owned but not yet active** and does not deplete through catch-up.
* **Never invent use because an item exists.** Catch-up consumes only products whose usage trigger or recurring routine has been established.
* Distinguish **exact consumption** from **estimated depletion**. Countable items such as pads decrement by units when the model supports that. Continuous products use an estimated lifespan/usage model rather than fabricated milliliter precision.
* Initial rates are **calibration values**, not permanent truths. Observed Emily-specific depletion overrides the starting estimate over time.
* Fixed-rate modeling is appropriate where ordinary use is predictable and logging individual events would add no useful information.
* Event-driven products consume only when the triggering event is established.
* Every catch-up-controlled state needs a **last reconciled through** marker or equivalent so running catch-up twice does not double-consume the same elapsed period.

**Inventory location is authoritative**

Inventory availability is **location-specific**. An item physically assigned to the purse, bathroom, closet, car, travel kit, or another location is available in that location; its existence elsewhere does not automatically satisfy stock for a different location.

* **Purse stock is not bathroom/home stock.** An item carried in the purse may satisfy an away-from-home or purse-readiness requirement, but it does not count as bathroom or household supply merely because Emily owns it.
* **Home stock is not purse stock.** A product in the bathroom does not make the purse prepared unless a purse unit is actually assigned there.
* Moving an item between locations is a **transfer**, not consumption and not creation of a duplicate. System-wide quantity remains unchanged unless a unit is consumed, discarded, lost, or replenished.
* Exact-count products must preserve both **system-wide total** and **location allocation** when those quantities matter. Bathroom period supplies and purse period supplies, for example, are separate location counts whose sum is the system-wide total.
* For continuous products, do not assume a full-size home product is available away from home unless the same physical product is intentionally carried or a separate travel/purse unit exists.
* Kate, what does Emily need?, Kate, catch up my stuff, purse-readiness checks, bathroom stocking, travel preparation, and other inventory workflows must evaluate the stock required **at the relevant location**, not merely whether an item appears somewhere in Emily's possessions.
* When a location-specific shortage can reasonably be solved by transferring existing surplus rather than buying more, report the transfer/reallocation option rather than manufacturing a shopping need.
