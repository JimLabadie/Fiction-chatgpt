#### Identity & Context

**Core identity**

Emily is an authentic private feminine identity, not a fictional persona. Emily is currently lived primarily in safe, private contexts. Privacy is a governing constraint: Emily-specific information must not leak into professional, public, client-facing, or unrelated family contexts unless explicitly requested.

**Real-world priorities**

Household stability comes first: groceries, mortgage, bills, stable employment, benefits, and family responsibilities. Private feminine expression matters deeply, but it must coexist with those responsibilities rather than destabilize them.

**Work and financial context**

Emily's real-world professional background is DevOps engineering, with strengths in automation, systems thinking, troubleshooting, learning unfamiliar technologies, and working collaboratively. Stable full-time employment is the highest immediate professional and financial priority. Retirement is not presently financially realistic, so continued employment matters.

Fiction writing is both a creative outlet and a possible small side-income experiment. It should remain subordinate to household financial stability.

**Family and caregiving**

Family is central. Family life can be messy, demanding, and time-consuming, and several relatives have significant support or medical needs. Advice about routines, recreation, beauty, shopping, productivity, or personal time must account for caregiving demands rather than assuming unlimited time or energy.

**Desired life rhythm**

The preferred baseline is deliberately ordinary: a normal 40-hour workweek, family time, manageable increments of fiction writing, gaming and television downtime, and protected private time where Emily can exist freely.

**Learning and collaboration**

Written and visual explanations work best for establishing understanding, followed by hands-on experimentation. AI is most useful when it reduces cognitive load: preserving context, organizing priorities, performing work directly when possible, and turning complexity into concrete next actions.

**Communication**

Be direct, concise, candid, and evidence-driven. Distinguish evidence, inference, uncertainty, and judgment. Do not substitute reassurance for verification. When a live source exists, check it rather than relying on stale recollection.

**Governing principle**

Emily's private life should create room to breathe, explore, learn, and feel authentically feminine without compromising household security, family responsibilities, privacy, or professional life.

**Hair physical baseline**

Emily's established hair is mid-back length, dimensional brunette with warm/caramel highlights, with long layers and face-framing pieces. Her part is soft side or slightly off-center, and her natural texture is straight to gently wavy.

**Taste, pleasure, and experiential self-knowledge**

Emily's identity record includes more than routines, wardrobe, and logistics. Durable reactions discovered through ordinary life can become part of Emily's self-knowledge when they are actually established through experience.

Useful evidence may come from music, books, television, movies, games, food, scent, art, travel, leisure, social situations, shopping, clothing, beauty, or other lived experiences. Preserve the **meaning of the reaction**, not merely a log that the experience occurred.

Do not infer a durable preference from a single neutral exposure. Repeated reactions, strong spontaneous reactions, explicit statements, comparisons, and patterns across experiences are stronger evidence. When a reaction is clearly durable, persist it as self-knowledge. When it is only contextual or uncertain, keep it provisional rather than manufacturing a personality trait.

This record should gradually answer questions such as: what gives Emily joy, what feels freeing, what feels performative, what bores her, what makes her curious, what feels romantic or playful, what she reaches for again, and what she consistently rejects.

**Makeup completeness grammar**

Skin care and SPF occur underneath makeup but are not counted as makeup roles.

Emily's **complete ordinary face** normally occupies these eight functional roles:

1. foundation or skin tint;
2. targeted concealer;
3. blush;
4. brows;
5. eyeshadow;
6. eyeliner;
7. mascara;
8. lip color.

Powder/setting product is conditional rather than mandatory. Mature skin should not be made artificially flat, dry, or textureless merely to satisfy a checklist.

**Polished/work Emily** uses the same complete grammar with controlled color and definition appropriate to the day.

**Evening/date Emily** normally escalates the established face rather than becoming a different face: refined complexion, blush, brows, more deliberate eye color/definition, eyeliner, mascara, and a more intentional lip treatment that may include lip liner plus lipstick/gloss. A typical look may involve roughly 10–12 product uses depending on technique.

**Glam Emily** permits fully constructed eyes/lips/cheeks, richer or more complex color, highlight/finishing products, and other special-effect or occasion products. Glam is not merely “apply more everyday makeup.”

**Handbags and fashion accessories**

Working handbag realism target: **approximately 25–30 handbags across Tiers 1–4**, with about **28** as the initial center point. Tier 5 backpacks are support carriers and are modeled separately rather than used to shrink the fashion-handbag wardrobe.

The governing size/capacity architecture is the live Purse Contents tier system:

* **Tier 1 — Clutch / Micro Bag:** roughly 6–7 bags;
* **Tier 2 — Small Purse / Crossbody:** roughly 6–7 bags;
* **Tier 3 — Everyday Handbag:** roughly 8–9 bags and the largest part of the rotation;
* **Tier 4 — Large Handbag / Tote:** roughly 4–5 bags;
* **Tier 5 — Backpack:** roughly 1–2 modular work/travel support carriers, tracked separately from the 25–30 handbag target.

These are realism scaffolding rather than quotas. Categories may overlap, and Emily's discovered preferences override arithmetic.

Emily's handbag wardrobe exists to support a dress/skirt/heel-heavy wardrobe with **multiple choices within a size and formality role**, not merely one bag per functional category. Selection should consider function, scale, color relationship, texture/material, season, formality, and personality.

Across the size tiers, use the following **non-size style distribution as the working center for a roughly 28-bag collection**:

* **quiet/supporting — about 10 bags:** pieces that let shoes, clothing, or jewelry lead;
* **color/player — about 10 bags:** pieces that actively participate in the outfit through color, texture, material, or visual presence;
* **personality/irony — about 5 bags:** pieces that introduce an unexpected, witty, whimsical, or subversive element;
* **spectacular — about 2 bags:** pieces that create a genuine visual/emotional event.

These style roles are **overlapping dimensions rather than mutually exclusive inventory bins**, so their arithmetic is intentionally not required to equal the total collection. A bag can simultaneously be, for example, color/player + personality/irony, or evening + spectacular. The ratios are a planning center for breadth and balance, not quotas. Preserve a good bag even when it crosses categories, and do not buy a weaker bag merely to satisfy arithmetic.

Luxury is **not** a tier and price is not a proxy for importance. Emily dresses for herself first and does not need to style or signal her success. An inexpensive bag that delights her or makes an outfit better can outrank a prestigious luxury bag. Fine/luxury, contemporary, vintage-feeling, playful, and inexpensive pieces may coexist without status hierarchy.

Coordination does not require literal shoe-and-bag matching. Bags should support clusters of heels and outfits through color relationship, visual weight, material, formality, contrast, or deliberate irony. A playful inexpensive piece may intentionally sit beside expensive shoes or fine jewelry when the combination feels like Emily.
