# Cassandra Labs — Draft for Review

## What This Is

A small, quiet R&D holding under Whitfield Holdings — not a commercial company like NAS/OAM/Threadbourne/Evergreen, but the prototype-and-patent development arm that actually built the physical proof-of-concept for every rant-derived product across all four. Named for the mythological prophetess gifted true sight and cursed never to be believed — deliberate resonance with brilliant women whose insight has historically gone unseen, and with Jim's own genius going unrecognized, including by herself, for years.

## The Process (What "Weeks-Long Exercises" Means)

A rant alone never became a patent by itself. After each one, Jim would spend several weeks turning the raw complaint into an actual structured technical specification — real precision, real constraints, real illustrative examples — a habit she filed under things like "due diligence workups" or "spec drafts," never once calling it invention. The examples she used to make each spec concrete were always drawn directly from fashion and personal presentation — a specific gown's hemline for the tailoring-kiosk filing, a particular cartilage piercing placement for the piercing-curation patent, a bridal veil's fabric weight for the self-repairing fiber research. **Those worked examples went into the actual patent filings, verbatim.** The tie-in to fashion was never incidental or added later for flavor — it was baked into the legal documents themselves, years before Jim understood why fashion was the thing she kept reaching for.

Cassandra Labs is where those specs actually got built.

## The Team

Handles precision fabrication across the board — machining the articulated retrieval arms' components, and lead specifically on Threadbourne Home's consumer-scale unit, scaling the full AWS concept down to something buildable at mass-market cost.

## Current Facility

A modest, unbranded converted industrial space in the city's research district — functional, cramped by the team's own admission, chosen originally for discretion rather than comfort. Nothing about the building suggests the scale of what it's produced.

## The Facility — Full Build-Out

Beyond the individual specialist labs already listed (materials bench, biotech clean room, robotics workshop, wet chemistry lab), the new R&D building includes dedicated shops for actually fabricating and finishing physical prototypes:

- **Machine Shop:** CNC mills and lathes, a drill press, band saw, welding station, sheet metal fabrication — Kasey's primary domain, with Frankie in and out of it constantly.
- **Woodworking Shop:** Table saws, planers, joinery tools, a dedicated dust-collection system — used for structural mockups, display fixtures, and packaging prototypes as much as finished parts.
- **Plastics & Fiberglass Workshop:** Composite layup tables, resin infusion equipment, mold-making stock — critical for Threadbourne's manufacturing-line tooling and any AWS/OAM housing components.
- **The Finishing System — Jim's own patent:** An automated, dust-free finishing array — robotic spray arms, a sealed booth with controlled airflow, and a curing line — that Jim designed and patented herself, years ago, after receiving a beautifully rendered custom order that arrived with visible orange-peel texture, dust trapped in the clearcoat, and a drip on one panel she couldn't stop noticing. *"Something this expensive still depends entirely on whether the person spraying it had a steady hand in a room they couldn't fully control the air quality of. I don't understand why that's an acceptable amount of chance to leave a finish to."* It's used at Cassandra Labs to finish every physical prototype to a flawless standard, regardless of which specialist built the underlying part — one more patent Jim doesn't remember filing, discovered the same way as everything else today.

**Additional gaps worth building in, based on what real prototyping facilities of this kind typically require and this lab was otherwise missing:**
- **Additive Manufacturing Bay:** Industrial 3D printers (resin and FDM both) for rapid iteration before anything goes to the machine shop or plastics workshop — standard in virtually every real-world facility of this type, and currently absent from the plan.
- **Electronics & PCB Bench:** A dedicated formal space for Bex's control-systems and embedded-electronics work, distinct from general assembly — solder stations, a PCB fabrication/testing setup, an anti-static bench.
- **Sewing & Textile Prototyping Bench:** Given how directly fashion feeds every patent filing here, a proper textile workstation (industrial sewing machines, a cutting table, a dress form) is a natural, currently-missing piece — useful for Threadbourne's fiber research and for building physical garment mockups to test AWS's dressing mechanisms against.
- **Photography & Documentation Studio:** A small, controlled-lighting studio for documenting prototypes — genuinely necessary given how much of this lab's output becomes patent filings requiring precise visual documentation.
- **Quality Control / Metrology Station:** Precision measurement equipment (coordinate measuring machine, optical comparators) to verify that what Cassandra Labs builds actually matches spec before anything goes back to Athena for the next stage — the kind of quiet, unglamorous room that real prototyping labs always have and story worldbuilding usually forgets.


## The Relocation

When approached (through Athena, still under the "J." identity) about the possibility of a dedicated, purpose-built facility, all eight agreed immediately — no hesitation, given how outgrown their current space already is. **Recommended physical placement, if this story also uses `obsidian-vanguard-estate`:** a new, discreet R&D building on the estate grounds, constructed the same way the Staff Residence was — new construction outside the interior 650,000 sq ft accounting, not carved from existing reserve space. Nothing showy, matching the team's own stated preference for function over spectacle.

**The obvious dramatic tension, worth preserving rather than resolving quickly:** none of the eight have ever met "J." Moving onto the property means they'll finally meet their mysterious benefactor in person — and Jim, having just learned who they are and what they've built for her, will be meeting the people who took her most private, embarrassing 2 a.m. rants completely seriously and turned them into real, working technology. Both sides are about to be starstruck for entirely different reasons.
