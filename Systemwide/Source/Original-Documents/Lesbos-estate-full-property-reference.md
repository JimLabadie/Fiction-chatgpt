## Property Overview & Financial Metrics
- **Location:** The former Galleria at University Park — an upscale lifestyle center footprint on a heavily wooded, sprawling 42-acre parcel in a premier suburban enclave.
- **Acquisition & Conversion Cost:** $142,500,000 (full unencumbered cash acquisition of the 650,000-square-foot retail and structural footprint) plus an $85,000,000 master architectural rezoning, demolition, and luxury residential conversion budget, funded outright by the household's primary anchor.
- **Total Interior Square Footage:** 650,000 square feet of climate-controlled, structurally reinforced private living space.
- **Exterior Grounds & Lot Size:** 42.5 beautifully landscaped acres — mature old-growth oaks, manicured private gardens, secured perimeter greenbelts, and a fully enclosed, high-security private compound wall.

## The Outside & Arrival Experience

**The Approach & Gates:** A private, limestone-clad security pavilion with bullet-resistant glass and biometric vehicle recognition, opening onto a gated, half-mile tree-lined maple drive — and past the security pavilion, the drive gives way to something entirely unexpected: a sprawling, hyper-modular arrival plaza rendered in a vivid, unmistakably playful palette of magenta, hot pink, and teal against crisp white structural panels. The plaza is built on a reconfigurable grid — a heavy-duty, commercial-grade track-and-lock system that lets water features, sculptural slide-style transitions, and turf-integrated lawn segments physically relocate and rearrange over time, so the arrival sequence itself is never quite static. A grand, fully transparent, wheelchair-accessible elevator tower anchors the plaza as its central focal point, glowing softly at night, offering seamless universal access into the residential core. Every pathway is finished in high-gloss, weather-resistant material in the same bright signature palette — the whole approach reads less like a security checkpoint and more like driving straight into an oversized, lovingly built dollhouse, deliberately, joyfully at odds with the fortress-grade security tucked discreetly behind it.

**The Covered Parking Structure** (~25,000 sq ft): The former multi-level parking deck, retrofitted into a climate-controlled, subterranean-access motor court for 60+ vehicles, with EV charging, a professional detailing bay, and private elevators to the residential core.

**The Personal Fleet:** Fixed estate canon. Legally owned through the household's private holding company, never titled to any individual, the same ownership logic as everything else at this scale. Like the arrival plaza, the fleet fully commits to the dollhouse-bright aesthetic rather than treating it as a one-off: a vivid magenta convertible sports car (Corvette-style), a retired, privately converted Boeing 767 as the household's jet — sized deliberately to comfortably seat the full household plus staff and security in real comfort, rather than a cramped standard midsize private jet — a helicopter, a yacht plus a small complement of smaller boats, and — deliberately not drawn from any specific licensed toy lineup, an original addition to the household's own aesthetic rather than a direct homage — a small, absurdly girly pink convertible city car (Mini Cooper-style) in the same magenta/hot-pink/teal palette as the main gates. **Rose gold is a recurring material motif across the household generally** — hardware, trim, fixtures, accents — not confined to the fleet alone.

**Small Personal Transport:** A house fleet of small, everyday transport devices for getting around the grounds and the estate's vast interior — a mix of cruiser bikes and stand-up/seated scooters, all finished in the same signature magenta/hot-pink/teal palette with rose gold accents, kept on hand in generous, oversized numbers the same way the suite capacity is (not tied to the current household's headcount — plenty exist for guests, visitors, and future additions alike). A couple of stylish pink mopeds round out the two-wheeled category for longer grounds trips.

**The Family Camper:** An oversized, multi-room motorhome — original design, not drawn from any licensed product line, but unapologetically absurd in scale — with expanding pop-out components (a full pull-out kitchen, a small water slide, hammocks, a pop-up tent module) that unfold well past what the vehicle's closed footprint would suggest. Sized the same headcount-independent way as everything else — built enormous on principle, not fitted precisely to whoever's currently living at the estate. Same signature palette, rose gold trim throughout.

**Off-Road & Grounds Vehicles:** A pink Jeep-style 4x4 and a dune buggy, both practical for the estate's turf field, greenhouse paths, and wooded grounds, and both fully committed to the house palette regardless of practicality.

**Additional Watercraft:** Alongside the yacht and smaller boats, a couple of jet skis and a few kayaks round out the water side of the fleet, kept at the marina/boathouse with everything else.

**The running joke, played entirely straight:** among staff and in the household's own private, affectionate gallows humor, there's a standing (obviously false, never depicted as literally true) bit that prolonged proximity to the fleet, the arrival plaza, or the estate's aesthetic generally risks "feminizing" whoever spends too long around it — a tongue-in-cheek exaggeration of just how thoroughly, inescapably girly this whole world is. Staff and visitors alike are in on the joke; nothing about it is ever real.

**Grounds & Recreation:** A regulation-sized soccer/multi-sport turf field, a private competition-grade heated infinity pool with cabanas, an organic greenhouse and farm-to-table garden, and a fully enclosed private daycare and Montessori center with secure rubberized play yards and sensory gardens. *(Exterior grounds — not counted against the 650,000 sq ft interior total. Present on the property regardless of whether the current household includes children.)*

**The Staff Residence:** A separate residential tower on the grounds, apart from the main structure, holding 20–25 nicely appointed staff apartments with their own dedicated parking. New construction on the estate's 42.5 acres rather than converted mall footprint, so it does not draw against the 650,000 sq ft interior total.

**The Cassandra Labs R&D Building:** A separate, discreet research-and-fabrication building on the grounds, new construction like the Staff Residence rather than converted mall footprint. Sized modestly for a nine-person team with real room to grow, holding individual specialist labs, a machine shop, woodworking shop, plastics/fiberglass workshop, the anchor's own patented automated finishing system, an additive manufacturing bay, electronics/PCB bench, sewing and textile prototyping bench, documentation studio, and quality control station.

**The Deep Archive:** A four-story underground couture storage and preservation facility, excavated beneath the property rather than converted from existing structure, so — like the parking structure and the Staff Residence — it does not count against the 650,000 sq ft interior total. It connects underground to both the main house and directly to the Whitfield Museum of Fashion's own climate-controlled special-storage level — making the Deep Archive the shared spine that feeds the everyday wardrobe and later seeds the museum's exhibits, rather than three separate, disconnected collections.
- **Discreet Delivery Elevator:** A single hidden elevator shaft, positioned at the architectural hub of the residential suite wing, connecting three points: the Anchor's Dressing Room, the Partner Dressing Room, and, beneath them, the Grand Wardrobe Complex and Deep Archive. This lets any member of the household move between their own closet and the shared partner closet privately, and lets deep inventory move up from the Archive without a visible trip through the rest of the house.

## The Grand Interior Layout & Specialized Wing Design

### 1. The Family Sanctuary & Master Quarters (~26,000 sq ft) — the Residential Home Wing

**Central Living Room:** The everyday heart of the home wing — oversized, deep-seated sectionals, plush woven textiles, and warm ambient lighting built for genuine daily relaxation and group gatherings rather than formal display.

**The Anchor's Suite:** A deeply personal sanctuary, architecturally sculpted with soft, curving contours meant to evoke warmth, safety, and enclosure — a cocoon of comfort rather than a showpiece. The palette commits fully to the estate's signature dollhouse-bright aesthetic: rich magenta, soft pastel rose, and playful blush wrap the room in light. Curated with the discerning eye of someone building a genuine haven for women who love women, the suite features fine art and small sculpture by feminist artists, chosen with real intention rather than as decoration. At its center sits a massive, harem-style bed — an extravagant haven of comfort, layered with a seemingly limitless array of plush, velvet pillows in luxurious pink fabrics. Ambient lighting is soft, warm, and artfully layered throughout, built for genuine intimacy rather than a showpiece's formal glow — the suite is built with the physical capacity to comfortably host a substantial gathering (up to roughly 20) for a shared night of intimacy.

The adjoining ensuite is an expansive, highly detailed, spa-inspired haven thoughtfully designed to accommodate 14 women preparing for the day simultaneously in absolute luxury. Its centerpiece is the **Cascade Array** — a circular arrangement of twelve individually zoned rainfall stations, each reading the entrant's biometrics on approach to pre-set water temperature, pressure, and a light, personalized mist of scent, capable of running all twelve simultaneously without any drop in pressure — flanking three gargantuan soaking tubs wrapped in polished marble at staggered depths: the largest seating up to ten for shared morning routines, two smaller flanking tubs seating four apiece for quieter company. To effortlessly handle the peak morning rush, the space is generously appointed with fourteen high-end, salon-quality vanity stations, each equipped with professional haircare and cosmetic tools, an illuminated mirror, and premium amenities, so all 14 guests can get ready at once. Each vanity mirror doubles as an OAM Aura-Matrix Preview Array — an AR-style preview surface that can render a hairstyle, makeup look, or color change before OAM's actual automated systems execute it. Eight private water closets line the far wall. Comprehensive heating systems — radiant heated floors and plush towel warmers throughout — and four subtly placed, curtained intimate nooks, each built for two, combine to weave complete warmth, cozy comfort, and an inviting atmosphere throughout the grand architectural layout. Storage is seamless throughout, including a dedicated robe-and-shoe alcove for all fourteen, leading into the two dressing room complexes described immediately below.

**The Anchor's Dressing Room:** Accessible directly from the shared ensuite, this is the anchor's own private closet complex — twenty individual walk-in closets, each fitted with three slide-out storage units, organized by item type (eveningwear, daywear, outerwear, loungewear, activewear, accessories, and further categories as the collection grows). One wall holds a reinforced door with a biometric scanner, opening into a combined vault holding both the household's fine jewelry and the Footwear Vault collection — the anchor's alone to access, keyed to her biometrics specifically. Because Jim personally owns a stake in a broad fashion-house portfolio, these closets are not empty by default — any time a house within that portfolio releases something new, one sample arrives automatically, in her current size, without her ever having to request or shop for it. A single standing, visible dressmaker's-form mannequin stands at the center of the room. The system's mobile retrieval robots (docked in the Partner Dressing Room) travel in through the connecting door whenever the anchor's mannequin needs dressing.

**The Partner Dressing Room:** A second closet complex, identical in format to the anchor's, also accessible from the shared ensuite — thirteen individual walk-in closets, three slide-out units apiece, organized the same way. Shared among the anchor's committed partners rather than assigned as strict one-per-person. Whether this room starts stocked or empty depends on how each partner arrived: a partner promised temporary courier-delivered clothing will have that arrive within the same day it's promised; a partner who was previously household staff and is only now formally joining the family brings her own real, existing wardrobe with her, moved in from staff housing the same day her new status is confirmed. This room holds thirteen standing, visible dressmaker's-form mannequins — each permanently paired with, and standing directly beside, one specific partner's closet. Six mobile retrieval robots (warehouse-style bases with vertical telescoping reach and articulated human-mimetic arms and hands) dock in dedicated alcoves along one blank wall of this room when idle, and travel between closets — and into the Anchor's Dressing Room, or down to the Grand Wardrobe Complex and Deep Archive via the Discreet Delivery Elevator, when needed. There is no distance limit on where the robots will travel; they recharge in their alcoves as needed. The robots themselves are the only concealed component; the mannequins are always visible, standing fixtures.

**Individual Partner Suites:** Distinct private bedroom suites flanking the anchor's suite, each an independent luxury apartment with its own morning kitchen, spa bathroom, walk-in dressing room, and private sitting lounge — built out well beyond what any typical household needs, occupied one at a time as the household actually grows rather than all at once on day one. These dressing rooms are ordinary closets only — no WIS interface, no mannequins, no robots. AWS technology exists in exactly four places total: the Anchor's and Partner Dressing Rooms (residential use), Cassandra Labs (development/testing), and the Innovation Showroom (client/investor demonstration) — never in these individual suites. This is a soft design ceiling, not a hard cap — nothing about the architecture stops the household from growing dramatically further; the reserve space exists precisely so the building can keep growing rather than running out of room.

**Family Dining Room:** A dedicated, inviting dining space separate from the formal banquet hall, centered on a large wooden table sized for the household's daily meals together.

**Functional Daily Kitchen:** A warm, practical kitchen tailored for routine family cooking — open shelving, well-loved cookware, a cozy breakfast booth — distinct from the more formal Epicurean Kitchen elsewhere on the property.

**Family Room:** A casual, low-key space set aside for informal recreation, media, board games, and quiet evenings in.

**Private Outdoor Terrace:** A secluded garden terrace with comfortable patio seating and lush greenery.

**The Great Gathering Hall** (~40,000 sq ft): The former central concourse under a soaring glass atrium — custom acoustic paneling, massive stone fireplaces, continuous conversational seating built for the whole family, and scalable enough to comfortably host staff and guests for larger occasions.

### 2. The Epicurean Kitchen & Dining Pavilions (~45,000 sq ft)

The zoning is built for collaboration — several people can work dinner prep, baking, and plating simultaneously without anyone bumping elbows.

**Dining Spaces:** An intimate family dining room, a formal banquet hall for up to 500, and an outdoor kitchen terrace overlooking the pool.

## Full Staffing Plan

*Grounded in real-world staffing practice and current U.S. market compensation for private-estate and family-office roles. Live-in roles typically command the lower end of a range in exchange for housing/meals; non-resident roles the higher end.*

### Staffing Philosophy (Fixed In-Universe Canon)
Every role on this estate, without exception — every table below, including the museum wing — is filled exclusively by women who are lesbian, cisgender or transgender. Treat it as absolute and non-negotiable across every position — leadership, household staff, wellness/spa, childcare, transportation, and museum staff alike. This is the same rule that governs the wider empire's workforce (see `fashion-empire`'s `holdings-map.md`), including the practical femme/butch role-fit texture that becomes visible once the empire's workforce scales into the tens of thousands. At estate scale, that same texture shows up more subtly — grounds, security, and physically demanding maintenance roles trending butch; hospitality, childcare, wardrobe, and salon/spa roles trending femme — without needing its own separate rule.

### A note on availability vs. activity
Several roles on this estate need to be *available* around the clock without being *actively working* the whole time. Real staffing practice handles this with a mix of:
- **Shift coverage** (security, some medical) — staff are actively on duty and paid full wages for the shift, because the job is the availability itself.
- **On-call/standby pay** — a lower retainer-style rate for hours where staff aren't on-site or actively working but must be reachable within a defined window.
- **Contracted/retained professionals** — not headcount at all; paid a retainer for availability plus fee-for-service when actually used.
