# Change Toolkit — Addendum: Digital-Life Swap

*A reusable sub-pattern under Module 1 Sections 3/3A, for any instance where what swaps is a character's personal digital footprint and its associated social record — not their body, mind, or a physical possession. Distinct from an ordinary object/possession swap (like the Joe/Chloe footwear instance) because the swapped material is a record of a life — messages, photos, relationships, social history — and it carries a retroactive belief change in third parties, not just a redistributed object. Fill in the bracketed fields per-instance; the load-bearing rules below (the four questions and the two standing patterns) are the reusable part and shouldn't be rewritten per-story.*

## Core Distinction

A **Digital-Life Swap** exchanges the personal (non-professional) digital footprint of two or more characters — the record of who they've talked to, what they've said, what they look like doing it, and what history other people believe they share. Nothing about either character's actual body, mind, memory, or real-world competence moves. What moves is the record, and — critically — what other people believe about that record.

This is NOT the same as a Perceptual Swap (Section 3E): a Perceptual Swap changes how observers *currently perceive* a character in person, in real time. A Digital-Life Swap changes what the *digital record* shows and what third parties believe about *history* — it's a records-and-belief swap, not a live-perception swap. A story could in principle use both, but they answer different questions and shouldn't be conflated.

## The Four Governing Questions (answer explicitly for every instance — do not default)

Every Digital-Life Swap instance must settle these four questions before a scene is written. Leaving one unanswered and defaulting to the narrower reading is the most common failure mode observed so far.

### 1. Content-Level vs. Identity-Level
- **Content-level:** Existing relationships stay with the right person; only specific facts, quotes, or attributions get reassigned between people who *already know* the third party in question. Produces detail-level bluffing among people the character genuinely knows.
- **Identity-level:** The third party's entire belief about *who they've been relating to* changes — including third parties who have **never met** the other swapped character at all. Produces exposure to total strangers with an established "relationship" and zero real information behind it.
- **This instance:** [content-level / identity-level — state which]

### 2. Retroactive-Only vs. Ongoing
- **Retroactive-only:** The swap is a one-time edit to the existing historical record. New content created after the trigger is unaffected and behaves normally.
- **Ongoing:** The swap is a live, continuing property. It applies with full force to anything newly created going forward — a text sent five minutes ago, a photo taken this morning — exactly as it applies to years-old content.
- **This instance:** [retroactive-only / ongoing — state which; do not assume "retroactive" implies "settled and static" without confirming]

### 3. Scope Window
- Does the swap reach back to **before** the characters' relationship/acquaintance began, or only to the record generated **since**? (In the source instance, the swap was scoped to couple-era-only: pre-relationship history and existing real relationships — e.g., in-laws who already know the character as family — stayed completely real and untouched.)
- **This instance:** [full history / relationship-era-only / other window — describe]

### 4. Photo/Video Representation
Three structurally different outcomes all sound similar in casual description — confirm which is meant against at least two concrete, opposite-direction examples before writing any scene with photos or video:
1. **Same person, other's style overlay:** the original real person's own face/body appears, but dressed/groomed in the other party's typical style.
2. **Full substitution, own natural style:** the other party's real face/body appears instead, in the *substituted* person's own typical style.
3. **Full substitution, carrying the original moment's real presentation:** the other party's real face/body appears instead of the original person's, but wearing/styled exactly as the *original* person actually was in that specific moment — identity and presentation move independently, in opposite directions. (This was the outcome confirmed in the source instance, and is easy to under-specify from an abstract "everything swaps" description.)
- **This instance:** [1 / 2 / 3 — state which, and confirm against a concrete example in each direction]

## Standing Pattern A — Retroactive Belief Change (not a cover story)

When Question 1 resolves identity-level and Question 2 resolves ongoing, third parties don't just fail to notice the swap — they **genuinely, permanently believe** the swapped record has always been true. This is a real, actual belief change in every affected third party, not a passing-unnoticed cover story. It is a different category from Module 3's usual "unaware" status (which implies a fact the character is concealing or that simply hasn't come up) — log it in Module 3's Disclosure Status Map as its own status if this addendum's identity-level option is in play.

## Standing Pattern B — No Borrowed Competence

Per Section 3A: unless a specific instance explicitly says otherwise, a Digital-Life Swap moves **Surface/Manifestation** (the record itself) but leaves **Functional Capability**, **Habitual/Automatic Behavior**, and **Declarative Knowledge** untransferred. The swapped characters gain a record other people believe about them; they do not gain the underlying skill, memory, or knowledge that record implies. (An early draft of the source instance mistakenly let this bleed — a character "instinctively" produced professional judgment belonging to the swap partner. This was corrected out; it over-extends a records swap into something closer to a mind/trait swap, which is a different mechanism per Section 0 and should be a separate, deliberate story decision if wanted.)

**Episodic/Personal History does not transfer either** — this is usually the emotional engine of the scene: each character retains full, accurate memory of their own real life, while the record and everyone else's belief describe a past they didn't personally live through in the way it's now attributed to them.

## Section 3C Note

Baseline inventory facts (Section 3C) for a Digital-Life Swap should cover, per character: established relationships and social circles within the swap's scope window (Question 3), typical real-life presentation/style (for cross-referencing against Question 4), and occupation (relevant for confirming what stays excluded — professional accounts and work-related digital content are excluded by default unless a specific instance says otherwise).

## Explicitly Excluded by Default (override per-instance if needed)

- Professional/work accounts and work-related digital content
- Physical bodies, in-person appearance, or gender
- The characters' actual real-world relationships to each other (a swap doesn't reassign or erase who is married to, related to, or dating whom)
- Physical device hardware (only delivered content changes, not ownership)
- Public records — news, professional listings, anything outside personal/social context
