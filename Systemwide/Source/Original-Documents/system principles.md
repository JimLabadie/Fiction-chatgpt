definitions

* gender - refers to anatomical gender
* presentation - refers to anything that is not anatomically changeable. things like hair, piercings and nails are not considered anatomical, because they can easily be changed
* based on the real world masculine presenting is what men wear in the real world, feminine presenting is what women wear in the real world.
* clothes are defined to include all aspects of clothing
* pants have a fabric separator in them they are masculine, this does exclude hosiery (pantyhose, stockings and tights) those are considered feminine.
* shoes will be addressed separately, but the core is men fear feminine shoes, women wear masculine shoes
* accessories include all forms of accessories



background

* this is set in a world that is rigid gender binary, but all aspects of presentation are flipped and rigidly defined.
* this system ignores reality, safety and common sense. 
* its based on the core principles.
* Styling elements are binary.
* some elements of style that are more ambiguous are not ever used (medium length hair is one example)



core principles

images

* all images created are to be full body photo realistic.
* all styling rules are absolute.



character styling

top level principles

* men have male anatomy, but present feminine
* women have a female anatomy, but present masculine
* men and women present in almost an extreme interpretation.
* men never will wear pants or any other masculine items, no exceptions.
* Women never will wear feminine items, no exceptions.
* Women will never wear dresses, skirts or any other feminine items no exception. in fact they only ever wear pants.



clothing

* unless a uniform is required for a role characters don't have special clothing based on their role. as an example doctors, lawyers and bankers could wear the same tie in the case of women or heels in the case of men.
* the environment does factor into the degree of formality in what a character wears, but its more environment based than anything else.
* examples of this are formal business is expected in a courtroom, uniforms in a diner or restaurant and an office business casual. these may change based on testing
* uniforms must always be beneath presentation, as an example, overalls for men cant exist because the have pants elements.
* if an item like a jacket is expected for masculine presentation it is expected for feminine presentation, assuing it doesnt violate gender requirements.



hair

* hair is a styling element and therefore its binary based, men can only wear long feminine hair and women only wear short masculine hair.
* there is no neutral or medium hair permitted.



Makeup 

* makeup is considered feminine, for men full makeup is mandatory, female are never allowed to wear it. any real world examples that are masculine or neutral do not exist.



nails

* nails again follow the same rigid binary. manicures and pedicures are mandatory for men, forbidden for women



shoes

* shoes are also rigidly binary, women only wear clearly masculine shoes, men wear only feminine shoes. 
* feminine shoes have a minimum of a 4" heel incline. masculine shoes follow real world height.
* again realism is not a criteria here an example a male hockey player would have skates whose blade have a 4 inch incline.



accessories

* are equally binary, men can only wear feminine accessories and women only use masculine accessories.
* purses are mandatory feminine accessories for men. they are not required to be held, they can be near them.
* accessories are completely optional for men
* men are required to at a minimum wear earrings, necklaces and bracelets
* women generally never wear jewelry



Props

* props that are expected for one gender are expected for another. gender variants are required. a hard hat is an example. a man would have a pink colored hard hat, a woman would have a yellow safety hat.
* Props are context-dependent and must align with the character's role and environment. For example, a hard hat may appear in construction or manufacturing roles, but would not be present in a courtroom or office setting.
