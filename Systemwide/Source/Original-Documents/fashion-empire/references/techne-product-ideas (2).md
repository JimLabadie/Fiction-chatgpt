# Techne — Product Ideas Document

*Running list of computer-controlled equipment concepts, either replacing genuinely manual/legacy processes already sitting in the acquired Metis Werks facility, or filling gaps elsewhere in the manufacturing pipeline. Not yet canon — a working brainstorm document.*

## Identified Replacement Targets (Already Sitting in Metis Werks)

1. **Shuttle looms (×30)** — genuinely obsolete weaving technology, largely phased out of real modern mills decades ago. The most obvious, satisfying first target for a computer-controlled replacement once Techne is operational.
2. **Ring spinning frames (×80)** — labor-intensive, slower, more manual-attention-heavy than the facility's own open-end (OE) rotor spinning units. Currently outnumbers OE units 2:1, skewing the facility toward the older method.
3. **Jigger dyeing machines (×12)** — an older, small-batch, hands-on dyeing method sitting alongside the much more automated jet dyeing and continuous dyeing ranges already on the same floor.

## Additional Computer-Controlled Product Ideas (Brainstorm)

4. **AI-driven fabric defect inspection** — computer-vision-based automated inspection replacing manual visual defect-checking, which is slow, inconsistent, and eye-fatigue-prone even for skilled inspectors. Ties naturally into the Automation & Robotics Lab's machine-vision work already established for Techne.
5. **Automated leather cutting & hide-grading system** — leather cutting is traditionally one of the most wasteful, skill-dependent manual processes in the whole industry (grading hide quality by eye, hand-laying cutting patterns to minimize waste). A computer-vision hide-grading system paired with AI-optimized cutting-layout software could meaningfully cut material waste and standardize quality — a strong commercial licensing product on its own.
6. **Digital draping & 3D-to-pattern generation** — traditional draping is done by hand on a physical dress form. A system that takes a 3D body scan (or the same body-modeling data already used by NAS/OAM) and generates an accurate garment pattern digitally, before any fabric is cut, would reduce sampling waste and speed up design iteration significantly.
7. **Computer-controlled dye-recipe formulation** — matching a dye lot precisely by hand is notoriously difficult and a major source of manufacturing waste (rejected batches, re-dyeing). An automated, sensor-verified dye-formulation system would standardize color matching the way ChromaBlend already does for cosmetics.
8. **Precision hemming & finishing robotics** — the fine "blind stitch" hemming and finishing work on higher-end garments is still largely hand-done even in automated factories. A robotic finishing arm precise enough to match hand-finishing quality would be a genuine, hard technical win worth real licensing value.
9. **Automated embroidery digitizing** — converting a hand-drawn or freehand design into a precise, machine-embroiderable digital pattern is currently a specialized manual skill. Software/hardware that does this automatically, feeding directly into the multi-head embroidery machines already at Metis Werks, would speed up custom and small-batch embroidery work considerably.
