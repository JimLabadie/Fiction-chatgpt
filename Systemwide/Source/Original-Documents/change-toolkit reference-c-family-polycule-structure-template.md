# Change Toolkit — Reference C: Family/Polycule Structure Agreement Template

*A read-only checklist of the categories a blended or multi-partner household typically needs to have decided, for stories involving a polycule or other non-traditional family structure.*

> **Instructions for AI Context Integration:** This is a reference, not a fillable log — read it whenever a story involves a polycule, blended family, or other multi-partner household that needs its internal norms and agreements established. Use it as a prompt list to identify which categories that story's own instance file (or master document) should address, then have the story define the actual content for each — this reference supplies the checklist of *what needs deciding*, not the answers. Names/roles below are genericized (e.g., "incoming member," "household children") — replace with the story's actual cast in that story's own file.

## 1. Agreements & Decisions

- **Sleeping arrangements** — how they're structured and how that's decided/rotated, if applicable.
- **Family calendar** — how it's managed and where household members access it.
- **Voting/decision-making system** — how household-level decisions get made when members disagree (majority, consensus, veto rights, etc.).
- **Communication guidelines** — house norms for conflict conversations (e.g., active-listening structure, "I"-statement framing, an agreed check-in or pause signal).
- **Household contact/reference sheet** — address, emergency contacts, key logistics in one place.
- **Affirmation or appreciation practices** — any structured way members express appreciation to each other, if the story uses one.
- **General boundary-respecting norms** — baseline expectations for privacy and consent within the household.
- **Integrating incoming/newer members** — how a new partner or household member is folded into existing routines and agreements.
- **Guidelines for household children** — age-appropriate privacy, communication expectations, and safety norms for any minors in the household; content here is a per-story decision grounded in that story's specific characters and their ages, not prescribed by this reference.
- **Designated alone/quiet space** — a location and usage rules for members who need solitude.
- **Move-in logistics** — schedule and division of responsibilities when a member relocates in.
- **Shared bathroom logistics** — scheduling/usage guidelines where facilities are shared.
- **Shared food & household supplies** — grocery lists, meal planning, and any designated personal vs. shared snack/drink areas.

## 2. Property Management

- **Inventory of incoming household items** — tracking what each member brought into the shared household.
- **Duplicate-item decisions** — keep, donate, store, or rotate policy for redundant items.
- **Decoration/style decisions** — how a cohesive shared aesthetic is negotiated, and how sentimental items are handled.
- **Disposition of any now-unused prior residences** — sell, rent, keep as a vacation/rotation property, with a pros/cons framework per option.

## 3. Family Traditions

- **Holiday traditions** — which existing traditions carry over, and which are newly combined/created.
- **Birthdays, anniversaries, and other recurring occasions** — how these are celebrated across the whole household.

## 4. Personal Space & Alone Time

- **Designated quiet space** — location and rules (may overlap with 1, listed separately if the story treats it as its own category).
- **General privacy-respecting norms** — baseline expectations distinct from the household-wide boundary norms in Section 1.

## 5. Financial Management

- **Household budgeting system** — how shared finances are structured.
- **Expense tracking** — method and who's responsible.
- **Shared grocery/household lists** — overlaps with Section 1; financial angle specifically covers cost-splitting.
- **Financial planning for a growing household** — implications of new children or new members joining financially.

## 6. Conflict Resolution

- **Standing strategies for disagreements** — the household's agreed approach before conflict escalates.
- **Mediation process** — who mediates (a designated member, an outside party) when the household can't resolve something internally.

## 7. Guest Policy

- **Rules for friends/family visiting** — general visitor norms.
- **Overnight guest guidelines** — a more specific sub-policy where relevant.

## 8. Pet Policy

- **Responsibility division** — who handles feeding, vet care, walks, etc.
- **General logistics** — how pet ownership fits into the shared household structure.

## 9. Technology & Screen Time

- **Household ground rules** — phone, computer, and TV/streaming norms, especially where they intersect with household children.

## 10. Chores & Responsibilities

- **Division of household tasks** — how labor is split among members.
- **Cleaning schedule** — cadence and rotation, if used.

## 11. Family Evolution

- **Process for major changes** — how the household discusses and decides on births, a member leaving, or a new member joining.
- **Parenting roles** — how responsibilities are divided among the adults in the household, especially where not all adults are a given child's biological parent.
- **Trial-period guidelines** — if the household uses a provisional/trial period before someone fully joins, what that period looks like and how it's evaluated.

## 12. Special Occasions Log

- **A running log** — birthdays, anniversaries, and other dates worth tracking across a household large enough that they're easy to lose track of.

---

*This reference is a checklist, not a narrative template — it exists to make sure a story's own instance file has actually addressed each category, the same way Module 1's Section 3C exists to make sure baseline facts are established before a scene assumes them. Content for any category is written by the story, grounded in that story's specific characters and relationships, not supplied here.*
