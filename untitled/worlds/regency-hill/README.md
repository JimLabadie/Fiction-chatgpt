# Regency Hill

**Status:** ESTABLISHED REUSABLE SETTING — LIGHTLY POPULATED, STANDARD REGIONAL INFRASTRUCTURE

## Purpose and scope

Regency Hill is a reusable urban neighborhood in the same unnamed major city as St. Claire. It is a nearby upper-class environment and a normal city neighborhood outside St. Claire's particular community constraints.

Regency Hill uses the same standard regional record infrastructure as St. Claire and future regions. The infrastructure may be complete even when many Regency Hill values remain unspecified. Pencil governs **content depth**, not schema: do not manufacture residents, businesses, transit details, schedules, vehicles, history, or civic detail merely to fill available fields.

Regency Hill is also available as an ordinary outside setting for characters who do not fit St. Claire's particular population/community model, including ordinary non-egg male characters.

## Regional architecture — approved 2026-09-27

* A reusable world named **Template** is to define the canonical empty regional skeleton.
* Template, St. Claire, Regency Hill, and future regions use identical standard infrastructure/page names. Region-specific extensions may exist in addition to the standard skeleton.
* Template defines where information belongs and the fields/relationships available. It does **not** impose St. Claire's degree of completeness on every region.
* St. Claire is intentionally highly specified. Regency Hill uses the same schema but may remain much less rigid and leave unused facts unknown until needed.
* Existing St. Claire page boundaries are not automatically authoritative architecture. Named business/location pages and `Development — ...` pages may be AI/WIP artifacts. Their contents must be audited and normalized rather than blindly copied into Template.
* The Template page list and field schema are developed **incrementally**. Every approved decision is merged into GitBook as it is made; later decisions may revise it. There is no waiting for a final or complete schema before persistence.
* **Persistence workflow:** approved work is live work. Merge approved decisions immediately as development proceeds. Do not create a limbo state such as “waiting for final,” “pending promotion,” or “not yet canonical” merely because further changes are expected. Normal later revision is the mechanism for change.

### Development retirement and variance preservation

* **Established entities never disappear during Development cleanup.** Any additional person, household, organization/business, place, pet, or other established entity found in a Development record is migrated into its proper maintained record. Duplicate records for the same entity are consolidated without losing valid established facts.
* **Non-entity differences do not block cleanup.** When Development and the maintained record differ in a rule, mechanic, guidance, descriptive fact, schema instruction, ratio, process, or other non-entity material, do not stop the retirement pass to adjudicate the conflict.
* Preserve the **Development-side version only** of each unresolved non-entity difference in a dedicated **variance-root** record. The variance record is recovery evidence for later review; it is not a second governing source and does not replace the maintained version.
* A variance entry must identify its Development source and the subject of the difference closely enough to recover the Development version later. Do not copy the maintained version merely to create a side-by-side duplicate.
* Once all entities and valid entity facts have been migrated and every unresolved non-entity Development variance has been captured in variance-root, the corresponding Development file can be retired. Development files are therefore temporary recovery inputs, not permanent parallel sources of truth.
* File naming for these recovery records uses **variance-root** as the root name so they are unmistakably separate from governing regional records.

### Record normalization requirements

* **People** store people and their durable personal facts.
* **Households** store household relationships and household-level facts.
* **Organizations** store the operating facts of businesses/institutions.
* **Places** store physical premises, geography, buildings, units, and spatial facts.
* A named business can therefore involve an Organization, a Place, and People records rather than requiring a standalone business page.
* Named employees remain People assigned to Organizations; their biographies are not duplicated into Organization records.
* **Canonical work schedules belong to the Organization record.** If a story character enters an organization Wednesday at 3:00 PM, its normal schedule must be capable of resolving who normally works at that time so later scenes remain consistent.
* Staffing guidance/reference material answers how many and what kinds of employees a business of a given type/size plausibly needs. The resulting staffing structure and schedule for a specific business belong to that Organization.
* If a separate WIP/development file exists only because a governing record lacked a needed field, repair the governing schema and migrate valid information rather than preserving the workaround as a separate standard record type.
* Transportation and other mundane continuity systems matter where a region is modeled deeply: public transit, vehicle access/ownership, ordinary movement, employment, schedules, and similar facts need authoritative homes in the schema. St. Claire may resolve these extensively; Regency Hill is not required to fill them merely for completeness.

## Established identity

* **Name:** Regency Hill
* **Type:** Urban neighborhood in an unnamed major city
* **Class/economic register:** Lower end of upper class — affluent, polished, expensive, visibly comfortable without everyone being ultra-wealthy
* In the same city as St. Claire and directly borders St. Claire
* Regency Hill remains outside St. Claire; proximity does not confer St. Claire residency, membership, business, community rules, or bindings
* Regency Hill has zoning/geographic organization appropriate to a normal urban neighborhood. It does **not** inherit St. Claire's six-zone scheme.

## General texture

Regency Hill can plausibly support attractive walkable commercial frontage and expensive independent retail alongside an affluent residential character. This is a broad register, not permission to invent a complete commercial district or fixed architectural history.

## Current story binding — Rich Menace, Paris Edition

* The opening encounter occurs on a weekday outside Maison Belle.
* Friday morning is the controlling chronology; approved Chapter 1 prose retains an older late-afternoon/fading-light inconsistency explicitly accepted by Jim without manuscript revision.
* Tim Johnson is waiting for a bus because his car died/is being repaired; he has a laptop backpack and ordinary clothes.
* Allison Kensington approaches in a polished expensive fitted skirt suit, blouse, restrained jewelry, hair and makeup, and spectacular high stilettos worth thousands; exact brands and colors are not established.
* Tim is a Regency Hill resident with a clean, organized apartment a couple of miles from the Maison Belle bus stop.
* Allison is also a Regency Hill resident; her house is at the opposite end of Regency Hill from Tim/Maison Belle, along the edge touching St. Claire. It is warm, feminine, and family-capable. Its proximity does not make the property or Allison part of St. Claire.
