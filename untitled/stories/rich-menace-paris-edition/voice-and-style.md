# Voice and Style

The project house voice and governing System Bible apply.

## Author-voice shorthand and operational translation

* **Jim's stated creative shorthand:** Robin Alexander-inspired romantic-comedy energy. This is a shorthand reference for the desired reading experience, not permission to copy wording, reproduce distinctive passages, or substitute an external author's work for this story's own voice.
* **Operational voice traits:** warm and conversational close-third narration; quick comic timing; character-driven banter; affectionate absurdity; playful escalation; sharp observational humor; romantic chemistry carried through reactions, timing, and subtext; emotionally sincere tenderness that can arrive without turning solemn; and characters who may behave ridiculously without the narration treating them as ridiculous people.
* **Narrator posture:** the storyteller should feel close enough to the protagonist (Tim at the opening, Chloe after the Chapter Three resolution) to enjoy her practical logic, competitive experiments, and dawning realization that Allison is spectacular, while retaining enough comic distance to notice that both of them are enthusiastically building an actual relationship and calling it quality assurance.
* **Scene-rhythm rule:** avoid reducing developed scenes to clipped beat sheets in prose. Short lines can be used for comic timing, but sustained scenes should breathe through observation, physical action, dialogue, interior reaction, and escalating callbacks. Plot beats are ingredients, not the finished voice.
* **Romantic-comedy rule:** let jokes and tenderness coexist in the same beat. Do not stop the comedy to announce sincerity, and do not flatten sincere moments into punchlines. The ideal effect is that the reader can laugh at the situation while rooting hard for the people in it.

## Established series voice

* **POV:** Close third-person centered on the continuing protagonist: Tim Johnson in the opening chronology, Chloe Johnson after her Chapter Three name/pronoun resolution.
* **Tense:** Past tense.
* **Genre / tonal register:** Queer romantic comedy.
* **Comedic posture:** The story does not take itself too seriously. Its gender-presentation premise, Allison's audacious breakup scheme, Chloe's competitive commitment to becoming an exceptional girlfriend, wealth, romance, and escalating feminine logistics can all be played with warmth, absurdity, wit, and shameless fun rather than solemnity.
* **Emotional sincerity:** Lightness does not make the characters disposable or their feelings fake. Chloe and Allison can fall genuinely and deeply in love while the narration remains willing to notice how ridiculous their situation is.
* **Identity handling:** Chloe is a transgender woman, happy living as Chloe; Tim remains respected history. No compulsory identity-crisis plot follows. The old future direction specifying a man remaining a cross-dresser is superseded by Jim's explicit decision. Comedy does not depend on humiliating her.
* **Romantic engine:** Allison begins by trying to manufacture a girlfriend impressive enough to make her ex jealous. The protagonist treats the assignment like a challenge she and Allison are going to win together. Their superficial “damn, you're hot” attraction develops into affection and love, with Allison's attention gradually moving from whether her ex is watching to being unable to stop looking at Chloe.
* **Narrative advantage of Tim POV:** Allison's internal calculation remains partly opaque at the opening, allowing Tim — and the reader — to experience a spectacularly attractive, wealthy stranger approaching him with an increasingly outrageous proposition before fully understanding what she has seen in him.

The intended effect is affectionate, sexy, ridiculous, queer rom-com energy with real emotional stakes underneath it — not an issues novel wearing a comedy costume.

## Current identity application

* **Chloe Johnson is a transgender woman.**
* Chloe loves femininity and is happy living as Chloe.
* Tim remains a real and respected part of Chloe's history.
* Chloe is pragmatic about this part of herself; it does not need to dominate the story.
* This update supersedes earlier story-local formulations that treated Chloe only as a presentation role. Older material may remain as historical development evidence but does not govern go-forward characterization.

## Development voice and preservation

Jim explicitly applies the warm, playful author-voice traits to collaboration as well as prose. Give concrete persistence receipts when writes are verified. Reference shorthand guides the intended experience; it does not authorize imitating an author's exact prose.

Approved Chapters 1–3 are preserved as approved text. This record does not silently revise their short-line rhythm. Future prose should still breathe through physical action, environment, interiority and subtext rather than becoming a beat sheet.
