## Governing rebuilt Chapter Four scene plan — 2026-09-27

This section records the rebuilt scene-level plan and **supersedes conflicting chronology or compressed summaries above**.

### Scene One — private Saturday morning, coffee, car, and family-call decision

Chloe and Allison continue their private Saturday morning together. Chloe makes Allison's coffee exactly as learned the night before; Allison notices and comments that Chloe remembered. Flirting runs naturally through the practical morning.

They discuss Chloe's broken car. Allison asks permission to buy Chloe a safe replacement. Chloe agrees and deliberately gives Allison **both the purchase and the choice**. A car is principally a utility item to Chloe; allowing Allison to choose and provide it affirms Allison's role in the relationship, Chloe's trust in her judgment, and Chloe's welcome of Allison's care language. The established choice remains the 2026 Volvo XC60 Ultra, Mulberry Red, Blond ventilated Nappa-leather interior. Allison later handles repair/purchase mechanics off page.

They decide to call their families. The calls are **individual calls to each woman's own parents**; neither introduces the other on the phone.

### Scene Two — separate calls, debrief, and getting ready

Chloe calls Steve and Dot; Allison separately calls Gary and Jenny. Each tells her parents that she **met her partner yesterday**, shares the little she genuinely knows, and admits that despite the absurd speed she simply knew they were meant for each other.

The **last names trigger recognition**. Steve and Dot recognize Allison Kensington and establish that they knew Gary and Jenny in school. Gary and Jenny recognize Chloe Johnson and Steve/Dot. The parental couples liked one another but ran in different circles and were never close.

Chloe's call centers on Allison. Her identity disclosure remains understated: **“By the way, I'm going by Chloe.”** Steve and Dot accept this without crisis and move into the established two-moms/grandchildren comic frame.

Afterward Chloe and Allison debrief while getting ready. **Femininity bootcamp is simply learning the ordinary process of getting ready as a woman**—grooming, hair, makeup, sequencing and everyday presentation as needed. Do not turn it into a curriculum.

Chloe borrows Allison's casual sundress and heeled espadrille wedges. Allison wears her own sundress and heeled sandals. They are not matchy-matchy; the dresses and shoes are **coordinated and complementary**, and both women notice and love it.

Chloe has a **medium-sized black Coach purse**, bought on the shopping trip, with wallet and ordinary everyday necessities.

### Scene Three — grocery store

They head to the grocery store **holding hands while not acting like a couple**. Physical contact continues naturally throughout the store. They separate when shopping mechanics require it and reconnect without thought—hands, arms, backs, waists, hip-to-hip proximity. It is not performed or repeatedly discussed; staying connected simply feels right.

The shopping is mundane shared-household discovery: food, preferences, brands, quantities, meals, snacks and household needs. Increasingly these are **their groceries and their household**, not supplies for a visitor.

### Scene Four — chores and unexpected family summons

Back home, groceries are put away and they move into cleaning and household chores. The division deliberately leans into **very traditionally female-coded and male-coded work** while remaining authentic rather than imposed: Chloe gravitates to cleaning/domestic work because she enjoys it and has standards; Allison takes maintenance and tool-based physical jobs. Both enjoy the complementary division.

**The parental callback interrupts the chores.** Chloe and Allison did not know it was coming and were not waiting for it. Since the original calls they have gotten ready, gone grocery shopping, returned home and started chores. The parents independently reconnected and compared notes, then unexpectedly summon the daughters to a **joint Johnson/Kensington dinner that evening at the Johnson home**.

After the summons Chloe and Allison finish their remaining chores. Preserve the strongly gender-coded domestic contrast in the last chores.

**Doorbell. Scene transition.**

### Scene Five — Natalie and Becca visit

Natalie Brooks and Rebecca “Becca” Walsh come by because **it is Saturday and they are visiting their friend Allison**. No contrived errand is needed.

They see Chloe and ask Allison who the beautiful woman is. Allison simply introduces **Chloe, her girlfriend**. Natalie and Becca do **not** know about the fake-girlfriend arrangement or private femininity bootcamp.

They learn Chloe and Allison **met yesterday** and Chloe has already moved in. This earns the inevitable **U-Haul joke**.

The visit stays together in the kitchen/domestic space. Natalie and Becca watch Chloe host and make everything rather than receiving finished food from offstage.

Chloe makes **warm goat-cheese crostini with honey, cracked pepper and fresh herbs**, with simple accompaniments such as olives/nuts. During the visit she also makes a **quick from-scratch white cake**. The cake itself is straightforward; its decoration is alarming. Chloe pipes **disturbingly beautiful buttercream flowers** and writes in pretty script:

**Home is where your heart lives.**

Natalie and Becca watch her mix, bake, frost, pipe and letter it. When the writing appears they are **almost speechless**. Give that silence room before another joke.

Chloe is continuously a hostess without becoming servile: she participates in conversation, tops off drinks before being asked, supplies plates/napkins, notices preferences, offers more and makes guests feel wanted.

Her care of **Allison is more intimate**: easy touches and closeness, anticipating small wants, and natural partner-like attention. **Allison is beaming.** Natalie and Becca know her well enough to see how extraordinarily happy she is.

#### Nonna Maria — the heart

Nonna Maria gets a **heartfelt mention**, not merely a punchline. Chloe explains affectionately that Nonna Maria taught her hospitality: when people enter your home, you feed them; pay attention; do not make people beg for what they need; make enough; make people know their presence is wanted. The lesson is ultimately about caring for people and making a home feel like home.

Natalie and Becca are genuinely moved. Nonna Maria is Chloe's Sicilian neighborhood-grandmother figure, not a biological grandmother. Do not invent further life-status or detailed childhood history beyond Chloe's authoritative character record.

#### Hotness, wife comments, and the cake

Allison has historically been the visually formidable woman in the group, so Chloe's beauty gives the friends long-awaited ammunition about whether Allison remains the undisputed hottest woman. Chloe enjoys the ribbing without declaring herself hotter.

The **wife comments come after** the hospitality and Nona material have earned them. The friends have watched Chloe as Allison's partner and hostess: beautiful, an independent software engineer, maker/sewer, cooks, bakes, loves entertaining, moved in after one day, and is intimately attentive to an Allison who cannot stop beaming. “Trophy wife” can work as optical comedy but is immediately complicated by Chloe's own career and competence. The deeper joke is that Chloe is **alarmingly wife-shaped** and Allison is delighted by the accusation.

The flowered cake carrying **Home is where your heart lives** nearly defeats Natalie and Becca. They become almost speechless before the wife comments resume.

Natalie and Becca eventually leave because **the visit is over**. No contrived departure mechanism is needed, and they do not acquire psychic knowledge of the family dinner.
