## Governing chapter purpose

Chapter Three turns the fake-girlfriend project into shared domestic life. Chloe enters Allison's home, chooses her name and pronouns, handles the immediate work/HR consequence, begins actually living in Allison's space, and the two women start learning each other beyond presentation.

The chapter also establishes family/friend connections, Chloe and Allison's complementary fashion knowledge, Allison's photography and Clara history, Julie's first visit, deliberate physical affection without sex, and the first overnight/morning of twenty-four-hour training.

The chapter ends Saturday morning with the fake relationship functioning badly as a fake and extremely well as a developing relationship.

## Arrival at Allison's house

The chapter begins directly from Chapter Two's U-Haul joke. Chloe and Allison travel to Allison's house with the purchases from Maison Belle.

Allison's home is a warm, substantial Colonial rather than an impersonal rich-person showroom. The white picket fence, inherited swing set and doghouse become comic evidence against Allison's claim that she has sworn off relationships. The house is expensive but lived-in, feminine and personal.

Chloe recognizes that this is where Allison actually comes home, which matters more than the price of the furnishings.

## Making room

Allison brings Chloe into her bedroom/closet/ensuite and begins physically making room for her new things.

The act is deliberately ordinary rather than ceremonial: Allison moves hangers, opens drawer space and clears bathroom room; Chloe fills those spaces. Chloe's apartment, furniture, computer equipment, sewing things and accumulated possessions remain elsewhere for later retrieval.

The domestic pattern begins immediately through small practical corrections: storing bras, putting away heels, handling jewelry and learning how Allison organizes the private infrastructure of presentation.

## Chloe

The closet creates the practical naming problem: Allison's supposed girlfriend cannot plausibly continue publicly as Tim.

The solution becomes more than a fake-girlfriend alias. Chloe recognizes that she likes the clothes, femininity, being beautiful and the social language she has begun learning, and that the idea of simply taking all of it off when the experiment ends no longer makes sense.

She chooses **Chloe Johnson** and **she/her**.

The chapter does not erase Tim. Thirty-five years remain Chloe's real history rather than something shameful or false that must be destroyed to validate Chloe.

Allison checks whether Chloe is genuinely okay. Chloe's answer is that she is happy.

## Immediate work consequence

Chloe realizes she cannot simply arrive at work Monday and surprise HR.

She sends a concise work email communicating what work needs to know without autobiography or manifesto. She treats this as an operational dependency and proofreads before sending.

The approved chapter establishes that the message is sent. It does not establish every later HR response, legal-name process, employer policy or Monday workplace scene.

## Domestic learning becomes life

Twenty-four-hour training stops looking like formal lessons and starts looking like shared life.

Allison teaches Chloe small practical things as they arise. Chloe learns by watching, copying, adapting and living rather than performing a checklist.

The family room becomes their informal headquarters. Chloe deliberately sits close enough for their thighs to touch because she wants physical contact, then uses “quality assurance” as the joke covering an increasingly genuine desire for closeness.

## Relationship boot camp

Chloe recognizes that convincing couple behavior requires knowing ordinary details about each other: coffee, family, friends, habits and preferences.

The exchange is conversational rather than an interview.

Established family information includes:

* Chloe's parents Steve and Dot Johnson;
* Chloe's sister Allison;
* Allison Kensington's parents Gary and Jenny Kensington;
* Allison's sister Renee;
* Steve/Dot and Gary/Jenny knew each other in school, liked one another, but moved in different circles.

Both women immediately recognize that their parents knowing one another will become a thing.

## Friends

Chloe has five close friends.

Allison names six: Julie, Natalie, Becca, Sam, Lauren and Megan. Julie is the bride.

Knowing these names is not the same as Chloe having met the entire friend group. Chapter Three's actual friend encounter is Julie's later visit.

## Sewing and shared fashion territory

Chloe sews, including making prom dresses for girls who cannot afford them.

Allison recognizes in retrospect that this explains Chloe touching seams at Maison Belle. Their fashion knowledge is complementary rather than identical:

* Chloe knows construction/making;
* Allison knows wearing, aesthetics and social presentation.

Fashion becomes shared territory where each has expertise the other lacks.

## Allison's photography and Clara

Chloe notices photographs distributed naturally around Allison's home, including Clara.

Clara was important to Allison. Chloe does not demand that the photographs be removed and refuses to compete with a photograph or treat Allison's prior love as an insult.

Chloe notices that many of the displayed images are Allison's own photographs and recognizes that Allison is talented.

Allison used to photograph more. After Clara, she put more of her time into work; this is not framed as corporate tragedy or sacrifice. Chloe encourages her to make time for photography again and says she would like to see what Allison shoots **now**.

The chapter establishes discovery, encouragement and the possibility of new work. Allison has not yet resumed photography on page.

## Julie's visit

The doorbell introduces Julie unexpectedly.

Julie sees Chloe in the domestic context and rapidly understands that Allison's supposedly fake arrangement has become something much more couple-shaped.

Julie teases them, including the U-Haul theme and wedding/bouquet implications, but the underlying beat includes genuine recognition that Chloe may be good for Allison.

Julie independently invited Chloe to the Paris wedding context and becomes one of the very small number of people who knows the fake-girlfriend premise rather than merely observing them as a real couple.

After Julie leaves, Chloe and Allison's attraction turns into another private kiss with no audience, Clara purpose or quality-assurance requirement.

## Bed and sex boundary

Allison expects Chloe to sleep in her bed under the comic justification of twenty-four-hour training.

Before bed, they explicitly discuss sex.

Both agree **not yet**. The reason is not lack of attraction—both acknowledge strong attraction—but that they have known each other for less than a day and are already moving extraordinarily fast. Neither wants sex to become another thing they do simply because neither seems to know where the brakes are.

The operative answer is **later**, not never.

They explicitly welcome:

* kissing;
* touching;
* cuddling;
* sleeping close.

Their physical comfort is intentional. It is not an accidental-cuddling plot where they wake embarrassed by contact they supposedly meant to avoid.

## Nightgown and private Chloe

The nightgown completes the logic begun in Chapter Two: presentation must work when Chloe is tired, private and not being evaluated in the boutique.

Alone at the bathroom mirror, Chloe sees herself at the end of a long day and remains Chloe. The beat is quiet rather than a melodramatic revelation.

Allison teaches her gentler makeup removal and other small nighttime habits. Chloe then wears the nightgown to bed, and Allison's response is personal rather than a technical assessment.

## First night

In bed they acknowledge the absurdity of the day: they met that morning, Chloe now lives there, she has chosen her name, emailed HR, and Julie is already threatening bouquet-related nonsense.

The emotional register becomes quieter. Allison tells Chloe she was beautiful at the bus stop, before the makeover/presentation work.

Chloe jokes that she is trying not to fall in love before midnight and later calls herself “your menace,” which lands more seriously than the joke pretends.

They settle into deliberate cuddling and continue learning ordinary details until sleep. Allison tells Chloe she likes her name; Chloe says she does too.

## Saturday morning

Chloe wakes tangled with Allison and initially remains because Allison sleepily asks her to stay.

Morning exposes exactly why twenty-four-hour practice matters: tired Chloe's hair, nightgown and movement are less polished. Allison notices differences and gives small corrections while Chloe continues adapting.

Downstairs Chloe learns Allison's kitchen, remembers Allison's coffee, and they make breakfast cooperatively rather than as a demonstration of either woman's domestic superiority.

Their behavior is strikingly normal and couple-like despite the speed of the arrangement.

The chapter closes with a warm kitchen embrace/kiss and their shared conclusion that twenty-four-hour training works.

The fake relationship is performing terribly. Everything else is working perfectly.
