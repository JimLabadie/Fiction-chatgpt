# Scene 1 — Welcome Home

“So, bestie, we should go home.”

Sabbi looked at me.

“Where?”

That probably should have been my first indication that I had neglected to mention something.

Possibly several somethings.

In my defense, we had a pink Barbie convertible, the top was down, and Sabbi had spent the last ten minutes singing badly enough to constitute a moving violation. Administrative details had slipped my mind.

“Home.”

“Yes, Chloe. I successfully identified that word. I was asking where it is.”

I smiled at her.

She narrowed her eyes.

“No.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You did that thing with your face.”

“What thing?”

“The thing where you're pleased with yourself and I'm about to discover why I should have asked more questions twenty minutes ago.”

I put the car in gear.

“Seat belt, bestie.”

“Oh, fuck.”

The Barbie convertible was pink.

Not tasteful pink.

Not _technically blush if viewed under warm lighting_ pink.

Barbie pink.

It had white leather seats, enough chrome to communicate questionable judgment at highway speeds, and a dashboard Athena had somehow persuaded to greet me that morning with **HI, BARBIE!**

I had chosen not to investigate.

Some gifts are best received without forensic analysis.

Sabbi buckled herself in.

“For the record, if you're murdering me, I think I'm entitled to say I'm disappointed in the vehicle choice.”

“You don't like the car?”

“I fucking love the car. That's the problem. If the police find my body, they're going to think I got into this willingly.”

“You did.”

“I know. Humiliating.”

I pulled away from the curb.

St. Claire rolled past us in the warm afternoon, alive in that comfortably busy way cities sometimes managed when nobody had apparently scheduled an apocalypse. Women sat outside cafés. Somebody was walking a dog wearing a sweater despite the dog having clearly expressed an opinion on the matter. Two women were arguing cheerfully outside a florist with enough hand gestures to suggest either marriage or homicide.

Sabbi pointed.

“Lesbians.”

“Excellent field identification.”

“I've been studying.”

We stopped at a light.

A woman in the next lane looked over at the car.

Then at us.

Then back at the car.

Sabbi waved.

The woman waved back.

“See?” Sabbi said. “Community outreach.”

The light changed.

I drove.

After a few minutes, Sabbi glanced over.

“So.”

“So?”

“Where do you live?”

“St. Claire.”

She stared at me.

I kept my eyes on the road.

“Chloe.”

“Yes, bestie?”

“You're being suspiciously literal.”

“I am answering every question you've asked.”

“You are answering them like a lawyer with access to a shredder.”

“That seems unnecessarily hurtful.”

“You own this car.”

“That is not evidence of anything.”

“It is evidence of at least three things.”

I turned.

Sabbi stopped talking.

That was impressive enough that I glanced at her.

She was looking ahead.

“Chloe.”

“Yes?”

“Why are we going toward the Galleria?”

“We aren't.”

“The giant building in front of us would like to participate in this discussion.”

I smiled.

“Oh.”

Her head turned slowly.

“No.”

I kept driving.

“Chloe.”

The entrance opened ahead of us.

“Chloe.”

I waved at the gate.

It opened.

“Chloe.”

“Yes, Sabbi?”

“What the fuck?”

There it was.

I had been waiting for that.

I drove through.

The former Galleria rose ahead of us, except calling it a former shopping mall was a little like calling Versailles a fixer-upper with acreage.

Sabbi twisted in her seat, trying to take in the building, grounds, landscaping, security, and general architectural announcement that somebody somewhere had lost all adult supervision.

She looked at me.

She looked back at the Estate.

She looked at me again.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“Absolutely fucking not.”

I pulled the Barbie convertible around the drive.

“Welcome home.”

Silence.

Actual silence.

From Sabbi.

I briefly considered checking her pulse.

She stared at the Estate.

Then at me.

Then at the Estate.

“Home?”

“Home.”

“This is your house?”

“Our house.”

Her head snapped toward me.

I parked.

There are moments when a person has too many emotions to select one, and their face simply gives up.

Sabbi's face gave up.

I unbuckled.

She didn't move.

“Bestie?”

“You said _our_.”

“I did.”

“You live here.”

“We live here.”

“I don't live here.”

“You're sitting in the driveway.”

“That isn't how property law works.”

“Good news. We have lawyers.”

“Of course you have lawyers!”

I got out.

After a second, Sabbi climbed out too.

She stood beside the Barbie convertible, staring upward.

I walked around the hood and stopped beside her.

“You okay?”

“No.”

“Good no or bad no?”

“I don't know yet.”

“Fair.”

She looked at me.

There was still comedy in her face because Sabbi could probably find comedy during a controlled aircraft descent, but underneath it was something quieter.

I didn't poke it.

Instead, I held out my hand.

“Come on.”

She looked at it.

Then took it.

We walked toward the doors.

They opened before we reached them.

A familiar voice surrounded us.

“Welcome home, Mom.”

Sabbi stopped.

I smiled.

“Hi, sweetheart.”

There was a pause.

Then Athena said, “You brought company.”

Sabbi whispered, “Your house talks.”

“My daughter talks.”

Sabbi turned to me.

“Your what?”

“Athena, this is Sabbi. Sabbi is my bestie.”

“I gathered.”

“She's going to live here.”

Sabbi made a tiny choking noise beside me.

Athena, traitor that she was, ignored it.

“Understood.”

“And, Athena?”

“Yes, Mom?”

“Treat her as if she were a partner.”

This time Sabbi's choking noise was not tiny.

I looked at her.

“Household access.”

“Oh.”

“Best friend.”

“Yes.”

“Not proposing.”

“I knew that.”

“You looked concerned.”

“I was deciding whether I needed to buy a hat.”

“For our lesbian wedding?”

“I don't know how rich-people lesbian emergencies work!”

Athena said, “I have several hats available.”

I pointed toward the ceiling.

“Do not encourage her.”

“I was providing information.”

“You were being a smartass.”

“I learned from you.”

Sabbi stared upward.

Then she looked at me.

“Oh my God.”

“What?”

“I love your daughter.”

Athena answered immediately.

“I like her.”

I sighed.

This was going to be a problem.

A wonderful, wonderful problem.
