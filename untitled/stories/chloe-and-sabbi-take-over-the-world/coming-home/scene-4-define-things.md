# Scene 4 — Define Things

Athena didn't answer.

That was concerning.

“Athena?”

“I am determining what Sabbi means by _things_.”

Sabbi looked at me.

“Your daughter is stalling.”

“She gets that from lawyers.”

“I do not.”

“Then answer the question.”

“Please define the collection boundary.”

Sabbi stopped walking.

“What?”

Athena continued.

“Are you asking about currently wearable personal clothing, footwear, handbags, jewelry, accessories, museum holdings, fashion research objects, pieces awaiting conservation assessment, incoming designer pieces, or all objects managed through the wardrobe and collection infrastructure?”

Sabbi stared upward.

Then at me.

Then upward again.

“I asked how many clothes we have.”

“Yes.”

“That should not require a jurisdictional hearing.”

I rubbed my forehead.

“Athena, regular clothes.”

“Define regular.”

“Oh, fuck off.”

Sabbi gasped.

“You can't tell your daughter to fuck off.”

“She'll survive.”

“I will,” Athena confirmed.

“See?”

Sabbi shook her head.

“Okay. Shirts. Dresses. Skirts. Bras. Panties. Jeans. Sweaters. Coats. Stuff normal women own.”

There was a pause.

Athena said, “Normal women?”

Sabbi looked at me.

I looked at her.

“That was judgment,” she said.

“That was absolutely judgment.”

Athena said nothing.

Which was worse.

“Fine,” Sabbi said. “Stuff women own.”

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome, Your Honor.”

We resumed walking.

“So?” I asked.

“For ordinary wardrobe inventory, a single aggregate number would be misleading.”

Sabbi stopped again.

“For fuck's sake.”

I started laughing.

“No. Bestie, she's right.”

“Do not take her side.”

“I'm not taking her side. I'm recognizing the warning signs.”

“What warning signs?”

I pointed vaguely back toward the Grand Wardrobe.

“That.”

Sabbi looked behind us.

Then at me.

“Oh.”

“Yeah.”

Athena continued, perfectly calm.

“The Estate wardrobe is maintained as an active system rather than a fixed capsule collection. Garments enter and leave active rotation, move through cleaning and maintenance, are stored seasonally, may be transferred between display and wearable status, and may arrive through established fashion relationships.”

Sabbi blinked.

“Arrive?”

I smiled.

Athena had made a tactical error.

“Established fashion relationships?” Sabbi repeated.

“Athena.”

“Yes, Mom?”

“Maybe don't.”

“Don't what?”

I sighed.

“Too late.”

Sabbi grabbed my arm.

“What fashion relationships?”

“Bestie, we're going to the spa.”

“What. Fashion. Relationships.”

“Fashion Empire.”

Silence.

“Oh no,” Sabbi whispered.

“It sounds worse than it is.”

“Nothing you have said today has ever turned out to be less insane than it sounds.”

“That seems statistically unlikely.”

“Athena?”

“Sabbi's observation is substantially supported by today's events.”

“Traitor.”

“I provide information.”

Sabbi was grinning now.

“What is Fashion Empire?”

“A company.”

She waited.

I waited.

She narrowed her eyes.

“A fashion company?”

“Yes.”

“You own it?”

“Yes.”

“All of it?”

“That's a surprisingly complicated question.”

“Chloe.”

“Enough of it for this conversation.”

She closed her eyes.

“Okay.”

“You okay?”

“No. Keep going.”

“That doesn't seem medically advisable.”

“Keep. Going.”

I shrugged.

“It has fashion houses.”

Her eyes opened.

“Houses.”

“Brands.”

“How many?”

“I don't know.”

She stared at me.

“You own a fashion empire called Fashion Empire and you don't know how many fashion houses it has?”

“I have people.”

“You have _people_.”

“Excellent people.”

Athena said, “The organizational records are available.”

Sabbi pointed upward.

“Later.”

I blinked.

“Later?”

She nodded firmly.

“I have learned something.”

“What?”

“If I ask follow-up questions in this house, I don't get answers. I get additional infrastructure.”

I considered that.

“That is remarkably perceptive.”

“I need to pace myself.”

“Good plan.”

“Also, I still want the spa.”

“Excellent plan.”

We started walking again.

Sabbi managed almost twenty steps this time.

A personal record.

Then Athena said, “For context, the Fashion Empire's patron relationship also contributes pieces to Chloe's personal collection.”

Sabbi stopped.

I stopped.

Slowly, we both looked upward.

“Athena,” I said.

“Yes, Mom?”

“What did we just discuss?”

“Pacing.”

“Yes.”

“I provided context.”

Sabbi whispered, “Contributes how?”

I pointed at her.

“No.”

“But—”

“No. You invoked pacing. You live with your choices.”

“That's cruel.”

“Spa.”

She pouted.

In twenty-million-dollar shoes.

It was difficult to take seriously.

We kept going.

The spa entrance appeared ahead.

Sabbi's pout vanished.

“Oh.”

I smiled.

“Yeah.”

“Oh, this is bullshit.”

“What?”

“This is not a spa.”

“It says spa.”

“This is what happens when a spa wins the lottery.”

We stepped inside.

The atmosphere changed immediately.

Quieter.

Warmer.

The kind of place where every surface seemed to have been specifically selected to convince your shoulders they had been behaving unreasonably.

Sabbi looked around.

“This is ours?”

I didn't even need to answer.

She caught herself.

“Our spa.”

I smiled.

“Very good.”

“Don't make it weird.”

“You're wearing nineteen-point-nine-million-dollar shoes to a home spa.”

“Approximately.”

I stared at her.

She smiled sweetly.

Oh, she was learning far too quickly.

A staff member greeted us.

And that mattered.

Not a hologram.

Not a robot.

Not some magical house manifestation.

A woman doing her job in our ridiculous home.

“Good evening.”

“Hi,” I said.

Sabbi looked down at her shoes.

Then at the woman.

Then at me.

I knew that look.

“No,” I whispered.

“What?”

“Do not ask her if she's seen the shoes.”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You absolutely were.”

The woman's professional expression remained flawless.

Which meant she'd probably seen much worse.

That was concerning in entirely new directions.

“What would you like this evening?” she asked.

Sabbi opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Looked at me.

I shrugged.

Her choice.

She looked around the spa again.

Then down at the Moon Shoes.

Then back at the woman.

“This is going to sound stupid.”

“I doubt that.”

“I am currently wearing approximately twenty million dollars on my feet.”

The woman nodded.

Not even a twitch.

Sabbi looked at me.

I mouthed, _people_.

She elbowed me.

“And,” Sabbi continued, “I would very much like to take them off before I become the first person in history to drop a diamond shoe into a foot bath.”

“A sensible precaution.”

“I like her,” Sabbi said.

“I heard that.”

“You were supposed to.”

The woman smiled.

There.

Tiny.

Professional.

But definitely a smile.

“We can secure them for you.”

Sabbi hesitated.

Then looked at me.

“Secure?”

“Estate systems.”

“Meaning?”

“They won't disappear.”

She looked down at the shoes.

For the first time, I saw it.

Not fear of damaging them.

Attachment.

She'd worn them.

Walked in them.

Laughed in them.

Somewhere between the vault and the spa, they'd stopped being _the twenty-million-dollar shoes_ and become _the stupid shoes she had worn with her best friend_.

I bumped her shoulder.

“They live here.”

She looked at me.

Then smiled.

“Right.”

She stepped out of them carefully.

The Moon Shoes disappeared into the Estate's custody.

Sabbi watched them go.

Then exhaled.

“Okay.”

“Okay?”

“Now I can relax.”

She looked around.

Her grin returned.

“Bestie.”

“Yes?”

“We have a spa.”

“We have a spa.”

“And a fashion empire.”

“Apparently.”

“And an unknown number of clothes.”

“Correct.”

“And twenty-million-dollar shoes.”

“Approximately.”

She took my hand.

“What the fuck is our life?”

I squeezed hers.

“I don't know yet.”

And for once, neither of us asked Athena.
