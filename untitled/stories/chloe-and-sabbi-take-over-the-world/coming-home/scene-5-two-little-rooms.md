# Scene 5 — Two Little Rooms

There is something deeply grounding about a robe.

Not emotionally.

Structurally.

A robe says: _Whatever bullshit was happening before this, we have temporarily agreed to stop wearing pants._

Sabbi emerged from the changing room in hers and held out both arms.

“Bestie.”

“Yes?”

“I have a robe.”

“I can see that.”

“Our robe.”

“No.”

She dropped her arms.

“What happened to pronouns?”

“You don't have to share the robe.”

“Oh.”

“You can have the whole robe.”

Her eyes widened.

“Mine?”

“Yours.”

She pulled it tighter around herself.

“I take back everything bad I've ever said about capitalism.”

“That seems premature.”

“I have pockets.”

She shoved both hands into them.

“Deep pockets.”

“Careful. That's how they get you.”

She walked over to me.

Barefoot now.

The Moon Shoes were somewhere secure, presumably being guarded by people with qualifications and anxiety disorders.

I had a robe too.

Mine also had pockets.

I chose not to make this competitive.

“Okay,” Sabbi said. “What do we do?”

“Whatever we want.”

“That is becoming an alarmingly common answer.”

“Massage?”

“Yes.”

“Facial?”

“Yes.”

“Manicure?”

She held up her hands.

“Yes.”

“Pedicure?”

She looked toward wherever the Moon Shoes had disappeared.

“Those feet have earned government assistance.”

“Pedicure.”

“Steam room?”

“Yes.”

“Sauna?”

“Yes.”

“Pool?”

“Yes.”

“Hair?”

She touched hers.

“Maybe.”

“Excellent.”

She stared at me.

“What?”

“That's too many things.”

“We live here.”

Her face changed.

I grinned.

“There it is.”

“Stop weaponizing residency.”

“Never.”

The woman who had greeted us returned.

“Would you like me to arrange a sequence?”

Sabbi looked at me.

I looked at Sabbi.

We both looked at her.

“Yes,” we said.

She smiled.

“I'll take care of it.”

Sabbi watched her walk away.

Then leaned toward me.

“Bestie.”

“Hmm?”

“I think I could get used to having people.”

“They're staff, not Pokémon.”

“I know that.”

“You said it like you were collecting them.”

“I meant people who know what the fuck they're doing.”

“Oh.”

She considered.

“Can I collect those?”

“Probably not.”

“Shame.”

We started with pedicures.

Because apparently removing twenty million dollars from your feet creates a psychological need to apologize to them.

Sabbi settled into the chair beside mine and immediately began pressing buttons.

“What does this do?”

“I don't know.”

The chair moved.

She yelped.

I laughed.

“You knew.”

“I absolutely did not.”

“You're a terrible person.”

The chair started massaging her back.

Her eyes closed.

“Oh.”

“Still terrible?”

“Don't talk to me.”

I smiled.

The pedicure began.

For several glorious minutes, nothing happened.

Nothing exploded.

Nobody revealed a multinational corporation.

Athena did not provide context.

There were no museums.

No secure vaults.

No diamonds.

Just warm water, competent hands, quiet music, and Sabbi slowly dissolving into her chair.

“This,” she murmured, eyes still closed, “is civilization.”

“I thought the shoes were civilization.”

“The shoes were evidence against civilization.”

“Fair.”

Someone offered us drinks.

Sabbi opened one eye.

“Can I have champagne?”

“Yes.”

She looked at me.

I shrugged.

“Apparently.”

“Two, please.”

I raised a finger.

“One for me.”

Sabbi looked offended.

“I wasn't ordering both for myself.”

“You absolutely were.”

“You can't prove that.”

“I live with Athena.”

“Fuck.”

The champagne arrived.

Sabbi lifted her glass.

“To our first day home.”

That caught me.

Not enough to hurt.

Just enough to matter.

I touched my glass to hers.

“Home.”

We drank.

Then Sabbi ruined it.

“And to not breaking the twenty-million-dollar shoes.”

“Approximately.”

She nearly inhaled champagne.

Worth it.

After pedicures came massages.

Then facials.

Then the steam room, where Sabbi lasted four minutes before announcing that she had discovered what it felt like to be a dumpling.

The sauna was apparently different because it was “dry cooking.”

I did not ask.

We found the soaking pools.

We found a quiet relaxation room with loungers so comfortable I briefly considered abandoning all responsibilities and becoming furniture.

We found treatment rooms.

Hair stations.

Manicure stations.

Skin care.

A beautifully equipped salon.

Everything made sense.

Expensive sense.

Ridiculous sense.

But sense.

It was a spa.

A spectacular spa.

A spa belonging to women who had somehow ended up living inside a former shopping mall with enough resources to make reasonable budgeting mostly theoretical.

But still a spa.

Which was why the two little rooms bothered me.

We were walking back toward the salon when Sabbi slowed.

I kept walking.

She stopped.

I stopped.

Shit.

“Bestie.”

“Hmm?”

“What are those?”

I did not turn around.

“What are what?”

There was a pause.

Then:

“Oh, you little bitch.”

I turned.

Sabbi stood in the corridor, robe tied around her, hair pulled messily away from her face, freshly pampered and looking entirely too pleased with herself.

Behind her were two doors.

Small rooms.

Unremarkable doors.

Nothing dramatic.

No giant warning lights.

No chrome archways.

No glowing tubes visible through glass.

Just two rooms tucked neatly into the spa.

Sabbi pointed.

“You know what those are.”

“I know what most rooms in my house are.”

“Our house.”

I smiled despite myself.

“Very good.”

“Don't distract me with pronouns.”

“It's worked before.”

“What is in there?”

“Things.”

Her mouth dropped open.

“Things?”

“Yes.”

“You made me define _things_ for your daughter!”

“I learned from the experience.”

She walked toward the first door.

I stepped sideways.

She stopped.

“Oh.”

“What?”

“You blocked me.”

“I moved.”

“You moved directly between me and the door.”

“Coincidence.”

“Chloe.”

“Sabbi.”

She looked at the door.

Then at me.

Then at the other door.

“Are they dangerous?”

“No.”

That came easily.

Her expression changed.

“Private?”

“No.”

“Restricted?”

“Not from you.”

That landed.

I watched it land.

Then her eyes narrowed again.

“Expensive?”

I considered.

“Probably.”

“Everything is probably expensive.”

“Excellent. You're acclimating.”

She pointed at the first door.

“What's in there?”

“No.”

“No what?”

“No.”

“That wasn't a yes-or-no question.”

“I know.”

She stared at me.

I smiled.

She pointed at the second.

“That one?”

“No.”

“Chloe.”

“Spa day.”

“We are in the spa!”

“Exactly.”

“These are spa rooms!”

“Technically.”

Her eyes widened.

“Technically?”

Fuck.

I had said technically.

Sabbi knew it too.

She came closer.

“What kind of technically?”

“The kind where we are getting our nails done.”

“We already got our feet done.”

“Hands remain tragically neglected.”

“Chloe.”

“Your cuticles are crying out.”

“My cuticles can wait.”

“They've suffered enough.”

She stepped around me.

I stepped with her.

She stepped the other direction.

I matched her.

We stood there.

Two grown women in expensive robes having a silent defensive-football dispute in a luxury spa.

Sabbi's grin appeared.

“You're scared.”

“No.”

“Oh my God.”

“I am not scared.”

“You're fucking scared.”

“I am exercising judgment.”

“That's worse.”

“How?”

“You don't do that.”

“That is hurtful.”

“Athena!”

Oh, fuck.

“Yes, Sabbi?”

“What is in these rooms?”

I looked upward.

“Athena.”

“Yes, Mom?”

“Don't.”

Silence.

Sabbi's eyes became enormous.

“You just told the all-knowing house daughter not to tell me.”

“Athena is not all-knowing.”

“Not the point.”

Athena said, “Correct.”

“Whose side are you on?” I demanded.

“I was correcting an inaccurate characterization.”

Sabbi pointed at the ceiling.

“See? Responsible one.”

“I regret introducing you.”

“No, you don't.”

“No.”

She smiled.

Then looked at the doors again.

Her smile softened.

“Seriously.”

I knew what she meant.

So I gave her the real answer.

“Later.”

She looked at me.

“Why?”

I could have joked.

I almost did.

Instead I looked at those two ordinary little doors.

Then back at my best friend.

“Because those deserve their own day.”

Sabbi didn't say anything.

Neither did Athena.

The corridor suddenly felt very quiet.

Not frightening.

Not heavy.

Just quiet.

Sabbi studied my face.

Whatever she saw there made her stop pushing.

“Okay.”

I blinked.

“Okay?”

“Okay.”

She pointed at me.

“But I reserve the right to become completely fucking unbearable about this.”

“That seems fair.”

“Questions.”

“Expected.”

“Speculation.”

“Dangerous.”

“Attempts at bribery.”

“Encouraged.”

“Seduction?”

I stared at her.

She burst out laughing.

“Not me, asshole. Staff. Information networks.”

“Oh my God.”

“I have champagne and no ethical framework.”

“You've had one glass.”

“I am a lightweight with ambition.”

I put my arm around her shoulders and turned her away from the doors.

“Come on.”

“Where?”

“Manicures.”

She allowed herself to be steered.

After three steps she looked back.

I didn't.

After another three:

“Bestie?”

“No.”

“I didn't ask anything.”

“You were going to.”

“I was going to say I love you.”

I stopped.

She smiled innocently.

“You're awful.”

“I love you too.”

We kept walking.

Behind us were two little rooms.

Nothing glowing.

Nothing humming ominously.

Nothing announcing itself as impossible.

Just two doors in our spa.

Waiting until we were ready to open them.
