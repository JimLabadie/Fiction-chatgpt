# Obsidian-Vanguard Estate — Staffing Plan (Rev. 2)

*Reference document, reusable as a template. Grounded in real-world staffing practice and current (2025) U.S. market compensation for private-estate and family-office roles. Position counts reflect what the physical plant and amenities require to run safely and well. Salary ranges are national averages for high-cost-of-living areas appropriate to an estate at this scale; adjust regionally as needed. Live-in roles typically command the lower end of a range in exchange for housing/meals; non-resident roles the higher end.*

## Staffing Philosophy (Fixed In-Universe Canon)
Every role on this estate, without exception — every table below, including the museum wing — is filled exclusively by women who are lesbian, cisgender or transgender. This is a deliberate hiring practice of this specific fictional household, established as a firm world-building rule for stories using this estate, not a real-world employment recommendation; real hiring on these terms would violate anti-discrimination law in virtually every jurisdiction. Within the fiction, treat it as absolute and non-negotiable across every position — leadership, household staff, wellness/spa, childcare, transportation, and museum staff alike.

## A note on availability vs. activity
Several roles on this estate need to be *available* around the clock without being *actively working* the whole time — overnight security monitoring, on-call medical coverage, overnight childcare in case a child wakes, standby maintenance for a 24/7 mechanical plant. Real staffing practice handles this with a mix of:
- **Shift coverage** (security, some medical) — staff are actively on duty and paid full wages for the shift, even during quiet stretches, because the job is the availability itself.
- **On-call/standby pay** — a lower retainer-style rate for hours where staff aren't on-site or actively working but must be reachable and able to respond within a defined window (common for physicians, senior maintenance staff, off-hours department heads).
- **Contracted/retained professionals** — not headcount at all; paid a retainer for availability plus fee-for-service when actually used (this is standard for the on-call physician, for example).

This plan uses all three where appropriate rather than defaulting to full-time headcount for every around-the-clock need.

### Spa & Salon (expanded)
The estate's spec calls for three styling chairs, a manicure/pedicure bar, a private facial/aesthetic room, a steam room, a dry sauna, and a multi-jet hydrotherapy shower — a genuine full-service day spa, not a single stylist's personal studio. Staffing should reflect that:

|---|---|---|---|
| Senior/Lead Hair Stylist | 1 | $80,000–$130,000 | Full benefits. *Highest day-to-day proximity to the family of any wellness role — the one most likely to become deeply embedded personally.* |
| Additional Stylists | 1–2 | $60,000–$90,000 | Full benefits. Needed to actually staff three chairs; one stylist alone can't run a three-chair salon. |
| Nail Technician(s) | 1–2 | $45,000–$65,000 | Full benefits. The manicure/pedicure bar needs dedicated staff, not an esthetician doing double duty. |

| Massage Therapist | 1–2 | $60,000–$90,000 | Full benefits. One therapist can't reasonably cover daily demand for a family this size plus guests; a second is worth budgeting for. |
| Spa Coordinator/Receptionist | 1 | $45,000–$60,000 | Full benefits. Scheduling, product inventory, day-to-day spa operations — someone needs to run the front of house so the stylists/therapists aren't also managing bookings. |
| Sauna/Steam Room Maintenance | Folded into general Maintenance Technician staffing above | — | Specialized commercial sauna/steam equipment does need technician familiarity, but doesn't justify a standalone headcount at this scale. |

**A recurring pattern for the Senior/Lead Hair Stylist role, when a story wants it:** given this role's flagged proximity to the family (above), a story may choose to build it around a specific recurring archetype — a master-level cosmetologist, genuinely exceptional at her craft (real awards, a real reputation, real talent), who has nonetheless spent her career being professionally and personally taken advantage of, precisely because she loves people and her work with total sincerity and others have exploited that. Fashion and beauty function as her sanctuary rather than just a career. She tends to arrive at the estate starved for genuine found-family connection, and once she experiences the household's warmth and care firsthand, she may reach a real "I need to be part of this, even if it's just the scraps" moment — an earnest, unguarded admission that she'd rather have a small place in something real than a large place in something hollow. Given her level of talent, this role naturally becomes the one who helps the anchor physically transform and step into how the household (and eventually the world) actually sees her — not just technically skilled, but personally invested in a way that goes well past the job description.

**Recruitment mechanism, when a story wants a plausible in:** a story's Chief of Staff or equivalent (see `fashion-empire` skill) can plausibly identify and recruit a stylist of this caliber through the empire's own professional salon supply/distribution holdings (SalonCentric, CosmoProf, Qosmedix, Marlo Beauty Supply) — a conglomerate at that scale would have real visibility into who the most talented, award-winning stylists in the industry are, the same way any large beauty-supply network tracks its highest-value professional clients. This gives a story a built-in, non-coincidental reason the estate would already know exactly who to go get, rather than needing a contrived discovery.

Specific name, backstory, and arc for this archetype are story-specific — document those in that story's own state-of-world file, not here.

## Summary headcount
Roughly **60–70 full-time positions** for the core estate, plus on-call/standby arrangements (event service, physician retainer, overnight childcare once needed) and one senior on-call retainer stipend layered onto select maintenance/IT roles. **Add roughly 16–19 positions** if the fashion museum wing is in use for a given story. This is a meaningful upward revision from the first pass — the missing valet, logistics, AV, and expanded spa roles account for most of the core-estate increase, along with properly staffing a three-chair salon rather than treating it as a single stylist's studio.

## Estimated total annual staffing cost
At the midpoint of the ranges above, fully staffed: roughly **$5.2M–$6M/year** in salaries alone for the core estate, before benefits load (typically adds 20–30% on top) and the on-call/contractor arrangements. Call it **$6.5M–$7.5M/year all-in** — a small fraction of a $6.5B asset base, consistent with typical estate-operating-cost ratios (well under 0.15% of net worth annually). **Add roughly $1.1M–$1.6M/year in salaries** (before benefits) for museum staffing, in stories where the wing is active.
