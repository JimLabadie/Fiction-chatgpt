# St. Claire — Founding Pioneers

Companion to St_Claire_00_Concept.md. A deliberately small, curated roster of structurally load-bearing figures — institutions elsewhere in this project already point to them by name or legacy.

**Structure:** 12 total. The Mayor + an 11-person council beneath her, avoiding any tie in the council's own consensus process. Each of the twelve holds one of the twelve patronage domains below, until retirement, resignation, or death — successors chosen through the same consensus-appointment process established in Informal Justice & Social Accountability.

**Every founding mother has two records.** This document holds her founding-era legacy. A separate entry in the general population/character document holds her current status — active, retired, or deceased — even when minimal. This applies without exception, including to retired or deceased founders.

## The Mayor — leadership & governance

**Ruth Halloway.** Born June 14, 1952. Led the 1974 squat — the organizing figure behind the working-class butches and feminist printers who took the riverfront factories.

## Odessa Calloway — history & oral tradition

One of the working-class butches who took the riverfront factories in 1974. Decades later, when the parish closed in the early 2000s, she was instrumental in directing resources the squatters' generation had fought for toward preserving the parish's physical legacy — the bridge between St. Claire's two histories, made concrete in one person's life. Treats the past as something living and taught, not just commemorated once a year; responsible for capturing the community's oral history directly. Founded Odessa Calloway's Keeping House, a real, lived-in community archive (see St_Claire_05_Organizations.md) — deliberately funded independent of outside money to preserve grassroots autonomy.

## Marion Castellano — journalism & press

Founded Marion Castellano's St. Claire Beacon on a stated strategy of transparent sharing, celebration, and fighting injustice — real, exceptional recognition in journalism at large, not just local acclaim. Editorial mission: demystify, not just celebrate — show that lesbians and trans people are real, authentic human beings, not the alien "other" outside perception often makes them. Built mentorship directly into the newsroom's culture, training other women to find and use their own voice through the paper.

## Dr. Amanda Watson — medical care

Recognized early in the community's growth that St. Claire needed real healthcare infrastructure of its own, not dependence on the wider city — founded and still leads Amanda Watson – St. Claire Medical Center. Her defining principle: gender-affirming care isn't a separate specialty bolted onto normal medicine here, it's simply part of normal medicine. That founding stance shaped the clinic's whole culture and staffing from the start.

## Constance "Connie" Ibarra — legal aid & justice

Founded Constance Ibarra – St. Claire Legal Aid Society, building the district's actual access-to-justice infrastructure — real legal representation for people the private practices couldn't or wouldn't serve. Distinct from and complementary to the district's private legal practices; her work is specifically about making sure money was never the reason someone went unrepresented.

## Dr. Evelyn Reed — education

Principal of St. Claire Public Academy & Childcare Center, and the administrative force who has kept its progressive curriculum alive against real, ongoing outside pressure — not a frictionless institution. Recognized education as needing a real fighter behind it, not just a building.

## Betty "Ma" Higgins — commerce

Owner of The Sunrise Counter for four decades, and the district's clearest embodiment of small commerce done right — present at the counter most mornings the whole time. A living demonstration that ordinary, unglamorous business, sustained over decades, is itself a form of community leadership.

## Josefina "Fina" Alvarado — feminist organizing / the printer thread

The third distinct thread of the 1974 squat, alongside Ruth Halloway's working-class butches — the feminist printers who ran a press out of a squatted factory floor. Everything the district's protest ephemera, banners, and zine culture became traces back to that original press. Present at the actual founding, carrying a movement thread of her own rather than being folded into Ruth's.

## Marceline "Marci" Woods — safety net & mutual aid

Founded Marceline Woods – Haven House, the district's real youth crisis/drop-in resource, out of her own unmet need decades before the institution existed. The honest origin of the whole Safety Net thread in one person's life — built from lived necessity, not abstract charity.

## Billie Sandoval — fashion & aesthetics

Owner of Second Chapter, and the district's clearest voice on why fashion and aesthetics matter as real history — treats butch tailoring and retro femme history as distinct, equally serious categories, not one flattened aesthetic.

## Rev. Alana Sterling — the parish/spiritual transition

Chaplain at Sanctuary, continuing the former parish's pastoral function in fully secular form — the clearest living link between the district's Catholic origin and its present-day religious pluralism (see St_Claire_00_Concept.md, Religion & Morality). Operates without doctrine and with real compassion.

## Teodora "Teo" Alcalá — founding figure, outside the twelve domains

Not one of the twelve patronage domains, but a real founding-era figure in her own right: the trained landscape architect who joined the squat within its first year and designed the district's original 1976 "womb plan," sponsored politically by Ruth Halloway from the start. Fought and lost the 1988 zoning battle over Downtown's street layout — the same year as the district's land-trust victory, which is exactly why Working Riverfront kept her vision while Downtown didn't. Kept working for decades afterward (Halloway Park's Hollow trail network wasn't finished until 2003), mentored a generation of later planners including current architect Elena Rostova, and died in 2019 having built something that outlived her working lifetime by design. See St_Claire_00_Concept.md, Womb-Based Street & Trail Network, for the full history.
