# St. Claire — Population and Staffing Integration

Status: ACTIVE MECHANICS EXTENSION
Authority: operational extension of `St Claire 00B Rules and Mechanics.md` for population, household, and organization staffing work. If this file and 00B appear to conflict, stop and reconcile the conflict rather than silently choosing one.

## Purpose

St. Claire is built ahead of the scene. Businesses are staffed by real residents with complete lives, not convenience NPCs. Staffing therefore operates on the existing population and household ecology before creating additional residents.

## Population-first staffing sequence — mandatory

For every organization staffing batch, use this order:

1. Define the organization's real staffing requirement: roles, headcount, operating hours, shifts, coverage, days off, and any on-call needs.
2. Search `St Claire 03 Population.md` plus all active incremental named-person staffing records for existing residents whose occupation, employment state, skills, schedule, or established history could plausibly fill the role.
3. Explicitly check existing unemployed, underemployed, between-job, part-time, retired-but-working, apprenticeship-seeking, or otherwise available residents before inventing a new employee.
4. Search `St Claire 04 Households.md` and active incremental records for the household/partner network of candidate workers. A named partner or household member is a real resident, not a relationship placeholder.
5. Prefer an appropriate existing resident when the established facts support the placement. Do not rewrite a person's established career, identity, history, or preferences merely to avoid creating someone new.
6. Only after the existing population has been checked may a genuinely new resident be created to fill a remaining role.
7. Every new named resident receives the complete Population schema and must be connected to a complete Household record or an explicitly established single-person/non-romantic household state.
8. Every newly named partner, triad member, quad member, family member, or other resident introduced through that person's record must itself be resolved against the existing population. If genuinely new, that person also receives a complete record, including employment/occupation status. Do not create Partner-Shaped Objects.
9. Re-run demographic and relationship-structure checks against the aggregate named population after each small batch, not merely against the employees just created.
10. Update the organization staffing record so a requested day/time resolves to the named people actually scheduled to be there.

## Household employment dispersion — default rule

For small businesses and ordinary organizations, members of the same household or immediate family normally work in different workplaces.

The default is **employment dispersion**, not family clustering. Partners, triad/quad members, siblings, parents, adult children, and other close family members should ordinarily have independent careers, workplaces, routines, coworkers, and social networks. Their existence should help populate the wider St. Claire employment ecology rather than automatically adding another worker to the first family member's business.

Two or more members of one household may work for the same small organization only when there is a concrete established reason, such as:

- the organization is genuinely family-run or partner-founded;
- a documented succession or apprenticeship relationship;
- complementary professional partners who deliberately built a practice together;
- temporary or emergency coverage that does not become their normal employment;
- another specific history that makes the shared workplace organic rather than convenient.

Shared employment is therefore an exception requiring an in-world reason, not a generation shortcut. Large institutions can naturally employ unrelated members of the same household by coincidence more often than a six-person shop can, but the same anti-clustering check still applies.

## Partner and household completion rule

A relationship statement creates a population obligation. Writing `partnered with Jane Doe`, `triad with...`, or `quad with...` is not complete unless every named person resolves to an existing complete resident record or is created as one.

For each named adult household member track at minimum:

- full name and full birthdate;
- physical description/presentation and voice;
- occupation and actual employment state;
- organization/title and schedule when employed;
- household and shared relational dates where applicable;
- arrival/born-here state;
- milestones, trait, routines/haunts, and connections;
- demographic fields needed for aggregate checks.

Partners do not inherit one another's workplace. Unemployment is allowed when it is real and established; it is not a blank field to be silently ignored.

## Existing-person preservation rule

Population-first does not mean forcibly assigning every unemployed resident to the next vacancy. Existing character agency and established facts win. A resident may be unemployed by choice, unable to work, retired, studying, caregiving, between careers, holding out for a different field, or simply wrong for the opening. Record the reason when it matters rather than treating unemployment as a defect to erase.

## Scene-ready completion test

An organization is scene-ready only when:

- normal staffing coverage is fully named;
- each worker is a complete resident;
- every named adult partner/household member introduced through those workers is itself resolved and complete;
- existing available residents were checked before new workers were invented;
- household members are not implausibly clustered into the same small workplace without a specific reason;
- schedules permit day/time scene resolution;
- aggregate demographic checks have been run.

A staffing file that has complete employees but unresolved named partners is **STAFF-COMPLETE / POPULATION-INTEGRATION-PENDING**, not fully scene-ready.

## Repair rule for staffing created before this mechanic

The Hearth & Home, Washday, Moving & Delivery, Quiet Harbor, Clearview, and Physical Therapy staffing batches were created before this population-first integration check was established. They must be audited before additional businesses are staffed.

For each previously created worker:

1. resolve the worker against Population;
2. resolve every named partner/household member;
3. identify any pre-existing unemployed/underemployed resident who plausibly should have been considered for the role;
4. preserve the created worker unless an existing resident is a materially better canonical fit and reassignment does not damage established facts;
5. complete missing partner/household records and distribute their employment across the district;
6. check for household co-employment at the same small business and retain it only with a concrete reason;
7. rerun aggregate demographic checks.

Until that audit is complete, those batches remain usable character material but are not considered fully population-integrated.
