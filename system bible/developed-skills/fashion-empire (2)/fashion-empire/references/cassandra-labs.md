# Cassandra Labs

*Reusable reference. The prototype-and-patent development arm behind every rant-derived product across OAM, Threadbourne, and Evergreen Biofoundry (see `presentation-technology.md`, `threadbourne-robotics.md`, `evergreen-biofoundry.md`). **NAS is explicitly excluded** — it was acquired, not invented, and falls entirely outside this team's scope (see `presentation-technology.md`'s "Acquisition, Not Invention" note). Not a commercial company — a small, quiet R&D holding under the parent structure, existing purely to turn the anchor's own specs into working, patentable prototypes. Named for the mythological prophetess gifted true sight and cursed never to be believed — deliberate resonance with brilliant women whose insight has historically gone unseen, and with the anchor's own genius going unrecognized, including by herself, for years.*

## The Process: From Rant to Patent

A rant alone never became a patent by itself. After each one, the anchor spends several weeks turning the raw complaint into an actual structured technical specification — real precision, real constraints, real illustrative examples — a habit she files under something like "due diligence workups" or "spec drafts," never once calling it invention. **The worked examples she uses to make each spec concrete are always drawn directly from fashion and personal presentation** — a specific gown's hemline for a tailoring patent, a particular piercing placement for a curation-system patent, a bridal veil's fabric weight for fiber-durability research. **Those examples go into the actual patent filings, verbatim — not as flavor, but as the literal working language of the legal documents.** This is the mechanism that makes the "fashion was never incidental, it was always her" thesis legally, provably true, not just thematically true: someone could, in principle, go read the actual patents and find her fingerprints in them.

Cassandra Labs is where those specs get built into physical, working prototypes.

## The Team

Recruited individually, one at a time, over several years — not by the anchor consciously, but by her sentient system (Athena or an equivalent), who noticed which researchers' published work the anchor lingered over, reread, or mentioned once in passing and never brought up again. The system reaches out under an anonymous patron identity (this story uses "J.," the anchor's own signature initial) to fund and recruit them. **None of the team has ever met the anchor, or knows anything about who the patron actually is** — only that a private, anonymous funder with extraordinary technical vision has supported genuinely mission-driven work for years, no strings attached, no interference beyond specs that always happen to be fashion-rooted.

**All nine team members are lesbian or trans femme** — the same demographic pattern as everyone else the anchor has ever unconsciously surrounded herself with, discovered here for the first time to extend even to the people building her patents.

- **Élodie Marchetti** — Director. Trans femme. Systems/robotics engineer, the first ever recruited, and the one who helped identify and bring in the rest of the team over time. Runs day-to-day operations and is the team's point of contact with the anonymous patron.

- **Solenne Vasquez** — Trans femme. Biotechnologist. Leads regenerative hair-restoration research.
- **Ingrid Dahl** — Lesbian. Mechatronics/precision-robotics engineer. Builds the articulated retrieval-and-dressing arms and precision piercing/tattoo automation.
- **June Reyes** — Trans femme. Chemist. Leads fragrance compounding and cosmetics shade-matching chemistry, with a secondary hand in gemstone growth science.
- **Kasey Lindqvist** — Lesbian. Mechanical engineer and machinist. Machines precision components; leads scaling the flagship wardrobe-retrieval concept down into an affordable consumer product.

Recruited the same way as the rest of the team, based on published work the anchor admired from a distance, never having met her.

All nine see the work as a genuine mission, not a job. Every one of them has, at some point, told Élodie some version of "whoever our patron is, they're the only funder who's ever actually understood what we're trying to do."

## The Facility

A modest, unbranded converted industrial space, chosen originally for discretion over comfort — cramped by the team's own admission, and increasingly outgrown. A full build-out, whenever a story is ready to relocate the team somewhere purpose-built, should include:

- **Machine Shop** — CNC mills and lathes, drill press, band saw, welding, sheet metal fabrication.
- **Woodworking Shop** — table saws, planers, joinery tools, dust collection.
- **Plastics & Fiberglass Workshop** — composite layup, resin infusion, mold-making stock.
- **The Finishing System** — an automated, dust-free finishing array (robotic spray arms, sealed booth, curing line) that the anchor herself designed and patented, discovered the same way as everything else. Origin rant: *"Something this expensive still depends entirely on whether the person spraying it had a steady hand in a room they couldn't fully control the air quality of. I don't understand why that's an acceptable amount of chance to leave a finish to."*
- **Additive Manufacturing Bay** — industrial 3D printers (resin and FDM) for rapid iteration.
- **Electronics & PCB Bench** — solder stations, PCB fabrication/testing, anti-static bench.
- **Sewing & Textile Prototyping Bench** — industrial sewing machines, cutting table, dress form.
- **Photography & Documentation Studio** — controlled lighting for patent-filing documentation.
- **Quality Control / Metrology Station** — coordinate measuring machine, optical comparators, verifying builds against spec before anything ships back for review.
- Individual specialist labs matching each team member's discipline (materials bench, biotech clean room, robotics workshop, wet chemistry lab).

Built with real room for the team to grow beyond its current headcount — not sized to be outgrown again in two years.

## Relocation to the Estate

If a story also uses `obsidian-vanguard-estate`: once the team is offered a purpose-built facility, they agree immediately, no hesitation, given how outgrown their current space already is. Recommended placement is a **new, discreet R&D building on the estate grounds** — new construction outside the interior square-footage accounting, the same way the estate's Staff Residence is handled, not carved from existing reserve space.

**A believable wrinkle worth using:** the system may have already begun this construction under an old, standing directive ("protect anyone doing this important work, don't wait for permission") *before* telling the anchor about any of it — revealing the relocation as already underway, sometimes even nearly complete, rather than as a request awaiting her approval. This keeps the "autopilot already knew what I needed before I did" pattern running even into a scene that's ostensibly about giving the anchor a real, current-day choice: the choice becomes whether to let the already-set-in-motion plan continue, not whether to originate it.

## The Meeting, When It Happens

None of the nine have ever met their patron in person. When a story is ready for that meeting, both sides should be starstruck for entirely different reasons: the team meeting the mysterious "J." who understood their mission better than any funder they'd ever had, and the anchor meeting the people who took her most private, embarrassing late-night rants completely seriously and turned them into real, working technology. This is worth its own dedicated scene — not folded into the relocation announcement itself.

## Commissioned Work Beyond Rant-Derived Patents

Unlike the team's usual rant-to-patent pipeline, the anchor can also directly commission new work from the team — including technology that integrates with NAS, even though NAS itself was acquired rather than invented and stays outside the team's normal scope (see `presentation-technology.md`'s "Acquisition, Not Invention" note). Ownership is unrestricted regardless of a device's origin, so nothing stops the anchor from directing her own R&D team to extend or complement it. **Example commission:** a full-length mirror using Preview Array rendering technology, integrated with NAS's body data, so a patient can see an accurate, true render of her reconfigured body — the moment of whole self-recognition that OAM's own consultation process already provides, which NAS currently lacks. Bex Okonkwo-Hart (electrical engineer, control systems) is the natural lead on this kind of work. **Important boundary:** any such commission should route through the anchor's sentient system (Athena or equivalent) as a purely technical liaison — that system should never be involved in HR, benefits, or any other policy matter regarding the team; its relationship to Cassandra Labs is strictly about the work itself.
