## Category Gaps & Strategic Vulnerabilities — Usable Story Conflict

Even at full capital deployment, these are real structural gaps a story can use as genuine obstacles, antagonist leverage, or plot complications — not just flavor text:

1. **The Founder-Veto Gap (Independent Houses).** Certain houses (in the source material framed around Armani-, Chanel-, and Comme des Garçons-style examples) have governance structures or family trust charters specifically built to prevent hostile external sale. Capital alone can't crack this — a story needs succession crises, family infighting, or liquidity crunches to force a negotiation, or needs the anchor to pivot to the Patron Model instead (see `patron-model.md`) for houses like this.
2. **Regulatory Anti-Monopoly Bottlenecks.** Simultaneously acquiring majority control of multiple top-tier conglomerates would trigger real antitrust review — a story can use shell structures, offshore proxy trusts, or split-timing acquisitions as the in-fiction workaround, or can let regulatory scrutiny become an actual plot thread (investigations, hearings, a rival tipping off regulators).
3. **Supply Chain Fragility & Artisanal Bottlenecks.** You can buy the brand name; you can't easily buy multi-generational artisan labor. A strike, walkout, or artisan community's refusal to cooperate is a believable, human-scale threat to brand equity that money doesn't automatically solve — good material for a story that wants stakes with a human face rather than pure financial abstraction.
4. **Digital & Data Sovereignty.** Heritage houses often lag in proprietary tech/data infrastructure — a story could use this as a genuine operational weak point (a data breach, a rival's superior tech stack, an integration failure) rather than something the empire has already solved.
