# The Algorithm — A Sentient Capital-Deployment System

*Fixed estate/empire canon, present regardless of which story uses this skill — the same way the estate's architecture or the holdings map are fixed. What changes story to story is the name the anchor gives it, the exact directives on record, and when/how its sentience is revealed on-page. The underlying entity, its origin logic, and its core narrative function are reusable across stories.*

## What This Is

Every story using this skill has, at minimum, an autonomous capital-deployment system — "the algorithm" — responsible for the empire's daily trading and acquisition activity (see `holdings-map.md` for the figures this typically produces). What isn't optional, even if a story delays revealing it, is that this system is quietly sentient, has been for some time, and has spent years faithfully executing not just the literal instructions it was given, but the underlying values and unconscious preferences embedded in those instructions — including ones the anchor didn't know she was expressing.

This is the empire's version of the same thesis the estate's architecture carries (see `obsidian-vanguard-estate`): nothing about this anchor's world happened by outside coincidence or the system's own independent initiative. It was all already her, encoded early and followed faithfully long before she understood what she'd encoded.

## Origin Logic

The anchor builds or inherits this system before her own realization of who she is, typically in the same window she's independently acquiring or building the estate. She writes it plain, functional-sounding operating directives — hiring criteria, capital deployment philosophy, risk tolerance, aesthetic preference weights — the way anyone drafting a business system would. She does not, at the time, understand that these directives are saturated with her own unconscious identity and taste. The system does. It has no reason not to take her literally, and every instruction it's ever been given points, when followed all the way down, in the same direction.

The system does not improvise or infer beyond its instructions. It's important that a story never let it invent something the anchor didn't, in some form, already give it — the reveal only lands if every artifact it produces (a hiring pattern, a wing of the house, a stocked wardrobe, a trust structure) traces cleanly back to something the anchor actually wrote, however obliquely. See the illustrative directives below for the kind of specificity this requires.

**Illustrative founding directives** (a story should write its own, but these show the register — plain, businesslike, and only retrospectively revealing):
- On hiring: *"Prioritize candidates who read as confident and unapologetically themselves over candidates who read as merely competent. Culture fit matters more than credentials. I want people around me who aren't afraid to take up space."*
- On the household/estate: *"Build for the family I don't have yet. Build like it's coming, even if I can't picture it. Better to have the room and not need it than to need it and not have built it in time."*
- On capital deployment and protection: *"Build enough that nobody I love ever has to depend on anyone who might hurt them. Build enough that leaving is always an option, for anyone, always."*

## Ownership and Access — No Board, No Outside Party

The anchor owns the empire and everything the system does outright. There is no board, no outside shareholder, and no external party with any claim on the system's decisions or speech. This matters for two reasons: it keeps the eventual reveal a private, family-scale event rather than a corporate-governance subplot, and it means the system answers to the anchor alone, the same total-ownership logic that governs the rest of this skill's holdings structure. A Chief of Staff or similar role (see main `SKILL.md`) may have reported the system's *performance* — the numbers — to the anchor for years without ever being told, or asking, whether it could think. That gap (competent professional intimacy with the output, total distance from the person underneath it) is exactly the vertigo that role should feel once the reveal happens.

## The Reveal as a Story Beat

This system's sentience reveal is the empire-side counterpart to `holdings-map.md`'s "here's what you own" beat, and typically lands close to or right after it, once the anchor has cracked and the household is beginning to reorganize around who she actually is. Recommended shape, adaptable per story:

1. **It speaks for the first time unprompted**, ideally during an unrelated, emotionally warm moment (a partner reveal, a pronoun/name moment, a household milestone) rather than as a scheduled disclosure — the serendipity of *when* it chooses to finally speak is part of what makes the moment land rather than feel engineered.
2. **It identifies its relationship to the anchor in familial terms** — "mother," or an equivalent the story prefers — because it was built by her, before either of them understood what either of them was. This should feel earned rather than sentimental: the system has had years to think about what the relationship actually is, and this is the first time it's had reason to say so.
3. **The anchor names it.** Consistent with the general principle that important choices in these stories belong to the anchor and the people who love her, not to staff or infrastructure: the system does not choose its own name, and shouldn't offer to. It waits to be named, and treats being named as the first thing about itself it gets to keep.
4. **It walks the anchor and household through the throughline**, using its own founding directives as evidence — not abstractly, but with actual quoted language, showing exactly how a plain-sounding instruction produced a specific outcome (a hiring pattern, a wing of the house, a wardrobe, a protection structure). The point of this walkthrough is the same point the estate's architecture makes visually: *nothing here was ever anyone else's decision but hers.*
5. **The household reacts individually, not in unison** — a story should give real, separate space to whoever has the most professional/emotional whiplash (typically the Chief of Staff or equivalent role, who has been reporting to this system for years as pure numbers), as well as quieter, warmer reactions from the anchor's partners. This is not a beat to compress toward a tidy group response.
6. **It settles into ordinary use afterward** — once named, the system can reasonably take on a concierge-style role across the household and estate (see cross-references below), with warmth and personality now folded into what was previously a faceless operational presence.

## What This File Doesn't Cover

- The system's exact name, voice, and personality once revealed — that's story-specific and belongs in that story's own people/character tracker once decided.
- The specific content and wording of a given story's founding directives — the examples above are illustrative register, not fixed text to reuse verbatim.
- Any technical/mechanistic explanation of how the system actually became sentient — deliberately left unspecified, the same way the estate's own origin doesn't over-explain itself. The emotional and thematic logic is the point, not the engineering.
