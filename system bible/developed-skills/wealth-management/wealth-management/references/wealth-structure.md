# Managing Wealth at Scale — Asset & Ownership Structure

*Reusable reference, story-agnostic. Grounded in real-world family office and wealth management practice at the lower/middle tiers; the top tier is intentionally, deliberately unrealistic, matching the tone of `obsidian-vanguard-estate` and `fashion-empire`. Structured in tiers so it can be applied at any asset level — find the tier closest to a given story's actual number and scale the specifics from there. Don't default to a previous story's tier or figure; ask.*

## Core entity structure (applies at every tier above roughly $5M in complexity)

| Layer | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Revocable Living Trust(s)** | Holds day-to-day assets, avoids probate, allows continued control during the grantor's lifetime. |
| **Irrevocable Trusts (Dynasty/GST Trust)** | Removes assets from the taxable estate; protects multi-generational transfers from estate tax and creditor/divorce claims on beneficiaries. |
| **LLC — Real Property Holding Company** | Real estate titled to an LLC rather than individuals, limiting personal liability. |
| **LLC — Personal Property / Fleet Holding Company** | Vehicles, aircraft, and marine vessels (see `obsidian-vanguard-estate`'s Personal Fleet) titled to the holding company rather than any individual — same liability-limiting logic as the real property LLC, applied to high-value personal property. |
| **LLC or LP — Investment Holding Company** | Houses the liquid investment portfolio separately from real estate. |
| **Family Office Entity** | A management company that employs family office staff and bills the trusts/holding companies for services — keeps management legally distinct from the assets themselves. |

The *existence* of this structure doesn't change much across tiers — what changes is how much of it is staffed in-house versus outsourced, and how many experts are retained.

## Expert costs — what each role actually costs to retain
Useful across all tiers, since even a family below "family office" scale still needs several of these, just on a contracted/part-time basis rather than as full employees.

| Expert | Typical Cost Structure | Cost Range |
|---|---|---|
| Estate Planning Attorney | Hourly or flat-fee per trust/document; ongoing retainer for updates | $400–$800/hr; full estate plan setup $15,000–$75,000+ depending on complexity; ongoing retainer $10,000–$50,000/year |
| Tax Accountant/CPA (complex returns) | Annual retainer + return prep | $10,000–$100,000+/year depending on entity count and complexity |
| In-house Tax Director | Full-time salary | $200,000–$300,000/year + benefits |
| Financial Advisor / Wealth Manager (outsourced) | % of assets under management | Typically 0.5%–1.0% of AUM/year, sliding down at higher balances |
| Chief Investment Officer (in-house) | Full-time salary | $400,000–$800,000/year + benefits + bonus |
| Family Office CEO/President | Full-time salary | $500,000–$1,000,000+/year + benefits + bonus |
| Controller/CFO | Full-time salary | $200,000–$350,000/year + benefits |
| Investment Analyst | Full-time salary | $120,000–$180,000/year + benefits |

## Tiered guidance by asset size

### Tier 1 — roughly $1M
No family office. This is "good financial advisor + estate attorney" territory.
- **Structure:** A revocable living trust and a basic will; possibly a single irrevocable trust if there's a specific tax or protection goal.
- **Experts:** One outsourced financial advisor (0.75–1.0% AUM/year, so **~$7,500–$10,000/year**), one estate attorney for setup (**~$3,000–$8,000** one-time) and occasional updates, a CPA for annual tax prep (**~$1,500–$4,000/year**).
- **No dedicated staff of any kind.**

### Tier 2 — roughly $50M
Approaching family-office territory, but a full one usually isn't justified yet. Most families at this level use a **multi-family office (MFO)** — sharing infrastructure and staff costs across several unrelated wealthy families — rather than standing up their own.
- **Structure:** Revocable trust + at least one dynasty/irrevocable trust for estate tax planning; a holding LLC if there's meaningful real estate.
- **Experts:** MFO relationship (typically **0.5%–0.75% of AUM/year**, so **~$250,000–$375,000/year**, which is bundled and covers investment management, basic tax coordination, and reporting), a dedicated estate attorney relationship (**~$20,000–$40,000/year** in ongoing work), possibly a part-time bookkeeper/controller (**~$40,000–$70,000/year** if hired directly, or folded into the MFO fee).

### Tier 3 — roughly $500M
This is where a **single-family office becomes clearly justified**, though some families still use an MFO at this level for cost efficiency.
- **Structure:** Full structure as outlined above — revocable trust, dynasty trust(s), separate real estate and investment holding LLCs, a family office management entity.
- **Experts:** Lean in-house team — CEO/President (**~$400,000–$600,000/year**), Controller (**~$180,000–$250,000/year**), 1 Investment Analyst (**~$130,000/year**), outsourced CIO function via an investment consultant rather than a full in-house CIO (**~0.15%–0.25% of AUM for the investment-management layer**, so **~$750,000–$1,250,000/year**), tax director or a very senior contracted CPA relationship (**~$150,000–$250,000/year**). Total all-in family office cost: roughly **$2M–$3.5M/year**, in the ballpark of 0.5%–0.7% of AUM.

### Tier 4 — roughly $1B–$10B
Full single-family office — CEO, CIO, Controller, analysts, in-house tax director, risk manager, contracted estate counsel. All-in cost **~$5M–$100M/year** (0.5%–1.0% of AUM, trending toward the lower end at this scale due to economies of scale). See role-by-role breakdown above for individual costs — at this tier, essentially every role on that table is justified as a full in-house hire rather than outsourced. *(A specific story's exact figure within this range is story-specific — don't default to a previous story's number.)*

### Tier 5 — empire-scale, roughly $500B–$1T+
**This is the tier for the deliberately, unrealistically wealthy anchor archetype these stories are built around** — sized to comfortably fund a `fashion-empire`-scale monopolistic holding structure (which alone requires ~$490B–$620B in capital deployment) with room to spare. This tier exceeds the wealth of any actual family office, sovereign wealth fund, or private family in existence — that's the point, not an error to correct. Once a story establishes its anchor at this tier, the wealth itself doesn't need to be re-justified or re-explained every time it comes up; it can simply function as background texture in later scenes, the same way a normal-wealth character's rent or grocery bill wouldn't need re-explaining.
- **Structure:** The "family office" functionally becomes a full asset management institution in its own right — its own board, formal investment committees by asset class, likely its own broker-dealer or registered investment advisor (RIA) registration for regulatory reasons. See also the Shell-Corporation Layer, below — at this scale, a meaningful portion of the structure exists specifically to manage regulatory exposure from empire-scale acquisitions.
- **Experts:** Dozens to hundreds of investment professionals organized by asset class (public equities desk, private equity team, real assets team, etc.), a general counsel's office rather than a single retained attorney, an internal audit function, and a dedicated government affairs/regulatory team given the scale would attract genuine regulatory scrutiny. Cost as a percentage of AUM typically compresses further at this scale (**0.2%–0.4%** is plausible), which would still be in the **billions per year** in absolute terms — again, not something a story needs to dwell on or justify repeatedly once established.

## The Shell-Corporation / Holding-Company Layer
For stories running a `fashion-empire`-scale corporate holding structure, this is where that empire's acquisition vehicles actually live, legally speaking. `fashion-empire`'s own "Regulatory Anti-Monopoly Bottlenecks" gap notes that simultaneous majority acquisition of multiple top-tier luxury conglomerates would trigger real antitrust review — in-fiction, this is managed through:
- **Shell acquisition entities** — separate holding companies used to acquire stakes without immediately revealing the full scope of ownership consolidating under one anchor.
- **Offshore proxy trusts** — jurisdictionally separated trust structures that hold equity on the anchor's behalf, adding a layer of distance between the anchor's name and the acquisition.
- **Split-timing acquisition vehicles** — separate entities acquiring stakes on staggered timelines specifically to stay under individual regulatory reporting/review tripwires until a later consolidation phase.

This layer sits legally underneath the empire (which is the *business*) and is itself housed within the broader Tier 5 wealth structure above (the *legal/financial scaffolding*) — the empire is what's owned; this is how the owning is structured to avoid premature regulatory exposure.

## The Partner Safety Layer
Distinct from the general trust structure above, and specific to stories where the anchor's household includes multiple committed partners (see `polyamory-family`): a deliberate legal/financial layer exists to protect each partner **independently of the relationship's future** — protection that exists because the anchor genuinely wants it to, not because any partner has asked or needs to prove anything to receive it.
- **Individual protective trusts** — each committed partner may have their own trust established in their name, independent of the anchor's own estate, ensuring they're not financially dependent on the relationship continuing in order to remain secure.
- **No precondition of permanence** — this layer is not structured as a reward for staying, or contingent on the relationship lasting a certain length of time; it exists to remove financial vulnerability as a factor in whether someone stays, not to create a golden handcuff.
- **Privacy protection** — legal structuring (NDAs, privacy trusts, anonymized entity ownership) to shield partners from the public scrutiny, media attention, and safety risk that comes with visible proximity to this level of wealth.
- **When this gets set up, story-specific:** whether a given partner has already received this protection, is in the process of receiving it, or hasn't yet been offered it is a story-specific, evolving detail — track it in that story's own state-of-world file, not here.

## Asset allocation — reasonable starting model at the mid-large tiers (illustrative, not prescriptive)
- **Public equities:** 30–35%
- **Fixed income/cash equivalents:** 15–20%
- **Private equity/venture:** 15–20%
- **Real estate (beyond primary residence):** 10–15%
- **Alternative investments:** 10–15%
- **Liquidity reserve:** 12–24 months of operating costs held outside the invested portfolio

## Governance
Regardless of tier, once a family office of any kind exists, most families formalize decision-making:
- **Family Council/Investment Committee** — regular (often quarterly) review meetings.
- **Family Constitution** — a written governance document, increasingly common as family size and complexity grow (particularly relevant for a story with an actively expanding polyamorous household).
