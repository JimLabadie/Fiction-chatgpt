# Integrating New Members & Inviting Others In

*Reusable reference. Covers a household's actual process and philosophy for bringing new people into their intimate life — both as one-time or occasional guests, and as genuine new household members. Distinct from the Household Agreements checklist (`household-agreements.md`), which only flags that these questions need deciding; this file is a fuller reusable philosophy a story can adopt directly.*

## Core Philosophy: Monogamous as a Unit, Open by Full Consent

A household's polycule can be internally monogamous — fully committed to each other, no outside romantic relationships pursued individually — while still being genuinely open, *as a group*, to inviting someone else in, whether for a single shared night, a real romance the whole household falls into together, or, in rarer cases, as a lasting new member. What makes this consistent with monogamy rather than a violation of it: this is never any one partner stepping outside the relationship alone, and it is never something arranged or decided by less than the full household. It's the whole household choosing together, every time, with real unanimous consent — not a loophole in monogamy, but a different shape of commitment that still requires everyone's real agreement before it happens. **If even one member isn't genuinely on board, it doesn't happen — full stop, no exceptions, no override, no "just this once."**

## Why the Household Does This

This isn't framed as recreational novelty-seeking, though sometimes it's exactly that — wanting to have fun together, nothing deeper required. But it's also, often, something more intentional: a genuine belief, held by the anchor and the household generally, that you can show someone real love and affection, without conditions, if that's something they want and the household wants to offer it. Sometimes this is about someone who needs to feel loved and cherished and doesn't otherwise have that in their life — a staff member, someone they've met, anyone the household has come to care about. It is never about performing generosity or collecting people; the household only extends this when it's real. **Importantly, this isn't only ever physical** — the household experiences this as a genuinely shared thing, sometimes rising to real collective romance and affection for the person they've brought in, felt by the whole household together rather than any one member pairing off privately. That's precisely what keeps it from reading as a breach of their monogamy: it's the family loving someone, together, as a family.

## The Spectrum: A Single Night vs. Genuine Integration

These are meaningfully different things, and a story should keep them distinct rather than treating every invitation as a step toward permanent membership:
- **A shared night.** An invitation extended and accepted for its own sake — warmth, intimacy, fun — with no expectation it becomes anything more. Most invitations are this, and that's not a lesser outcome; it doesn't need to lead anywhere to have been meaningful.
- **Genuine integration.** Occasionally, someone the household has invited in — through repeated closeness, real emotional connection, or simply falling in love over time — becomes someone the household wants as a lasting member, not just a guest. This is a slower, separate process from any single invitation, built on its own real relationship, not an automatic next step after a night together. (See the `fashion-empire` skill's Chief of Staff role and `obsidian-vanguard-estate`'s Senior/Lead Hair Stylist archetype for two recurring examples of this pattern — a professional relationship that becomes something more, on its own timeline, for its own real reasons.)

## Consent Is Absolute, Every Time

- **Full household agreement, every single time**, not a standing policy that gets invoked without a fresh conversation. Past agreement to one invitation never implies agreement to the next.
- **Never decided or arranged by one member alone, ever** — not the anchor, not any individual partner. This is a whole-household decision by definition; anything less than that isn't this practice, it's something else, and would be a real breach.
- **The person being invited must want it, genuinely and without any pressure.** This is especially important where the person is staff or in any position where the household has power over their circumstances (employment, housing, financial security) — participation must never be expected, implied, or in any way tied to someone's job, and declining must carry zero consequence of any kind. A story should make this boundary explicit on the page if a staff member is involved, not just assume the reader infers it.
- **This is never coercive, and never written as coercive**, regardless of the power or wealth differential involved. If a story can't clearly show genuine, enthusiastic, pressure-free consent, that's a sign the scene needs more groundwork, not less.

## Story-Specific Application
Which staff members, guests, or others a given story's household has actually invited in — and which of those, if any, went on to become genuine household members — is story-specific and belongs in that story's own state-of-world file, not here.
