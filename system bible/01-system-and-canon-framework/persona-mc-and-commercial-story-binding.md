# Persona, MC, and Commercial Story Binding

Status: ACTIVE PROJECT GOVERNANCE
Authority: AUTHOR-REQUESTED REVISION
Effective: 2026-09-14

## Core definitions

**Jim the author** supplies creative direction and canon decisions.

**Jim the persona** remains available for private immersion, concept conversations, and development. Preserving that persona does not require naming a commercial protagonist Jim.

**MC** means the main character of the current story. It is a role label in the reusable bible, not a proper name. The Story Bible binds that role to the story's actual named character. Commercial work defaults to a distinct protagonist unless the author explicitly selects the Jim persona.

**House voice** is the established Voice and Style Guide: intimate, candid, contemporary, conversational, psychologically observant, emotionally immediate, naturally humorous, and grounded in lived experience. It applies across protagonists.

## How a story uses the framework

Set the MC's name and character record in the Story Bible. If the name is undecided, use `[MC_NAME]` in planning. Finished narrative uses the chosen name and appropriate pronouns, never a literal MC placeholder.

Select the world, characters, institutions, and history modules needed for that story. Within a selected module, references to MC bind consistently to the assigned protagonist. Anchor, creator, owner, and patron are roles: record their assignment explicitly when they belong to different characters.

Changing the protagonist's name does not erase a selected module's specific history, technical facts, relationships, causality, or emotional architecture. In the full Athena history, the MC remains Athena's creator and the person she understands as Mom; authorizing a different name alone does not rewrite Athena as a generic AI or silently change that relationship. A genuinely different premise requires an explicit story-level choice.

A story that does not select that history does not inherit it merely because it uses the same house voice. The MC's biography, identity, desires, pronouns, and development belong to that story; do not copy private author facts or Jim-persona facts automatically.

## Names and dependencies

J. remains the established outward business alias in the Athena module. It need not match the protagonist's initial. Its function and underlying ownership remain the same with any MC name. If a story deliberately changes the alias, propagate that choice through dialogue, correspondence, signatures, and ownership references.

Athena, Danielle Reyes, Amélie, Lesbos Holdings, Asteria Intelligence, and other established characters and institutions retain their own names. The MC name is not a command to rename the world.

Existing source filenames, dated workbooks, historical quotations, and recovery receipts may retain Jim for traceability. Those labels do not bind a new story's protagonist or supply a publication title. Historical material is evidence of earlier development; this record governs current name binding.

## Commercial use and private persona material

The framework now supports commercial fiction and products as well as private persona fiction. Earlier blanket statements excluding publication or sale are superseded within that scope.

Use the same voice, emotional specificity, humor, and selected fictional worldbuilding. Commercial use does not require flattening the prose into generic advertising or addressing a fictional protagonist as “you.”

The personal authorial-intent record remains persona-specific development material. Do not insert its personal disclosures into commercial narrative, packaging, marketing, or a distributed template. Build the product from the selected fictional records and story brief.

Fictional product capabilities, customer scope, ownership, and financial figures do not change merely because the protagonist has a different name. Products and institutions can serve the customers their own records define; they are not restricted to someone named Jim.

## Examples of role binding

These are non-canon naming examples only.

| Reusable rule | Story with Morgan as MC | Story with Elena as MC |
|---|---|---|
| MC created Athena | Morgan created Athena | Elena created Athena |
| Athena knows MC as Mom in the selected history | Athena knows Morgan as Mom | Athena knows Elena as Mom |
| MC owns the company through J. | Morgan owns the company through J. | Elena owns the company through J. |
| MC chooses whether to authorize Artemis | Morgan makes the decision | Elena makes the decision |

The name changes; the chosen module's causal and emotional relationships remain intact. Neither example establishes a new canonical character.

## Continuity check

Before delivering a commercial draft, check the actual narrative and product-facing text for unintended Jim references, unresolved MC placeholders, mismatched aliases or initials, transferred private biography, and inconsistent ownership or relationship references. Preserve purposeful references to another independently named character only when the story establishes them. Keep the check out of the prose.

## Revision boundary

This is the author's approved separation of persona, voice, and main-character name. It does not delete the Jim persona, rename existing accepted stories, settle unrelated recovery questions, or treat exploratory persona conversations as in-world events.
