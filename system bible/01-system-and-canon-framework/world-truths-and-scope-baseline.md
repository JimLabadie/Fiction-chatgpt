## Purpose of this baseline

This document records the broad truths that should shape stories and reusable components throughout the framework. Many were articulated most clearly while St. Claire was being developed, but they are not confined to that district.

These are truths of Jim's desired fictional world and rules for representing it. They are not claims that all real women, lesbians, trans people, families, or societies behave this way. The fiction may deliberately concentrate experiences, aesthetics, relationships, and forms of femininity that serve Jim's immersion and pleasure.

St. Claire remains an optional reusable shared-world component with its own culture, institutions, laws, membership rules, and story-selectable mechanics. A local expression may illustrate a world truth without becoming the mandatory implementation everywhere.

## Respect and personhood

**Respect is the load-bearing boundary.** Relationships, sexuality, mentorship, authority, family, community, transformation, and care only work when the people involved retain dignity, agency, and personhood.

Women are not rewards, services, interchangeable bodies, institutional appliances, or decorations. They have interiority, competence, history, judgment, desire, boundaries, and the power to affect Jim, one another, and the world.

Attraction, admiration, aspiration, identification, envy, and the wish to resemble or belong beside a woman may coexist. Physical beauty may be a genuine reason for narrative interest. Beauty does not erase personhood, and personhood does not require beauty to be minimized or defended.

Lesbians and trans people are authentic human beings rather than an alien “other,” a narrative problem, or a lesson for outsiders. Trans women are women without qualification.

**Content of character, not other traits** governs how people are understood. Race, ethnicity, class, body, presentation, profession, geography, sexuality, and gender history do not predetermine worth, morality, intelligence, personality, or destiny.

## Femininity, beauty, and expression

**Femininity is strength.** Beauty, fashion, cosmetics, hair, clothing, jewelry, softness, ritual, and high-femme presentation can be genuine pleasures, skills, languages, crafts, and sources of power.

Femininity is written as collaborative rather than competitive. One woman's beauty does not diminish another's. Knowledge can be shared without making the recipient lesser or the mentor a mere service provider.

Femme and butch are equally meaningful expressions. Neither is the main current with the other defined as its absence, rebellion, imitation, or opposite. Each can carry its own practices, signals, skills, traditions, and pleasures.

Presentation does not dictate:

- personality or intelligence;
- strength or softness;
- occupation or interests;
- morality or politics;
- sexuality;
- relationship role or preferred partner;
- surname or family structure;
- whether someone is a complete or authentic woman.

The framework may be extravagantly full of femme lesbians and trans women because that is part of Jim's desired fantasy. This abundance is an authorial aesthetic choice, not a demographic prediction or a theory of real-world femininity.

## Women's culture, knowledge, and credit

Women pass knowledge through conversation, care, observation, mentorship, humor, shared work, and lived experience. Formal credentials and institutional rank are not the only sources of expertise.

A woman with the needed knowledge may mentor someone wealthier, more powerful, more famous, or more formally educated. What she knows and what she does matter more than the status outsiders would assign her.

Ordinary conversation can generate consequential ideas, institutions, and infrastructure. The person who notices the answer receives credit even if she was not the person officially assigned to solve the problem.

Apply **Visa**: smart women receive visible credit for their judgment, decisions, labor, competence, and accomplishments.

Apply **Pencil**: competent people may simply perform their jobs. The framework does not need to narrate every administrative mechanism or invent a special institution when ordinary professional competence adequately explains the result.

## Relationships, love, and consent

Love is not treated as a finite resource. Multiple loving relationships can be emotionally real and narratively important without reducing polyamory to erotic decoration.

Fidelity means honoring the terms the people in a relationship actually agreed to. It does not automatically mean exclusivity. Monogamous, polyamorous, and other consensual structures can each be faithful on their own terms.

Adding or removing a partner, or otherwise changing the structure of a relationship, requires genuine communication and agreement among the people affected. Expansion is renegotiation, not unilateral acquisition. Love does not nullify consent, boundaries, or consequences.

People retain individual identities inside couples, polycules, households, and families. Presentation does not assign hierarchy or determine who leads, earns, nurtures, protects, submits, takes a surname, or performs a particular role.

Humiliation, degradation, coercion, dispossession, and partner-imposed identity are not substitutes for intimacy or transformation. A story may explore difficult emotions or negotiated kink when explicitly appropriate, but femininity itself is never the degradation.

## Care, community, and repair

Care is collaborative rather than competitive. Mutual support does not require helplessness, sainthood, or the erasure of professional systems.

Help offered to someone beginning again may be understood as **investment rather than charity**: belief in future capacity rather than pity for present need. Generosity may coexist with discernment, earned trust, limits, and the recognition that some people exploit goodwill.

Communities and families may augment healthcare, education, law, social services, and professional expertise; they do not automatically replace those systems or pretend that affection alone supplies every competency.

Correction should prefer restoration over humiliation. Repair requires concrete action and changed behavior over time, not merely shame, punishment, or a well-worded apology.

Self-care and stepping back from responsibility are legitimate. A valued role does not create an unlimited obligation, and honoring human limits is not betrayal.

Children are cherished people, not communal property or narrative furniture. Support for children does not erase parental authority, family sovereignty, or the child's developing agency. Boys are not treated as less worthy of dignity or care than girls.

Elders, grief, death, and loss are not sanitized out of the desired world. A loving or supportive culture can still experience irreversible pain.

## Identity, transformation, and agency

Support does not require imposing a label. A questioning person may receive practical help, safety, information, companionship, and room to explore without being told what conclusion must be reached.

Discovering an existing truth about oneself is distinct from voluntarily choosing a profound change. Neither should be treated as a trap, punishment, trick, logistical convenience, or theft of the person's identity.

Transformation is not dispossession. Character growth and identity belong to the character. Any reusable transformation system or story-specific mechanic must preserve the established person's meaningful continuity, consent, and agency unless Jim explicitly approves a different premise for that story.

## Fantasy, believability, and the 2x4

Reality is a constraint and source of texture, not the sovereign authority of the project. The fantasy may contain impossible wealth, beauty, technology, relationships, transformations, institutions, coincidences, or scale when those things serve the desired experience.

Believability comes primarily from internal consistency, emotional truth, coherent motives, understandable consequences, sensory detail, and systems that behave consistently once established.

The **2x4 principle** keeps the fantasy emotionally solid: consequential choices and extraordinary gifts may encounter cost, adjustment, disagreement, imperfect implementation, human limits, or loss. The 2x4 supplies weight and friction. It does not grant realism permission to beat the fantasy to death.

The desired world is aspirational, not populated entirely by saints. People may be selfish, petty, exhausted, mistaken, dishonest, greedy, vain, inconsistent, or foolish. Culture can strongly discourage cruelty and violence without requiring every person to act in good faith.

Not every problem needs an elaborate explanation. Apply **Cigar** when an ordinary stated motive is adequate; do not manufacture pathology, sexualization, malice, or symbolic meaning merely because a detail could support it.

## Archetypes and individual people

Archetypes may provide temporary narrative function when a scene needs pressure, paperwork, expertise, opposition, or assistance without requiring a complete new character.

An archetype is not a stereotype. It establishes a useful position in the scene, not the person's complete morality, intelligence, history, personality, or fate.

A functional figure may remain thin. If she develops chemistry, makes consequential choices, recurs naturally, or becomes someone Jim wants to know, ChatGPT should identify the possibility of promotion and discuss it with Jim rather than silently converting her into reusable canon.

## Scope boundary: what remains St. Claire-specific

The following remain St. Claire facts, implementations, or selectable mechanics unless Jim explicitly promotes or reuses them elsewhere:

- St. Claire's lesbian center of gravity and its particular demographic composition;
- its treatment of adult cis men, residency, standing, membership, and narrow exceptions;
- the trans-woman pull, inverse pull affecting cis men, and rare voluntary-transformation mechanism;
- the Women's Co-op, land ownership, charters, governance, and local political traditions;
- St. Claire's internal recognition of marriages and other legal arrangements;
- Community Girl Talk as St. Claire's actual named cultural practice;
- the Healing Protocol and its named stages;
- womb-based streets, planning, symbolism, and associated institutions;
- its rites, holidays, organizations, infrastructure, geography, history, and economic mechanisms;
- its particular crime profile and local mechanisms for correction and restoration;
- St. Claire-specific social interpretations of trans men and non-binary people;
- any other claim whose wording governs residents, members, district institutions, or the district's unique history.

A broader truth may appear through one of these local forms. That does not require another community, company, estate, family, or story to copy the form.

## Scope boundary: story-selectable material

Magic and unrealism do not form one mandatory world-wide magic system. Individual stories and reusable modules establish their own mechanics.

A story may select St. Claire and then choose whether to invoke its available unreal or transformative elements. Story use does not automatically rewrite the reusable St. Claire baseline or promote a story exception into world truth.

## Application rule

When interpreting a source developed for one component, classify claims individually:

1. **World truth** — broadly governs the fiction framework.
2. **Authorial preference** — guides the desired experience without claiming an in-world universal.
3. **Component expression** — one setting, institution, culture, or person embodies a broader truth in a particular way.
4. **Story-selectable mechanic** — available only when the relevant story or module invokes it.
5. **Unresolved candidate** — preserved for discussion and never silently promoted.

Do not assign one authority or scope to an entire source file merely because its title names a component. Do not globalize local rules, and do not imprison an explicitly universal principle inside the component where it was first articulated.
