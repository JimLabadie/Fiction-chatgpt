## Circulation systems

The Estate separates five networks:

1. private household movement inside Anchor A and to selected family destinations;
2. invited guest movement through residential and hospitality areas;
3. scheduled Anchor B visitor movement through its independent arrival and reception;
4. staff movement through back-of-house corridors, lifts, carts, and service rooms;
5. commercial delivery movement ending at the external operations district before screened transfer inside.

Quiet low-speed carts serve the enormous interior. Passenger elevators, freight lifts, B1/B2 service drives, protected stairs, and staff-only corridors prevent distance from turning daily operation into a parade through private rooms.

## Adaptation control

Future revisions begin with the inherited space assigned in this ledger. Expansion must take a named adjacent unit or named reserve. Reduction returns a named space to reserve. Basement space cannot inflate above-grade program totals, and reserve space cannot be treated as a finished room without a recorded allocation.

Anchor B's internal partition is deliberately flexible because its later unified Mnemosyne program supersedes the earlier separate 20,000-square-foot museum and 8,000-square-foot gallery allocation. The 125,000-square-foot shell is fixed; its exact gallery and laboratory walls may change with the integrated visitor journey.
