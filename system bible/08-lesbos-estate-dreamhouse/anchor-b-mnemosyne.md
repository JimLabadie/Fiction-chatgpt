## Identity

Anchor B is **Mnemosyne**, one unified institution occupying the complete approximately 125,000-square-foot former department-store anchor.

It is not a fashion museum, art gallery, and corporate showroom that happen to share a lobby. It is a single journey through what fashion is, how it is made, how it changes over time, and how it relates to bodies, labor, technology, art, identity, culture, commerce, and the world.

Earlier plans for separate museum, gallery, Innovation Showroom, Metis showroom, and Techne showroom buildings are superseded. Their useful functions survive inside Mnemosyne, organized by the story of fashion rather than corporate boundaries.

## Physical shell and access

Anchor B contains two approximately 62,500-square-foot floors with:

- an independent scheduled-visitor entrance;
- shared reception and orientation;
- a controlled connection to the Great Gathering Hall;
- rear receiving and secure loading;
- passenger and freight elevators;
- direct controlled service access to B1;
- a secure lift to the B1 conservation and staging lab;
- enough clear-span and modular space to change exhibitions without structural rebuilding.

Mnemosyne has no ordinary walk-in hours. Family members and designated staff can activate access. Scheduled guests may attend exhibitions, educational visits, business demonstrations, conferences, galas, and industry events. Once an event is opened, guests experience the intended route normally; they are not individually escorted past every object.

There is no standing public gift shop, ticket desk, or cafe. Event hospitality comes from the Estate culinary operation or selected outside providers.

## The integrated visitor journey

The visitor route is chronological and thematic rather than a march through company booths.

### Core Gallery

The Core Gallery traces fashion through time using garments, footwear, jewelry, accessories, textiles, beauty objects, photographs, advertising, fine art, film and performance costume, tools, patterns, retail artifacts, historic machines, and social context.

Important objects are not displayed as isolated trophies. A section can place a garment beside the materials, labor, pattern logic, body ideals, art, historical machinery, and social conditions that made it possible.

### Making fashion

Historic techniques and equipment sit beside working or demonstrable modern systems from Techne, Metis, Threadbourne, Evergreen, NAS, OAM, and other holdings when those systems exist at the selected story date.

The companies supply tools and expertise to the institution; they do not own separate attractions inside it. A visitor may move from hand work to mechanization, digital patterning, automated cutting and sewing, adaptive textiles, robotics, optical quality control, beauty technology, preservation science, and speculative future systems without leaving the narrative thread.

### Future

The Future program presents prototypes, experimental materials, advanced fabrication, body and fit systems, emerging aesthetics, and questions about what fashion may become. It may use live demonstrations, working machinery, simulations, interactive displays, and commissioned art.

Future is not advertising copy. It can present benefits, risks, labor consequences, access questions, environmental cost, identity, and disagreement.

### Rotating programs

Modular halls support designer retrospectives, material studies, cultural histories, guest-curated exhibitions, contemporary runway work, film-costume programs, technology demonstrations, and large scenography. Partitions carry power and data; ceiling grids support theatrical light; directional audio limits bleed; projection surfaces can place runway or production footage beside physical objects.

### Soapbox and debate

Mnemosyne includes formal places for argument: talks, panels, workshops, debates, artist conversations, labor history, criticism, and public questions. The institution does not pretend fashion is politically or socially neutral.

### Aglaea

Aglaea provides the footwear-facing presentation and interpretive function inside the unified journey. It connects shoes to design, craft, status, technology, movement, pain, gender, performance, collecting, and personal use rather than isolating footwear as a decorative side room. Its exact internal architecture remains open pending the full footwear and collection reconciliation.

## Fine art throughout Mnemosyne

Mnemosyne's fine-art commitment is structural, not confined to one detached gallery.

### Permanent collection

Approximately 15,000 square feet supports the household's standing fine-art collection, including established and historically significant feminist and lesbian artists as well as work selected by Mnemosyne's curatorial team. Acquisitions may occur through purchases, gifts, bequests, direct commissions, and transfers accepted by the collection.

Permanent works may also appear throughout the chronological and thematic fashion journey when the relationship is meaningful.

### Traveling exhibitions

Approximately 15,000 square feet supports loan exhibitions from museums, galleries, artists, and private collections. The program requires real agreements, insurance, condition reporting, climate-controlled transport, receiving, unpacking, installation, packing, and return. Exhibitions are scheduled far enough ahead for those people and institutions to do their work.

### Erato Collective

Approximately 20,000 square feet supports Erato's working studios, fellowship, broader collective access, and its own exhibition rhythm.

- The Fellowship supports approximately six artists at a time through application and interview.
- The broader Collective provides studio access beyond the funded cohort.
- Artists work in any medium appropriate to their practice.
- Housing is off-site; artists commute rather than living inside Anchor B.
- Finished work may enter Mnemosyne's permanent collection, fulfill a commission, sell during an exhibition, or remain the artist's property.
- Erato is an independent artists' organization related to Mnemosyne, not an anonymous pool of decorators or a corporate art department.

Erato work can be integrated throughout Mnemosyne instead of being confined to the studio wing.

## Collections and ownership

Research targets, desired acquisitions, loans, owned objects, product copies, commissioned work, and examples used only for interpretation are different statuses. CLIO records those distinctions.

Every object owned by the MC remains the MC's property wherever it is stored or displayed. Mnemosyne is the institution that interprets, conserves, documents, and presents the collection; it is not a separate legal owner standing between the MC and an object.

The following rules control:

- ownership overrides curatorial status;
- a wearable owned object may be worn;
- conservation advice informs the choice but does not silently convert advice into authority;
- personal product copies and historically important collection objects may be the same physical item or separate examples, but ownership remains explicit;
- moving an item from Anchor A to B1, B2, or a gallery changes location and state, not identity or owner;
- a research list is not proof of acquisition;
- a loan does not become owned because it is displayed at Lesbos.

The collection can encompass thousands of objects. Mnemosyne's visible footprint remains curated because underground storage, rotation inventory, conservation staging, crates, transport equipment, and objects resting off display occupy B1 and B2.

## Display and conservation

Garment galleries use controlled temperature, humidity, light, dust, and handling. Dark microcement, velvet or other low-reflective surfaces can make textiles visually dominant. Museum-grade vitrines use low-heat, low-UV lighting. Mannequins and understructures are fitted to each garment's historical construction rather than forcing every object onto one modern body.

Digital stations allow close inspection of weave, embroidery, seams, labels, internal construction, provenance, and condition without exposing the original object to handling. Projection, directional audio, and demonstrable equipment add context without replacing the object.

The B1 lab contains:

- flat textile drawers and roll storage;
- isolated filtration for dust, lint, and moisture;
- quarantine and incoming inspection;
- condition assessment and documentation;
- textile, beadwork, embellishment, footwear, and accessory workstations;
- mannequin and mount preparation;
- photography and records;
- secure staging for installation and return;
- a one-way smart-glass viewing connection that can frost when privacy or object safety requires it.

## Institutional operations

Mnemosyne requires directors, curators, registrars, conservators, preparators, educators, studio management, visitor services, event coordination, specialized security, collection handlers, and technical operators. Staffing details are in [Staffing and Operations](staffing-and-operations.md).
