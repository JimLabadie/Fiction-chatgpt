## Monogamous as a unit

The fourteen Lesbos partners form one polyamorous relationship that is monogamous as a unit. No partner independently pursues an outside romantic or sexual relationship. The relationship can collectively invite another adult into a shared experience without violating that commitment because the decision and experience belong to the relationship as a whole.

This is not a loophole. Every partner and the invited adult must genuinely consent each time. One no stops the invitation. Past agreement creates no standing permission, and no partner—including the transgender family anchor—can arrange or authorize it alone.

## Why an invitation may happen

An invitation may arise from simple shared fun, warmth, or attraction. It may also come from sincere collective affection and a desire to love and cherish someone the partners have come to care about. The connection can be romantic and emotionally meaningful rather than merely physical.

The invited adult is not a rescue project, acquisition, generosity performance, reward, or applicant auditioning for membership. She does not owe the family intimacy because they care about her or have helped her. Sometimes people want one beautiful night together because they want it. Cigar applies.

## A complete shared experience

Most invitations are complete in themselves. A night of intimacy, affection, and fun does not need to become a courtship or permanent relationship to matter. No one should imply that a shared experience was a lesser outcome because it ended happily without changing the household.

Story detail follows narrative value. An ordinary joyful night may be summarized; a moment that changes a person or relationship may receive emotional and sensual attention. The intimacy framework controls treatment and never requires mechanics-first erotica.

## Lasting integration is separate

Occasionally, repeated closeness and genuine mutual love may grow into a desire for a lasting partnership. That is not an automatic next stage, presumed reward, or consequence of an intimate invitation. It develops on its own timeline and requires a separate, fresh, unanimous decision by the existing relationship and the prospective partner.

The prospective partner is already a complete adult, not a candidate being evaluated by a panel. The family may need ordinary conversations about compatibility and shared life, just as any established relationship would, but polyamory does not turn love into institutional admission.

## Consent under structural power

Where the invited person is an employee, resident, beneficiary, patient, artist, or otherwise has less institutional or economic power, verbal reassurance is insufficient by itself. The Hestia Principle creates independent, enforceable protection before the intimate choice matters:

- refusal does not affect employment, compensation, advancement, housing, healthcare, education, reputation, or community;
- departure remains materially survivable;
- promised protection does not vest only if intimacy occurs or continues;
- independent counsel and professional structures do not depend on MC's continuing goodwill;
- the protected outcome remains the same whether the adult says yes, says no, later leaves, or is left.

This protection is not payment for intimacy because intimacy does not change the benefit or penalty.

## Individual agency inside the relationship

Collective consent does not erase individual boundaries. A partner may pause, withdraw, dislike an experience, enjoy only part of it, or decide never again. Enjoyment does not prove earlier reservations were false. Consent once is not consent forever.

The same is true for the invited adult. Momentum, preparation, gifts, affection, travel, or the fact that everyone is already present cannot become consent.

## Story-state boundary

It does not establish that any particular invitation happened, name a guest or future partner, create a staff-to-partner arc, or fill an open household role. Those are explicit story decisions recorded in the appropriate state and character files.
