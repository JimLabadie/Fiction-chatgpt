# Household Practical Prompts

Status: OPTIONAL REFERENCE BANK — NOT A FAMILY CONSTITUTION
Authority: REUSABLE QUESTIONS ONLY; ANSWERS MUST COME FROM THE ACTUAL RELATIONSHIP

## Use boundary

These are ordinary practical subjects that any sufficiently large household may need to discuss. Polyamory does not make them formal, unusual, or mandatory. The Lesbos partners are people in a relationship, not a committee or governing body. They may talk, compromise, delegate, develop habits, or never need an explicit rule.

Do not assume voting systems, scheduled negotiations, written agreements, mediation machinery, or trial periods. Ask Jim if a story needs an answer that no controlling record supplies.

## Daily household life

- Sleeping arrangements, including whether anything rotates or simply follows preference.
- A shared calendar and how members reach it.
- Communication habits during ordinary disagreement.
- Contact and emergency information where useful.
- Informal or deliberate ways people express appreciation.
- Privacy, consent, and boundary-respecting habits.
- How a newer resident learns routines without being treated as an applicant.
- Age-appropriate privacy and safety for any children.
- Quiet or alone space.
- Move-in logistics and division of the physical work.
- Bathroom access and timing where facilities are shared.
- Groceries, meals, and personal versus shared food or supplies.

## Property and home

- What an incoming resident brings and what remains personally theirs.
- What happens to duplicate household items: keep, donate, store, or rotate.
- How shared aesthetics make room for personal and sentimental objects.
- Whether a prior residence is sold, rented, retained, or repurposed.

## Traditions and dates

- Which holiday traditions continue, combine, or begin anew.
- Birthdays, anniversaries, and recurring occasions important to the people involved.
- A practical date list if the household wants one; no mandatory ceremonial log exists.

## Money

- Which expenses are personal, shared, or institutionally supplied.
- Who handles routine tracking and payment.
- How grocery and household costs are handled.
- Financial effects of children, new residents, or changed circumstances.
- Independent security and choice-neutral protection under the Hestia Principle wherever structural power differs.

These prompts do not replace the controlling Lesbos ownership, trust, employment, or Hestia records.

## Conflict and guests

- How the people normally address disagreements and when they seek outside help.
- Visitor access and overnight arrangements.
- The difference between a guest, a shared intimate invitation, a resident, and a lasting partner.

## Pets, technology, and work

- Responsibility for feeding, veterinary care, walking, and other pet needs.
- Household expectations around phones, computers, screens, and children when relevant.
- Division of ordinary chores, cleaning, and household labor, including what professional staff perform.

## Family change

- Practical consequences of births, departures, new residents, or a lasting new relationship.
- Parenting roles where more than two adults participate in family life.
- The space, property, privacy, and schedule changes created by a move.

No item in this bank establishes that the Lesbos family has adopted a policy. Use only the questions a scene or continuity decision actually needs.
