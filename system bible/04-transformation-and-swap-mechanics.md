## Taxonomy

- **Body swap:** people exchange physical forms.
- **Identity or persona swap:** people assume another role, name, or documented identity.
- **Consciousness or cognitive transfer:** minds, memories, knowledge, or selected traits move.
- **Role reversal:** duties, status, or power positions change without an inherent body transfer.
- **Change:** one person or system changes without reciprocity.
- **Perceptual change:** observers or records interpret a person differently.

An active instance must name its type. Unspecified dimensions do not change.

Memory alteration is not prohibited. Memory, personality, knowledge, skill, habit, desire, perception, digital history, social response, and legal/institutional state are independently selectable layers when a specific change or swap defines them. None changes merely because another layer changes.

## Required instance record

Before Gate 1, record participants and baseline; trigger; physical scope; mental, memory, knowledge, skill, and personality scope; perception scope; digital/legal/documentary scope; duration and reversal; costs and side effects; transformation consent; and what each person knows.

## Personhood and competence

Identity, age, memories, history, maturity, relationships, and agency continue unless the ledger explicitly changes them. A physical template supplies physical traits only unless stated otherwise. Memory, information, practiced skill, muscle memory, habits, posture, voice, and micro-expression are separate fields. A changed body may impose new balance, reach, strength, senses, or motor limits without granting competence.

## Digital life

Answer four questions for every instance: content-level or identity-level; retroactive-only or ongoing; exact accounts/devices/dates in scope; and photo/video representation. Retroactive belief change is sincere memory within scope, not a cover story. Banking, credentials, employment, medical files, ownership, biometrics, private messages, and recommendations stay unchanged unless included.

## Consent and reversal

Unwanted magical, technological, or accidental transformation may occur, but never grants romantic or sexual consent. “Temporary,” “reversible,” and “easy to reverse” are different: record time limit, required actor, resources, risks, partial reversal, and what happens to memories and records.

A transformation may also become necessary because of genuine external conditions. Necessity may constrain the available choices without erasing agency: the affected person may refuse and accept consequences, agree reluctantly, choose among remaining options, stop where stopping is possible, and later decide whether the experience is welcome, unwanted, mixed, or never to be repeated. A good outcome does not create prior consent. See [Power, Control, Agency, and the Hestia Principle](01-system-and-canon-framework/power-control-agency-and-hestia-principle.md).
