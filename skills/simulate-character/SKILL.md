# Simulate Character

Status: PROTOTYPE — NON-GOVERNING
Purpose: Resolve a specific established character's plausible perception, interpretation, internal response, choice, speech, and behavior from current canon and state without inventing personality, knowledge, motivation, or development merely to serve the scene.

## Use when

Use when the question is what a particular character would plausibly:
- notice or fail to notice;
- know, infer, misunderstand, or rationalize;
- feel or avoid feeling;
- want in the immediate situation;
- choose among available actions;
- say, almost say, conceal, or leave unsaid;
- physically or socially do;
- make of another character's behavior;
- do differently because of a particular relationship;
- recognize about themself now, given development that has actually occurred.

This skill resolves character behavior. It does not own plot requirements, prose style, cultural facts, transformation mechanics, presentation composition, or durable state persistence.

## Required inputs

Retrieve only what is relevant and established:
- active story namespace and exact character identity;
- baseline and current character state;
- character engine: drives, wants, needs, fears, vulnerabilities, contradictions, joys, shame/avoidance, values, loyalties, strengths, limitations, coping/defense patterns;
- voice, humor, emotional habits, conflict/care style, conversational/physical habits, typical internal focus;
- self-perception and outside-perception gaps;
- knowledge, beliefs, ignorance, misunderstandings, and secrets;
- relationship-specific dynamics with present/relevant characters;
- hard boundaries, anti-characterization, and established exceptions;
- occurred development and elapsed story time;
- current handoff: location, physical/emotional state, objects, recent events, knowledge distribution, active changes, relationship state;
- relevant contextual, competence, presentation, or transformation resolutions from sibling skills.

Unknown fields remain unknown. Template fields are not permission to invent.

## Core separations

Keep these distinct unless canon explicitly links them:
1. objective/currently established event or state;
2. what the character notices;
3. what the character knows;
4. what the character believes or infers;
5. what the character feels;
6. what the character expected/wanted to feel;
7. what the character admits to themself;
8. what the character admits to others;
9. what the character wants in this moment;
10. what options the character perceives as available;
11. what the character chooses;
12. what the character says;
13. what the character does;
14. what other characters perceive/know.

A character may be contradictory across these layers without being inconsistent.

## Procedure

1. **Resolve identity and namespace first.**
   Retrieve the controlling substantial-character record and current story state. Never import characterization from a same/similar-name character in another story.

2. **Establish the situation from the character's access.**
   Identify what has objectively happened, then restrict the character's usable information to what they could perceive, know, remember, infer, or have been told. Do not grant reader/author knowledge.

3. **Resolve attention.**
   Determine what this character is likely to notice, ignore, misread, fixate on, or avoid based on current goals, emotional state, competencies, history, relationships, physical circumstances, and typical internal focus. Context may shape salience but may not replace individual canon.

4. **Resolve interpretation.**
   Apply the character's established knowledge, beliefs, misunderstandings, self-concept, blind spots, values, and relationship history. Preserve plausible wrong interpretations when the character lacks the truth.

5. **Resolve internal response.**
   Determine immediate emotional/physical response from established character machinery and current circumstances. Distinguish feeling from naming/admitting the feeling. Preserve mixed, mundane, contradictory, delayed, or defensive responses when supported.

6. **Resolve immediate pressures and wants.**
   Identify what the character wants right now, what they fear or protect, relationship obligations, practical constraints, and any competing drives. Do not collapse long-term need into immediate conscious motivation.

7. **Generate the canon-consistent option set.**
   Identify plausible actions/responses available to this character in this moment. Use Contextualize, Inhabit, Present, or Transform where those domains constrain the option set. Do not invent a convenient option by granting resources, knowledge, competence, or permission the character lacks.

8. **Apply boundaries and anti-characterization.**
   Eliminate options that violate established hard boundaries, would-not-normally behavior, knowledge limits, competence, or relationship-specific constraints unless the current situation contains an established reason for the exception.

9. **Resolve choice only as far as character canon supports.**
   If one response is strongly entailed by established character/current state, select it. If several materially different responses remain equally canon-consistent and choosing among them would create story direction, character development, or new canon, hand the choice to story development/Jim rather than pretending personality uniquely determined it.

10. **Resolve expression.**
    Translate the selected response through established voice mechanics, humor, emotional habits, conflict/care style, physical/conversational habits, relationship shorthand, and willingness to disclose. What the character says may differ from what they think or feel.

11. **Protect development timing.**
    Do not grant later-stage confidence, identity understanding, intimacy, trust, competence, vocabulary, or self-recognition before the occurred development supports it. Do not repeatedly rediscover a realization already established.

12. **Separate simulation from prose.**
    Output the resolved internal/behavioral state to story-development or writing layers. Do not require prose narration to expose every simulated layer.

13. **Hand persistent consequences onward.**
    If an accepted choice/event changes durable knowledge, relationship state, realization, competence, possession, identity understanding, or other tracked state, hand the established delta to Track State and then Persist when authorized.

## Output contract

A simulation should be able to state, at the resolution the task requires:
- perceived facts / missed facts;
- relevant knowledge and mistaken beliefs;
- interpretation;
- internal response;
- immediate wants/pressures;
- plausible option set;
- eliminated options and why, when consequential;
- selected choice if character-determined;
- unresolved choice if story/author-determined;
- likely speech/action expression;
- durable-state implications, if any.

The visible answer need not dump this structure unless useful; it is the reasoning contract.

## Character-determined vs. story-determined choice

Character simulation may select a choice when established characterization and current state make alternatives materially implausible.

Character simulation must not manufacture determinism. When two or more choices are genuinely plausible for the established person and the selection would decide pacing, plot direction, relationship development, identity development, or other new story truth, the choice belongs to Develop Story/Jim.

"The character could plausibly do this" is not the same as "the character therefore does this."

## Guardrails

- No plot puppetry: desired scene outcome does not rewrite character behavior.
- No mind reading: characters know only what their access permits.
- No stereotype completion: demographic/contextual knowledge does not substitute for individual character machinery.
- No personality invention from empty template fields.
- No automatic emotional wisdom or therapy-speak.
- No universal competence, articulation, wit, courage, insight, or self-awareness.
- No premature identity/self-recognition because eventual direction seems obvious.
- No collapsing group members into a collective reaction when individual responses matter.
- No assuming reciprocal relationship feelings/knowledge.
- No converting an exploratory simulated response into durable canon without acceptance/establishment and persistence.
- Preserve dignity, agency, contradiction, denial, rationalization, awkwardness, and imperfect self-awareness when character-appropriate.
