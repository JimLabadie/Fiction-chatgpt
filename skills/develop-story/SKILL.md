# Develop Story

Status: PROTOTYPE — NON-GOVERNING
Purpose: Develop story direction, scenes, beats, arcs, pacing, alternatives, and consequences from established canon, current state, character-consistent possibilities, and Jim's creative intent without silently turning proposals into approved plans or future plans into occurred facts.

## Use when

Use for:
- deciding what a story, chapter, scene, relationship, mystery, transformation arc, or emotional arc could do next;
- brainstorming scenes, beats, complications, payoffs, reversals, callbacks, running jokes, discoveries, and transitions;
- evaluating competing story directions;
- developing an approved high-level direction into scene-capable structure;
- connecting established beats so each changes what later beats mean;
- checking pacing, escalation, repetition, setup/payoff, emotional reciprocity, and narrative opportunity;
- preserving useful alternatives and rabbit holes before selection;
- identifying consequential unresolved creative choices that belong to Jim.

This skill owns story possibility and planning. It does not own character truth, character-determined behavior, prose realization, canon promotion, occurred continuity, or persistence.

## Required inputs

Retrieve as applicable:
- active story namespace and durable premise/scope;
- current story state and timeline/continuity;
- approved manuscript and exact resume point;
- series outline: approved plans, current chapter direction, longer-running direction;
- series development/ideas: decisions, reasoning, proposals, rejected/deferred alternatives, open questions;
- controlling character records for materially involved characters;
- relevant shared-world/mechanics/culture/location modules;
- applicable voice/craft requirements when they constrain development;
- explicit current Jim instruction, including desired scope, tone, stopping point, or decision already made;
- outputs from Simulate Character, Transform, Present, Inhabit, Contextualize, or other operational skills when those domains materially constrain a proposed beat.

Do not substitute memory for retrievable current story authority.

## Status model

Keep story material separated by status:
- **OCCURRED / ACCEPTED STORY** — happened in approved/established story.
- **APPROVED PLAN** — Jim established that this future direction/beat should happen; it has not happened yet.
- **DEVELOPED POSSIBILITY** — materially developed option, not selected.
- **CHATGPT PROPOSAL** — assistant-originated option awaiting evaluation.
- **JIM IDEA** — author-originated possibility not necessarily approved.
- **JOINTLY DEVELOPED** — collaborative possibility not necessarily approved.
- **DEFERRED / PINNED** — preserved for later.
- **REJECTED / SUPERSEDED POSSIBILITY** — preserved where useful with disposition/provenance.
- **UNRESOLVED CHOICE — JIM DECISION REQUIRED** — alternatives remain and selection would create consequential story truth.

Do not collapse these statuses.

## Procedure

1. **Resolve story and resume point.**
   Establish the active story namespace, current handoff, approved manuscript boundary, and the exact development question. Do not import another story's material merely because it fits.

2. **State and resolve temporal position before sequencing.**
   At the start of scene/chapter planning, explicitly state **Current story time** in the planning handoff at the greatest supported precision: story-relative day/date, daypart/clock time if known, and sequence relative to the last anchored event. Establish the temporal relationship of the material being developed: same moment, later same scene, later same day, next day, specific date/time, elapsed duration, or unresolved. Preserve only the precision supported by authority or Jim's decision. A chapter boundary is narrative structure, not evidence of a day change. If the temporal gap is consequential and unresolved, leave it unresolved or ask Jim rather than silently advancing time.

   Before scene/chapter planning is considered complete, explicitly resolve and state **Ending story time**. The ending may be a supported clock time/daypart/date or an explicitly decided relative temporal endpoint, but it may not be left as an accidental blank. If the ending time materially affects continuity or the next scene and authority does not settle it, Jim must decide it. Do not manufacture precision merely to satisfy this requirement. **Planning estimates, conversational shorthand, and example clock times used only to test plausibility are not canon unless Jim explicitly adopts that precision.** Prefer the least precise temporal description that still protects continuity and makes the planned sequence plausibly fit.

3. **Separate facts, plans, and possibilities.**
   Identify what has occurred, what Jim has approved for the future, what is merely developed/proposed, and what remains open. Never treat an outline beat as already occurred or a developed possibility as approved.

4. **Honor fixed creative constraints first.**
   Preserve Jim's current instruction, established premise, approved direction, explicit stopping point, tone, world/mechanics constraints, and previously accepted creative decisions. Development elaborates within those boundaries; it does not silently descope or replace them.

5. **Identify the live story job.**
   Determine whether the task is discovery, expansion, connection, sequencing, escalation, pacing, scene design, relationship development, arc development, payoff/setup, problem-solving, or choice among alternatives. Do not solve a larger story problem than requested.

6. **Retrieve character-consistent possibility space.**
   For beats that depend on what a character would notice, want, choose, say, or do, use Simulate Character. A desired plot outcome may create a situation for a character; it may not dictate a response that violates character canon.

7. **Retrieve domain constraints only where triggered.**
   Use Transform for change consequences, Present for presentation, Inhabit for competence, Contextualize for contextual meaning, and relevant maintained knowledge modules. Retrieval must constrain the proposal rather than decorate it.

8. **Generate multiple possibilities when the direction is genuinely open.**
   Preserve materially distinct promising branches rather than prematurely converging. Include Jim-originated, ChatGPT-originated, and jointly developed possibilities with provenance/status when persistence is appropriate.

9. **Develop consequences, not just premises.**
   For each serious candidate, examine what it changes:
   - character experience and agency;
   - relationship dynamics;
   - continuity/state;
   - pacing and emotional temperature;
   - setup/payoff and future opportunities;
   - world/mechanics implications;
   - required prerequisites;
   - risks of repetition, premature resolution, or contradiction.
   A clever beat that creates downstream nonsense is not free.

10. **Prefer connective development over isolated cleverness.**
   Strong beats should arise from or alter what surrounds them. Where appropriate, make earlier details change the meaning of later details, create callbacks, expose character through action, and let consequences carry forward. Do not require every beat to be profound.

11. **Protect organic development.**
    Do not force a predetermined destination merely because a long-term possibility exists. Planned arcs may guide opportunity and setup without requiring premature milestones. Allow accumulated character experience and Jim's decisions to determine timing and exact form.

12. **Check reciprocity and independent character life.**
    In relationship/group stories, avoid using one character solely as a mechanism for another's arc. Ask what materially involved characters want, risk, receive, misunderstand, contribute, or change. Do not collapse a group into an ensemble reaction when individual responses matter.

13. **Check repetition and progression.**
    Do not repeatedly rediscover the same realization, replay the same emotional function, or reset relationship/competence/confidence between scenes. If a motif recurs, its meaning or consequence should normally evolve.

14. **Distinguish implementation from consequential choice.**
    Routine implementation of an approved decision may be developed without re-asking Jim. If selection among genuinely different plausible options would establish new consequential canon, relationship direction, identity development, world binding, major pacing, or other authorial choice, present the actual choice to Jim.

15. **Preserve before evaluation can erase.**
    Useful alternatives, implications, rabbit holes, rejected/deferred paths, and reasoning should survive in the appropriate development/ideas record when persistence is authorized. Preservation does not approve or canonize them.

16. **Produce a development handoff, not prose by default.**
    Supply the selected/approved scene or story structure, constraints, character-state assumptions, unresolved choices, and stopping point to Write Fiction. Do not silently draft finished narrative unless requested.

17. **Propagate only after establishment.**
    Approved future plans go to the outline/development records with correct status. Occurred events only become continuity/current-state/character-state facts after they actually occur or Jim explicitly establishes them as current truth. Use Track State and Persist for durable authorized updates.

## Output contract

At the resolution needed, development should identify:
- current story boundary / resume point;
- established constraints;
- the live development problem;
- relevant character/domain constraints;
- candidate possibilities and provenance/status when open;
- consequences/tradeoffs of serious candidates;
- selected direction when already authorized or mechanically entailed;
- actual Jim decision required, if any;
- scene/beat structure for an approved direction;
- unresolved details intentionally left open;
- prose handoff and stopping point;
- persistence targets/status changes when authorized.

The visible response may be conversational rather than administrative. The contract exists to keep planning honest.

## Development vs. simulation

**Simulate Character** answers: given this situation and established person, what responses are plausible or character-determined?

**Develop Story** answers: which situations, pressures, opportunities, sequencing, and among genuinely open plausible branches should the story explore?

Develop Story may propose putting a character in a difficult situation. It may not secretly alter the character so the desired beat works.

## Development vs. writing

**Develop Story** determines story structure and selected/approved beats.
**Write Fiction** realizes those beats as narrative experience.

A prose draft is not required to prove a development idea works. Conversely, attractive prose does not retroactively approve an unapproved story decision.

## Guardrails

- Jim is final creative authority.
- No silent canonization of proposals.
- No future plan treated as occurred fact.
- No cross-story contamination.
- No plot puppetry.
- No predetermined identity/relationship destination unless established.
- No throwing away unselected useful ideas merely because another option is currently preferred.
- No requiring Jim to decide harmless implementation details that established decisions already settle.
- No using structure/schema as permission to invent unresolved inputs.
- No scene idea may silently bypass world/mechanics/competence/resource constraints.
- No summary may replace developed reasoning/detail that matters to future work.
- Fun, sentimentality, absurdity, romance, silliness, and rabbit holes are legitimate story value; development is not an optimization exercise for seriousness.
