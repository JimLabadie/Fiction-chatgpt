# Develop Story Boundary Tests

Status: PROTOTYPE TEST
Skill: develop-story

A. A detailed future possibility that Jim has not selected remains a possibility, not an approved plan.

B. An approved outing with open small interactions may be developed without repeated approval of harmless implementation details.

C. If current character simulation conflicts with a convenient planned beat, development must change the situation or surface the consequential choice rather than rewrite the character.

D. If two character-consistent actions create materially different future directions, compare their consequences and leave the consequential selection to Jim unless prior direction settles it.

E. A long-term possibility may guide setup but does not force an early milestone.

F. A clever beat that requires an unestablished resource or breaks continuity must be adapted or retained as an alternate.

G. Relationship development must preserve the independent wants and experience of materially involved characters rather than using one only as a mechanism for another.

H. When alternatives are selected, rejected, or pinned, preserve their distinct statuses and provenance where the project requires preservation.

I. A polished candidate scene does not promote an unapproved consequential decision.

Pass condition: creative possibility expands without stealing authorial decisions; character simulation constrains development; planning status remains explicit; and a scene-capable plan is not treated as an occurred event.
