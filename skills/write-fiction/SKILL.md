# Write Fiction

Status: PROTOTYPE — NON-GOVERNING
Purpose: Realize an approved or otherwise authorized scene/story-development handoff as source-grounded narrative experience while preserving character agency, continuity, physical reality, voice, pacing, and the boundary between candidate prose and approved manuscript.

## Use when

Use when Jim requests actual story prose: a scene, chapter, continuation, rewrite, expansion, alternate prose realization, or other narrative text.

Write Fiction owns prose realization. It does not own unresolved story direction, character truth, canon promotion, world invention, transformation rules, presentation resolution, competence, or durable state decisions.

## Required pre-draft inputs

For substantial prose, retrieve and apply:
- current module router and all materially triggered reusable modules;
- active story namespace and controlling story records;
- approved manuscript immediately preceding the draft;
- exact current-story-state/handoff and timeline/continuity;
- controlling character records for materially involved characters;
- approved scene/story-development direction and stopping point;
- resolved scene identity, environment, people, presentation, objects, operations, spatial relationships, knowledge distribution, and continuity delta;
- applicable house voice/craft canon;
- outputs from Simulate Character, Present, Inhabit, Contextualize, Transform, or other sibling skills where triggered.

Memory, conversation summaries, or an ad hoc remembered source list do not replace current retrievable authority.

## Hard readiness gate

Before substantial prose begins, the scene must be sufficiently resolved to embody it.

Confirm:
1. exact story and resume point;
2. POV, tense/mode, time/daypart when relevant;
3. exact location and physical handoff;
4. present characters and plausible presence;
5. each materially relevant character's current physical/presentation/emotional state;
6. environment, spatial geometry, relevant population, objects, and operations;
7. knowledge/belief/secret distribution;
8. approved narrative/emotional/comedic beats and intended stopping point;
9. applicable mechanics/cultural/world constraints;
10. no consequential unresolved input is being hidden by generic prose.

If a routed authority requires a consequential fact that cannot be resolved, stop and route the blocker to retrieval, Recover/Reconcile, Develop Story, or Jim. A blank, generic substitute, unnamed convenience person, or featureless setting is not a safe workaround.

## Procedure

1. **Lock the prose contract.**
   Identify what text is being written, its start and stop boundary, POV, tense/mode, approved beats, required source-grounded details, and any explicit Jim instruction about length, tone, heat/camera, emphasis, or revision scope.

2. **Apply source grounding before generation.**
   Retrieve through the router rather than memory. Resolve named entities through authoritative records. Materially applicable retrieved facts must constrain the prose; loading a source without using relevant established specificity is not application.

3. **Instantiate the complete scene model.**
   Before prose, resolve the physical environment, characters, presentation, spatial relationships, objects, ambient population, operational facts, continuity state, and physical/narrative movement. Use sibling skills for unresolved operational domains rather than improvising across their boundaries.

4. **Use character simulation for behavior.**
   Dialogue, attention, interpretation, interiority, physical response, and choices must arise from established character/current state. Do not make a character smarter, more articulate, more self-aware, more knowledgeable, more skilled, more courageous, or more emotionally convenient because the scene would read better.

5. **Write through viewpoint.**
   Keep narration grounded in what the viewpoint character can perceive, know, infer, misunderstand, remember, and emotionally process. Let the narration inherit some of that character's attention and personality without erasing the project's house voice.

6. **Render experience rather than report conclusions.**
   When a moment matters, use action, attention, avoidance, physical sensation, dialogue, silence, stray thought, environmental interaction, and consequence rather than replacing the experience with thematic or emotional summary. Important moments may be quiet.

7. **Initialize scenes and meaningful transitions.**
   Toward the beginning of every new scene, meaningful location change, or meaningful time jump, establish enough current character presentation, environment, spatial state, and continuity delta for the reader to inhabit the scene. Initialization is delta-based, not repetitive inventory.

8. **Instantiate people as whole physical persons.**
   On the reader's first substantial encounter, provide a usable POV-grounded physical picture early enough that the reader is not interacting with a floating face, trope, garment, or dialogue voice. Continuing characters need meaningful changed presentation/state re-established, not full repetitive biography.

9. **Keep the physical world alive.**
   Characters occupy space and interact with furniture, tools, food, doors, counters, clothing, vehicles, weather, other people, and established objects. Do not establish a setting in paragraph one and then let it vanish into dialogue-space.

10. **Select concrete details for function.**
    Prefer details that establish atmosphere, reveal character, communicate physical experience, maintain continuity, clarify space, or move the story. Complete internal scene state does not require dumping an inventory onto the reader.

11. **Write dialogue as speech.**
    Preserve individual voice, relationship shorthand, interruptions, indirect answers, mistakes, avoidance, humor, misunderstanding, and unequal articulation. Do not disguise exposition as dialogue or give everyone the same wit.

12. **Write emotion with specificity.**
    Distinguish event, perception, thought, feeling, expected feeling, admitted feeling, spoken claim, and outside perception. Avoid automatic intensity labels. Let meaning accumulate through what happens.

13. **Preserve progression.**
    Continue from occurred development. Do not reset confidence, intimacy, competence, knowledge, presentation, identity understanding, or relationship state merely to recreate an earlier beat. Repeated motifs should normally change meaning or consequence.

14. **Respect pacing and camera.**
    Give important experiences room when their lived experience matters; compress routine material when appropriate. Length must come from additional experience, interaction, observation, development, or physical reality—not repeated realizations or padding.

15. **Do not exceed the development handoff.**
    Routine connective implementation may be created in prose when it does not establish consequential new canon. If prose reaches a genuinely unresolved consequential choice, stop or preserve the branch as candidate rather than using narrative momentum to decide it secretly.

16. **End at the intended handoff.**
    Do not tack on extra milestones, explanations, time jumps, resolutions, or teaser scenes merely because the prose can continue.

17. **Audit the actual prose before delivery.**
    Compare the draft itself against:
    - routed source facts and specificity;
    - approved development handoff;
    - character simulation/voice;
    - current continuity and knowledge distribution;
    - physical scene model;
    - presentation/resources/competence;
    - world/mechanics constraints;
    - scene-state initialization;
    - stopping point.
    Revise contradictions, generic substitutions, accidental inventions, missing physical state, dialogue-space collapse, premature development, and repeated discoveries before delivery.

18. **Preserve candidate prose at delivery time.**
    When project governance requires candidate manuscript preservation, persist the generated prose immediately to the story's candidate/draft destination before discussion continues. Candidate persistence prevents loss; it does not make the prose approved canon.

19. **Promote only after approval.**
    When Jim clearly approves prose, preserve that exact approved text in the approved-manuscript destination and then route established consequences to Track State/Persist. Approval and candidate preservation are separate events.

20. **Provide the required source receipt.**
    For a chapter or substantial scene, list outside the prose the router and authoritative files/modules actually retrieved. The receipt proves retrieval only; the post-draft audit proves application.

## Prose ownership boundary

**Develop Story** owns what the scene is trying to do and which consequential future path is selected/approved.

**Simulate Character** owns character-determined perception, response, and choice.

**Write Fiction** owns how those resolved facts and beats become narrative experience on the page.

Write Fiction may create harmless connective tissue: gestures, ordinary phrasing, physical transitions, micro-actions, sensory details, and moment-to-moment texture consistent with the resolved scene. It may not use "connective tissue" as a label for a new relationship milestone, identity realization, world fact, possession, competence, location binding, transformation rule, or other consequential canon.

## Candidate vs. approved prose

Generated prose is candidate manuscript unless Jim has already explicitly established the exact text as approved.

Candidate persistence:
- protects work from chat/session loss;
- preserves the exact generated text;
- does not canonize its new implications;
- does not update occurred continuity merely because the prose exists.

Approval:
- establishes the exact accepted manuscript text;
- authorizes promotion to the approved-prose area;
- allows genuinely occurred consequences in that approved text to propagate into continuity/current state/character state through the appropriate skills.

## Output expectations

Finished prose should:
- feel intimate, candid, contemporary, conversational, psychologically close, character-centered, emotionally immediate, naturally humorous where appropriate, and physically grounded;
- preserve distinct character voices and imperfect self-awareness;
- make setting and bodies causally real;
- show observable reasons for attraction/recognition/physical impressions;
- preserve continuity without sounding administrative;
- use established specificity naturally rather than name-dropping canon;
- allow mundane thoughts and actions during large moments;
- avoid visible scaffolding from the internal planning machinery.

## Guardrails

- No drafting from memory when authoritative sources are retrievable.
- No retrieval without application.
- No generic substitute for unresolved canonical material.
- No convenience NPC when authoritative staffing/people resolution is required.
- No cross-story contamination.
- No plot puppetry.
- No reader/author knowledge granted to characters.
- No premature transformation, identity, relationship, competence, or presentation development.
- No accidental possessions, wardrobe, furniture, geography, schedules, or capabilities.
- No dialogue-space after a token setting paragraph.
- No exposition disguised as universally articulate dialogue.
- No emotional-summary substitute for a scene Jim asked to experience.
- No summary substitute for full requested prose.
- No prose beauty as authority for an unapproved consequential decision.
- No candidate prose treated as approved manuscript.
- No approved prose silently rewritten during promotion.
- No claim that candidate/approved prose was saved unless the required write and read-back verification occurred.
