# The Neon Sign — Series Outline

Status: ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

This outline separates established development from future/proposed progression. An outline beat is not a completed event until accepted prose establishes it.

## Chapter One — Settled Science

Established title: **Settled Science**.

Opening development centers on Steve, Brittney, Chloe, and Blush. Steve enters Blush to prove Brittney wrong. Steve meets the seven-woman group — Brittney, Liz, Chloe, Nat, Maddie, Tori, and Emily — and Britt eventually brings her “stray” home.

The chapter establishes the collision between Steve's existing self-understanding and a social world in which Steve fits far too naturally.

## Developing middle progression

Established development includes:

- The group chat **Absolutely Not A Cult 🌈** becomes part of the social machinery.
- Steve's independent friendships and sparks with individual women need room to breathe rather than racing directly to a single romantic conclusion.
- “Not dates” provide individual time with members of the polycule.
- Each not-date has pre/post texting and later private or group-chat spillover.
- The women have honest conversations between these encounters about attraction and the possibility that this may mean something.
- Each not-date contains a distinct, blatantly anti-male Steve moment that endears Steve to the group; these moments are character beats, not gender clues.
- Steve independently chooses a green dress during the developing presentation arc.
- Feminine pronoun movement begins later; preserve the exact accepted boundary rather than advancing it by assumption.

Calendar breathing room is intentional. Steve's life changes before Steve understands why.

## Current Saturday sequence

Established current scene: seven women plus Steve at a diner.

Steve remains in jeans, sweater, and sneakers, now with fresh red lipstick and a green purse. The diner includes a powder-room/lipstick initiation, discussion of purse theory, and an affectionate practical purse “ceremony.”

The women contribute:
- Maddie — tissues and mints.
- Emily — pain relief and blister bandages.
- Nat — power bank and cable.
- Tori — two pens and safety pins.
- Britt — nail file and comb.
- Chloe — stain pen and sanitizer.
- Liz — wet wipes and an emergency $20.

Steve owns the lipstick. Steve does **not** yet own a compact. Steve embraces the purse rather than treating it as a prop imposed by the women.

## Next chapter progression — established plan, not yet completed event

1. Leave the diner normally; preserve physical travel and possessions.
2. Go to **St. Claire Pharmacy** at St. Claire Medical Plaza for practical/travel-size supplies and the “life hack” side of purse ownership.
3. Steve begins optimizing and customizing the purse system rather than simply accepting the women's inherited purse wisdom: organization, access, size, redundancy, and practical usefulness become a Steve problem to solve.
4. Continue to the appropriate boutique.
5. Steve discovers that function is only one axis: a flashy purse can also simply be **cool**. Color and style become legitimate selection criteria alongside utility.
6. Steve discovers that femininity/presentation upkeep and quality handbags are expensive.
7. Steve chooses and purchases a quality, larger, more personally expressive purse. The choice is Steve's and marks the point where Steve starts trying to “win” the purse thing.
8. Within roughly a week, Steve's normal mastery instinct has produced extensive handbag research and informed, potentially questionably expensive opinions/choices. This is later continuity, not something the pharmacy scene must complete immediately.

## Longer relationship progression

Attraction develops into honest investigation rather than an instant group verdict. Individual time makes the situation more complicated, not less. The emotional movement is toward recognition that “it isn't just attraction,” followed eventually by a real boundary/relationship conversation.

A parallel social thread is the women teaching Steve how to be a lesbian, including flirting, without collapsing Steve into a joke or removing Steve's agency.
