# Chapter Eight — Remove the Tags

“Teach me.”

For perhaps two full seconds, nobody moved.

Steve stood in front of the long mirror at Second Chapter with the oxblood handbag hanging from his shoulder, one hand still resting against its smooth leather as if someone might attempt to take it away.

Nobody was going to attempt to take it away.

The problem was considerably worse than that.

Seven women had just heard Steve voluntarily request fashion instruction.

Nat’s eyes widened.

Maddie covered her mouth.

Tori slowly turned toward Britt.

Britt pointed at all of them.

“Don’t.”

“I didn’t say anything,” Nat said.

“You were about to.”

“I was breathing.”

“You were breathing suspiciously.”

Chloe leaned against the end of a rack, arms folded. “For the record, I think we should respect his request.”

Steve narrowed his eyes at her.

“That sounded reasonable.”

“Thank you.”

“Which means I’m concerned.”

“You’re learning.”

Emily was looking at the handbag, then at Steve, then at the rest of Steve.

That was when Steve noticed.

“What?”

Emily winced.

It was tiny.

Barely perceptible.

But Steve had spent enough time around these women now to recognize the expression.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Emily.”

“It’s a really nice bag.”

Steve looked down at it.

“I know.”

“Really, really nice.”

“Yes.”

“Beautiful, actually.”

“Emily.”

She sighed.

“The bag is winning.”

Steve stared at her.

Then he looked into the mirror again.

The handbag was still magnificent.

Deep wine leather. Clean structure. Restrained hardware. Enough presence to be interesting without announcing its manufacturer from across the street. It sat against him with the sort of casual authority possessed by objects that knew perfectly well they were expensive.

Then Steve looked at the rest of himself.

Jeans.

Sweater.

Sneakers.

He looked at the bag.

Back at himself.

Back at the bag.

“Oh, fuck.”

Nobody spoke.

Steve stepped closer to the mirror.

“Oh, fuck.”

Britt’s mouth twitched.

“Don’t.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

Steve turned around.

“I can’t carry this.”

Seven expressions changed simultaneously.

The delight disappeared so fast it was almost frightening.

Liz straightened. Britt took half a step toward him. Emily’s face fell.

Then Steve pointed furiously at himself.

“Looking like this.”

Silence.

Nat made a small choking noise.

Steve swept a hand down his body.

“Look at me. This thing looks like it costs more than my first car and I’m standing here dressed like I got separated from a tour group.”

Chloe blinked.

“Oh.”

“Yes, oh.”

Britt’s grin returned.

“Oh.”

“Stop enjoying this.”

“I can’t.”

Steve turned toward Billie.

The owner of Second Chapter had been watching from behind a nearby display, her black hair secured in its low bun, the pleats of her midi skirt shifting around her legs as she leaned one hip against the counter.

“Can you fix this?”

Billie looked at him.

Then at Greta.

Then at Constance.

“Oh,” Nat whispered. “He asked the professionals.”

“I hate every single one of you.”

“No, you don’t,” Maddie said brightly.

“That is increasingly becoming a problem.”

The next fifteen minutes taught Steve something important about fashion.

He had opinions.

This was deeply inconvenient.

The first blouse was wrong.

He couldn’t explain why.

The second was worse.

The third was pretty, but something about the neckline bothered him.

“You keep touching it,” Constance observed.

Steve looked down.

His fingers were indeed worrying the collar.

“I don’t like it.”

“Good.”

Steve looked up. “Good?”

Constance already had the garment moving toward the return rack. “Knowing why something doesn’t work for you is useful. Knowing that it doesn’t is enough to put it back.”

A skirt arrived.

Steve stared at it.

The skirt stared back.

It was perfectly innocent.

Probably.

Steve picked it up by the hanger.

“This is considerably more lesbian than I was planning to be when I woke up this morning.”

Chloe didn’t even look up from the rack she was examining.

“You went to brunch with seven lesbians.”

“Circumstantial.”

“You’re holding a skirt.”

“I’m evaluating it.”

“Against your body.”

Steve held it a little farther away.

“That is where clothes go, Chloe.”

“Good. We’re making progress.”

He tried it.

He liked it.

That was inconvenient too.

It was also not right.

Steve emerged from the fitting room, looked at himself, turned once, and shook his head.

“No.”

Nat looked personally wounded. “That’s adorable.”

“I don’t want adorable.”

That stopped them.

Steve looked at the mirror again.

“I mean—I don’t hate it.”

“Different thing,” Billie said.

“Right.”

He studied himself.

“I want…”

The sentence wouldn’t come.

He looked toward the oxblood bag, waiting safely beside Britt.

“I want that to make sense.”

Billie nodded once.

No one laughed.

They tried again.

And again.

Something soft and floral made Steve feel like he had borrowed somebody else’s personality.

A sleek black dress looked objectively good and did absolutely nothing for him.

A burgundy wrap dress fought with the bag so aggressively that even Steve could see the problem.

Then Greta stopped bringing him clothes.

Steve noticed her watching instead.

Greta stood a few feet away in her polished blouse and high-waisted pencil skirt, soft brown waves framing her cat-eye glasses. Her four-inch heels made an occasional precise click against the floor as she moved around him, studying the way different garments sat on his body.

She rejected one dress before Steve even got his hands on it.

Then another.

Steve finally caught her.

“Why do you keep doing that?”

Greta paused.

“Doing what?”

“Saying no before I try things.”

“Because they’re wrong.”

“How do you know?”

Greta took the dress she had just rejected and held it in front of him.

“This bodice wants volume you don’t have. The shoulder is going to fight you. The waist is going to land in the wrong place, and by the time we correct all three you’ll have spent more on alterations than the dress deserves.”

Steve looked down at himself.

“So I’m shaped wrong.”

Greta’s expression changed immediately.

“No.”

Not gentle.

Not reassuring.

Certain.

“The dress is shaped wrong for you.”

Steve looked at her.

Greta put the hanger back.

“Women’s clothes aren’t made for women.”

Steve blinked.

“That seems like a design flaw.”

“It is one of fashion’s longer-running jokes. They’re made from blocks. Proportions. Assumptions about shoulders, bust, waist, hips, torso length. Then actual women have the discourtesy to arrive with actual bodies.”

Steve laughed.

Greta smiled.

“Some bodies shaped by male puberty create particular fitting challenges. Broader shoulders sometimes. Different rib-cage proportions. Less difference between waist and hip. Longer torso. None of those is universal, and none of them means you can’t wear beautiful clothes.”

She touched her own chest lightly.

“I know because I’m trans.”

There was no lowering of her voice.

No glance around the room.

No confession.

Greta said it with the same uncomplicated certainty with which Billie had said she owned the store.

Steve looked at her.

Greta smiled.

Proudly.

“I learned how to dress this body. I learned what fought me, what worked with me, and eventually what made me feel beautiful.”

She tipped her glasses down slightly.

“So when I tell you your shoulders aren’t a problem, Steve, I’m not being nice.”

She picked up the rejected dress.

“This dress is incompetent.”

Steve barked out a laugh.

Across the store, Tori muttered, “I like her.”

“I heard that,” Greta said.

“You were supposed to.”

Steve looked at Greta again.

“So you know what this feels like?”

Greta considered him for a moment.

“I know what this feels like.”

She gestured between Steve and the racks surrounding them.

“Standing in women’s clothing without knowing what the hell works on your body? Absolutely.”

Then her expression softened.

“Anything bigger than that belongs to you. I’m not going to tell you what you’re feeling.”

Something in Steve’s chest loosened.

He nodded.

Greta turned back toward the racks.

“I spent years trying to make my shoulders disappear.”

Steve glanced at her.

“Did it work?”

“No. Mostly I bought ugly clothes.”

He laughed again.

“One day somebody put me in a dress that made them look intentional.”

“The shoulders?”

“The shoulders.”

Greta smiled at herself in the mirror.

“I stopped fighting them.”

Steve took in the pencil skirt, the blouse, the heels, the soft waves, the entire polished feminine certainty of her.

“Apparently.”

Greta’s smile became wicked.

“Honey, I didn’t transition to be subtle.”

Nat made a sound of profound agreement from somewhere behind a rack.

Greta ignored her.

She looked at Steve’s reflection.

“Most people start by asking what they should hide. Shoulders. Stomach. Hips. Whatever they’ve decided is unacceptable that week.”

“What do you ask?”

Greta looked at him.

“What can I make beautiful?”

Then she froze.

Her eyes moved over Steve once.

Shoulders.

Torso.

Waist.

Face.

Red lipstick.

Then the oxblood handbag.

“Oh.”

Steve had learned to distrust that sound.

“What?”

Greta was already walking away.

“What?”

“Stay there.”

“Why?”

“Because I said so.”

“That’s not an answer.”

Greta disappeared between two racks.

Billie had been rehanging a blouse.

She stopped.

Looked toward where Greta had vanished.

Then at Steve.

Then toward the back of the store.

“Oh.”

Steve pointed at her.

“No. Absolutely not. You don’t get to do it too.”

Billie’s smile spread.

“I know what she’s getting.”

“What is she getting?”

Billie resumed hanging the blouse.

“I’m not ruining this for her.”

“What is happening?”

Maddie bounced once on her toes.

“Maybe it’s cursed.”

“Thank you, Maddie.”

“You’re welcome.”

Greta returned carrying green.

Not ordinary green.

Deep, saturated emerald with enough blue beneath it to become almost peacock when the fabric caught the light.

Steve stopped talking.

The dress had a sculpted portrait neckline, wide enough to make the shoulders part of its architecture rather than something to apologize for. Three-quarter sleeves narrowed toward the forearm. The bodice was structured, its seaming drawing inward toward a sharply defined waist before releasing into a full tea-length skirt with enough weight to move instead of merely hang.

It was feminine without being fragile.

Dramatic without being ridiculous.

At least Steve thought it wasn’t ridiculous.

Then he noticed everyone looking at him.

“No.”

Greta frowned. “You haven’t tried it.”

“I know what that look means.”

“What look?”

“The one all of you have.”

Chloe glanced around.

“We have several.”

“This one says you’re about to ruin my life.”

Greta held out the dress.

“Try it.”

Steve took it.

The fabric had weight.

Quality.

He looked at the neckline.

Then at Greta.

“You did the shoulder thing.”

“I did.”

“And the waist thing.”

“I did.”

“And the giant skirt?”

Greta looked offended.

“We needed more visual weight below.”

“This is a lot of visual weight.”

“I said more. I didn’t say cowardly.”

Five minutes later, Greta zipped him into it.

The zipper reached the top.

The dress settled.

And Steve looked up.

Nobody said anything.

The neckline changed everything.

His shoulders weren’t gone.

Greta hadn’t hidden them.

She had made them look deliberate.

The bodice created shape where Steve hadn’t known clothing could create shape. The waist appeared with startling certainty, and below it the skirt fell outward in a long, elegant sweep that moved when he moved.

His red lipstick suddenly looked less like an isolated experiment.

It belonged to something.

Steve turned slightly.

The skirt followed a fraction of a second later.

“Oh.”

Greta adjusted one small fold at his waist.

“There.”

Steve stared.

The word landed differently now.

There.

Britt had gone suspiciously quiet.

Liz had both hands clasped around the strap of her own handbag.

Emily was smiling.

Maddie looked approximately four seconds from exploding.

Nat simply said, “Holy shit.”

Tori nodded slowly. “Yeah.”

Chloe studied him.

“That’s extremely annoying.”

Steve looked at her reflection. “What is?”

“You look fantastic.”

He looked back at himself.

Greta stepped away.

“That’s your dress.”

Not the dress.

Your dress.

Steve swallowed.

Then Britt handed him the oxblood bag.

He slipped it onto his shoulder.

And the entire picture clicked.

“Oh, fuck.”

Liz smiled.

Steve turned one way.

Then the other.

The bag wasn’t overdressed anymore.

Neither was the dress.

They were talking to each other.

And somehow—

somehow—

they were talking about him.

Steve smiled.

Then his gaze traveled downward.

Emerald skirt.

Bare lower legs.

Sneakers.

His smile vanished.

“Oh, come on.”

Emily followed his eyes.

“Oh.”

Steve pointed.

“Those.”

Seven women looked at his feet.

Silence.

Steve slowly raised his head.

“You all knew.”

“We were waiting,” Chloe said.

“For what?”

“Your eyes to start working.”

Steve glared at her.

Somewhere behind him came Nat’s voice.

“Guys.”

Nobody turned.

Steve was still staring accusingly at his sneakers.

“I cannot wear these with this.”

“Guys.”

“I don’t even know anything about shoes and I know this is a fucking crime.”

“Guys.”

Something in Nat’s voice finally made everyone turn.

She was crouched beside a low display near the rear of the store.

One shoe rested in her hand.

Black.

Elegant.

A classic pump stripped of everything unnecessary, its lines clean and wickedly feminine, the heel high enough that Steve distrusted it immediately.

Nat rotated her wrist.

The sole flashed red.

Every woman in the vicinity went still.

“Oh, no,” Emily whispered.

Steve looked at Nat.

Then the shoe.

Then Nat.

“No.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“Put it down.”

Nat hugged it against her chest.

“I don’t think I can.”

Billie came around the counter.

Her eyebrows rose.

“Well.”

Steve pointed at her. “That’s not helping.”

“I wondered who was eventually going to fit those.”

Steve stared.

“What does that mean?”

“They’ve been here a while.”

“How long is a while?”

“Long enough to break several hearts.”

Nat looked inside the shoe.

Then at Steve.

Then down at his feet.

Steve backed up one step.

“No.”

Britt folded her arms.

“Nobody’s making you do anything.”

“I know that.”

“Okay.”

Steve looked at the shoes.

That was the problem.

He wanted to try them.

He wanted to try them very badly.

Which was absurd.

He had never in his life looked at four inches of heel and thought anything except that someone had made an architectural error.

Now he was calculating what they would do to the line of the dress.

“Oh, fuck you,” he told the shoes.

Maddie nodded solemnly. “They’re very smug.”

Nat brought them over.

Steve sat.

For once, nobody teased him.

He pulled off one sneaker.

Then the other.

Nat handed him the first pump.

Steve slid his foot inside.

It stopped exactly where it should.

He blinked.

“No.”

“What?” Britt asked.

“It fits.”

Nat handed him the other.

Steve put it on.

That one fit too.

He stared at his feet.

The shoes changed the line of his legs even sitting down. The black worked against the emerald without competing with it. When he moved one foot, the red sole appeared for a second beneath it.

Steve moved it again.

Red.

“Oh, fuck me.”

Nobody laughed.

He looked up.

Greta was smiling.

“Stand.”

Steve looked at the heels.

Then at her.

“I will die.”

“You’re sitting down.”

“Because I have excellent survival instincts.”

Britt offered one hand.

Liz offered the other.

Greta stood in front of him.

“Stand.”

Steve took both hands.

Slowly, carefully, he rose.

His ankles wobbled.

“Jesus FUCK.”

Britt tightened her grip.

“Got you.”

Liz steadied his other side.

Steve found the floor.

Then found his balance.

Then, very slowly, straightened.

The dress settled around him.

The bag rested against his hip.

His posture changed.

And Steve looked into the mirror.

Everything stopped.

Emerald.

Red lips.

Oxblood leather.

Black heels.

A flash of impossible red beneath one shoe.

Steve stared.

He didn’t know the person in the mirror.

That wasn’t quite right.

He did.

That was worse.

Or better.

Something.

The woman—

The thought never finished.

Steve smiled.

A huge, helpless, completely unguarded smile.

Then something warm touched his cheek.

He blinked.

Touched beneath his eye.

Looked at his fingertip.

Wet.

“What the fuck?”

Nobody moved.

Steve blinked again.

Another tear escaped.

“Oh, come on.”

Britt took half a step closer.

Steve laughed.

That made another tear fall.

Maddie was already crying.

“Why are you crying?” Steve demanded.

“I don’t know!”

“That’s not helpful.”

Tori silently offered Steve a tissue.

He took it.

Chloe looked pointedly at his mouth.

“Careful.”

Steve sniffed. “What?”

“You paid money for that lipstick.”

Steve laughed hard enough that he had to grab Britt again.

“Fuck you.”

“There he is.”

The laugh passed.

The tears didn’t quite.

Emily’s voice was soft.

“Good cry?”

Steve looked back at the mirror.

He turned one foot.

The red sole flashed.

His eyes filled again.

He laughed at himself.

“Yeah.”

Nobody said anything.

Steve stared at the reflection.

“I just…”

His voice caught.

He cleared his throat.

“I really like how I look.”

Greta looked away for a second.

Steve caught it.

“Are you crying too?”

She carefully touched beneath one eye.

“Professional satisfaction.”

“Bullshit.”

“I’m billing you for this.”

Steve smiled.

Then looked at himself one more time.

Something settled.

His shoulders came back.

His chin lifted.

He looked at Billie.

“Remove the tags.”

Billie didn’t ask which ones.

The dress.

The shoes.

The bag.

Snip.

Snip.

Snip.

Steve watched the final tag fall into Billie’s hand.

Then he looked down at the heels.

He took one step.

His ankle went sideways.

Britt caught him.

Liz grabbed his arm.

Steve froze.

“Okay.”

Chloe waited.

Steve drew himself upright with as much dignity as a man clinging to two lesbians in four-inch heels could reasonably assemble.

“I have identified a technical deficiency.”

“Gravity?” Chloe asked.

“Fuck you.”

Greta laughed.

Steve looked around at them.

All seven.

Billie.

Constance.

Greta.

Then at the shoes.

Then at himself.

He held out one hand.

“Somebody teach me how to fucking walk.”

Greta stepped forward.

“Short steps.”

“I know how steps work.”

“You know how your steps work. Those aren’t the same steps.”

“That sounds fake.”

“Walk.”

Steve took one.

Wobbled.

Another.

“Stop looking at your feet.”

“I need to know where they are.”

“They’re attached to you.”

“That’s an assumption I no longer feel comfortable making.”

“Again.”

Heel.

Toe.

Shorter stride.

Weight centered.

Steve made it three steps.

Then four.

The skirt moved differently when he walked properly. He could feel the swing of it around his legs, the click of the heels beneath him, the handbag against his hip.

He reached the end of the open aisle.

Turned.

Badly.

One heel skittered sideways and Steve pinwheeled just enough to send Constance lunging for a nearby hat stand.

“I saved the merchandise,” Constance announced.

Britt caught Steve by the elbow.

“Nice to know where I rank.”

“You have health insurance.”

Steve stared at the hat stand.

Then at his feet.

Then at Greta.

“How do I turn?”

“Smaller steps.”

“Everything is smaller steps with you.”

“Because your normal stride is trying to murder you.”

Steve reset.

He walked back.

Heel. Toe. Shorter. Don't look down.

The second turn was ugly but survivable.

“Again,” Greta said.

Steve walked it again.

Then again.

On the fourth pass, the turn happened without Britt reaching for him.

Steve stopped.

Looked down.

Caught himself looking down.

Looked immediately back up.

Greta smiled.

“Again.”

Steve grinned.

“Again.”

The lesson took over a clear strip of floor between the fitting rooms and the handbag displays. Billie kept running her store around it. Constance returned rejected clothes to their racks. Customers flowed past with the casual tolerance of people for whom a woman learning to walk in spectacular shoes apparently ranked somewhere below weather as a notable event.

Steve, meanwhile, approached the problem like faulty machinery.

“So the heel isn't the problem.”

“The heel is absolutely part of the problem,” Emily said.

“No, I mean mechanically. I'm fighting the shoe.”

Greta pointed at him. “There.”

“There what?”

“Stop doing that.”

“Doing what?”

“Fighting it.”

Steve took another step.

The pump landed.

His weight moved forward.

The skirt swayed.

“Oh.”

Greta nodded.

Steve tried again.

“Oh.”

Chloe leaned toward Tori. “He's reverse-engineering femininity.”

Steve pointed without looking at her.

“I heard that.”

“You were supposed to.”

Another pass.

Another turn.

A wobble.

Recovery.

Again.

By the time Steve made it from one end of the aisle to the other, turned, and came back without reaching for anybody, he was smiling so broadly his cheeks hurt.

The red soles flashed beneath him with every other step.

“Again.”

Britt laughed. “You realize you're eventually going to have to walk outside.”

Steve stopped.

For one brief second, the storefront windows seemed much larger.

Beyond them was Greenleaf Core.

People.

Sidewalk.

Saturday.

The entire world in which Steve had arrived wearing jeans and sneakers and a green purse.

He looked at his reflection.

Emerald.

Oxblood.

Black.

Red.

Him.

His fingers tightened around the handbag strap.

Then his chin came up.

“That's why I'm practicing.”

Nobody made a joke.

Billie quietly collected his jeans, sweater, sneakers, and green purse from the fitting room and folded them into a Second Chapter shopping bag. Steve transferred the organized contents he needed from the green purse into the oxblood one with the concentration of a man changing command centers.

Lipstick.

Compact.

Wallet.

Phone.

The little systems he had built only an hour earlier slid into their new home.

The oxblood bag swallowed them without losing its shape.

Steve looked inside.

“Okay, that's extremely satisfying.”

Nat pressed a hand to her heart. “He's nesting.”

“I will put you in a garment bag.”

“Threatening me with organized storage isn't going to work.”

Steve closed the bag.

Then he walked.

Across the aisle.

Past the mirror.

Toward the front door.

The heels still demanded concentration. His stride was shorter. His turn around a display table required planning permission from several federal agencies.

But he was upright.

More importantly, he was delighted.

At the door he stopped.

The bell above it waited.

Britt came up beside him.

“You good?”

Steve looked through the glass.

Then down at himself.

Not at the shoes this time.

At all of it.

He had walked into Second Chapter in jeans, a sweater, sneakers, red lipstick, and a green purse he'd only recently stopped thinking of as ridiculous.

Now an emerald skirt moved around his knees.

An oxblood handbag rested against his hip.

Four inches of black heel held him above red soles that were still, frankly, trying to kill him.

He smiled.

“Yeah.”

Steve opened the door.

The bell rang.

Saturday afternoon moved around him without stopping.

A woman passing with a coffee glanced at the dress and smiled.

Another looked briefly at the shoes.

Nobody gasped.

Nobody pointed.

Nobody asked for an explanation.

Steve put one heel onto the sidewalk.

Then the other.

Careful.

Short.

Heel.

Toe.

The seven women spilled out behind him, along with enough suppressed excitement to power a municipal grid.

Steve took three more steps.

Four.

Five.

The skirt moved.

The bag settled against his hip.

A red sole flashed.

He grinned.

Britt came alongside him.

“Where to?”

Steve took another careful step.

Then another.

“I have no fucking idea.”

He looked down the street.

Then caught himself.

Eyes up.

He smiled wider.

“But apparently I need a lot more clothes.”
