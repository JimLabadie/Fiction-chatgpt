# Purse Theory

Steve came out of the powder room feeling as though considerably more had happened in there than should reasonably be possible in a room containing toilets.

She was still herself.

That seemed worth establishing.

Same faded jeans. Same charcoal sweater pushed casually up her forearms. Same comfortable sneakers she'd put on that morning because she'd been told brunch and *maybe* shopping, and she had no intention of spending an afternoon being tortured by footwear.

But her mouth was red now.

Not bright red. Maddie had called it something Steve couldn't remember and Britt had corrected her with another name Steve also couldn't remember.

Steve called it red.

It looked good.

Annoyingly good.

And she had learned something else in the powder room.

Mirrors were dangerous.

Specifically, women looking at other women through mirrors while applying lipstick were dangerous.

More specifically, *Steve* looking at women through mirrors while applying lipstick was apparently dangerous.

She was still enjoying that discovery.

The diner swallowed them again as they came through the little hallway from the restrooms: overlapping conversations, silverware striking heavy white plates, the hiss of the grill through the kitchen pass, the smell of coffee and bacon and syrup hanging comfortably in the air. Sunlight poured through the broad front windows and caught chrome trim along the booths.

Their table looked exactly as they'd left it.

A glorious disaster.

Eight coffee mugs in various states of abandonment. Water glasses. Plates pushed toward the center. Maddie's syrup container somehow sitting beside Chloe's phone. Napkins. Silverware. Tori's sunglasses.

And Steve's green purse.

Sitting beside Chloe.

Steve walked straight past it.

She reached her chair and sat.

Chloe looked at her.

Then at the purse.

Then back at Steve.

Steve noticed Chloe looking at her mouth.

Ah.

Opportunity.

She leaned back slightly.

“Something wrong?”

Chloe's eyes flicked to hers.

“No.”

Steve let the corner of her newly red mouth turn upward.

“You sure?”

Chloe stared.

Steve held her eyes.

One second.

Two.

Three.

Chloe's expression barely changed.

But Steve saw it.

The tiniest loss of composure.

“Oh, this is *fun*.”

Behind her, Maddie made a delighted noise.

“I told you!”

Chloe recovered.

“You're going to become insufferable.”

“Become?”

Nat laughed.

Steve reached for her coffee.

Chloe said, “Forget something?”

Steve looked at her.

“What?”

Chloe glanced toward the green purse.

Steve followed her eyes.

“Oh.”

There it was.

Exactly where Steve had left it before following the women to the powder room.

Steve reached over and picked it up.

“Thanks.”

She hooked the strap over the back of her chair.

Seven women went quiet.

Steve slowly turned back.

“What?”

Nobody answered.

Steve looked from face to face.

Britt.

Liz.

Maddie.

Emily.

Nat.

Tori.

Finally Chloe.

“What?”

Chloe looked at the purse.

Steve looked over her shoulder.

Then back.

“What about it?”

Britt asked, “Where was your purse while you were in the bathroom?”

Steve pointed.

“Right there.”

“Yes.”

“Safely surrounded by lesbians.”

Nat choked on her coffee.

Steve continued.

“I fail to see the problem.”

Liz smiled.

“What if we hadn't been here?”

“I wouldn't have left it.”

Seven expressions suggested skepticism.

Steve frowned.

“I wouldn't.”

“You forgot it existed,” Chloe said.

“I did not.”

“You walked away without it.”

“I was going to the bathroom.”

“Yes.”

“I didn't need my wallet to pee.”

“That isn't the point.”

Steve leaned back.

“All right. What is the point?”

Tori folded her hands.

“Your purse goes with you.”

Steve stared.

“To the bathroom.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because it's your purse.”

“That is circular reasoning.”

“It contains your things.”

“Three things.”

Britt's eyebrows rose.

“Three?”

“Wallet. Phone. Keys.”

Seven women stared.

Steve looked around.

“What?”

Maddie leaned toward her.

“That's all you have in there?”

“What else would I have?”

The women exchanged looks.

Steve recognized that immediately.

“No.”

Nobody had said anything.

“No.”

Liz laughed.

Steve pointed around the table.

“I don't know what you're about to do, but no.”

Emily reached beneath her chair and brought up her handbag.

Nat did the same.

Then Tori.

Britt.

Liz.

Maddie.

Chloe already had hers tucked beside her.

Seven purses appeared around the remains of brunch.

Steve looked at them.

Then at the women.

Then at her own lonely green purse hanging behind her.

“Oh, Jesus Christ.”

Tori said, “Purse Theory.”

“That is not a thing.”

“It is now,” Chloe said.

“You stay out of this.”

“I've been carrying a purse longer than you.”

“You've been carrying a purse longer than I've been carrying a purse by approximately forever.”

“Exactly. Respect your elders.”

Steve laughed.

Then Britt reached over and lifted Steve's purse from the chair.

Steve held out her hand.

“Hey.”

Britt gave it to her.

“Keep it with you.”

Steve dropped it into her lap.

“There. Purse secured. Lesson complete.”

“No,” Emily said.

Steve sighed.

“Of course not.”

Britt nodded toward the bag.

“Open it.”

Steve did.

Wallet.

Phone.

Keys.

And—

Steve stopped.

The lipstick Maddie had given her in the powder room.

She picked it up.

Maddie smiled.

Steve looked at it for a moment.

Then placed it carefully back inside.

“Four things.”

Britt looked horrified.

Steve laughed.

“What?”

“That's not a purse.”

Steve held it up.

“It has handles, a strap and a zipper. I feel confident in the classification.”

“It's a pocket.”

“A very expensive pocket,” Chloe said.

Steve looked at Britt.

Britt shrugged.

Steve decided not to ask.

Tori tapped the table.

“A purse is an ecosystem.”

Steve stared.

“A what?”

“An ecosystem.”

“You keep frogs in yours?”

“Only Thursdays.”

Steve laughed despite herself.

Tori continued.

“Your wallet, phone and keys are the obvious things. They're not the reason purses are useful.”

“What is?”

“The other stuff.”

“What other stuff?”

Seven women opened their purses.

Steve looked around.

Something shifted in her expression.

“Wait.”

Maddie was already rummaging.

“No. Seriously. What do you all have in there?”

That changed the energy.

The joke was still there.

But Steve was actually curious now.

Emily started pulling things out.

“Tissues.”

Nat:

“Charger.”

Tori:

“Pens.”

Britt:

“Comb.”

Liz:

“Pain reliever.”

Maddie:

“Gum!”

Chloe:

“Human remains.”

Steve stared.

Chloe removed a packet of hand sanitizer.

“Disappointing.”

“You're an asshole.”

“You like me.”

Steve smiled.

“Unfortunately.”

Chloe's eyes dropped briefly to Steve's red mouth.

Steve caught it.

*Oh, yes.*

She was absolutely practicing that again later.

Tori began arranging things on the table.

“Purse Theory is simple. Small problems happen all day.”

“Okay.”

“You can either be annoyed by them—”

“Or have the thing that fixes them,” Steve finished.

Tori pointed at her.

“Exactly.”

Steve looked around at the open purses.

That was—

Actually brilliant.

“So this is basically everyday contingency planning.”

Chloe groaned.

“Oh, no.”

“What?”

“We've explained it in Steve.”

Nat laughed.

Steve ignored her.

“You're carrying solutions to predictable low-level problems.”

“Yes.”

“Why did nobody tell me this?”

Seven women stared.

Steve looked down at herself.

Jeans.

Sweater.

Male clothes.

Then at the seven handbags.

“Right.”

Maddie's expression softened.

Steve noticed.

“Don't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You made the face.”

“What face?”

“The sweet face.”

“I have a sweet face.”

“You have a dangerous face.”

Maddie beamed.

Steve shook her head.

“Okay. So what do I actually need?”

Britt smiled.

Steve immediately pointed.

“No.”

“What?”

“That smile means shopping.”

“We were already going shopping.”

Steve remembered.

Brunch and *maybe* shopping.

“Hypothetical shopping.”

“Of course.”

Tori looked at Steve's nearly empty purse.

“You don't know what you need yet.”

“Exactly.”

“So you start with what works for us.”

Steve looked around.

“Meaning?”

Liz understood.

“Oh.”

Maddie understood next.

Her eyes lit up.

“Oh!”

Steve became suspicious.

“What?”

Liz looked at the others.

“Seed stock.”

Steve stared.

“You're going to reproduce my purse?”

“Ecosystem,” Tori reminded her.

“Stop calling it that.”

Maddie was already digging through her bag.

Steve watched.

“What are you doing?”

“Contributing.”

She produced a small packet of tissues.

Steve looked at them.

Then at Maddie.

“Why?”

“Because you don't have tissues.”

“I have napkins.”

“You don't carry diner napkins everywhere.”

Steve considered.

Fair.

Maddie placed the packet on the table between them.

Then she added a little package of mints.

Steve pointed.

“One.”

“They're a set.”

“They are absolutely not a set.”

“They are emotionally a set.”

Steve looked at Liz.

“Can things be emotionally a set?”

“No,” Liz said.

Maddie looked betrayed.

“However,” Liz continued, “you should probably carry mints.”

Maddie brightened.

Steve sighed.

“Tissues and mints.”

Maddie pushed them toward her.

Steve picked them up.

Something about the gesture suddenly felt more significant than two tiny drugstore items had any business feeling.

They had been in Maddie's purse.

Now she was giving them to Steve.

Steve opened the green purse.

Put them inside.

Maddie smiled.

Steve smiled back.

“Thank you.”

---

Emily went next.

She pulled a small travel tube from her purse.

“Pain reliever.”

Steve took it immediately.

“No argument?”

“I get headaches.”

“Exactly.”

Emily started to hand it over, then paused.

“I need some of these.”

“Obviously.”

Emily opened the container over her palm, counted several tablets into a tiny spare pill pouch from inside her bag, then handed the little pouch to Steve.

Steve stared.

“You have tiny pill bags?”

Emily shrugged.

“Purse.”

Steve laughed.

“I'm beginning to understand that word explains a disturbing amount.”

She put the pain reliever inside.

Emily dug again and produced two blister bandages.

Steve looked at her.

Emily looked pointedly at Steve's sneakers.

Then at Steve.

“Not today.”

Steve understood immediately.

“The heels.”

“The heels.”

Steve took them.

“That is survival equipment.”

“Correct.”

Into the purse.

---

Nat held up a compact power bank.

Steve's eyes narrowed.

“You carry that every day?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“My phone occasionally develops a gambling problem with its battery percentage.”

Steve laughed.

Nat unplugged a short charging cable from it.

“I have another cable.”

“You're giving me the whole battery?”

“I have two.”

“Why do you have two?”

Nat looked at her.

“Purse.”

Steve closed her eyes.

“Right.”

She took it.

The power bank had weight.

Real utility.

She put it into the green purse.

The bag settled slightly differently in her lap.

Steve noticed.

---

Tori put a pen on the table.

Then another.

Steve looked at both.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“Why two?”

“One fails.”

“They're pens.”

“One fails.”

“They're not fighter aircraft.”

Tori pushed both toward her.

“One fails.”

Steve stared at her.

“You've been hurt before.”

“I've lived.”

Steve picked up the pens.

“One.”

“Two.”

“One.”

“Two.”

Steve looked around.

“Anybody?”

Seven women shook their heads.

“Cowards.”

Both pens went in.

Tori smiled.

Then removed two safety pins from a tiny interior pocket in her purse.

Steve blinked.

“You actually carry loose safety pins?”

“Not loose.”

Tori showed her.

They were fastened neatly to a little fabric tab inside the pocket.

Steve leaned closer.

“Huh.”

“Broken strap. Loose button. Small tear. Zipper problem.”

Steve took them without argument.

Tori smiled.

“Learning.”

“Shut up.”

---

Britt opened a little cosmetic pouch.

Steve immediately became wary.

“Makeup requires authorization.”

“I'm aware of the Accords.”

Britt pulled out a small nail file.

Steve looked at it.

“My nails are approximately four millimeters long.”

“You can still snag one.”

Steve ran her thumb across one thumbnail.

She had, in fact, snagged one two days ago and spent the afternoon catching it on things.

“Fine.”

She accepted it.

Then Britt produced a small folding comb.

Steve looked at the comb.

Then at Britt.

Neither spoke.

Steve touched her short hair.

Britt remained perfectly neutral.

Steve thought about the photograph Britt had sent.

Long hair.

Extensions.

*Absolutely not.*

Followed two minutes later by:

*How long do those last?*

Steve held out her hand.

Britt gave her the comb.

Steve put it into the purse.

“Hypothetical.”

“Obviously.”

Nobody laughed.

Nobody pushed.

Steve appreciated that more than she wanted to admit.

---

Chloe held out a stain-removal pen.

Steve stared.

“That's surprisingly normal.”

“Fuck you.”

Steve took it.

“Do these actually work?”

“Yes.”

“How often do you spill things on yourself?”

“I live with Maddie.”

“Hey!”

Steve laughed and put it in.

Chloe held up a tiny bottle of hand sanitizer.

Steve looked at it.

Then at Chloe.

“Two things.”

“I'm generous.”

“You're contaminating the experimental design.”

“Then reject it.”

Steve reached for it.

Chloe pulled it back.

Steve frowned.

“Hey.”

Chloe smiled.

Steve held out her hand.

“Give me the sanitizer.”

“You want it?”

“I have touched this table.”

Chloe handed it over.

Steve put it into the purse.

“You're still an asshole.”

“You're welcome.”

---

Liz had been watching the whole process.

Steve looked at her.

“Well?”

Liz opened her handbag.

“What do you think you're missing?”

Steve frowned.

“That seems like a trick question.”

“It isn't.”

Steve looked into the green purse.

It was no longer empty.

She could see pieces of them inside.

Maddie's tissues and mints.

Emily's pain reliever and blister bandages.

Nat's battery.

Tori's absurd second pen and safety pins.

Britt's nail file and little comb.

Chloe's stain remover and sanitizer.

Steve looked back at Liz.

“I don't know yet.”

Liz smiled.

“Good answer.”

She reached into her purse and pulled out a small travel packet of wet wipes.

Steve laughed.

“Seriously?”

“Seriously.”

“I have tissues.”

“Different job.”

“I have sanitizer.”

“Different job.”

Steve looked around.

“Are you all like this?”

“Yes.”

Steve took the wipes.

Then Liz reached into a little interior pocket and removed a folded twenty-dollar bill.

Steve frowned.

“No.”

“Emergency cash.”

“I have a wallet.”

“And cards stop working.”

“I have cash.”

“Do you always?”

Steve opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Liz held out the folded bill.

“This doesn't go in your wallet.”

“Why?”

“Because wallet money is money.”

Steve waited.

“This is purse money.”

Steve stared.

“Those are the same currency.”

“Not psychologically.”

Chloe nodded.

“She has you there.”

“She absolutely does not.”

Liz put the folded twenty in Steve's palm.

“Put it somewhere inside. Forget it's there.”

Steve looked at it.

“For what?”

“Parking machine doesn't take your card. Card reader goes down. You need to tip somebody. Your phone dies and you can't use an app. Whatever.”

Steve considered.

Then found a small zippered pocket inside the purse.

She tucked the twenty into it.

Zipped it closed.

“Emergency cash.”

“Emergency cash.”

Steve looked at Liz.

“Thank you.”

Liz squeezed her hand.

“You're welcome.”

---

Steve sat back.

The green purse rested open in her lap.

She looked inside.

For the first time, it actually contained something.

Not just her pocket contents relocated into a bag.

An infrastructure.

No.

She refused to call it infrastructure.

An ecosystem.

Fuck.

She wasn't calling it that either.

Steve began moving things around.

Phone where she could reach it.

Wallet beside it.

Keys somewhere they wouldn't scratch the phone.

Power bank lower down.

Lipstick—

Steve stopped.

She picked it up.

Looked at the tube.

Then around at seven women.

“Problem.”

Britt leaned forward.

“What?”

“How am I supposed to know if this needs fixing?”

She held up the lipstick.

Maddie smiled.

“Mirror.”

Steve looked inside her purse.

No mirror.

She looked at Maddie.

Maddie opened her own purse and produced a compact.

Steve reached for it.

Maddie pulled it back.

“No.”

Steve blinked.

“What?”

“This one's mine.”

Steve stared.

Maddie smiled.

“You need your own.”

Steve looked at the compact.

Then at her purse.

Then at the lipstick.

“Oh.”

Britt smiled.

Steve pointed.

“Don't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

Steve put the lipstick back.

“Fine. I need a mirror.”

“A compact,” Maddie corrected.

“I need a mirror that closes.”

“A compact.”

Steve glared.

“A compact.”

Maddie beamed.

Steve looked into the purse again.

Then something occurred to her.

“What else am I missing?”

Seven women smiled.

Steve recoiled.

“Oh, no.”

Liz laughed.

“You'll figure it out.”

“I don't like the way you said that.”

“You're going shopping anyway.”

“Hypothetically.”

Britt looked at the purse.

“Of course.”

Steve zipped it closed.

Then, without thinking, started to hook it over the back of her chair again.

Seven voices:

“STEVE.”

She froze.

Looked at the chair.

Looked at them.

“Oh, fuck off.”

Steve pulled the purse back into her lap.

The table erupted.

“This is ridiculous.”

Tori smiled.

“Purse custody.”

“It's surrounded by seven women!”

“Six,” Chloe said. “You are now one of the women surrounding the purse.”

Steve stopped.

Nobody laughed.

The diner continued around them.

Plates clattered somewhere behind Steve. A server called an order toward the kitchen. Afternoon sunlight warmed the edge of the table.

Steve looked at Chloe.

Chloe's expression hadn't changed.

Matter-of-fact.

*You are now one of the women surrounding the purse.*

Steve looked down.

Green leather.

Her hand resting over the zipper.

Things from all seven of them inside.

Steve swallowed.

Then she stood.

“All right.”

Liz looked up.

“All right what?”

Steve put the strap over her shoulder.

It settled against her jeans.

“We need to go to a drugstore.”

Seven faces lit up.

Steve immediately pointed around the table.

“For practical reasons.”

“Obviously,” Britt said.

“I need a compact.”

“Obviously.”

“And apparently there are things I don't know I need.”

Tori stood.

“Many.”

Steve narrowed her eyes.

“We're not going crazy.”

“Of course not,” Liz said.

Steve looked at seven women who were entirely too agreeable.

She sighed.

“I'm going to regret this.”

Chloe picked up her purse.

“No.”

Steve looked at her.

Chloe smiled.

“You're going to need a bigger bag.”

Steve stared.

“Fuck you.”

Chloe walked toward the door.

Steve followed her.

With her purse.
