# Chapter Seven

## Second Chapter

By the time they reached Greenleaf Core, Steve had opened his purse four times.

Not because he needed anything.

Testing.

The first time, he retrieved his phone.

The second, his compact.

The third, the emergency twenty.

The fourth—

Brittney caught him.

“What are you doing?”

Steve zipped the green purse closed as they walked.

“Nothing.”

“You’re testing it.”

“No.”

“You are absolutely testing it.”

“I was verifying the organization.”

Brittney looked over her shoulder at the others.

“He’s benchmarking the purse.”

“I am not benchmarking the purse.”

Nat adjusted the strap of her own handbag. “How’s retrieval latency?”

“Excellent, actually.”

There was a beat.

Steve sighed.

“Fuck.”

Chloe’s expression remained perfectly flat.

“Benchmark confirmed.”

They were laughing when they reached the busier storefronts around Greenleaf Stage.

The change from the Medical Plaza wasn’t dramatic so much as cumulative. Coffee shops spilled people onto the sidewalk. Someone came out of the printshop carrying a flat brown-paper parcel under one arm. Beyond the storefronts, the community garden broke up the brick and glass with late-season green. People moved between errands without much urgency, and Steve had the odd sensation he’d been having increasingly often in St. Claire—that the neighborhood had been assembled out of things people actually used instead of things someone had decided looked good on a development brochure.

Then Brittney stopped.

“There.”

Steve followed her finger.

Second Chapter.

He looked at the window.

Then at Brittney.

“This is a thrift store.”

Seven women reacted as if he’d kicked a puppy.

Liz actually stopped walking.

“No.”

Steve looked at the sign again.

“It sells used things.”

Tori’s face became solemn. “You need to apologize.”

“To the store?”

“To fashion.”

“I haven’t even gone inside.”

Maddie touched his arm.

“You can still turn your life around.”

Steve stared at her.

Emily opened the door.

“Just go in.”

A little bell sounded overhead.

Steve stepped through.

And stopped.

“Oh.”

Behind him, Chloe said, “There it is.”

Second Chapter wasn’t organized like any secondhand store Steve had ever seen.

There were racks, yes, but not endless metal rows of random clothing arranged by increasingly optimistic interpretations of size. The deep shop floor unfolded in sections of color and era, so that burgundy and wine became rust and amber before giving way to green; somewhere beyond that, shapes changed too, decades talking to one another across the aisles.

Handbags weren’t confined to one wall.

They appeared everywhere.

On shelves.

On tables.

Beside dresses.

Under scarves.

Near jewelry cases.

Structured leather bags sat beside soft slouching ones. Tiny evening bags glittered under the lights. There were enormous totes Steve could probably fit a moderately cooperative terrier inside. Vintage top-handles. Bright patent leather. Woven bags. Satchels. Clutches. Things with chains. Things with buckles. Things whose purpose Steve could not immediately determine but whose designers had apparently possessed very strong opinions.

Shoes appeared beneath clothing displays, but Steve deliberately did not look at those.

There were mirrors everywhere.

Not department-store mirrors positioned like traps, but big useful ones integrated into the browsing space, as if the entire point was to pick something up and hold it against yourself.

Women were doing exactly that.

One stood in front of a mirror with two scarves over one shoulder while her companion held up a third.

Another had three handbags lined along her arm.

Nobody appeared to be behaving as though any of this required permission.

Steve slowly turned.

“This is not a thrift store.”

Liz smiled.

“No, mijo.”

“What is it?”

Brittney pushed him gently between the shoulder blades.

“Trouble.”

⸻

Three women were working the floor.

Steve noticed that immediately because all three looked as though they belonged in the store rather than merely being employed by it.

The woman nearest the front was rearranging a display and wearing what Steve realized, after a second look, had almost certainly once been something else. Billie Sandoval had taken whatever original garment had existed and apparently argued with it until it became a fitted feminine knit top that worked perfectly with her pleated midi skirt. Her black hair was gathered into a neat low bun, and her block-heeled pumps added nearly four inches without slowing her as she shifted a handbag six inches to the left, considered it, and moved it two inches back.

She looked at the eight-person invasion coming through her door.

Then at Steve’s green purse.

Then at the seven women behind him.

Her eyes narrowed with immediate professional interest.

“Oh,” she said.

Steve didn’t like the tone of that.

“What?”

Billie smiled.

“Nothing.”

Steve turned around.

“Why does everybody keep saying that to me today?”

“Coincidence,” Chloe said.

It absolutely was not.

Farther into the shop, another woman looked up from examining the inside seam of a jacket.

Constance Parker was impossible to mistake for someone who considered clothing an afterthought. Their close-cropped gray hair sharpened rather than softened the rest of the look: a sculpted midi dress with a dramatic feminine silhouette, polished jewelry, a precisely defined lip, and four-inch stilettos. A few pins and a length of thread still clung to one sleeve, the tiny evidence that this wasn’t costume.

This was Tuesday.

Or, technically, Saturday.

Steve looked from Constance to the racks.

Then back.

Constance noticed.

“What?”

Steve shook his head.

“Nothing.”

One corner of their mouth moved.

“Apparently contagious.”

And then a third woman emerged from behind a rack carrying three dresses over one arm.

Cat-eye glasses.

Soft brown waves.

Polished blouse.

Pencil skirt.

Four-inch heels.

Greta Lindstrom took one look at Steve, the purse, and the seven women surrounding him.

She brightened.

“Oh, fun.”

Steve pointed at her.

“No.”

Greta stopped.

“I haven’t done anything.”

“You were going to.”

“I don’t even know you.”

“Neither did the pharmacist. That didn’t save me.”

Billie laughed.

“First purse?”

Steve looked down at the green bag.

“No.”

All three women looked at it.

Steve felt suddenly defensive.

“This is a perfectly good purse.”

“I didn’t say it wasn’t,” Billie said.

“It has excellent internal organization now.”

“I’m sure it does.”

“I fixed it.”

Constance glanced at Chloe.

Chloe said, “He bought pouches.”

Constance nodded slowly.

“Ah.”

Steve looked between them.

“What does ah mean?”

Greta hung the dresses on a nearby rack.

“It means you came to the right place.”

⸻

Steve had expected purse shopping to involve purses.

This had been naïve.

Apparently purse shopping involved seven women immediately dispersing through a fashion boutique like they’d been given individual military objectives.

Brittney went left.

Chloe went right.

Nat disappeared toward a display Steve suspected contained expensive things because everything had more space around it.

Maddie made directly for the least practical bag in the building.

Tori didn’t touch anything at first. She stood back, arms folded, watching Steve look.

Emily stayed nearest him.

Liz wandered toward Billie.

Steve remained exactly where he’d been abandoned.

“This seems coordinated.”

Emily looked innocent.

“It isn’t.”

“Emily.”

“Okay, it might be a little coordinated.”

“How coordinated?”

From across the store, Brittney yelled, “Try this!”

Steve turned.

A handbag came toward him.

He caught it.

Barely.

He looked down.

It was small.

Very small.

Steve looked at Brittney.

“It’s tiny.”

“I didn’t ask if it could transport medical supplies.”

“I need things.”

“You need six fewer things than you think you do.”

“I have exactly the correct number of things.”

Maddie appeared at his elbow holding something glittering and absurd.

“I found your purse.”

Steve stared at it.

“No.”

“You didn’t even touch it.”

“I have eyes.”

“It has a bird.”

“It has a rhinestone bird.”

“Exactly.”

“No.”

Maddie looked genuinely disappointed.

“Control group,” Chloe said as she passed.

Steve’s head turned.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You said control group.”

“I said beautiful bag.”

“You absolutely did not.”

Chloe kept walking.

Steve looked at Emily.

Emily suddenly became fascinated by a scarf.

“Oh, you fuckers are conducting an experiment.”

Seven women, from seven different places in the store, said:

“No.”

Billie leaned against a display.

Greta covered her mouth.

Constance looked down at the jacket they were examining, shoulders moving once.

Steve pointed around the room.

“You’re terrible liars.”

⸻

The problem was that the experiment worked.

Steve rejected black first.

Not consciously.

Chloe handed him a structured black satchel.

Steve put it over his shoulder, looked in the mirror and frowned.

“What’s wrong with it?” she asked.

“Nothing.”

“Then why don’t you like it?”

“I didn’t say I didn’t like it.”

Chloe took it back.

“You made the face.”

“What face?”

“That face.”

“I don’t have a face.”

“You have several. That one’s no.”

She disappeared.

A minute later she returned with blue.

Not navy.

Blue.

Deep, saturated, unapologetic blue.

Steve took it.

The leather was soft but not floppy. The shape held itself. The hardware caught the light without looking gaudy.

He slipped it over his shoulder.

Looked in the mirror.

“Oh.”

Across the room, Nat pointed.

“Blue.”

Emily said, “I told you.”

“You said green,” Brittney said.

“I said saturated color.”

“You absolutely said green.”

Steve turned around.

“Are you taking notes?”

Tori immediately put her phone away.

Steve stared.

“Tori.”

“I got a text.”

“From who?”

Tori glanced at the screen.

“Jesus.”

“Jesus texted you?”

“He works Saturdays.”

Steve looked at Liz.

Liz shrugged. “Busy man.”

Steve looked back into the mirror.

The blue bag looked—

Better.

Not more useful.

He didn’t know anything about its pockets yet.

He hadn’t checked the zipper.

Hadn’t looked at the lining.

Hadn’t tested the strap.

It just looked better.

Steve frowned.

“Why?”

Billie, who had been pretending not to watch, stopped pretending.

“Why what?”

Steve pointed at the black bag Chloe still held.

“That one and this one do the same thing.”

“Probably.”

“This one looks better.”

“To you.”

“Yes.”

Billie smiled slightly.

“That’s usually how taste works.”

“No, I mean why?”

Now Constance looked interested.

Steve took the blue bag off and put the black one on again.

Black.

Fine.

Blue.

Better.

He switched again.

Black.

Fine.

Blue.

Better.

“This is stupid.”

Greta gasped.

“It is not.”

“I don’t mean fashion is stupid. I mean there should be a reason.”

“There is,” Constance said.

“What?”

“You like blue.”

Steve looked at them.

“That cannot be the whole explanation.”

“It can be.”

“I don’t accept that.”

Nat called from the expensive section, “We know.”

⸻

The next forty minutes became increasingly dangerous.

Steve learned that he liked structure.

Usually.

He liked hardware, but not too much hardware.

He liked color much more than he’d expected.

He disliked logos large enough to be read from orbit.

He liked leather that felt substantial.

He liked bags that held their shape when set down.

He wanted enough room for his increasingly organized collection of necessities, but apparently there was a line somewhere between useful and luggage.

Brittney found that line before he did.

“No.”

Steve held up a large tote.

“It has compartments.”

“You could carry groceries in that.”

“That is versatility.”

“You could carry me in that.”

Steve looked at her.

Looked at the tote.

Looked back at her.

“Probably.”

“Put it down.”

He put it down.

Maddie handed him another tiny bag.

“No.”

“You’re becoming closed-minded.”

“It holds a credit card.”

“And hope.”

“I cannot organize hope into pouches.”

“Not with that attitude.”

Greta laughed from behind a rack.

Steve was beginning to like Greta.

That seemed dangerous too.

⸻

Nat eventually returned carrying a handbag like she had found buried treasure.

“Okay.”

Everyone looked.

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“No.”

“You haven’t seen it.”

“I saw your face.”

“What about my face?”

“That’s your expensive face.”

Nat looked offended.

“I don’t have an expensive face.”

Chloe glanced at the bag.

“You absolutely have an expensive face.”

Nat handed it to Steve.

He took it.

And immediately understood the problem.

“Oh, fuck you.”

Nat beamed.

The leather was gorgeous.

The construction was gorgeous.

The hardware was restrained.

The color was somewhere between oxblood and deep wine, depending on the light.

It was larger than the green purse without being enormous. Structured without being rigid. Feminine without being delicate.

Steve opened it.

Constance appeared beside him.

“Good stitching.”

Steve glanced over.

“You can tell?”

Constance gave him a look.

“From across the room.”

Steve examined the seam.

Then the lining.

Then the hardware.

Then the base.

Billie watched the progression.

“You inspect everything like that?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Steve looked up.

“Good?”

“Fashion isn’t improved by ignorance.”

Something in that sentence landed harder than he’d expected.

He looked back down at the bag.

Greta joined them.

“Try it.”

Steve put it over his shoulder.

He turned toward the mirror.

And stopped.

Nobody said anything.

That was unusual enough by itself.

The bag sat against his hip.

His jeans and sweater were still exactly what he’d put on that morning. His sneakers were still sneakers. His lipstick was still bright red. The green purse was temporarily hanging from Emily’s shoulder behind him.

Nothing had changed.

Except—

Steve adjusted the strap.

The bag settled.

He tilted slightly.

Looked again.

There.

His chest did something small and strange.

Not unpleasant.

Recognition without explanation.

He liked it.

No.

That wasn’t quite right.

He liked the blue one.

This was different.

“This one.”

Behind him, the seven women exchanged looks.

Steve saw them in the mirror.

“Oh, don’t.”

Nobody spoke.

“You’re doing the thing.”

“What thing?” Brittney asked.

“The silent communication thing.”

“We’re standing here.”

“You’re communicating.”

“Humans do that.”

“Not telepathically.”

Chloe said, “Skill issue.”

Steve turned back to the mirror.

He touched the bag.

“Why is this different?”

Liz came to stand beside him.

“Because fashion is a conversation.”

Steve looked at her.

“With who?”

“With everybody.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

Liz smiled.

“Sometimes it is.”

Billie leaned against the edge of a display.

“Sometimes it’s history.”

Greta said, “Sometimes it’s flirting.”

Constance looked at Steve’s reflection rather than at Steve.

“Sometimes it’s belonging.”

Brittney said, “Sometimes you just wanna look fucking hot.”

“That one I understand.”

“Of course you do.”

Steve looked at Liz again.

“You said everybody.”

“I did.”

“So who else?”

Liz’s expression softened.

“You.”

Steve frowned.

She gestured toward the mirror.

“Sometimes you put something on because you want other people to see something.”

She paused.

“And sometimes you put something on, look in the mirror, and go, there.”

Steve looked at himself.

There.

He understood that.

Not the reason.

Not what it meant.

But the sensation.

He’d felt it with the green dress.

He’d felt a smaller version of it when Brittney fixed his lipstick.

He felt it now.

There.

“That,” Steve said quietly.

Liz didn’t move.

“What?”

Steve touched the side of the bag.

“That one says something.”

Nobody answered.

Steve looked at her.

“I don’t know what.”

Liz smiled.

“You don’t have to.”

Steve looked back into the mirror.

He considered the bag again.

There was a sentence.

Apparently.

He just couldn’t read it yet.

⸻

Then Steve found the price tag.

“WHAT THE FUCK?”

The entire store returned to normal.

Nat folded in half laughing.

Steve held the tag between two fingers as though it were contaminated.

“This is used!”

Billie looked deeply offended.

“Vintage and secondhand are not synonyms for worthless.”

“I didn’t say worthless! I said—”

Steve looked at the tag again.

“Jesus Christ.”

Tori said, “He’s still working Saturdays.”

“Apparently he needs the overtime!”

Constance took the bag from Steve and turned it slightly.

“Full-grain leather.”

Steve stopped.

“What?”

“Full-grain.”

Steve looked at the bag.

Then at Constance.

“What does that mean?”

Seven women went very still.

Billie looked at Greta.

Greta looked at Constance.

Constance looked at Steve.

Steve saw it happen.

“No.”

Constance smiled.

“It’s about which part of the hide—”

“No.”

“—and how much the surface has been altered—”

“I said no.”

Billie was already reaching for another bag.

“This one’s top-grain.”

Steve stared at her.

“What the fuck is top-grain?”

Brittney whispered, “Oh, we’re fucked.”

Steve heard her.

He pointed.

“No. You started this.”

“I did not teach you leather.”

“You brought me here.”

“I brought you purse shopping!”

“And now apparently purses have metallurgy!”

“Leather isn’t metal!”

“The buckles are!”

Constance’s eyes lit up.

“Yes, actually, hardware quality—”

“NO.”

Greta was laughing hard enough to take off her glasses.

Steve looked around at all of them.

Then down at the oxblood bag.

Then at the price tag.

Then at the stitching.

He opened it again.

Checked the lining.

Tested the zipper.

Adjusted the strap.

Looked in the mirror.

There.

“Goddammit.”

Nat straightened.

“Are you buying it?”

Steve looked at her.

“I’m thinking.”

“You’ve been thinking for thirty seconds.”

“I have to determine whether it’s worth it.”

Billie nodded approvingly.

“Correct.”

Steve looked at her.

“You’re not going to tell me?”

“Tell you what?”

“Whether I should buy it.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because you’re the one who has to carry it.”

Steve looked down.

That answer bothered him.

Mostly because it was obviously correct.

He walked back to the blue bag.

Tried it again.

Still liked it.

A lot.

Then the oxblood.

There.

Blue.

Cool.

Oxblood.

There.

Steve stared into the mirror.

“Fuck.”

Brittney came up beside him.

“You don’t have to buy either.”

“I know.”

“You can keep looking.”

“I know.”

“You can come back.”

“I know.”

She smiled.

Steve glanced at her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Britt.”

“I like watching you figure out what you like.”

That shut him up.

For almost five whole seconds.

Then Steve looked back at the bag.

“It’s really expensive.”

“Yes.”

“Like, offensively expensive.”

“Yes.”

“For a purse.”

Brittney shrugged.

“You tell me.”

Steve opened it again.

The compartments were good.

Not perfect.

But good.

The dimensions worked.

The strap worked.

The construction was excellent.

And it was beautiful.

That word came easily enough that Steve didn’t even notice he’d used it.

Beautiful.

He closed the bag.

“I want this one.”

Brittney’s smile became enormous.

Steve pointed at her.

“Do not.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You’re glowing.”

“Fuck you.”

⸻

Billie rang him up.

Steve watched the total appear.

His soul briefly left his body.

Then returned because apparently it had financial obligations.

“This is insane.”

Billie folded the tissue around the bag’s storage pouch.

“You chose well.”

“That doesn’t make it less insane.”

“No.”

Steve handed over his card.

“This is how it starts, isn’t it?”

Billie looked at him.

“How what starts?”

Steve gestured around Second Chapter.

All the racks.

The colors.

The mirrors.

The jewelry.

The handbags.

Greta helping a woman compare two dresses.

Constance crouched beside a hem, explaining something about construction with the intensity of an engineer discussing a bridge.

Seven women scattered through the store, still touching things, still laughing, still occasionally looking toward Steve with expressions they apparently believed were subtle.

“This.”

Billie smiled.

“Learning what you like?”

Steve considered that.

“Yeah.”

“That can get expensive.”

“I knew you were going to say that.”

“It can also get fun.”

Steve looked back toward the mirror.

The oxblood bag waited on the counter.

His bag.

His choice.

Not because Brittney handed it to him.

Not because Maddie declared it spiritually necessary.

Not because Chloe manipulated the experimental variables.

Not because Nat found the expensive one.

Not because Liz explained the theory.

Because Steve had tried enough things to recognize the answer.

He picked it up.

The green purse wasn’t obsolete.

Obviously.

That would be ridiculous.

The green purse was smaller. Useful. Casual. Already organized perfectly.

This one was different.

Different job.

Different look.

Different sentence.

Steve settled the new bag against his hip.

Then he looked at Billie.

“So.”

Billie’s eyes sharpened.

Steve pointed toward the shelves.

“If full-grain is better, what else am I supposed to be looking for?”

From somewhere behind him came Brittney’s voice.

“Oh, for fuck’s sake.”

Constance appeared almost instantly.

“Edge finishing.”

Steve turned.

“What?”

“Edge finishing. Stitch consistency. Hardware attachment. Lining construction. Reinforcement at stress points.”

Steve stared.

Greta came over.

“And era.”

“Why does era matter?”

Billie leaned both hands on the counter.

Nat whispered, “We’re losing him.”

Chloe shook her head.

“No.”

Steve looked between Billie, Greta, and Constance.

Three women who suddenly seemed to possess an entire body of information he had not known existed.

Chloe watched his face.

“We lost him twenty minutes ago.”

Steve pulled out his phone.

Brittney groaned.

“What are you doing?”

“Taking notes.”

“You’re taking notes about purses?”

Steve looked at her like she’d asked whether gravity remained operational.

“I don’t know anything about them.”

“That has never stopped a normal person.”

“Yes, well.”

Steve opened a note.

Handbags.

Under it he typed:

Full-grain leather.

Then:

Edge finishing.

Stitch consistency.

Hardware attachment.

Lining construction.

Stress-point reinforcement.

He looked up.

“What else?”

Seven women stared at him.

Billie smiled.

Constance visibly brightened.

Greta pushed her cat-eye glasses back into place.

And Brittney sighed with the weary affection of a woman who had just realized she’d accidentally introduced a raccoon to lock-picking.

“Nothing,” she said.

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“Bullshit.”

He looked at the three women behind the counter.

“Teach me.”

Greta’s smile became positively radiant.

“Oh, honey.”

Steve immediately regretted the phrasing.

But only a little.

Because the oxblood bag was still against his hip.

And every time he caught it in the mirror—

There.

There he was.
