# Chapter Four — Remedial Lesbianism

Steve discovered Saturday morning that the Steve Accords had a loophole.

A very large loophole.

At 10:27, three minutes before the negotiated pickup time, a black SUV stopped in front of her house.

Steve was waiting.

Jeans.

Sneakers.

Sweater.

No dress.

She had considered the dress.

That information would die with her.

Steve grabbed her wallet, phone and keys, opened the front door—

Then stopped.

Went back.

Picked up the green purse.

“No pockets,” she muttered.

That was all.

Pure logistics.

She absolutely did not notice that the purse looked better with her sweater than her old wallet bulging in her back pocket.

Steve walked outside.

The passenger window lowered.

Chloe was driving.

Britt occupied the passenger seat.

Maddie and Nat were in back.

All four stared at Steve.

Steve stopped halfway down the walk.

“What?”

Nobody answered.

Steve looked down at herself.

“What?”

Britt pointed.

“The purse.”

Steve looked at it.

“What about it?”

“You brought it.”

“It carries things.”

Chloe nodded.

“Groundbreaking.”

“Fuck you.”

“Good morning, pretty girl.”

Steve smiled before she could stop herself.

“Good morning, asshole.”

Chloe's eyebrows rose.

Nat leaned forward between the seats.

“Oh.”

Steve opened the rear door.

“What?”

“That.”

“What?”

“You flirted.”

Steve froze with one leg inside the car.

“I did not.”

“You absolutely did.”

“I told Chloe to fuck herself.”

Chloe looked pleased.

“Exactly.”

Steve climbed inside.

“I have been telling Chloe to fuck herself since approximately twenty minutes after meeting her.”

“Yes,” Britt said.

“And?”

Four women looked at one another.

Steve immediately became suspicious.

“What?”

Maddie leaned closer.

“Steve?”

“Yes?”

“Do you know you've been flirting with us all week?”

Steve stared at her.

“No.”

“Oh my God.”

“I HAVE NOT.”

Nat started laughing.

“With Chloe constantly.”

“I argue with Chloe.”

Chloe started the car.

“You argue with me differently.”

Steve looked toward the front.

“How?”

Chloe met her eyes in the mirror.

“You enjoy losing.”

Steve's jaw dropped.

“I have never lost an argument to you.”

Chloe smiled.

“See?”

Steve stared at her.

Something happened in the air.

Tiny.

Electric.

Chloe held her gaze through the mirror.

Steve felt heat crawl up her neck.

Nat slapped both hands against her thighs.

“THAT!”

Steve jerked her eyes away.

“What?”

“That was lesbian flirting!”

“It was eye contact!”

“Exactly!”

“Straight people also have eyes!”

Maddie collapsed against the seat laughing.

Steve folded her arms.

“I hate this family.”

Nobody spoke.

Steve realized what she'd said.

*Family.*

Her face changed.

So did theirs.

Chloe looked at her in the mirror again.

This time there was no teasing.

Steve swallowed.

“Well.”

Britt turned around.

“Too late now.”

Steve looked at her.

“For what?”

“We heard it.”

“I misspoke.”

“No take-backs.”

“That isn't in the Accords.”

Nat was already typing.

Steve lunged.

“Do NOT amend the Accords while the vehicle is moving.”

“Safety provision,” Nat said.

“You are impossible.”

Maddie leaned against Steve's shoulder.

Steve automatically leaned back.

Family.

She let the word remain.

---

Brunch was apparently a trap.

Steve should have known.

The first warning was when Liz, Emily and Tori were already waiting at the restaurant.

The second was when there was an empty chair between Liz and Chloe.

Steve stopped.

“No.”

Liz looked innocent.

“What?”

“You've assigned seating.”

“We saved you a chair.”

“Between the two most dangerous people here.”

Chloe looked offended.

“Liz isn't dangerous.”

Liz kicked her under the table.

Steve saw it.

She laughed and sat down.

“Okay. What's today's scam?”

“No scam,” Emily said.

“Shopping,” Britt added.

“Within treaty parameters.”

“Of course.”

“And brunch.”

“Obviously.”

“And Lesbian School,” Maddie said.

Steve slowly lowered her menu.

“I'm sorry?”

Maddie beamed.

“Lesbian School.”

“No.”

“That's not something you can veto.”

Steve looked around the table.

“Excuse me?”

Chloe opened her menu.

“The Accords govern modifications to Steve.”

“Correct.”

“Education is not modification.”

Steve stared.

“You found another loophole.”

“It's not a loophole. It's jurisdiction.”

“I hate that I've taught you the word jurisdiction.”

“You didn't.”

“Shut up.”

Chloe smiled.

Steve looked at Liz.

“Lesbian School?”

Liz was visibly trying not to laugh.

“Maddie's term.”

“What does it mean?”

Maddie raised her hand.

Steve sighed.

“Yes, Maddie.”

“We teach you how to lesbian.”

Steve closed her eyes.

“That isn't a verb either.”

“It is now.”

“What exactly do you think I need to learn?”

Seven women looked at one another.

Steve pointed.

“No committee consultation.”

Liz took pity on her.

“Nothing you *need* to learn.”

“Good.”

“But there are things you might enjoy.”

Steve opened one eye.

“Such as?”

“Flirting,” Nat said.

“I know how to flirt.”

Seven women became very interested in their menus.

Steve sat upright.

“Fuck you. I know how to flirt.”

Chloe turned a page.

“I'm sure you're excellent.”

“You are such an asshole.”

Chloe looked at her.

There it was again.

That little smile.

Steve felt the spark.

And suddenly, because Nat had pointed it out in the car, Steve recognized it.

Oh.

*Oh.*

Steve's mouth went dry.

Chloe saw the realization happen.

Her smile became devastating.

“Well?” Chloe asked.

Steve stared.

Chloe waited.

Steve had no idea what came next.

She looked desperately toward Liz.

Liz whispered:

“Hold her eyes.”

Steve looked back.

Chloe.

Eye contact.

Okay.

She could do eye contact.

Steve held it.

One second.

Two.

Chloe's expression shifted.

Less joke now.

More something else.

Steve's heart started pounding.

“Now what?” Steve whispered.

Seven women groaned.

Chloe laughed.

The spell shattered.

Steve covered her face.

“Oh, fuck all of you.”

Liz rubbed her back.

“You were doing really well.”

“I asked for instructions in the middle!”

“That was adorable,” Emily said.

“I don't want adorable.”

Chloe leaned closer.

“What do you want?”

Steve lowered her hands.

Chloe was right there.

Close enough that Steve could smell her perfume.

Steve's brain stopped functioning.

“I…”

Chloe waited.

Steve looked at her mouth.

Then realized she'd looked at her mouth.

Then looked back at her eyes.

Chloe's eyebrow rose.

Steve felt something click.

Not confidence exactly.

Understanding.

She leaned slightly closer.

“What do *you* want?”

Chloe stopped smiling.

The entire table went silent.

Steve felt a thrill.

Oh.

That had worked.

Chloe recovered.

Barely.

“Better.”

Steve leaned back.

A grin spread across her face.

“Holy shit.”

Nat pointed at her.

“There she is.”

Steve looked around.

“That was flirting?”

Liz laughed.

“Yes.”

“That was easy.”

Chloe recovered enough to pick up her coffee.

“You had coaching.”

Steve turned.

“Are you threatened?”

Chloe almost choked.

Steve's eyes widened.

Then she smiled.

“Oh my God.”

Chloe put down the coffee.

“Don't get cocky.”

“You're blushing.”

“I am not.”

“You absolutely are.”

Steve leaned toward her.

Chloe leaned back.

Steve's smile grew.

“Interesting.”

Maddie whispered loudly:

“She's learning so fast.”

Chloe pointed at Steve.

“You created a monster.”

Liz shook her head.

“No. Apparently she was already there.”

Steve sat back, ridiculously pleased with herself.

“I like Lesbian School.”

“Of course you do,” Britt said.

---

Lesson two was compliments.

Steve objected immediately.

“I know how to compliment women.”

“Demonstrate,” Nat said.

Steve looked around.

“Fine.”

She turned to Emily.

“Your dress looks nice.”

Seven women groaned.

“What?”

Emily laughed.

“Thank you.”

“But?”

Liz explained.

“You complimented the dress.”

“Yes.”

“Compliment *her*.”

Steve frowned.

“Okay.”

She looked at Emily again.

“You look beautiful in that dress.”

“Better.”

Steve shrugged.

“That isn't particularly complicated.”

“Now make it flirt.”

Steve looked at Emily.

Emily smiled.

Steve suddenly became nervous.

“Why is this different?”

“Because you're thinking about it,” Tori said.

Steve considered that.

Then looked at Emily again.

She noticed things.

The earrings.

The neckline.

Emily's hair.

Her smile.

Steve had always noticed women.

She'd simply trained herself not to stare.

Now seven women were effectively telling her:

*Look.*

So Steve did.

Emily's smile changed slightly under the attention.

Steve understood.

“You have really beautiful shoulders.”

Emily blinked.

Steve continued, gaining confidence.

“And that neckline is completely unfair.”

Emily's cheeks went pink.

The table erupted.

Steve laughed.

“That worked too!”

Emily pointed at her.

“You're enjoying this way too much.”

“I've apparently been given superpowers.”

“No,” Chloe said. “We've removed your training wheels.”

Steve looked at her.

Chloe immediately realized she'd made a mistake.

Steve's eyes narrowed playfully.

“Jealous?”

“No.”

“Would you like a compliment?”

“No.”

“Your mouth is gorgeous.”

Chloe froze.

Steve froze.

Everyone froze.

Steve hadn't planned it.

It had simply come out.

Chloe's eyes dropped briefly to Steve's lips.

Then back.

“Oh,” Maddie whispered.

Steve's pulse jumped.

Chloe leaned closer.

“So is yours.”

Steve forgot every lesson she'd received.

“Thank you.”

Chloe smiled.

Nat put her head on the table.

“We lost her again.”

---

Lesson three happened accidentally.

A beautiful woman walked past their table.

Steve glanced.

Once.

Then again.

She caught herself.

Britt caught her too.

“She's cute.”

Steve nearly knocked over her water.

“Who?”

Britt stared.

“Steve.”

“What?”

“You can say women are attractive.”

“I know.”

“Apparently not.”

Steve looked toward the woman again.

She was cute.

Very.

“Okay.”

Britt waited.

Steve sighed.

“She's attractive.”

“Was that painful?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Steve didn't have an answer.

Liz did.

“You've spent a long time worrying about making women uncomfortable.”

Steve looked at her.

That landed.

Hard.

Because yes.

Steve knew what men could be like.

She had seen it.

She had never wanted to be that man.

So she'd learned caution.

Don't stare.

Don't assume.

Don't corner.

Don't make friendliness sexual.

Don't make women manage your attraction for you.

Steve had thought that was simply decency.

It was.

But somewhere along the way she'd become afraid of expressing desire at all.

“I don't want to be creepy,” she said quietly.

The joking stopped.

Liz touched her hand.

“Then don't be.”

Steve gave her a look.

“Brilliant.”

Liz smiled.

“You already know the important part.”

“Which is?”

“Pay attention.”

Steve waited.

“Flirting isn't something you *do to* somebody. You're seeing whether she's playing too.”

That made sense.

More sense than Steve expected.

“So eye contact.”

“Can be.”

“Compliments.”

“Can be.”

“Teasing.”

Chloe lifted her coffee.

“Obviously.”

Steve smiled.

“And if she doesn't return it?”

“You let it go.”

“That's it?”

“That's it.”

Steve considered.

“That seems remarkably straightforward.”

Chloe looked at her.

“Most things are when you actually listen.”

Steve met her eyes.

There was no joke this time.

“I listen.”

“I know.”

Something warm passed between them.

Steve smiled.

---

By the time brunch ended, Steve had acquired a curriculum.

She objected to the word.

Nobody cared.

**REMEDIAL LESBIANISM — WEEK ONE**

“Eye contact.”

“Fine.”

“Compliments.”

“Fine.”

“Reciprocal flirting.”

“Fine.”

“Recognizing when a woman is flirting with you.”

“I can recognize that.”

“Fuck you.”

“I've got that.”

Steve kissed the top of her head without thinking.

Silence.

Steve looked around.

“What?”

Maddie slowly lifted her head.

Steve realized what she'd done.

“Oh.”

Maddie's expression melted.

“Do it again.”

Steve laughed.

She kissed Maddie's forehead.

Maddie made a tiny happy noise and collapsed against her.

Steve's heart nearly exploded.

Nat added something to the curriculum.

“What did you write?”

“Forehead kisses.”

“That's not a class.”

“Advanced practicum.”

“What else?”

“Receiving compliments without arguing.”

“I don't do that.”

Seven women stared.

“Fine.”

“Accepting affection.”

“I accept affection.”

More staring.

“Fine.”

“Understanding lesbian time.”

“Excellent educational institution you've established.”

“We're regionally accredited.”

“By whom?”

“Lesbians.”

“Which lesbians?”

Chloe gestured around the table.

“These.”

Steve laughed.

Then Nat reached the final item.

“Recognizing when you're on a date.”

“That seems unnecessary.”

Silence.

Steve looked around.

“What?”

Nobody answered.

Steve looked at Liz.

Liz bit her lip.

Steve's eyes narrowed.

“No.”

Maddie giggled.

Steve looked around the restaurant.

At the eight of them.

At the flowers on the table.

At the way she was sitting between Liz and Chloe.

At Maddie's hand casually resting on her arm.

At Britt smiling.

At Emily and Tori watching her.

At Nat trying desperately not to laugh.

Steve stared.

“You said brunch!”

“BRUNCH IS NOT A RELATIONSHIP STATUS.”

“There are eight people here!”

“I KNOW WHAT POLYAMORY IS.”

Steve pointed at her.

“What?”

“Are you having a good time?”

Steve stopped.

Really looked.

Her women.

“That might be the smartest thing anybody has said all morning.”

“Thank you.”
