# Chapter Six — St. Claire Pharmacy

Steve knew the outing had changed shape when Brittney turned right instead of left.

He stopped on the sidewalk.

The green purse bumped lightly against his hip.

“Wait.”

Seven women stopped in various stages of not actually stopping.

Britt turned around. “What?”

“I thought we were going purse shopping.”

“We are.”

Steve pointed in the direction Brittney had been walking. “Then why are we going that way?”

“Pharmacy first.”

He stared at her.

Britt stared back.

Steve looked toward the others for assistance.

This was a tactical error.

Liz had already started walking again.

“Come on, mija.”

“I don't need anything from the pharmacy.”

Seven women laughed.

Steve frowned.

“What?”

Chloe tucked her hands into the pockets of her coat. “Nothing.”

“No. That was a group laugh.”

“It was not.”

“It absolutely was. You all laughed at exactly the same time.”

Nat nodded thoughtfully. “We should practice staggering it.”

“Thank you.”

“That wasn't support.”

“I know.”

Steve caught up with them, sneakers scuffing lightly over the sidewalk. The purse settled against his side again as naturally as if he'd been carrying it for years instead of—

Actually, Steve wasn't entirely sure how long he'd been carrying it anymore.

Long enough.

He'd stopped noticing it somewhere between the diner and the corner.

That was probably because purses were useful.

The evidence was overwhelming.

Wallet. Phone. Keys. Lipstick. Tissues. Mints. Pain reliever. Blister bandages. Power bank. Cable. Pens. Safety pins. Nail file. Comb. Stain pen. Sanitizer. Wet wipes. Twenty dollars in emergency cash.

All immediately available without turning his pockets into a junk drawer.

Objectively superior.

The only problem was that the little green purse was approaching what Steve privately considered an unacceptable utilization percentage.

He opened it while walking.

Emily caught him looking inside.

“What are you doing?”

“Nothing.”

“You're inventorying.”

“I am not inventorying.”

“You have your inventory face.”

Steve looked up. “I don't have an inventory face.”

“You absolutely have an inventory face.”

Maddie leaned over to inspect him.

“Oh, yeah. That's the face.”

“What face?”

“The one where your eyebrows do spreadsheets.”

“My eyebrows do not do spreadsheets.”

“They're doing pivot tables right now.”

Steve zipped the purse closed.

“I was merely observing that we're running out of room.”

Nobody said anything.

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“What?”

Brittney smiled.

It wasn't a friendly smile.

It was the smile of a woman who had just found ammunition and intended to conserve it until maximum yield.

“Nothing, sweetheart.”

“Oh, fuck off.”

“Love you too.”

---

St. Claire Medical Plaza was less glamorous than most of the district Steve had seen so far, and somehow that made him like it immediately.

It was a modest, purpose-built medical building rather than one of the renovated storefronts or older brick buildings that gave other parts of St. Claire their character. The lobby was clean and bright without trying to convince anyone that healthcare was a spa experience. Directories pointed toward the medical center, dental offices, counseling, and the pharmacy.

The pharmacy itself occupied a practical storefront off the main corridor.

No chandeliers.

No reclaimed wood.

No inspirational cursive telling Steve to *Live Laugh Heal*.

Shelves. Counters. Fluorescent lights. Medication. Toiletries.

Steve approved.

Behind the prescription counter, Odette Brennan looked up as the eight of them came in.

She was a woman Steve would have guessed was somewhere in middle age, with a compact build and the efficient bearing of someone accustomed to spending entire days on her feet. Her hair was neatly controlled away from her face, and her clothes were practical enough for work without looking careless. When she greeted them, her voice had the easy, measured cadence of someone who spent a lot of time explaining things to people who were sick, frightened, impatient, or all three.

“Afternoon.”

A chorus came back.

Steve lifted a hand.

“Hi.”

Odette's eyes flicked briefly across the group, landed on the green purse at Steve's side for no longer than anything else, and moved on.

“Let me know if you need anything.”

That was it.

Steve followed the others deeper into the store.

Nobody stared.

Nobody commented.

Nobody appeared remotely interested in the fact that Steve was wearing red lipstick and carrying a green purse.

Which was good.

Obviously.

Why would anybody care about a purse?

Steve opened it again.

Emily caught him.

“Inventory face.”

“Shut up.”

---

“Okay,” Steve said. “Compact.”

He found the cosmetics aisle and stopped.

There were more compact mirrors than he had expected.

This annoyed him.

A compact mirror was a small mirror.

There should not have been enough product differentiation in *small reflective surface* to require this many decisions.

Steve picked one up.

Round. Thin. Plain.

He opened it.

Two mirrors.

He looked at the label.

“One is magnified.”

Chloe stood beside him. “Yes.”

“Why?”

“So you can see things closer.”

Steve slowly turned his head.

“Thank you, Chloe.”

“You asked.”

“I asked what the use case was.”

“Seeing things closer.”

Steve looked toward Brittney.

“Can I hit her?”

“No.”

“Can I?”

“No.”

Chloe looked pleased.

Steve went back to the compact.

He checked the hinge.

Closed it.

Opened it.

Closed it again.

Then picked up another.

Tori appeared on his other side.

“What are you doing?”

“Comparing hinges.”

Tori looked over her shoulder.

“Girls.”

“No.”

“Girls.”

“I said no.”

Six women converged anyway.

Steve held up the second compact.

“This one has a better hinge.”

Liz blinked. “It's a mirror.”

“It's an object I'm going to carry around every day. Why would I buy the one with the shitty hinge?”

There was a small silence.

Nat pointed at him.

“That.”

Steve looked at her. “What?”

“That thing you just did.”

“What thing?”

Brittney folded her arms.

“You've owned a purse for about forty-five minutes and you're already evaluating component durability.”

“Because apparently I'm going to put a mirror in it.”

“A compact.”

“A mirror with a hinge.”

“A compact.”

“A hinged mirror.”

Liz made a tiny sound of suffering.

“Mija.”

Steve grinned.

Then he bought the one with the better hinge.

Because obviously.

---

The problem began three aisles later.

Steve had acquired the compact.

As far as he was concerned, the mission was complete.

Then Emily picked up a tiny packet.

“Those are useful.”

Steve looked.

Travel tissues.

“I have tissues.”

“You'll run out.”

“I have a purse, not a fallout shelter.”

Maddie gasped.

“Don't say that in front of the purse.”

Steve kept walking.

Nat stopped beside a rack of small plastic bottles.

“Hand lotion.”

“I have hands.”

“That is generally when lotion is indicated.”

“My hands are fine.”

“Winter.”

Steve paused.

Looked at his hands.

Looked at the small bottle.

Put it in the basket.

“Fine.”

Brittney's mouth twitched.

Steve pointed at her. “Don't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were going to.”

“I was thinking lovingly.”

“You don't think lovingly.”

“I think lovingly all the fuckin' time.”

“Name once.”

“Right now. I'm thinking, lovingly, that you need travel deodorant.”

Steve stopped.

“Why would I need deodorant in my purse?”

Seven women began talking.

At once.

Steve stood in the center of it.

“No—stop—one person.”

Nobody stopped.

“ONE.”

Silence.

Steve pointed at Emily.

“You. Apparently you're the least insane.”

Emily looked deeply touched.

“Thank you.”

“That wasn't a compliment.”

“I'll take it.”

She gestured toward the basket.

“The point isn't that you need every possible thing every day. It's that there are a handful of small things that are incredibly useful when you unexpectedly need them.”

Steve looked down at the basket.

Compact.

Lotion.

He looked at his purse.

Then at the shelves.

Something changed behind his eyes.

Chloe saw it first.

“Oh no.”

Steve turned toward the travel-size section.

Nat followed his gaze.

“Oh, shit.”

Brittney looked between them. “What?”

Chloe spoke very quietly.

“He understands the assignment.”

---

Steve took everything out of the green purse.

Right there.

On an empty section of counter near the end of an aisle.

Wallet.

Phone.

Keys.

Lipstick.

Tissues.

Mints.

Pain reliever.

Blister bandages.

Power bank.

Cable.

Pens.

Safety pins.

Nail file.

Comb.

Stain pen.

Sanitizer.

Wet wipes.

Emergency twenty.

Seven women watched.

Steve arranged the items into little groups.

“What are you doing?” Liz asked.

“Fixing this.”

“There is nothing wrong with it.”

Steve looked at her.

“Liz.”

She looked at the objects.

Then at him.

“What?”

“The stain pen and the sanitizer were loose next to my lipstick.”

“So?”

“Liquids.”

“The lipstick isn't—”

“Potentially messy things shouldn't be loose with things that go on my face.”

Chloe's eyebrows went up.

Steve continued.

“Also, the power cable tangles with everything. The safety pins are stupid loose. The pain reliever needs a smaller container. I don't need an entire packet of blister bandages, I need two. The tissues are taking up too much room for the number of tissues.”

Maddie whispered, “He has become purse.”

“I have not become purse.”

“He can hear us.”

“He's right there.”

Steve ignored them.

“What I need is modular containment.”

Nat closed her eyes.

“Oh, he's going to buy pouches.”

Steve turned.

“Pouches?”

Seven faces changed.

Steve saw it.

“No.”

Nobody moved.

“What are pouches?”

Brittney put both hands over her face.

“Oh, Jesus Christ.”

“What?”

Tori pointed toward another aisle.

Steve followed her finger.

Small zippered cosmetic bags.

Clear organizers.

Travel containers.

Little cases.

Steve walked toward them.

The seven women followed at a respectful distance, the way people followed wildlife when they weren't sure whether it was dangerous.

Steve picked up a small zippered pouch.

Opened it.

Looked inside.

Looked at his purse.

Looked back at the pouch.

“This would keep the stuff I don't need constantly together.”

Nobody answered.

“And then the things I use most often stay loose in the main compartment.”

Silence.

Steve picked up a second, smaller pouch.

“This could be medical.”

Emily made a strangled noise.

Steve looked at her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

He found a tiny hard case.

“Safety pins.”

Tori turned away.

Her shoulders were shaking.

“Oh, fuck all of you.”

Brittney leaned against the shelving, openly laughing now.

“You're reorganizing your purse.”

“Yes, because you people packed it like raccoons.”

Liz slapped a hand to her chest.

“Excuse me?”

“You heard me.”

“I gave you twenty dollars!”

“And it is currently folded between wet wipes and a comb.”

“Emergency money!”

“Emergency money deserves a designated location!”

That finished Nat.

She doubled over against Chloe.

Steve glared at all of them.

Then bought the pouches.

---

It got worse.

Or better.

Steve wasn't sure.

Travel-size products were, he discovered, frequently terrible value.

“This is robbery.”

“You're buying thirty milliliters of lotion,” Chloe said.

“I am buying thirty milliliters of lotion for almost half the price of the full bottle.”

“Convenience.”

“Extortion.”

“Femininity.”

“That is not an economic model.”

Chloe shrugged. “Give it time.”

Steve began calculating which products made sense to buy small and which should be bought full-size and decanted into reusable containers.

Nat watched him compare unit prices.

“You know we're going purse shopping after this, right?”

“Yes.”

“You've been in the pharmacy for forty minutes.”

“Because nobody told me your entire system was economically irrational.”

“Our system?”

“This.” Steve waved toward the travel section. “Tiny products. Disposable packaging. Duplicate purchases. No standardization.”

Brittney peered into his basket.

“You bought three little bottles.”

“Reusable.”

“Of course they are.”

“I can refill them.”

“Of course you can.”

Steve looked suspiciously at her.

“Why are you saying it like that?”

“Like what?”

“Like you're humoring me.”

Brittney stepped closer, reached up, and very gently wiped a tiny smudge of red from the corner of his mouth with her thumb.

The gesture was so automatic that Steve went still for half a second.

“There,” she said.

Then she smiled.

“Better.”

Steve touched his lip.

“Did I mess it up?”

“Barely.”

He immediately reached for his new compact.

Brittney's smile widened.

Steve opened it, checked his lipstick, fixed the edge with one careful fingertip, and snapped the compact shut.

Then he caught her expression.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Britt.”

“Not a goddamn thing.”

She kissed the top of his head as she passed.

Steve stared after her.

Then put the compact into the easiest-to-reach section of the purse.

Because obviously that was where it belonged.

---

At checkout, Odette looked at the collection Steve had assembled.

Compact.

Reusable travel bottles.

Hand lotion.

A tiny packet of tissues, despite Steve's objections, because Maddie had declared the existing tissues “emotionally temporary.”

A small pill container.

Two zippered organizer pouches.

A hard little case for the safety pins.

A few other practical odds and ends Steve had personally determined passed the utility test.

Odette rang them up without comment.

Steve packed everything himself.

That part mattered.

Medical items together.

Potentially messy items together.

Frequently accessed items immediately reachable.

Power cable secured instead of roaming loose like an electrical vine.

Emergency cash in a designated interior pocket.

Lipstick and compact together.

Keys where he could find them without excavating.

Phone accessible.

He zipped the purse closed.

Opened it.

Retrieved the compact.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

Retrieved his wallet.

Closed it.

Steve nodded.

“Better.”

Odette glanced at the purse.

“New?”

Steve looked at her.

“The purse?”

She nodded.

“Yeah.”

“How are you liking it?”

Steve considered the question seriously.

“Conceptually excellent. This one's too small.”

Behind him came a noise that sounded like seven women trying not to die.

Odette's mouth twitched.

“Well,” she said, handing him the receipt, “sounds like you know what your next problem is.”

“Oh, we're fixing that now.”

Brittney made a choking sound.

Steve turned.

“What?”

She shook her head.

“Nothing, sweetheart.”

“You keep saying nothing when there is very clearly something.”

“Come on.”

She pushed away from the counter.

“Let's go buy you a purse.”

Steve slung the green one over his shoulder.

“Another purse.”

The group stopped.

Steve stopped.

He looked around at them.

“What?”

Chloe's face had gone completely blank.

“You said another purse.”

“Yes.”

“Not a bigger purse.”

Steve frowned.

“I already have this one.”

Seven women stared at him.

Steve looked down at the green purse.

Then back at them.

“What am I supposed to do, throw it away?”

Nobody answered.

“It's a perfectly good purse.”

Brittney made a small, helpless gesture toward him.

Steve sighed.

“You're all being weird again.”

He headed for the door.

Behind him, Maddie whispered, much too loudly:

“He has multiple-purse logic.”

“I can hear you.”

“I know.”

Steve pushed through the pharmacy door and stepped back into the corridor.

The green purse settled against his hip.

Everything inside it was exactly where it belonged.

He liked that.

Quite a lot, actually.

And now that the practical problem was solved, Steve was ready to find something bigger.

Something better made.

Something that actually suited him.

He had absolutely no idea yet that **better** was about to acquire several extremely expensive new definitions.
