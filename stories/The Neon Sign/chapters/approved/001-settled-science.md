# Chapter One — Settled Science

Steve knew something had gone badly wrong when the beautiful Latina opened the front door, looked him up and down, and shouted over her shoulder:

“Liz! Your stray is here!”

Steve stood on the porch holding a bottle of wine and reconsidered every decision that had brought him to this moment.

“I’m sorry,” he said. “Your what?”

The woman grinned.

She was maybe five-four, dark hair tumbling around her shoulders, wearing jeans, bare feet, and a fitted black top. She had the sort of smile that suggested trouble wasn't something that happened to her. It was something she organized.

“You're Steve.”

“Yes.”

“Then you're the stray.”

“I don't think that's how names work.”

“You're funny, too.” She stepped aside. “Come in.”

Steve didn't move.

He'd endured enough bullshit in his life to develop a perfectly functional internal alarm system, and right now it was blinking yellow.

Not red.

Red meant leave.

Yellow meant *pay attention*.

“You are?”

“Britt.”

“The person who invited me was Liz.”

“Liz lives here.”

“Good.”

“So do I.”

“Oh.”

“And Chloe.”

Steve paused.

“And Nat.”

Another pause.

“And Maddie.”

“Of course.”

“And Tori.”

“Why wouldn't she?”

“And Emily.”

Steve stared at her.

Britt's grin widened.

“Problem?”

“No. I'm just wondering whether I've accidentally arrived at a sorority.”

“God, no. We're lesbians.”

That wasn't remotely the answer he'd expected.

“Okay.”

“Mostly.”

“That's somehow less helpful.”

Britt laughed and reached for the wine.

Steve didn't release it.

She raised an eyebrow.

“I brought that.”

“For dinner?”

“Yes.”

“Which is inside.”

“Yes.”

“So give it to me.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“I've known you for forty seconds.”

Something flickered across her face.

Not offense.

Approval.

“Fair.”

She stepped back.

Steve walked inside carrying his own wine.

The house was warm and loud and smelled like garlic.

Somewhere deeper inside it, several women were arguing.

“—absolutely not what she said.”

“It is literally what she said.”

“No, what she said was—”

A third voice cut through them.

“Can everyone shut the fuck up? Britt's stray is here.”

Steve stopped.

Britt looked delighted.

“I told them about you.”

“You apparently told the entire lesbian telephone tree about me.”

“Oh, honey.”

She patted his shoulder.

“This isn't the entire telephone tree.”

Then she walked away.

Steve considered the front door.

He could still leave.

He wasn't socially helpless. He wasn't frightened of women. He wasn't even particularly uncomfortable being the only man in a room full of lesbians, assuming that was what he'd just walked into.

But Steve had learned something important a long time ago:

When a situation felt wrong, he didn't owe anybody politeness at his own expense.

He could leave.

He could always leave.

That knowledge had cost him enough to learn that he wasn't surrendering it now.

Then a woman came around the corner carrying an enormous bowl of salad.

She saw him.

Stopped.

And smiled.

“Oh.”

Steve felt something peculiar happen somewhere behind his ribs.

She was gorgeous.

Not movie-star gorgeous.

Worse.

Real-person gorgeous.

Dark hair. Warm brown eyes. An apron over a red blouse. A little flour on one cheek.

She looked at Steve as though finding him standing in her hallway was somehow the best thing that had happened all afternoon.

“You came.”

“You're Liz?”

“I am.”

“Then yes.”

Her smile became ridiculous.

“Come here.”

She shifted the salad to one arm and hugged him.

Steve froze for perhaps half a second.

Then hugged her back.

And that was mistake number one.

Because she smelled good.

Because she was warm.

Because her cheek touched his.

Because Steve had spent an embarrassingly large percentage of his adult life pretending affection wasn't something he craved with almost physical intensity.

Liz pulled back.

“I'm so glad you're here.”

Nobody said things like that to Steve.

Not like they meant them.

Something inside him moved.

Dangerous.

Yellow light.

Pay attention.

“Thanks for inviting me.”

“Are you kidding? After listening to Britt talk about you for two weeks, we had to meet you.”

Steve looked toward the kitchen.

“Britt told me she lives here.”

“She does.”

“With several other women.”

Liz's mouth twitched.

“She gave you the roster?”

“She made it sound like roll call.”

“That tracks.”

Steve hesitated.

“Are you all…”

He made a vague gesture.

Liz waited.

Steve tried again.

“Together?”

Her smile softened.

“Mostly.”

“That was Britt's answer.”

“It's complicated.”

“I gathered.”

“Does that bother you?”

“No.”

The answer came easily because it was true.

Liz studied him for another second.

“Good.”

Then she took his hand.

Steve looked down at it.

Liz apparently saw nothing unusual about holding the hand of a man she'd met approximately ninety seconds ago.

Steve discovered he didn't particularly object.

That was mistake number two.

“Come meet everybody.”

She pulled him into the kitchen.

Steve followed.

And stopped dead.

There were women everywhere.

One was perched on the enormous island drinking wine. Another stood at the stove. A blonde was sitting cross-legged on the counter despite there being perfectly functional chairs approximately six feet away. An older woman was slicing bread with the focused aggression of somebody imagining the loaf had personally offended her.

Conversation stopped.

Every head turned.

Steve experienced the uncomfortable sensation of becoming an exhibit.

The woman on the island looked him over.

Not casually.

She started at his shoes and worked upward.

Steve waited.

She reached his face.

“Huh.”

Steve raised an eyebrow.

“Huh what?”

She tilted her head.

“Nothing.”

“That's clearly bullshit.”

A smile appeared.

“Oh, I like him.”

“Her,” said the blonde.

Steve turned.

“What?”

The blonde blinked.

“What?”

“You said her.”

“I did?”

“Yes.”

“Oh.”

She shrugged.

“Sorry.”

Steve relaxed.

“It's fine.”

The woman on the island was still staring at him.

Steve looked directly at her.

“Can I help you?”

“Probably.”

“Chloe,” Liz warned.

So.

Chloe.

Steve filed that away.

Chloe took another sip of wine.

“You have fantastic eyelashes.”

Steve stared at her.

“Thank you?”

“It's irritating.”

“I'll apologize to my parents.”

The blonde burst out laughing.

Chloe's mouth twitched.

Liz squeezed Steve's hand.

He'd forgotten she was still holding it.

He should probably take his hand back.

He didn't.

Introductions happened rapidly.

Nat was the blonde on the counter.

Maddie was at the stove and hugged Steve before anybody could stop her.

Tori was murdering the bread.

Emily arrived halfway through introductions, kissed Liz, kissed Britt, stole something from Maddie's cutting board, got slapped on the hand, and then greeted Steve as though none of this required explanation.

Steve's internal alarm system remained yellow.

But something else was happening.

He liked them.

That was inconvenient.

He liked Britt's shameless teasing.

He liked Chloe's dry hostility, which wasn't actually hostility at all.

He liked Maddie, who seemed constitutionally incapable of keeping any thought inside her skull.

He liked Nat's laugh.

He liked the way Emily automatically leaned against Tori.

And Liz—

Steve avoided examining that one too closely.

Dinner should have been awkward.

It wasn't.

That was mistake number three.

Within twenty minutes Steve had somehow become involved in an argument about whether Die Hard was a Christmas movie.

Within thirty, Chloe had called one of his opinions “intellectually indefensible.”

Within thirty-one, Steve had told Chloe exactly where she could put her intellectually defensible opinion.

Nat nearly choked on her wine.

Chloe stared at him.

Steve stared back.

Then Chloe smiled.

Not the little sarcastic twitch from earlier.

A real smile.

“Okay,” she said.

“Okay what?”

“You can stay.”

Steve looked around.

“I didn't realize my residency was under consideration.”

“It was.”

“Chloe,” Liz said.

“What? He passed.”

“She,” Maddie corrected absently.

Steve looked at her.

Maddie was spooning potatoes onto her plate.

“Maddie.”

She looked up.

“What?”

“You just called me she.”

Seven women went quiet.

Steve looked around the table.

“Oh, for God's sake. It's not a federal investigation. She misspoke.”

Nobody said anything.

Steve's eyes narrowed.

“Why is everybody looking at me?”

Britt slowly put down her fork.

“Okay.”

“No. That tone means absolutely nothing good has ever followed the word okay.”

Nat was biting her lip.

Emily looked fascinated.

Tori was openly studying him.

Steve looked at Liz.

Liz had the decency to appear conflicted.

“Liz.”

“Yes?”

“What?”

She glanced around the table.

“Oh, don't make me do this.”

“Do what?”

“Tell him,” Chloe said.

“Her,” Maddie said again.

“Maddie!”

“What? She is!”

Steve stared at her.

Maddie stared back.

There was no mockery in her face.

That was what stopped him.

Steve had been mocked before.

Plenty.

He knew what it looked like.

This wasn't it.

“You think I'm a woman?”

Maddie's nose wrinkled.

“No.”

Steve relaxed.

Then she continued.

“I think you're a girl.”

Steve's fork slowly descended to his plate.

“What the fuck does that mean?”

“I don't know.”

“Maddie!”

“I don't! Look at her!”

Steve looked at Liz.

“Please tell me somebody put something in the wine.”

Liz sighed.

“Okay.”

“There it is again.”

She turned toward him.

“You're obviously male.”

“Thank you.”

“Nobody is disputing that.”

“Excellent.”

“But…”

Steve closed his eyes.

“There should not be a but.”

“There really is,” Nat said.

Steve opened his eyes.

Nat gestured vaguely toward him.

“All of this.”

Steve looked down.

He was wearing khakis and a blue button-down shirt.

“What about it?”

“No, not the clothes,” Britt said. “Those are terrible.”

“My clothes are not terrible.”

Seven expressions suggested otherwise.

“Fuck all of you.”

Chloe smiled into her wine.

“See?”

“SEE WHAT?”

“That.”

Steve stared.

Emily leaned toward Tori.

“You see it too, right?”

“Immediately.”

“Would everyone stop seeing things?”

Liz touched his wrist.

Steve looked at her.

Her expression was gentle.

“Are you actually upset?”

There it was.

The question beneath the joke.

Steve checked himself.

Really checked.

Was he?

He'd had people tell him who he was before.

He'd had people decide what he ought to be.

He'd had people try to fit him into boxes because their comfort mattered more to them than his truth.

He knew the anger that caused.

This wasn't it.

This was absurd.

Confusing.

Maybe even insulting.

But looking around the table, Steve couldn't find cruelty anywhere.

Mostly he found affection from people who had known him for less than two hours and somehow behaved as though he'd been expected.

“No,” he admitted.

Liz nodded.

“If you become upset, we stop.”

Something in Steve's chest loosened.

“Okay.”

“Okay.”

He looked around the table.

“Now explain the collective hallucination.”

Seven women immediately began talking.

“Oh, Jesus Christ.”

“You're pretty,” Maddie said over everyone else.

Steve stopped.

Maddie continued as if she'd said the sky was blue.

“Like, really pretty.”

“I'm not—”

“Don't,” Britt said.

Steve turned toward her.

“Don't what?”

“Do that thing where somebody compliments you and you explain why they're factually incorrect.”

Steve opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Damn.

Nat pointed at him.

“Also that.”

“What?”

“You're doing girl.”

“THAT IS NOT A VERB.”

“It is tonight.”

Steve looked desperately at Liz.

“You're the reasonable one.”

The entire table erupted in laughter.

Liz looked offended.

“I am!”

Chloe nearly spilled her wine.

Steve put both hands over his face.

“This was a mistake.”

A hand rubbed his back.

Maddie.

Of course.

“You're okay.”

“I met you people two hours ago.”

“And you're doing great.”

Steve lowered his hands.

“This is insane.”

“Probably,” Liz admitted.

Steve looked at her.

She smiled.

His heart did the stupid thing again.

Goddammit.

Then Liz reached over and pushed a lock of hair away from his forehead.

The gesture was so casual Steve almost missed its intimacy.

Her fingers lingered for half a second.

“You would look beautiful with longer hair.”

Something happened.

Tiny.

Immediate.

Steve pictured it.

He didn't mean to.

Longer hair.

Not merely shaggy.

Long.

He saw it for one strange instant.

Then he shoved the image away.

“No.”

Liz's hand disappeared instantly.

“Okay.”

No disappointment.

No persuasion.

Just okay.

Steve noticed.

He looked around the table.

“You'd really stop?”

Britt frowned.

“Of course.”

“All of this.”

“If you wanted.”

“Even the…” He gestured vaguely. “Girl thing.”

Chloe answered.

“We can stop saying it.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

Chloe considered him.

“No,” she said finally. “I don't think I'd stop thinking it.”

Steve appreciated the answer more than he wanted to.

“But it's your life,” she added. “Not ours.”

That landed somewhere deep.

Steve looked down at his plate.

“Okay.”

Silence lasted three seconds.

Then Maddie said, “Can I still think you're pretty?”

Steve laughed despite himself.

“Yes, Maddie.”

“Good.”

Dinner resumed.

For approximately eleven minutes.

Then Steve caught Britt and Nat whispering.

He pointed his fork at them.

“No.”

“We haven't done anything.”

“You're planning something.”

“We are not.”

“You absolutely are.”

Britt looked at Nat.

Nat looked at Britt.

Britt sighed.

“Fine.”

She stood.

“No.”

“You don't even know what I'm doing.”

“I've known you long enough.”

“You've known me two hours.”

“Exactly.”

Britt disappeared from the kitchen.

Steve looked at Liz.

“What is she doing?”

“I genuinely don't know.”

“That wasn't reassuring.”

Britt returned carrying a garment bag.

Steve stared at it.

“No.”

“You still don't know what's inside.”

“I know enough.”

Britt hung it from the pantry door.

Steve folded his arms.

“What is it?”

“Nothing.”

“Bullshit.”

“Something I bought that didn't fit.”

Steve's eyes narrowed.

“And?”

“And nothing.”

“Britt.”

She unzipped the bag.

Green.

Deep emerald green.

Steve's brain stopped.

The dress wasn't ridiculous.

That would have helped.

It wasn't covered in lace or bows or anything he could dismiss as costume.

It was elegant.

Sleek.

Soft.

Feminine without being delicate.

Steve hated it immediately.

Because he loved it.

Nobody spoke.

Steve looked at the dress.

Then at Liz.

Then back at the dress.

“No.”

“Okay,” Britt said.

She started zipping the bag.

“Wait.”

Every woman at the table became unnaturally still.

Steve glared at all of them.

“I saw that.”

“Saw what?” Chloe asked.

“The prairie dogs.”

“What prairie dogs?”

“All your heads popped up.”

Maddie giggled.

Steve looked back at the dress.

“What size is it?”

Britt told him.

Steve frowned.

“That might actually fit me.”

“Probably.”

“You planned this.”

“I swear I didn't.”

Steve believed her.

Which was worse.

He stood.

Nobody moved.

“This means nothing.”

“Obviously,” Chloe said.

“Nothing.”

“Completely meaningless.”

“And nobody says anything.”

“Wouldn't dream of it.”

Steve took the garment bag.

“Where?”

Liz pointed down the hall.

“Second door.”

Steve walked away.

He could feel seven pairs of eyes following him.

He stopped.

Turned around.

They all abruptly looked elsewhere.

“You're terrible at this.”

Then he disappeared into the bathroom.

The dress fit.

Of course it fucking fit.

Steve stood in front of the mirror and stared at himself.

He had expected comedy.

There was none.

He had expected humiliation.

None of that either.

Instead—

“Oh.”

The person in the mirror was still Steve.

Same face.

Same body.

Same stubborn bastard who had survived every ugly thing life had thrown at him.

But the dress did something.

His waist looked smaller.

His legs looked—

Jesus.

His shoulders didn't ruin it.

He'd assumed they would.

The neckline—

Steve touched the fabric at his chest.

Something moved inside him.

Not revelation.

Not *I am a woman.*

Nothing so convenient.

Just:

*Oh.*

Steve stayed there too long.

Eventually somebody knocked.

“Steve?”

Liz.

“Yeah.”

“You okay?”

Steve looked at himself again.

“Yeah.”

“Want me to come in?”

He hesitated.

“Yes.”

The door opened slowly.

Liz stepped inside.

And froze.

Steve immediately folded his arms.

“Don't.”

Her eyes snapped to his.

“Don't what?”

“Whatever that face means.”

Liz closed the door behind her.

“What do you think it means?”

“I don't know.”

She walked closer.

Steve suddenly couldn't look at her.

That annoyed him.

Steve looked people in the eye.

He'd learned to.

He forced himself to meet hers.

Liz looked almost unbearably soft.

“You're beautiful.”

Three words.

That was all.

Steve had survived worse things than three words.

They nearly destroyed him anyway.

He swallowed.

“It's just a dress.”

“Yes.”

“It doesn't mean anything.”

“It doesn't have to.”

“I'm still me.”

Liz smiled.

“That's why you're beautiful.”

Fuck.

Steve looked away.

She didn't touch him.

Didn't crowd him.

Didn't tell him what any of this meant.

Steve realized she was waiting.

So he stepped toward her.

Liz opened her arms.

Steve went into them.

He didn't know these women.

That thought should have mattered more than it did.

Instead he stood in a bathroom wearing an emerald dress while a lesbian he'd met that evening held him, and something inside him that had been clenched for years loosened another fraction.

“This is fucking weird,” he muttered against her shoulder.

Liz laughed softly.

“Very.”

“I think your girlfriends are insane.”

“Definitely.”

“Chloe hates me.”

“Chloe likes you so much it's making her uncomfortable.”

Steve pulled back.

“Really?”

“Oh, yeah.”

For reasons he absolutely refused to examine, that pleased him enormously.

Liz noticed.

Of course she noticed.

She didn't say anything.

Steve looked at himself one more time.

Then smoothed the dress over his hips.

“Okay.”

“Okay what?”

“I'll show them.”

Liz's smile became radiant.

Steve pointed at her.

“Nobody makes a big deal.”

“I'll warn them.”

“And I'm changing afterward.”

“Absolutely.”

“And this establishes nothing.”

“Nothing whatsoever.”

Steve opened the door.

Liz followed.

They reached the kitchen.

Steve stepped around the corner.

Silence.

Complete.

Absolute.

Silence.

Steve's nerve evaporated.

“Oh, fuck. It's bad.”

Seven women moved at once.

“No!”

“Jesus Christ, no.”

“Holy shit.”

“Steve!”

Maddie actually clapped both hands over her mouth.

Nat stared openly.

Britt looked vindicated beyond reason.

Emily whispered something Steve couldn't hear.

Tori simply smiled.

And Chloe—

Chloe looked at Steve for a long moment.

Steve's heart started pounding.

He hated that Chloe's opinion mattered.

He hated that all of their opinions mattered.

He'd known them for one dinner.

This was ridiculous.

Finally Chloe lifted her wineglass.

“Well.”

Steve waited.

Chloe's eyes traveled over the dress once more before returning to his face.

“Peer review confirmed.”

Steve stared at her.

Then laughed.

He couldn't help it.

Something broke loose around the table.

Everyone started talking.

Steve held up both hands.

“No. Absolutely not. Nobody gets to claim victory.”

“Too late,” Nat said.

“There was no competition!”

“Science isn't a competition.”

“Stop calling this science!”

Maddie appeared beside him.

“Can I hug you?”

The question caught Steve by surprise.

He looked at her.

“Yes.”

She hugged him.

Hard.

Steve hugged her back.

Then Britt joined them.

Then Nat.

“Guys.”

Emily piled in.

“Oh, come on.”

Tori joined.

Steve disappeared into women.

He was laughing now.

Actually laughing.

“Liz! Help!”

“Nope.”

“You're responsible for this!”

“Probably.”

Someone kissed his cheek.

Steve couldn't tell who.

He didn't care.

And that realization should have terrified him.

Instead—

Home.

The word appeared without permission.

Steve went still.

The women noticed.

The pile loosened immediately.

Liz was watching him.

“You okay?”

Steve looked around.

At Maddie's ridiculous worried face.

At Britt.

Nat.

Emily.

Tori.

At Chloe pretending she wasn't concerned.

At Liz.

These women were insane.

They were intrusive and loud and entirely too affectionate.

They had informed him, within hours of meeting him, that they considered him a girl.

Steve should leave.

He knew how.

He'd done it before.

When people wanted him to become smaller, quieter, easier, more convenient, Steve left.

When someone crossed the line, Steve could find the door.

There was one twenty feet behind him.

He looked at it.

Then back at them.

He didn't want the door.

That frightened him more than anything else had tonight.

So naturally Steve covered it with sarcasm.

“I want it formally recorded that I reject your methodology.”

Chloe nodded solemnly.

“Duly noted.”

“And your conclusions.”

“Of course.”

“And I'm changing out of this dress.”

Britt frowned.

“Why?”

“Because I'm a man.”

“Sure.”

Steve pointed at her.

“That sounded patronizing.”

“It wasn't.”

“Good.”

He started toward the hallway.

“Steve?” Liz called.

He turned.

She smiled.

“Dinner next Friday?”

The answer came so quickly it embarrassed him.

“Yes.”

Seven women smiled.

Steve's stomach flipped.

Oh, he was in trouble.

Serious trouble.

Because Steve didn't do anything halfway.

Never had.

If he fought, he fought.

If he protected somebody, he protected them.

If he loved—

No.

Absolutely not.

He had known these women for one evening.

Steve continued toward the bathroom.

“Steve,” Maddie called.

He stopped again.

“What?”

“Keep the dress.”

Steve looked down at it.

He should say no.

Instead he ran his fingers over the green fabric.

“Fine.”

Maddie beamed.

“But I'm not wearing it next Friday.”

“Of course not.”

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Maddie.”

She was visibly vibrating.

“What?”

“What are you not telling me?”

“Nothing!”

Steve looked around the kitchen.

Everyone suddenly found something fascinating to examine.

“Oh, for fuck's sake.”

Chloe took a leisurely sip of wine.

Steve pointed at her.

“Whatever you're planning, no.”

“We aren't planning anything.”

“You're lying.”

“Yes.”

“Chloe!”

She smiled.

Steve tried to glare.

Unfortunately, he was still wearing the dress.

And surrounded by women he already liked far too much.

And Liz was looking at him like he was something precious.

Steve felt himself blush.

Chloe saw it.

Her smile became positively evil.

“Next Friday,” she said.

Steve should have run.

Instead he smiled back.

“Next Friday.”

The freight train left the station.
