# The Neon Sign — Series Bible

## Story identity

Title: **The Neon Sign**
Mode: private fiction
Main character: **Steve**
Primary setting: **St. Claire**
Opening narrative identity/presentation: Steve is male-presenting.
POV/tense: Not yet established in this repository record.

This story is not **A Life in Plain Sight** and is not the separate Jim/Claire story.

## Premise

Steve enters the orbit of Brittney and a seven-woman lesbian polycule in St. Claire. What begins with Steve walking into Blush to prove Brittney wrong and Britt bringing her “stray” home grows into friendship, attraction, family-like belonging, increasingly feminine choices, and a changing relationship to gender that Steve does not yet fully understand.

The women do not function as a makeover committee or a mechanism for taking Steve's agency. Steve's choices matter. The story repeatedly lets Steve choose, discover preferences, and surprise both the women and himself.

## Relationship engine

The polycule is boisterous and lovingly dysfunctional rather than bureaucratic. Steve meshes almost too naturally with them because Steve's own family already speaks a similar love-language: teasing, argument, protection, noise, loyalty, and affection.

The developing attraction is not reduced to a single group decision. Individual bonds matter. “Not dates” and independent friendships allow Steve and individual women to discover whether the chemistry means something. Pre/post texting and group-chat spillover are part of that social texture.

## Gender and presentation arc

Steve begins male-presenting. Feminine presentation develops through Steve's own participation and choices rather than partner-imposed forced feminization. Established progression includes Steve independently choosing a green dress, later accepting/allowing feminine pronoun movement, wearing fresh red lipstick, carrying and embracing a green purse, and deciding a better purse is worth shopping for.

Do not treat every Steve trait as a gender clue. In particular, the deliberately “anti-male” moments on individual not-dates are Steve's personality and values, not evidence of gender.

## Governing shared world

St. Claire shared-world canon applies. Story-local events instantiate the district without rewriting its reusable baseline. Established St. Claire businesses, places, residents, staffing, schedules, culture, and geography must be resolved from the live module before prose rather than replaced by generic inventions.
