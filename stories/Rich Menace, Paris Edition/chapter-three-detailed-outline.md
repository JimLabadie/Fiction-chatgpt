## Chapter job
Turn the Chapter Two U-Haul joke into lived reality without skipping the part that makes it funny and romantic. The chapter begins with two people who met that morning rationalizing cohabitation as quality assurance. By the end, a person who knows Allison well should be able to walk into the house, hear the absurd timeline, and nevertheless see that the supposedly fake relationship is already producing real intimacy.

The chapter must dramatize the central mechanism rather than summarize it: to convincingly fake knowing each other intimately, Chloe and Allison have to actually learn each other intimately.

---

## Scene 1 — From U-Haul joke to Allison's front door
**Time:** same evening, immediately after Chapter Two.
**Continuity:** no “next morning” opening and no montage.

### Action
- Leave Bistro Regency from the exact approved Chapter Two endpoint.
- Maison Belle bags and the nightgown already exist and travel with them.
- The absurdity of “you can move in” becomes practical during the ride/arrival: Allison has just invited someone she met that morning into her home.
- Allison maintains the cool CEO/logistics fiction that this is efficient training. Chloe recognizes how delighted Allison actually is without forcing her to admit it.
- Arrival gives Chloe her first real look at Allison's private home.

### House evidence
- Three-bedroom, three-bath Colonial in Regency Hill.
- White picket fence.
- Swing set and doghouse remain from the prior family.
- The house feels like a home rather than an heiress showroom.
- Chloe can tease Allison about the visual evidence without declaring an omniscient diagnosis. Allison's existing desire for marriage/children/family is canon, but Chloe's immediate interpretation should remain Chloe's inference.

### Relationship function
- The joke changes from “U-Haul stereotype” to “we are literally standing at your house with my overnight things.”
- Chloe is not yet fully moved in; this is the first night created by the agreement, not a skipped logistics solution.

## Scene 2 — The name problem becomes unavoidable
**Location:** inside Allison's house, same evening.

### Trigger
- They start talking through how 24/7 training is supposed to work.
- A practical contradiction surfaces: Allison's supposed girlfriend cannot plausibly move through ordinary life as “Tim” while being presented socially as Allison's girlfriend.

### Established resolution
- They discuss the problem rather than narration silently renaming the protagonist.
- They settle on **Chloe Johnson**.
- From the resolution forward the chapter's ordinary narration/social reference switches to **Chloe/she-her**.
- Tim remains real history, not a person Chloe repudiates.

### Tone
- Pragmatic, quick, intimate, lightly absurd.
- This is not written as a grand identity revelation.
- Chloe likes femininity and is happy living as Chloe; the fact that “Chloe” fits should be allowed to feel good without the story turning into an issues novel.

### Immediate dependency
- Chloe realizes work cannot remain administratively on a different social name/pronoun track.
- She sends HR a short practical update that she goes by Chloe and uses she/her.
- Exact email wording is still open; outline requirement is the action and Chloe's matter-of-fact attitude.
- Allison may be more stunned by the scale/speed of what just happened than Chloe is, which supports comedy without making Chloe's identity the joke.

## Scene 3 — What does “living here” actually mean?
**Location:** Allison's house, same evening.

### Practical discussion
- They confront the difference between “staying over for training” and actually creating convincing domestic life.
- Surprise visitors are possible. A fake girlfriend with a guest toothbrush and isolated suitcase will look like a fake girlfriend.
- Therefore Chloe is not assigned a separate fake-girlfriend bathroom/closet/domestic system.
- Her things are to be integrated into Allison's real daily space.
- Exact shelves, drawers, closet geometry, and room assignment remain implementation details to resolve only where blocking requires them.

### Relationship Boot Camp emerges
The training problem broadens naturally:
- Presentation alone will not convince Julie or anyone who knows Allison.
- They need to know each other's coffee, work, friends, family, routines, preferences, habits, history, likes/dislikes.
- This becomes **Relationship Boot Camp**.

### Sapphic/social fluency emerges
- Chloe also needs enough social/cultural fluency to move naturally through Allison's girlfriend world.
- This becomes the sapphic/lesbian social-fluency layer.
- It must not become stereotype drills and must not resolve Allison's still-open exact sexuality label.

### Each other's worlds
- Rehearsed trivia is insufficient. They need exposure to the lives the other already has.
- Reciprocal-world-learning is established.
- “Each Other Boot Camp” can be used only if Jim likes the label; the mechanism is what matters.

## Scene 4 — Sleeping arrangements: Allison's act of heroic self-sacrifice
**Location:** Allison's house, later the same evening.

### Trigger
- The practical discussion reaches nighttime.
- They cannot simply cut away because ordinary nighttime behavior is part of the 24/7 premise.

### Established beat
- Sleeping arrangements are explicitly discussed.
- Sex is several chapters away.
- Sharing a bed is still intimate and useful relationship/couple practice.
- Allison “selflessly” proposes that they sleep together.
- She should work comically hard to make this sound like rigorous quality assurance rather than the transparent desire to share a bed with the gorgeous woman she invited to move in.

### Chloe's side
- Chloe understands exactly what Allison is doing.
- Heavy flirting is welcome.
- Chloe can let Allison lead while making it clear she sees through the justification.
- The distinction between bed-sharing and sex remains clear without turning the scene solemn.

### Nightgown payoff continuation
- The nightgown already triggered the move-in argument in Chapter Two.
- Now it has an ordinary-life function: Chloe actually has to live in the feminine wardrobe when the public outfit comes off.
- Evening routine creates real learning opportunities when Chloe is tired and no longer consciously performing every movement.

## Scene 5 — First night: training stops being a fitting
**Time:** still the same night.

### Scene purpose
Do not summarize “they went to bed.” Show enough ordinary life for the boot-camp premise to become real.

### Material
- Removing the public-day presentation and changing for bed requires habits, organization, and choices Chloe has not automated.
- Allison coaches where she genuinely knows more.
- Chloe observes and learns rather than receiving instant expertise.
- Their proximity creates attraction and comic awareness.
- Relationship learning can happen in the quieter conversation that becomes possible once neither is performing for Maison Belle, the restaurant, Clara, Julie, or an imagined audience.
- Small facts about family, work, habits, and preferences can begin accumulating organically rather than as an interview questionnaire.
- Chloe's comfort letting Allison be competent should remain attractive to Allison.

### End beat
- They actually share the bed.
- Intimacy is the point; consummation is not.
- End the scene on the ridiculous fact that the first calendar day of knowing each other ends with them in the same bed because “quality assurance.”

## Scene 10 — Doorbell: Julie walks into the experiment
**Location:** Allison's house.
**Visitor:** Julie Smith.

### Why this happens after the preceding scenes
Julie must observe earned behavior. Putting her here allows the chapter to first create:
- shared space;
- one night/morning of ordinary routines;
- knowledge of each other's homes/lives;
- early domestic habits;
- real attraction and comfort.

### Truthful history
- Julie learns Chloe and Allison have only just met / barely know each other.
- They do not fabricate a long romance.
- They do not volunteer Chloe's private earlier history because Julie does not need it.

### Julie's first reaction
- Chloe is extraordinarily attractive.
- Julie can be openly impressed and tease Allison that she is punching above her weight.
- This is funny, but it is not Julie's important discovery.

### What Julie actually recognizes
Because Julie knows Allison:
- Allison is genuinely radiant/happy, not merely wearing a smug “look what I brought to Paris to annoy Clara” expression.
- Chloe and Allison orient toward each other naturally.
- They anticipate or make room for each other in small ways.
- Chloe behaves like someone inhabiting the home, not a visitor waiting for instructions.
- Chloe's domestic care is visible.
- Allison's grounding/protective attention to Chloe is visible.
- Use only micro-behaviors earned by earlier scenes; do not manufacture years of couple shorthand after one day.

### Julie's conclusion
- The timeline is absurd.
- The relationship nevertheless does not look emotionally fake.
- Julie demands that Chloe attend the Paris wedding as Allison's plus-one.
- This gives Chloe independent standing with the bride rather than making her merely Allison's anti-Clara accessory.

### U-Haul callback
- Julie explicitly recognizes the speed and invokes the U-Haul joke.
- The joke has evolved: Chapter Two meant “you met today and you're moving in”; Julie's version can carry “and somehow I don't think she's moving back out.”
- Exact wording remains open.

## Scene 11 — Chapter landing
**Purpose:** end on changed relationship reality, not a planning memo.

### What has changed during the chapter
At the start:
- cohabitation is a ridiculous training proposal;
- Chloe does not yet exist socially by name;
- they barely know each other's ordinary lives;
- “fake girlfriend” still sounds technically defensible.

By the end:
- Chloe has a name and ongoing social identity;
- work has been operationally updated;
- they have shared ordinary evening/night/morning life;
- Allison has entered Chloe's existing world;
- Chloe's belongings and care are entering Allison's home;
- Chloe has discovered Allison's family-home hopes and photography history;
- Clara's history has been encountered without destabilizing Chloe;
- Julie has independently observed genuine happiness/chemistry and personally wants Chloe at the wedding;
- the U-Haul joke now describes something increasingly real.

### Proposed final emotional/comic note for Jim to approve
End with Julie's U-Haul escalation or its immediate aftermath rather than skipping ahead to another day. The chapter should close with Chloe and Allison confronted by an outside witness who has just looked at their “fake” relationship and effectively called bullshit on the fake part.
