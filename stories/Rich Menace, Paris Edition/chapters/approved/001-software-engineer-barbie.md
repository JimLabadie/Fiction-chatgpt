# Chapter One — Software Engineer Barbie

By the time Tim Johnson decided his car was an asshole, the mechanic had already reached the same conclusion.

The difference was that the mechanic seemed happy about it.

Tim respected that.

Some people liked cars. Some people understood cars. Some people apparently enjoyed spending their afternoons elbow-deep in the mechanical entrails of a dead sedan while explaining things about timing chains with the reverence of a priest discussing transubstantiation.

Tim wrote software.

This was why civilization had specialists.

So Tim had handed over the keys, accepted the estimate with only a moderate amount of profanity, and was now standing at a bus stop in Regency Hill with his laptop backpack hanging from one shoulder, waiting for public transportation to deliver him home.

There were worse places to be stranded.

Regency Hill had the particular kind of money that didn’t need to put gold lions outside the houses to prove anything. The storefronts were clean without looking new, the sidewalks wide enough for people to walk three abreast without negotiating a treaty, and the trees looked suspiciously as if someone had been paid to make sure they dropped their leaves attractively.

Across the street, a restaurant was setting tables for dinner.

Beside Tim, the windows of Maison Belle displayed women’s clothing in the sort of artfully careless arrangement that probably required three people and a laser level.

Tim had not noticed any of that until his bus was late.

He checked his phone.

Still late.

“Fantastic.”

He shoved the phone back into his pocket.

Tim himself looked like someone who had put exactly six seconds of thought into getting dressed that morning and considered five of them wasted. Jeans. Plain T-shirt. Light jacket. Sneakers. Laptop backpack.

Done.

The clothes were almost aggressively unremarkable.

The man wearing them was not.

Long hair fell around a face that had spent thirty-five years being prettier than Tim had any particular use for. Fine features, high cheekbones, a lean frame, and the sort of accidental symmetry that made strangers occasionally look twice before figuring out what, exactly, had caught their attention.

Tim mostly used the face to get into his phone.

He checked the bus app again.

Still late.

“Your commitment to disappointment is impressive.”

Tim looked up.

And temporarily forgot about municipal transportation.

The woman standing several feet away was—

Well.

Fuck.

She was beautiful in a way that seemed professionally irresponsible.

Not cute. Not pretty. Not oh, she’s attractive.

Beautiful.

The kind of beautiful that made a man briefly wonder whether the universe had implemented character creation with wildly inconsistent point limits.

She was about his age, maybe a little younger, with the sculpted features and impossible composure of someone who could have stepped off a fashion campaign and immediately fired the photographer for wasting her time.

Her fitted skirt suit probably cost more than Tim’s current automotive emergency. The blouse beneath it was immaculate. Jewelry caught the fading afternoon light without screaming for attention. Her hair and makeup had the sort of precision Tim associated with people who knew what all the brushes at Sephora were actually for.

Then there were the heels.

Tim looked down.

He didn’t know shoes.

He knew enough to know those were not normal shoes.

They were spectacular, high, elegant things that made absolutely no concession to the possibility that their owner might someday need to run from a bear.

Tim looked back up.

The woman was staring at him.

Not glancing.

Staring.

Tim glanced behind himself.

Maison Belle.

He looked back at her.

She was still staring.

“Hi?”

Her eyes narrowed slightly.

Not hostile.

Evaluating.

Tim had seen that expression on senior engineers examining somebody else’s code.

He immediately distrusted it.

“Can I help you?”

“Possibly.”

That was not the answer he’d expected.

She stepped closer.

Tim noticed two things.

First, she was even more beautiful at conversational distance.

Second, she was looking at him with an intensity that had moved beyond ordinary attraction and into something dangerously close to project planning.

Tim shifted the backpack higher on his shoulder.

“Okay.”

“Are you waiting for someone?”

“The bus.”

Her gaze flicked toward the bus-stop sign.

Then back to him.

“The bus.”

“My car died.”

“I’m sorry.”

“It’s being repaired.”

“That isn’t what I meant.”

Tim stared at her.

The corner of her mouth moved.

Ah.

Sarcastic.

Excellent.

Tim liked her more already.

“The mechanic seemed happy,” he said.

“Perhaps he’s an optimist.”

“Or making boat payments.”

That got him the smile.

It changed her.

Not dramatically. She was still polished enough to make the entire block look underdressed, but something warmer slipped through the immaculate exterior.

Tim felt the first clean punch of attraction land somewhere behind his ribs.

Yep.

Definitely hot.

The woman looked at him again.

Hair.

Face.

Shoulders.

Waist.

Legs.

Back to his face.

There was absolutely no ambiguity about it now.

Tim raised an eyebrow.

“I’m starting to feel like I should’ve brought a résumé.”

“What’s your name?”

“Tim.”

“Tim what?”

“Johnson.”

She nodded, as though filing it.

“Allison Kensington.”

“Nice to meet you.”

“Are you married?”

Tim blinked.

“No.”

“Girlfriend?”

“No.”

“Boyfriend?”

“No.”

“Excellent.”

Tim stared at her.

“I’m beginning to think this isn’t about the bus.”

“It isn’t.”

“Good. Because I don’t work for the transit authority.”

That smile again.

Bigger this time.

Tim had the distinct impression that Allison Kensington had approached him with a plan and was becoming increasingly pleased that the plan had selected the correct victim.

She glanced toward Maison Belle.

Then at Tim.

Then back toward Maison Belle.

Then very slowly back at Tim.

Tim followed the sequence.

Dress shop.

Him.

Dress shop.

Him.

He looked down at himself.

Jeans.

T-shirt.

Sneakers.

He looked at her.

“Whatever you’re thinking, I feel like I should know about it.”

“I have a wedding in Paris.”

Tim waited.

Allison apparently expected that sentence to do more work.

“Congratulations?”

“Not mine.”

“Still happy for whoever’s making the mistake.”

“My ex is going to be there.”

There it was.

Tim nodded slowly.

“Ah.”

“I would like to arrive with someone.”

“Reasonable.”

“A girlfriend.”

Tim nodded again.

Also reasonable.

Allison continued staring at him.

Tim waited.

She waited.

A bus passed in the opposite direction.

Tim looked toward it, then back at Allison.

“Okay.”

“I want you to be my girlfriend.”

There were moments in life when the brain responded to new information efficiently.

This was not one of them.

Tim looked at Allison.

Then Maison Belle.

Then himself.

Then Allison again.

“You’re aware I’m a man.”

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

None.

Tim appreciated confidence.

“Okay.”

That answer seemed to throw her slightly.

Only slightly.

“You don’t seem surprised.”

“I am surprised. I’m just waiting for the part where this starts making sense.”

Allison folded her arms.

It was an extremely effective maneuver in that suit.

“My ex will be at the wedding.”

“We covered that.”

“I want her jealous.”

Her.

Interesting.

Tim filed that away.

“And?”

“And I want to win.”

There was enough conviction behind the words that Tim almost laughed.

Not because it was ridiculous.

It was ridiculous.

But because Allison Kensington had apparently decided that breakups had standings.

Tim understood competition.

“So you need a girlfriend.”

“I need an exceptional girlfriend.”

“And you’ve selected”—Tim gestured down his profoundly unexceptional outfit—“this.”

Allison’s eyes traveled over him again.

This time Tim understood what bothered him about the look.

She wasn’t looking at what was there.

She was looking at what she could do with it.

“Oh,” he said.

Allison’s smile became positively dangerous.

“There you are.”

“You’re mentally dressing me.”

“I’ve been mentally dressing you for approximately four minutes.”

“That seems invasive.”

“You’ll survive.”

Tim looked toward Maison Belle.

Then back at her.

“You saw me standing outside a dress shop and thought, girlfriend?”

“No.”

“No?”

“I saw your face.”

Tim blinked.

“My face.”

“And your hair.”

“My hair.”

“Your proportions.”

He looked down at himself again.

“My proportions.”

“And then I saw the dress shop.”

“Ah. So the dress shop was supporting evidence.”

“Exactly.”

Tim laughed.

He couldn’t help it.

This woman was insane.

Not dangerous insane, as far as he could tell.

Fun insane.

Possibly expensive insane.

Definitely hot insane.

Those were different categories.

“What exactly are you proposing?”

“A weekend in Paris. You come with me to the wedding as my girlfriend. I pay for everything necessary.”

Tim glanced at her suit.

Then at the shoes again.

“How broad is everything necessary?”

Allison followed his gaze downward.

“Very.”

“Because I feel like your definition of shoes and my definition of shoes may come from different tax brackets.”

That laugh surprised her.

It was short and bright and much less controlled than anything else about her.

Tim liked that too.

“I’ll pay for the clothes,” she said. “The travel. Hotel. Hair. Makeup. Whatever we need.”

“We.”

“Yes.”

“You’ve already made this a team project.”

“I don’t lose.”

That landed somewhere interesting.

Tim shifted his backpack again and studied her.

She was serious.

Utterly, magnificently serious.

Somewhere in Paris was a woman who had apparently broken up with Allison Kensington and was about to become the unwilling target of an international retaliatory girlfriend operation.

Tim almost respected the scale of it.

Almost.

“What do I get?”

Allison tilted her head.

“A weekend in Paris.”

“That’s nice.”

“First class.”

“Nicer.”

“Luxury hotel.”

“Very nice.”

“Everything paid.”

“Getting warmer.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“You negotiate quickly.”

“Software engineer.”

That made her pause.

“You’re a software engineer?”

“Yep.”

Allison looked at his backpack.

“Laptop?”

“Yep.”

Something about this information pleased her.

Tim had no idea why.

“What else?” she asked.

“What else what?”

“What do you want?”

Tim considered the question.

The weird thing was that he wasn’t offended.

He probably should have been. A stranger had walked up to him at a bus stop, looked him over like an underutilized piece of real estate, and proposed flying him to Paris so she could dress him as a woman and use him to emotionally carpet-bomb her ex.

There had to be a normal reaction to that.

Tim couldn’t find it.

What he could find was Allison Kensington.

Beautiful.

Funny.

Rich, apparently.

Completely shameless.

And, unless Tim had catastrophically misread the last ten minutes, attracted to him.

That last part mattered considerably more than the rich part.

Money was useful. Tim liked money. Money bought food, housing, computers and the services of mechanics who enjoyed touching engines so Tim didn’t have to.

But Allison having a lot of it didn’t particularly impress or intimidate him.

It did solve one obvious logistical problem.

Tim looked toward Maison Belle again.

Then down at himself.

Then at Allison.

“You want Software Engineer Barbie.”

Allison went completely still.

Tim watched the phrase hit her.

Slowly, her mouth opened.

Then closed.

“Yes,” she said.

It came out with considerably less dignity than anything she’d said so far.

Tim grinned.

There it was.

Interesting.

“You’re rich?”

Allison recovered enough to look suspicious.

“Yes.”

“Actually rich?”

“Yes.”

“Then you’re paying for Software Engineer Barbie.”

For the first time since she’d approached him, Allison Kensington appeared to have no prepared response.

Tim found that deeply satisfying.

He continued.

“You want dresses, shoes, makeup, hair, whatever”—he gestured toward her with one hand—“all of this?”

“All of this?”

“You know what I mean.”

“I’d like to hear you explain it.”

“No.”

“Coward.”

“Stranger at a bus stop.”

“Potential girlfriend.”

Tim laughed again.

God, she was trouble.

He liked trouble.

“Fine,” he said. “If I’m doing this, I’m doing it properly. I’m not flying to Paris looking like somebody lost a bet.”

Something changed in Allison’s expression.

Interest became focus.

“Oh?”

“You want your ex jealous?”

“Yes.”

“You want to win?”

“Yes.”

“Then why would I half-ass it?”

Allison stared at him.

Tim could practically hear machinery engaging behind those beautiful eyes.

“You’ve never done this before,” she said.

“Been somebody’s girlfriend?”

“Presented as a woman.”

“No.”

“And that doesn’t bother you?”

Tim shrugged.

“Should it?”

Allison’s expression softened by a fraction.

“I don’t know.”

“Neither do I.”

He thought about it.

Dress.

Makeup.

Hair.

Heels.

Being introduced as Allison’s girlfriend.

None of it reached anywhere near the part of Tim that contained being a man. Clothes were clothes. Makeup was apparently expensive face paint. Shoes were shoes, although Allison’s looked as if they might have their own insurance policy.

And Allison Kensington was looking at him like she’d just discovered buried treasure at a municipal bus stop.

Tim could live with that.

“Look,” he said. “You find me hot?”

Allison’s answer came immediately.

“Yes.”

Very immediate.

Tim smiled.

“Cool.”

Her eyebrows rose.

“Cool?”

“I’m a simple man, Allison.”

“I seriously doubt that.”

“Hot woman finds me hot. Strong opening position.”

“And the girlfriend part?”

Tim shrugged again.

“You need a girlfriend, I can do girlfriend.”

“You say that with extraordinary confidence for someone who has never worn heels.”

Tim looked down at hers.

“How hard can it be?”

Allison made a noise.

It might have been a laugh.

It might have been a prayer for patience.

Tim pointed at her.

“Don’t start.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“Your face did.”

“My face is extremely well behaved.”

“Your face just called me an idiot.”

“My face has excellent judgment.”

Tim grinned.

Allison grinned back.

And there it was again—that tiny shift.

For a second, neither of them was talking about Paris.

Or weddings.

Or exes.

They were just two attractive strangers standing on a sidewalk, enjoying each other.

Tim felt it.

From the look in Allison’s eyes, so did she.

He cleared his throat.

“One condition.”

Allison’s expression sharpened.

“What?”

“I still get to code.”

Silence.

Tim waited.

Allison blinked.

“You still get to—”

“Code.”

“You’re negotiating a fake-girlfriend weekend in Paris and your condition is that I don’t interfere with your software engineering?”

“Correct.”

“You understand we’re talking about a weekend.”

“I have a laptop.”

“I noticed.”

“I like my job.”

Allison stared at him for several seconds.

Then she started laughing.

Not the polished little laugh from before.

Really laughing.

She turned her face away, one hand coming up as though that might somehow restore her dignity.

It did not.

Tim waited, pleased with himself.

Eventually she looked back at him.

“You’re serious.”

“Completely.”

“I don’t want you to stop being a software engineer.”

“Excellent.”

“I want you to look spectacular in a dress.”

“Also achievable.”

“You don’t know that.”

Tim looked her up and down.

Not subtly.

Allison noticed.

“You seem confident in your skill set,” he said.

Her eyes narrowed.

“Oh, I am.”

“Then I guess we’ll find out.”

There it was.

The challenge.

Tim saw Allison recognize it at exactly the same moment he did.

Her posture changed almost imperceptibly.

So did his.

This was no longer simply a bizarre proposition.

Now there was a scoreboard.

Tim had absolutely no idea what the rules were.

That had never stopped him before.

Allison extended her hand.

“Weekend in Paris.”

Tim looked at it.

Then at her.

“Your girlfriend.”

“Yes.”

“You pay for the transformation.”

“Presentation.”

Tim raised an eyebrow.

“That’s the word?”

“For now.”

“Fine. You pay for the presentation.”

“Yes.”

“I still get to code.”

Allison’s mouth twitched.

“You still get to code.”

“And we’re doing this properly.”

Something positively wicked entered her eyes.

“Oh, Tim.”

He should probably have been worried about that.

Instead he took her hand.

“Deal.”

Her fingers closed around his.

Warm.

Soft.

Certain.

For one strange second, the ridiculousness disappeared.

Tim looked at her.

Allison looked at him.

And Tim had the extremely clear thought that pretending to be this woman’s girlfriend might be the best terrible decision he’d made in years.

Then Allison looked past him at Maison Belle.

Her smile returned.

Tim followed her gaze.

“No.”

“We should establish a baseline.”

“The bus is coming.”

“We can get another bus.”

“I’ve been waiting twenty minutes for that bus.”

“I’ll get you home.”

Tim looked at her.

“How?”

“I have a car.”

Of course she did.

Tim sighed.

“What kind?”

Allison’s smile widened.

“Does it matter?”

“No. I just wanted to see if you’d say something obnoxious.”

“It’s a Bentley.”

Tim stared at her.

Allison looked delighted with herself.

“Of course it is.”

She tugged gently on his hand toward Maison Belle.

Tim didn’t move.

Allison looked back.

“What?”

“Software Engineer Barbie,” he reminded her.

“Yes?”

“Your credit card.”

Allison Kensington’s smile could have powered Regency Hill.

“Obviously.”

Tim considered the boutique.

Then Allison.

Then the bus approaching in the distance.

The bus had never once looked less appealing.

He adjusted his laptop backpack.

“Fuck it.”

Allison’s eyes lit up.

And Tim Johnson let a beautiful rich woman drag him into a dress shop.
