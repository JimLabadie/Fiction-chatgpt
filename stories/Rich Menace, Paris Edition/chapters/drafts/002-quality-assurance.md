# Chapter Two — Quality Assurance

Camille Moreau had the expression of a woman who had just watched someone hand a loaded weapon to a golden retriever and realized the retriever understood ballistics.

Tim liked her immediately.

More than he already had, anyway.

He was still standing in the dress that had apparently broken something important in Allison Kensington’s brain. Sophie was still trying not to grin. Allison was still pretending she had not followed Tim half a step when he walked away from her.

Nobody was discussing that.

This was generous of them.

Tim looked at Camille.

“So what’s next?”

Camille studied him for a long moment.

Then she looked at Allison.

Allison straightened.

“What?”

Camille smiled.

Nothing about the smile was friendly.

Tim had known this woman for less than an hour and already understood that this was excellent news.

“You said properly,” Camille said.

Allison narrowed her eyes.

“I did.”

“You said no half measures.”

“I did.”

Camille’s smile deepened.

Sophie made a tiny happy noise.

Tim looked between them.

“I feel like I’m missing context.”

“You are,” Allison said.

Camille turned toward him.

“The dress worked because the cut is doing much of the work for you.”

Tim looked down at himself.

“Waist, neckline, skirt.”

“Yes.”

“And because I’m built close enough to the size range.”

“Yes.”

“And because apparently Allison has a medical condition.”

“I do not.”

Tim glanced at her.

Allison stared back.

He smiled.

Allison’s mouth tightened.

Camille watched the exchange.

That smile again.

Tim pointed at her.

“You’re enjoying this.”

“I own a boutique. I enjoy many things.”

Sophie coughed into her hand.

Tim turned back to the mirror.

“All right. The dress is doing a lot of work. What isn’t it doing?”

Camille moved beside him.

“Your body has its own proportions. Clothes can redirect the eye. They can create balance. They can emphasize one thing and soften another.”

“But.”

“But fabric is not anatomy.”

Tim nodded.

That made sense.

Camille touched the side seam near his hip, not moving it so much as indicating the line.

“Here, the skirt gives you shape. But if we choose something more fitted, we cannot ask fabric to invent volume that is not there.”

“Hips.”

“Yes.”

“And chest.”

“Yes.”

Tim considered himself in the mirror.

“Ass.”

Sophie choked.

Camille’s eyes closed briefly.

Allison made a sound that might have been a laugh and might have been a surrender.

Tim looked at them.

“What?”

Camille opened her eyes.

“Yes, Tim. Also your ass.”

“Good. We’re aligned.”

Sophie leaned against a display table.

“I love him.”

Allison looked at her.

“No.”

Sophie blinked.

“What?”

“No.”

Tim’s eyebrows went up.

Allison seemed to hear herself approximately one second after everyone else did.

She recovered with impressive speed.

“I mean no, you do not get to encourage him.”

Sophie smiled.

“Sure.”

Camille turned away so Allison would not see her face.

Tim saw it.

Oh, she was a smug bitch.

Wonderful.

Camille crossed toward a low cabinet near the fitting rooms and opened it.

“We begin underneath.”

Tim followed.

“Foundation garments.”

“Yes.”

“Shapewear?”

“Yes.”

“Smoothing?”

“Yes.”

“And the anatomy problem.”

Camille gave him a measuring look.

“The presentation problem.”

Tim nodded immediately.

“Better phrase.”

Allison watched him absorb that correction without defensiveness.

Tim noticed her watching.

He smiled at her.

Allison looked away.

Interesting.

Camille pulled several pieces from the cabinet, laying them across the padded bench outside the fitting rooms.

Nothing looked theatrical.

That surprised Tim.

He had vaguely expected—well, he did not know what he had expected. Something with more engineering drama.

Instead the pieces looked like ordinary clothing designed by people who had opinions about seams.

Camille noticed his expression.

“What?”

“I expected more NASA.”

Sophie laughed.

“This is NASA.”

Tim picked up one piece by the waistband.

“This?”

“You will understand after you put it on.”

“That sounds ominous.”

“It is shapewear, not a blood oath.”

“Good. I have plans later.”

Allison folded her arms.

“You have plans?”

Tim looked at her.

“I did.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“Did?”

“Well, this seems to be escalating.”

Sophie made another tiny sound.

Camille did not even try to hide the smile this time.

Allison pointed at her.

“You are becoming a problem.”

“No,” Camille said serenely. “I am solving yours.”

Tim looked at Allison.

“See? Quality vendor.”

“Do not call her that.”

Camille handed him the first set of garments.

“Changing room.”

Tim took them.

Then paused.

“What about smoothing?”

The room changed by half a degree.

Not badly.

Just carefully.

Camille’s expression became matter-of-fact.

“There are several ways to handle what the outer garment requires. Comfort and safety first. Some looks need more management than others. Some do not.”

“Tucking.”

“Yes.”

Tim nodded.

“Teach me what I actually need to know.”

No embarrassment.

No joke.

Just another piece of the system.

Camille’s gaze warmed.

“All right.”

She explained it plainly.

Not as an initiation.

Not as a secret.

Not as evidence that Tim was anything other than Tim.

Just a practical technique used by people who needed a smoother line under clothing, including trans women and other people managing the same anatomy problem.

Tim asked questions.

How long was reasonable.

When not to do it.

What kinds of garments made it unnecessary.

What kinds made it useful.

What hurt meant.

What numbness meant.

Camille answered what she knew and was careful about what she did not.

Tim listened.

Then repeated the safety points back to her.

Sophie watched him do it.

“So you actually study everything.”

Tim shrugged.

“I dislike avoidable failure.”

Allison murmured, “That explains a lot.”

Tim looked at her.

“About me?”

“About software engineers.”

“Rude.”

“Accurate.”

He smiled.

She smiled back before she could stop herself.

Camille saw that too.

Of course she did.

Smug bitch.

Tim went into the fitting room.

The next twenty minutes were less glamorous than television had led him to believe.

There was pulling.

Adjusting.

Re-adjusting.

A brief argument with a garment that appeared to have been manufactured one size smaller by spite.

And finally a moment when Tim stood in front of the fitting-room mirror wearing the foundation layer and thought:

Oh.

Not because he had become someone else.

He had not.

The face was still his.

The shoulders were still his.

The long hair was still his.

But the lines had changed.

His middle looked smoother.

His waist looked more intentional.

The dress had suggested something earlier.

This built the scaffolding for it.

Tim opened the door.

Sophie looked up first.

Her eyebrows climbed.

Camille nodded once.

Allison stopped breathing.

Tim saw it.

He pretended not to.

For approximately three seconds.

Then:

“Better?”

Allison’s eyes moved over him.

“Yes.”

Tim waited.

She looked at his face.

“Yes.”

He smiled.

“Excellent.”

Allison glared.

“You are doing that on purpose now.”

“Doing what?”

“That.”

Sophie whispered, “He absolutely is.”

Tim looked at Camille.

“What changes if we add volume?”

Camille’s answer came instantly.

“Everything.”

That was promising.

The hip and butt forms were next.

Again, the actual objects were less dramatic than the effect.

Camille explained placement.

Proportion.

Integration.

Why too much would look like costume.

Why too little would vanish under certain fabrics.

Why the objective was not to build the largest possible feminine silhouette but one that looked coherent on Tim.

Tim watched in the mirror while the line changed.

His waist appeared narrower because his hips now existed in a different relationship to it.

His lower body balanced his shoulders.

The shape of the dress from earlier suddenly made more sense as an idea.

Tim turned slightly.

Then farther.

“Oh.”

Sophie grinned.

“Yeah.”

Tim looked over his shoulder at the mirror.

“Oh.”

Allison had gone silent again.

Tim caught her reflection.

She was staring.

He shifted his weight onto one leg.

The shape changed.

Allison’s eyes changed with it.

Tim filed that away.

Camille saw him file it away.

Her mouth twitched.

“You are not subtle,” she said.

Tim looked at her in the mirror.

“I’m learning.”

“You are weaponizing.”

“Those are not mutually exclusive.”

Sophie actually clapped.

Allison stared at Camille.

“You did this.”

“I warned you he was a dangerous student.”

“You could stop helping.”

Camille looked genuinely offended.

“I am French.”

Tim waited.

Nothing followed.

He looked at Sophie.

“Is that an explanation?”

“For her? Usually.”

Camille handed him the breast forms.

Tim looked at them.

Then at Allison.

Allison looked back.

“Problem?” she asked.

“No.”

Tim weighed one in his hand.

“Just recalculating.”

“What?”

“My center of gravity.”

Sophie folded over laughing.

Even Camille lost composure for a second.

Allison put a hand over her mouth.

Tim stared at all three of them.

“What?”

“That,” Allison said through her hand, “was the least sexy possible response.”

Tim looked down at the forms.

“Give me ten minutes.”

Allison lowered her hand.

Bad decision.

Tim smiled.

Her composure flickered.

Camille took the forms from him before the experiment could become a public safety issue.

“Inside.”

Tim obeyed.

There were practical lessons.

Placement.

Support.

What different necklines required.

How movement changed with additional weight.

Why the bra mattered beyond simply holding something in place.

Why shape, spacing, and proportion affected how clothes hung.

Tim asked enough questions that Sophie eventually brought a notebook from behind the counter and placed it beside him.

He looked at it.

“What’s this?”

“For your spreadsheet.”

Tim laughed.

Camille did not.

“You should take notes.”

Tim picked up the notebook.

“Seriously?”

“Yes.”

He thought about it.

Then began writing.

Allison watched him.

“What are you writing?”

“Rules.”

“Of femininity?”

“No.”

Tim kept writing.

“Rules of this implementation.”

Camille’s eyebrows rose approvingly.

Tim read aloud as he wrote.

“Silhouette is composition, not concealment.”

Camille nodded.

“Foundation first.”

Another nod.

“Proportion beats maximum volume.”

“Yes.”

“Comfort and safety override appearance.”

Camille smiled.

“Yes.”

Tim looked at Sophie.

“What else?”

“Handbag is not optional.”

Tim frowned.

“That seems unrelated.”

“It is not.”

“We are on body architecture.”

“I am future-proofing.”

He wrote it down.

Allison stared.

“You wrote that?”

“I respect domain experts.”

Sophie beamed.

Tim closed the notebook.

“Next.”

Camille put him back in the dress.

The difference was immediate.

The earlier version had been beautiful.

This one was dangerous.

The dress no longer had to imply the full silhouette by itself. It fell over curves that supported what the cut was trying to do.

Tim looked taller somehow.

Not literally.

More composed.

More finished.

He turned.

The skirt moved.

His body moved underneath it.

Allison’s lips parted.

Tim saw.

Sophie saw.

Camille absolutely saw.

Camille stepped back.

“Well.”

Allison gave her a warning look.

Camille ignored it.

“Now we know.”

Tim looked at Allison.

“Know what?”

Allison answered before Camille could.

“That this was a terrible idea.”

Tim’s smile arrived slowly.

“For you?”

Allison’s eyes narrowed.

“For society.”

“Coward.”

Sophie gasped.

Camille covered her mouth.

Allison took one step toward him.

Tim held his ground.

“You are very pleased with yourself.”

“I’m standing here in your experiment.”

“My experiment?”

“You found me at a bus stop.”

“I remember.”

“You said you could work with this.”

“I remember that too.”

Tim spread his hands.

“Well.”

Allison looked him over.

That was the problem.

She looked him over slowly.

Tim felt the assessment.

Not abstractly.

Physically.

And something about it pleased him more than it had any right to.

He stepped closer.

“Working?”

Allison stared at him.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He let the word sit between them.

Then stepped away.

Allison did not follow him this time.

Progress.

Probably.

Camille said, “Shoes.”

Allison turned.

“No.”

Everyone looked at her.

She blinked.

Tim smiled.

“That sounded immediate.”

“I know what Camille means by shoes.”

Camille’s expression became angelic.

“You said properly.”

“I hate you.”

“No, you don’t.”

Sophie had already disappeared toward the back.

Tim watched her go.

“How bad?”

Allison looked at him.

“I am extremely good in heels.”

“I have observed.”

“Do not say it like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like you’ve been collecting evidence.”

Tim considered answering.

Decided not to lie.

“I have.”

Allison closed her eyes.

Camille’s smile could have powered a small city.

Sophie returned carrying two boxes.

Allison opened her eyes.

Looked at the boxes.

Looked at Camille.

“Those?”

“For later.”

Allison relaxed by perhaps three percent.

Camille opened the first box.

“These are not.”

Tim looked down.

High.

Very high.

Not circus high.

Worse.

Beautiful high.

The kind of heel that looked less like footwear and more like a contractual dispute with gravity.

Tim looked at Allison.

She was not smiling.

That got his attention.

“You’re worried.”

“I am not worried.”

“You looked at the shoes like they insulted your mother.”

“They are aggressive.”

Camille nodded.

“Yes.”

Sophie nodded.

“Yes.”

Tim looked back at the shoes.

“Can I walk in them?”

“No,” Allison said.

Camille said, “Eventually.”

Sophie said, “This is going to be amazing.”

Tim pointed at Sophie.

“Unhelpful.”

She grinned.

Camille handed him a lower pair first.

“Start here.”

Tim accepted them.

“That seems sensible.”

Allison exhaled.

Tim looked at her.

“You thought she was putting me directly into those.”

“She has done worse things.”

Camille looked insulted.

“I have never done worse things.”

Allison pointed at the second box.

“You have plans.”

Camille’s smile returned.

“Yes.”

Tim sat.

Put on the lower heels.

Stood.

Immediately sat back down.

Sophie made a noise.

Tim held up one finger.

“Recalibrating.”

Allison laughed.

Not politely.

Not delicately.

She laughed with her whole face.

Tim stared at her.

God.

She was spectacular.

That was inconvenient.

He stood again.

Slowly.

Camille corrected him before he took a step.

“Knees soft.”

Tim adjusted.

“Chest?”

“Natural.”

“Core?”

“Engaged enough to stabilize, not enough to look rigid.”

“Stride?”

“Shorter.”

Sophie added, “And stop trying to conquer the floor.”

Tim looked down.

“I’m standing.”

“You’re standing competitively.”

Allison laughed again.

Tim looked at her.

“You are enjoying my suffering.”

“Immensely.”

He smiled.

“Good.”

Allison’s laugh faltered.

There.

Still worked.

Excellent.

Tim took a step.

Bad.

Another.

Less bad.

Another.

Camille adjusted his pace.

Sophie adjusted where he was placing his feet.

Allison, once she stopped laughing, corrected his posture with frightening precision.

“No, you’re pitching forward.”

“I feel like I’m upright.”

“You’re not.”

“Show me.”

Allison stepped beside him.

She was still wearing her own spectacular stilettos as though gravity had signed a private nondisclosure agreement.

She moved one hand lightly to his upper back and another near his waist.

“Here.”

Tim adjusted.

“No.”

Again.

“Better.”

He turned his head.

She was close.

Very close.

Allison noticed at the same moment he did.

Tim smiled.

Her hands remained where they were for one second longer than necessary.

Camille looked away.

Not because she was polite.

Because if she looked directly at Allison, she was going to laugh.

Smug bitch.

Tim took another step.

Better.

Another.

Better.

He walked the open lane between the racks.

Turned too sharply.

Wobbled.

Recovered.

Sophie applauded.

Tim looked offended.

“That was not applause-worthy.”

“You didn’t die.”

“Low bar.”

“First day.”

He tried again.

By the fifth pass, he could cross the space without thinking about each foot individually.

By the eighth, he stopped looking like he was negotiating with the floor.

By the twelfth, Allison stopped laughing.

Tim noticed that immediately.

He turned at the far end.

Allison was watching.

Not amused now.

Interested.

Tim adjusted his shoulders.

Shifted his weight.

Remembered the dress.

Remembered the silhouette.

Remembered that movement was not a sequence of feminine gestures.

Awareness.

Space.

Effect.

He walked back toward her.

Not perfectly.

Not even close.

But naturally enough that the clothes moved with him instead of against him.

Allison went still.

Tim stopped in front of her.

“How bad?”

Her eyes flicked down.

Back up.

“Annoyingly not bad.”

“Day one.”

“I know.”

“Imagine week three.”

Allison stared at him.

Tim smiled.

Camille made the tiniest sound.

Tim looked over.

She was rearranging scarves with the concentration of a bomb technician.

Sophie had both hands over her mouth.

“What?”

“No one said anything,” Sophie replied.

“Higher?”

Allison’s eyes widened.

“No.”

“Camille.”

“You’re actually nervous.”

“Now I definitely want the shoes.”

“You are a terrible person.”

Camille smiled sweetly.

“I am helping you win.”

The heels were absurd.

Beautiful.

Elegant.

And absurd.

Then looked at Camille.

“You hate me.”

“No.”

“Allison?”

“Yes.”

“Useful distinction.”

“Oh.”

Allison folded her arms.

“Yes.”

“This is stupid.”

“Yes.”

“I love them.”

Actually laughing.

“They’re spectacular.”

Slowly.

Because he liked hard things.

He liked learning.

A useful problem.
