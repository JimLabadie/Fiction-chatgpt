## Creative reasoning and proposals

The approved series title supports visibility, self-recognition, and a life extending beyond the first night at Blush.
First-book title **The Night at Blush** remains approved.

The strongest current conception of the opening is not "Steve solves his gender" but "Steve acquires belonging before he understands why he belongs." The women first protect Chloe, then learn Steve, then protect Steve's ownership of his own realization.

The strongest conception of Chloe's romance is continuity of person: Chloe falls for Steve rather than for a hypothetical future Emily, and later gets to watch the person she already loves become more joyful.

The strongest conception of Athena's fit in this story is temporal and thematic rather than as a later bolt-on: Athena's hidden commercial world is already advanced while Steve worries about ordinary life, and Athena's character reflects the same compassion, curiosity, profanity, stubbornness, problem-solving, and rabbit-hole mind Chloe is learning to love.
