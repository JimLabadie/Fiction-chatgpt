Status: AUTHOR-APPROVED FICTIONALIZATION DIRECTION; REPLACEMENT NAMES NOT YET SELECTED
Scope: This commercial series. Shared source references retain their original names.

## Purpose

Track real-world inspirations and the distinct fictional people, businesses, brands, and products used in this story. The author intends playful fiction, openly created with AI assistance. This record supports creative consistency; it is not a legal clearance or a claim that renamed material cannot be recognizable.

## Working rules

- Use stable mapping IDs so a fictional name can change without losing its relationships.
- Track parent company, subsidiary, brand, product, and individual separately. A group name change must not accidentally merge distinct entities.
- Give fictional counterparts their own coherent history, people, visual identity, products, and behavior. Preserve the narrative function without implying that invented events actually happened to the real inspiration.
- Do not copy an actual person's identity and private history into a fictional romance or invented misconduct. Build a distinct fictional character.
- Naming approval does not automatically approve every associated event or trait.
- Proposed names remain pending until approved or covered by explicit author delegation. Candidate text may use clearly marked planning placeholders; approved chapters use resolved fictional names.
- After a mapping changes, update dependent character, setting, outline, chapter, and financial/ownership references consistently. Preserve dated source financial figures as source reference, not automatically as the fictional counterpart's established valuation.
- Check linked product/company names, initials, jokes, origin receipts, and cultural references when changing a name. Preserve the scene's meaning rather than blindly replacing strings.
- Existing invented shared-world names such as Athena, Lesbos Holdings, Asteria Intelligence, Blush, and established fictional characters are retained unless deliberately revised.
- Names inspired by mythology are not automatically modern real-person identities requiring replacement.
- Do not assume that a source phrase such as “the real Sarah” refers to an actual private person; check whether it distinguishes an in-world person from software before classifying it.
