## Steve's perception gap and comedy

Steve knows people see him as a beautiful woman, is annoyed, and tries to compensate poorly. Other people find that overcompensation even funnier. Preserve the difference between his interpretation and others' perceptions.

Write the humor through specific behavior, timing, and social interaction. Maintain the house guide's distinction between affectionate amusement and humiliation. Do not make Steve foolish in every domain or give everyone identical reactions. Specific behaviors and dialogue remain to be developed.

Blush is a new social situation, not the first time someone has perceived Steve as a woman. Recognition can develop without resetting what he already knows.

Steve is not the comparatively normal outsider adopted by a chaotic family. His own family is boisterous and lovingly dysfunctional in the same fundamental way as the polycule. He already understands affectionate roasting, overlapping conversation, arguments that do not threaten belonging, food and practical help as care, and family members interfering because they show up for one another. This is why he meshes with the polycule almost too well.

## Comic emphasis approved by the author

Steve's eventual identity is a femme lesbian. His present cluelessness and poorly managed attempts to convey masculinity offer sustained dramatic irony. The author approved the car enthusiasm/indifferent driving and clothing-construction competence examples as a direction for characterization.

Let sincere enthusiasm temporarily outrun self-monitoring, then allow a specific rationalization to expose the gap between what Steve thinks he conveyed and what others noticed. Specific exchanges remain to be written and approved. Do not repeatedly explain the joke or make every observer equally knowing.

The polycule is a boisterous, lovingly dysfunctional family, not five polished guides taking turns helping the protagonist. Preserve individual voices inside ensemble chaos. They interrupt, roast, form temporary alliances, revive old arguments, sometimes overstep, and repair. Public competence and private ridiculousness coexist.

Steve's fit with that rhythm is itself part of the opening comedy and belonging arc. He does not merely tolerate their chaos; he naturally argues, takes sides, deadpans, troubleshoots, and recognizes affection encoded in it. The women should gradually notice how quickly having Steve at the table feels normal.
