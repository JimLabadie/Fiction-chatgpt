## Durable premise

- This is the first planned commercial series using the reusable framework and its established house voice.
- The main character is Steve, distinct from the Jim persona: a 35-year-old software engineer.
- Steve is an egg: he has not yet recognized the identity implicit in the premise.
- Steve wanders into Blush.
- Everyone sees Steve as a beautiful lesbian, 5 feet 6 inches tall and 105 pounds.
- This is how everyone has always perceived Steve, including before he enters Blush. Blush does not initiate the perception.
- Steve is aware that people perceive and treat him as this beautiful woman. It annoys him; he does not recognize what it means about himself.
- He tries to compensate with poorly executed masculine overcompensation, which others find even funnier.
- The distinction is between noticing other people's responses and understanding himself. Do not write him as encountering this recurring experience for the first time at Blush.
- The intended format is a long-running series. Plan a few opening chapters before drafting.

The author's opening description uses he/him for Steve. Preserve that starting self-understanding in planning; use the approved close third-person, past-tense narration with initial he/him pronouns. Other characters' perceptions and forms of address can differ. Do not turn their perception into knowledge of Steve's private thoughts.

Framework constraint: characters participating in nightlife romance or attraction are adults. Steve is 35.

## Themes and series promise

Approved starting tension: Steve's understanding of himself differs from everyone's longstanding perception of him; his annoyed overcompensation contributes comedy.
Author-established emotional destination: Steve is loved, cherished, and living as his true self: a femme lesbian named Emily. This identity destination is author-established; recognition, presentation, and pronoun changes have not yet occurred or been scheduled.

His current life is not bad; it is grey. Through experience, he learns that this greyness is not an inevitable normal he must accept, nor the life he wants. Preserve this starting condition rather than inventing an abusive or catastrophic past to justify change.

Emily's eventual recognition is primarily characterized by relief and joy rather than by requiring a crisis of misery. The story may contain complicated feelings and lost-time grief when appropriate, but it does not need to manufacture severe prior dysphoria to validate her identity.

After recognition, Emily goes through a sustained period of exuberant overcorrection and experimentation. This is not a single makeover gag. She approaches femininity and lesbian culture with the same all-in, rabbit-hole temperament Steve has always brought to coding, interests, explanations, and masculine compensation. She enthusiastically tries feminine and lesbian-coded styles, experiences, traditions, jokes, hobbies, and stereotypes to discover what is actually hers.

The intended progression is: masculine overcorrection -> recognition, relief, and joy -> exuberant feminine and lesbian overcorrection -> settled, confident femme Emily. Transition does not replace Steve with a different person; Emily retains the interests, habits, relationships, competence, humor, and personality that were always hers.

## Satire, stereotypes, and reclamation

The series is satirical and affectionate. Emily's experimentation with feminine and lesbian stereotypes is not mockery of women, lesbians, trans women, femininity, masculinity, or butchness. Stereotypes do not define identity, and no interest or presentation is a test of whether someone is a woman or a lesbian.

The comic target includes social policing and the self-conscious pressure to avoid something one genuinely enjoys merely because outsiders may regard it as stereotypical. Emily increasingly reaches the position that other people are free to think what they want; she is free to enjoy what she enjoys.

This attitude becomes infectious beyond Emily's immediate family and eventual relationship circle. Her visible delight can prompt other queer women to reconsider their own accumulated rules about what they are supposed to avoid, downplay, or apologize for. Some may initially roll their eyes at Emily's excess and then join in because it is fun.

Emily is not establishing, spearheading, or becoming the ideological leader of a movement. The effect is social and organic: one woman is having such an obviously good time being herself that other people begin asking why they are being so uptight. Their choices remain individual. Some stereotypes will fit particular women, some will not, and some experiments will be tried once and abandoned.

Reclamation and permission are central to this comic mode: enjoyment comes before respectability. The women may roast society, themselves, and each other affectionately while refusing to let outside expectations determine what they are allowed to enjoy.

This also echoes Steve's childhood conditioning. Before kindergarten he learned to modify himself partly because of what other people might think or do. Mature Emily increasingly arrives at the opposite principle: let them think it. Her freedom is therefore broader than clothing or gender presentation; it is an unlearning of anticipatory self-policing.

## Relationship direction

Steve's eventual central relationship circle is the love interest and her polycule members. They are intended to become important to him, but their identities, personalities, individual goals, relationship structure, and exact entry into the story are not yet established.

The recovered author direction is that they genuinely see and cherish the woman in Steve before he understands himself, while still remaining distinct people with their own interests, personalities, reasons for noticing him, and lives beyond Steve. Steve's underlying qualities as established for this direction are loving, caring, kind, and selfless. Do not turn the polycule into a coordinated identity committee or use them to dictate Steve's self-discovery.

Emily's eventual transition is expected to be a major internal event for her but almost comically unsurprising to many people around her. Reactions may carry a "wait, I thought she already was" or "about time" quality. Their lack of surprise must not diminish the importance of Emily's own recognition.
