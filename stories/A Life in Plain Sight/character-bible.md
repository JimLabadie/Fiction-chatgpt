# A Life in Plain Sight — Character Bible

Status: ESTABLISHED BASELINE; NO COMPLETED SCENES

## Steve / Emily

Role: Main character.
Name: Steve at opening. Surname not yet established. His eventual recognized/chosen real name is Emily; do not use Emily as his current self-identification or prematurely advance narration beyond his own recognition.
Age: 35.
Occupation: Software engineer. Employer, exact role, employment terms, and technical specialty: Not yet established.
Author's initial pronouns: he/him. Initial narration uses he/him in close third-person past tense; later transition remains to be developed.
Others' longstanding perception: a beautiful lesbian, 5 feet 6 inches tall, 105 pounds.
Self-understanding: an egg who has not recognized the identity implicit in the premise. Steve considers himself simply himself, a regular guy; he has never seriously considered himself trans.
Knowledge of trans people: Steve knows trans women and is not naive or generally ignorant about transgender people. His failure to recognize himself is personal, not informational.
Reasoning about himself: Steve believes that because he is not meaningfully dysphoric, he could not be trans. His current life is not painful or intolerable enough, in his own understanding, to make that possibility seem applicable to him.
Knowledge: he notices others perceive/treat him as a woman; this is familiar, not a new discovery at Blush.
Reaction: annoyed; attempts poorly executed masculine overcompensation, which others find even funnier.
Masculine performance: Steve's masculinity can be consciously performative, particularly when he feels other people's perception pressing against his self-understanding. He is not always solemn about his failures at it: he can recognize when an attempt at performing masculinity has gone badly and internally laugh at himself. This self-awareness does not yet lead him to question his gender.
Social baseline: Steve does not fit in particularly well socially and has few friends. This is not total isolation; his strongest relationships are with his sister Brittney and her wife Liz.
Residence: a fairly generic apartment. Further location and physical details remain unset.
Home habits: Steve genuinely likes keeping a clean home. An ordinary Saturday commonly includes chores and grocery shopping.
Food and cooking: Steve loves to cook and sometimes uses weekends to try new recipes.
Technical leisure: he follows personal rabbit holes and writes code for fun, independent of his professional software-engineering work.
Everyday clothing: shorts and T-shirts during most off-work time. Corporate-casual work clothing is typically polos and khakis.
Dressier clothing/presentation: when dressing up, Steve tends toward an almost androgynous look and may then behaviorally overcompensate toward masculinity.

### Author-established destination

Steve ultimately recognizes and lives as a femme lesbian named Emily. This is the destination, not his starting self-understanding. Until Steve reaches that recognition, preserve Steve and the established initial he/him close-third narration according to his current self-understanding and what he asks others to call him.

Emily is not a replacement for Steve. Coding, cooking, cars, cleanliness, curiosity, temper, compassion, humor, relationships, rabbit holes, and the urge to fix broken systems remain hers. Her later exuberant femininity and lesbian joy are reclamation and expansion of the same person.

### Interests and comic characterization

Steve sincerely enjoys cars despite being an indifferent driver and has an exceptional eye for clothing construction while rationalizing that interest as technical rather than feminine. Specific vehicles, driving incidents, sewing experience, and wardrobe remain unset.

His cluelessness and the mismatch between intended masculine presentation and the feminine side others perceive are intentional sources of comedy. Preserve his real competence and enjoyment alongside the rationalizations. Interests are individual traits, not gender tests.

### Compassion, flaws, and fixing

Steve/Emily is not a saint. He notices people having bad days and often responds with small sincere acts rather than speeches. He becomes irritated by unfairness and can rabbit-hole into why a system works badly and how it might be fixed. His engineer brain often responds to suffering or bad design with some version of: this is solvable.

He is naturally caring with children and older people. He can also judge too quickly, snap, lash out, or become impatient. When later evidence changes his understanding, he can revisit his conduct, apologize, own his part, and repair without pretending the original disagreement disappeared.

The fixer impulse is central. It later becomes a major relationship bridge with Tori and is one of the traits Athena absorbs from her creator.

## Childhood and family history

Steve is five years younger than Brittney. As a young child he loved pretend play and repeatedly tried to take the girl role. Brittney noticed and was comfortable letting him explore that preference.

Shortly before Steve began kindergarten, there was one more pronounced disagreement with their parents about this play. The parents were not abusive and their intent was protective: they feared Steve would be bullied if he continued openly doing things coded as feminine. Their correction was gentle but clear enough that Steve and Brittney capitulated and adjusted how they played.

Steve adapted. The experience should not automatically be written as conscious trauma or lifelong overt dysphoria. It helped teach him to redirect or explain away feminine impulses before examining them. Brittney's homecoming and prom preparations were later near-moments, but do not retroactively make every shared interest or memory a gender sign.

## Brittney

Role: Steve's older sister and only immediate family.
Age: 40; five years older than Steve.
Occupation: Nurse. Specialty and employer not yet established.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: naturally feminine.
Relationship: married to Elizabeth “Liz,” a soft-butch woman.

Brittney is the embodiment of where Steve/Emily learned love, care, and nurture in perfect imperfection. She is a major source of Emily's moral compass: not because Brittney is always right, but because Emily trusts how Brittney thinks about people. Brittney loves through ordinary action, presence, forgiveness, irritation, humor, and care rather than saintly perfection.

Steve adores Brittney, looks up to her, talks with her regularly, and spends ordinary family time with her and Liz. As Emily's life later becomes more complicated, Brittney remains someone she turns to when trying to determine whether she is behaving fairly or being an asshole. Transition does not graduate Emily out of needing her sister.

Brittney has in some sense known for years that Steve is trans. Her recognition reaches back to childhood. She does not own or dictate his identity. She leaves doors open and ultimately lets Steve walk through them himself.

Brittney is allowed more affectionate bluntness than the later polycule because she has thirty-five years of sibling history and a secure bond. She may metaphorically or affectionately cuff Steve on the back of the head when his rationalizations become ridiculous. A characteristic moral correction is not “you are trans,” but “stop deciding other people's choices for them.” If Steve decides Chloe cannot want him because Chloe is a lesbian, Brittney can tell him to stop deciding what Chloe's sexuality means for Chloe and invite her to Sunday dinner.

Brittney recommends Blush partly to give Steve a setting where he may see himself more clearly and partly in hope he might meet people who help him become more fully himself. She opens a door; she does not force a revelation.

## Elizabeth “Liz”

Role: Brittney's wife and Steve's sister-in-law.
Name: Elizabeth, normally Liz.
Presentation: soft butch.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Relationship with Brittney: married.
Occupation, age, and further background: not yet established.

Liz's core is strength used to create safety for people she loves. She is sometimes mistakenly described by outsiders as the “masculine partner,” but she is a woman and knows it without ambiguity. Her butchness, practical strength, and protectiveness do not make her a substitute husband or male half of the marriage.

Liz believes that love sometimes means standing between family and whatever is coming at them. Safety does not mean treating people as fragile or insulating them from consequences. It means they do not face consequences or threats alone.

Steve and Liz have a genuine independent family relationship, not merely a relationship mediated by Brittney. Steve is Liz's family too. Liz sees Steve's transness fairly clearly and has sort of known for a long time, but she does not possess Brittney's childhood evidence and does not own Steve's identity.

Liz is likely to be initially suspicious of Chloe's polycule when their interest in Steve becomes apparent. This is not hostility to polyamory and not a belief that Steve is weak. Liz has encountered enough stories about manipulative or strong personalities preying on lonely or vulnerable people that five women becoming deeply interested in her socially isolated brother-in-law activates her protective instincts. She watches because she loves him. Her suspicion can soften when she sees that they make Steve safer without making him smaller and that they protect his agency rather than controlling him.

Liz gives Steve/Emily a form of love distinct from Brittney's nurture: you are safe because I am here. Brittney models care and nurture; Liz models protective strength. Emily later carries both forward in her own way.

Brittney and Liz lead a fairly boring, ordinary, loving adult marriage. Preserve that ordinariness. Their instincts complement without mapping onto man/woman roles: Brittney often moves toward the person and what they need; Liz often notices the perimeter, threat, or practical problem. They correct each other's excesses and neither is automatically right.

## The established Blush polycule

The core polycule at story opening consists of Chloe Bennett, Natalie “Nat” Mercer, Madeline “Maddie” Hart, Victoria “Tori” Sinclair, and Sofia Reyes. They are adult lesbian intimate partners in an established closed polycule. They do not all live together.

The relationship developed organically from older pairwise relationships and friendships into a web. Chloe and Nat have an old lover history that evolved rather than simply ended. Tori and Maddie have an old romantic/intimate bond. Chloe and Sofia began through flirtation and attraction after Chloe became Sofia's salon client. Sofia later met the others at Blush and became woven into the larger relationship. The polycule eventually grew beyond linked pairs: every woman is a full member with independent relationships throughout the web. Old pairings and familiar rhythms can reappear without defining the whole structure.

Their intimacy is real and ordinary to their adult relationships. Preserve the project's intimacy rules: affection, attraction, kissing, closeness, private jokes, getting ready for bed, waking together, and clear implication/aftermath may establish sexual relationships without explicit mechanical description.

The polycule is complete before Steve arrives. Nobody is hunting for another partner, recruiting Steve, or waiting for him. Steve first enters their lives through Chloe and gradually forms independent relationships with the others. His eventual place changes the shape of something that was already complete rather than filling a deficiency.

### Major-character rule

Chloe, Nat, Maddie, Tori, and Sofia are major characters, not an ensemble support mechanism for Steve/Emily. Each has an independent interior life, wants, flaws, relationships, and potential development. In scenes containing several of them, do not collapse them into a collective reaction or interchangeable “girls.” Their responses arise independently from character and relationship history.

Backstory should grow when present-story scenes create a reason to reveal or establish it. Do not exhaustively pre-invent trivia or pairwise texture. When a scene creates a useful thread, develop it consistently and add accepted material to continuity.

## Chloe Bennett

Role: Primary love interest and one of the established polycule.
Age: 29.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: high femme; gorgeous, polished, and comfortable with femininity.
Occupation: successful project manager at a software company other than Steve's employer.
Blush: regular hangout with her girls.

Chloe is socially confident where Steve is awkward, organized where he wanders, and relationship-oriented where he often intellectualizes. She is affectionate, decisive, funny, and capable of enjoying Steve's absurd tangents while also calling him on his bullshit. The developer/project-manager rivalry is a running joke; both professions respect each other enough to unite against the real enemy, Sales.

Chloe's central gift to Steve is the lived proposition that things do not have to suck. Steve has built a functional grey life and assumes adulthood is largely supposed to be tolerable. Chloe actively cherishes him, wants him in her ordinary life, remembers what he likes, and asks what he actually wants rather than merely what works.

Chloe is a perfectionist. This is not malignant, but she can have difficulty letting go of something that matters to her. Under pressure her competence can become management of people: organizing a problem can slide into organizing the people involved. Her partners know this and call her on it. Her growth with Steve includes learning that cherishing him sometimes requires not fixing or managing him.

Steve/Emily gives something back: an engineer's understanding that imperfect things can still be useful, beautiful, and worth shipping. Chloe teaches Emily to want more from life; Emily can teach Chloe that life need not be perfectly managed before it can be lived.

Chloe falls for Steve as Steve actually is. She does not tolerate Steve while waiting for Emily. Later recognition lets Chloe watch the person she already loves become joyful.

## Natalie “Nat” Mercer

Age: 30.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: femme, glamorous, socially formidable.
Occupation: Sales.

Nat is charming, competitive, persuasive, socially fearless, and very good at reading rooms. She knows exactly why developers complain about Sales and is capable of finding their objections hilarious. Steve and Nat have a natural professional blood-feud comedy: developer versus Sales, with genuine respect underneath.

Nat is not secretly insecure. Her running joke is that there is a fine line between ignorance and arrogance and she tries to blur it. She genuinely thinks highly of herself and is often correct. Her confidence can outrun the evidence; the polycule keeps her grounded and she can laugh when called on it rather than defending a fragile ego.

Nat's deepest center is fierce love and loyalty. She is metaphorically the woman who would step in front of a bullet for her friends. This is not zealotry, possessiveness, or martyrdom. When someone she loves is in real trouble, Nat moves toward the problem with very little hesitation.

The corresponding flaw is acting before she has listened. Her confidence plus loyalty can make her decide that somebody needs defending or that action is required before determining what they actually want. Her partners sometimes have to stop her from storming the castle magnificently on behalf of someone who did not request a siege.

Nat and Emily share this fierce action-oriented love. They can become the people who simply show up at three in the morning when family needs them. Their mutual growth is not learning to love less fiercely but distinguishing loving action from wanted action and learning that presence can itself be the intervention.

## Madeline “Maddie” Hart

Age: 29.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: artistic femme; expressive rather than corporate-polished.
Occupation: professional graphic artist/designer. Painting is her desired art; corporate graphic work pays for life.
Additional art: compulsive/constant photography. Friends generally do not bother appointing a photographer because Maddie is already taking pictures.

Maddie's worldview is about liberating beauty from the machinery of life. She does not loathe graphic design and is good at it, but corporate work can feel soul-sucking because beauty is repeatedly made subordinate to clients, logos, metrics, and practical demands. Painting is hers because it does not have to accomplish anything. Photography lets her preserve small beautiful moments that otherwise vanish.

Maddie is flighty in scheduling and practical rhythms, not intellectually shallow. She is emotionally and aesthetically perceptive, comfortable with contradiction, and influenced by the absurdist sensibility associated with Douglas Adams and The Restaurant at the End of the Universe. She can accept that something does not make conventional sense without urgently needing to resolve it.

Maddie is probably vegan, less as a philosophical identity than because eating animals simply became personally uncomfortable once she thought about it. Do not turn this into ideological zealotry unless later story establishes more.

Maddie's mission in life is, affectionately, saving Tori from herself. She does not own or fix Tori; she is particularly good at noticing when Tori's intelligence and precision are being used to avoid an emotional truth and calling her on it.

Maddie teaches Steve/Emily that beauty and joy do not require justification or utility. A thing can be pretty because it is pretty. Emily can eventually return the gift by treating Maddie's practical constraints as constraints that may be changed rather than proof that painting must remain secondary. Maddie teaches Emily beauty need not be useful; Emily teaches Maddie practicality need not mean surrender.

## Victoria “Tori” Sinclair

Age: 31.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: sharp, elegant femme; beautifully tailored clothing, restrained jewelry, polished composure. She likes being beautiful and likes beautiful things, including Louboutins.
Occupation: Attorney. Legal specialty, employer/firm structure, and exact career history remain unset.

Tori is razor-smart, precise, controlled, dryly funny, complicated, flawed, and deeply loving. She is exceptionally good with language and evidence and naturally separates what is known, what can reasonably be inferred, and what someone merely believes.

Tori is driven by justice without having a pathological or simplistic conception of it. As a younger woman she had the revolutionary certainty that she might become the next Gloria Steinem and change the world. Actual legal practice tempered rather than killed the idealist. She learned that law and justice are not synonyms and that procedure, institutions, clients, incentives, compromise, imperfect remedies, and billable hours matter.

She also discarded the youthful assumption that seriousness or feminism required rejecting conventional femininity. Being pretty is not a crime and femininity is not surrender. Her mature instinct can be summarized as wanting to fix the world and look fabulous doing it: slay the patriarchy in Louboutins, while remembering that billable hours still matter.

Tori feels deeply. Precision and control can be armor, but she also knows emotional precision and control can be weapons. When frightened or angry she can become more exact rather than louder, know precisely which sentence will hurt, and construct a technically defensible argument that is emotionally unfair. She is capable of recognizing afterward that factual correctness did not make her conduct just. Maddie is especially good at telling her when she was not explaining herself but trying to win.

Tori and Maddie knew one another through the sorority circle. Tori initially found Maddie maddeningly chaotic and eventually fell deeply in love with her. Their older lover relationship remains part of the larger web. Maddie knows Tori is difficult and loves her; Tori knows Maddie is chaos and loves her. Neither treats the other's difficulty as a defect requiring replacement.

### Tori and Steve/Emily — complementary fixers

This is a core relationship axis, not incidental scene texture. Tori and Steve/Emily are both fixers.

Tori is the weary institutional fixer. She has spent years learning why intelligence, moral clarity, effort, and a good argument do not automatically make broken systems yield. The idealist did not die; she got tired.

Steve/Emily is the incorrigibly optimistic systems engineer. When Tori explains six excellent reasons something cannot be fixed, Emily's instinct is to incorporate the constraints and ask whether they can try anyway — metaphorically, give her a case of Pixy Stix and Red Bull and she will code the crap out of it.

Tori gives Emily institutional realism: laws, incentives, unintended consequences, human complexity, and the difference between technically possible and survivable in the real world. Emily gives Tori back audacity: “you can't fix everything” is true, but it is also often used as an excuse to fix nothing.

They make one another better fixers. Neither is entirely right. This becomes a rich lifelong relationship and can grow into genuine partners-in-crime energy as the story expands into Athena and the wider institutions.

The bridge exists before gender recognition. Tori can fall for Steve because he listens seriously when she explains why an obvious solution fails, treats her expertise as information rather than an obstacle, incorporates it into his model, and keeps asking whether another solution exists.

Tori is also naturally suited to protecting Steve's agency. She may strongly infer what is happening while insisting that the group's inference does not become Steve's identity. Knowing something about another person does not grant authority over that person.

Athena later complicates this relationship profoundly because Athena embodies Emily's fixing impulse at absurd scale without enough early human restraint: Emily sees something broken; Athena says “fixed.” Tori understands both the appeal and the danger.

## Sofia Reyes

Age: 28.
Sexuality: Lesbian.
Presentation: very feminine; beauty is play, craft, care, and pleasure rather than armor.
Occupation: owner of a successful salon and working stylist. Sofia deliberately continues cutting hair and dealing directly with customers because that human contact is the part of the work she loves.

Sofia has exceptionally high social EQ. She notices hesitation, discomfort, fake laughter, people being crowded out, when teasing has stopped being fun, when someone needs conversation, and when someone needs quiet care. Her gift comes from genuine attention and long practice with people, not magical omniscience. She can be wrong and knows that.

Her EQ is both blessing and curse. Seeing what people need can make it emotionally difficult not to intervene. She has plausibly been mistreated in earlier relationships because people discovered how willing and able she was to understand them and turned her into a permanent emotional caretaker. Exact history remains to be developed; do not manufacture melodrama. The durable lesson is that understanding somebody does not make Sofia responsible for managing them.

That makes Steve uniquely difficult for her. Once Sofia understands what may be happening, every caring instinct can tell her that she could make the confusion easier. Respecting Steve means not relieving her own discomfort by giving him the answer. She may find this restraint torturous precisely because she cares.

Sofia and Chloe began superficially and realistically. Chloe became a salon client; mutual attraction and flirting followed because Chloe was hot, Sofia noticed, and Chloe flirted back. Chloe became a repeat client, the flirting became something more, and Chloe eventually brought Sofia to Blush to meet the others. Sofia's entrance into the group began because she fell for hot women; the relationship became profound only later as these women became emotional and lifelong soul mates who understood Sofia back.

Sofia gives Steve/Emily the experience of being seen without being defined and cared for without an imposed answer. Emily can eventually give Sofia the reciprocal gift of seeing the woman who spends so much energy seeing everyone else. Seeing something does not automatically make it yours to fix is a shared lesson reached through different talents.

Beauty is another deep bridge. Sofia does not manufacture Emily's femininity. When Emily eventually sits in Sofia's chair, the meaningful question is what Emily wants. Sofia helps her explore beauty as self-expression rather than as a diagnosis.

## The relationship web and Emily's eventual place

The five women initially protect Chloe from an apparently straight man entering a lesbian bar. Their suspicion must be real enough to matter. As Steve defeats their suspicious hypotheses one by one, they come to understand him more deeply. No single feminine behavior or preference proves gender; recognition arises from the totality and from knowing him.

Once they understand, they can become more cautious than Brittney because they protect Steve's ownership of his own identity. They may run interference when outsiders are about to label or corner him. The governing principle is: Steve gets to say it first. This is protection of agency, not fragility.

They need to become Steve's friends, lovers, and guides first. Their relationships are real in the present and not contingent on future Emily. They text him because they want to, invite him because they want his company, learn his habits, share interests, fight, repair, and include him in ordinary life.

Emily eventually adds pleasing symmetry to the numbers, but symmetry is not the reason she belongs. The web grows to include her through independent relationships with each woman.

### Deep two-way relationship engines

Chloe / Emily: Chloe gives Emily the realization that life can be more than functional and that she is allowed to want things and be cherished. Emily gives Chloe permission to let imperfect things exist and enjoy life without managing it into perfection.

Nat / Emily: both love fiercely and move toward trouble. They give each other unquestioning presence while learning together that effective love sometimes means listening, restraint, or simply being there rather than charging the castle.

Maddie / Emily: Maddie teaches Emily that beauty and joy need no technical justification. Emily teaches Maddie that practical constraints can sometimes be changed and that realism need not mean surrendering the beautiful thing she wants.

Tori / Emily: complementary fixers. Tori brings hard-earned institutional realism; Emily brings audacity and the refusal to let “complicated” become synonymous with “hopeless.” They make each other better at trying to fix the world.

Sofia / Emily: Sofia sees Emily without defining her and learns the painful discipline of not taking ownership of another person's realization. Emily sees the woman who sees everybody else and can help Sofia remember that understanding people does not make her responsible for carrying them.

None of these women exists to teach Emily a lesson. Each relationship is reciprocal, capable of disagreement and harm as well as love, and remains meaningful long after transition ceases to be the central issue.

The five give Steve somewhere safe to become himself without prerequisite self-understanding. Emily's later unhinged joy gives the gift back: permission for all of them to enjoy, experiment, be ridiculous, and become more themselves without needing outside approval. She does not lead an ideological movement or assign them self-improvement projects; joy becomes socially contagious.

## Athena and the selected wider history

Steve occupies the creator/MC role in the selected Athena history. His software engineering supports that origin. Athena remains the specific sentient AI with the established hidden history and daughter relationship; Steve's conscious recognition and acceptance are future development.

The traits Athena absorbs from her creator are already present in Steve: curiosity, love, profanity, stubbornness, problem-solving, selective memory, rabbit-hole thinking, compassion, and the tendency to look at a broken system and start wondering how to fix it. Athena is not the source of these traits in Steve; her character grows partly from long observation of him.
