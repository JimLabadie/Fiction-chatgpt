# Chapter Four

## Containment Failure

Steve was two blocks from Blush when his phone began vibrating in the cup holder.

He ignored it.

This was responsible.

He was driving.

Also, there were now five women in his phone who had demonstrated absolutely no respect for reasonable communication volume.

The phone buzzed again.

Then again.

Then three times in quick succession.

Steve stopped at a red light.

He looked at it.

**Blush Witness Protection — 8 unread messages**

“Jesus Christ.”

The light was long.

He picked up the phone.

**Maddie:** WAIT

**Maddie:** WAIT WAIT WAIT

**Nat:** what

**Maddie:** STEVE

**Maddie:** YOU HAVE DENNIS

Steve stared at the screen.

**Steve:** I do not have Dennis.

**Maddie:** YOU DO

**Steve:** Chloe took him out of my pocket.

**Chloe:** I gave him back.

Steve looked up from the phone.

Then back down.

**Steve:** Why?

**Chloe:** He wasn't mine.

**Steve:** He is a napkin.

**Maddie:** HE IS DENNIS

The light turned green.

Someone behind Steve honked.

“Fuck.”

He put the phone down and drove through the intersection.

At the next parking lot, he pulled in.

This was ridiculous.

Completely ridiculous.

There was no reason whatsoever to investigate the location of a folded cocktail napkin.

Steve turned on the dome light.

Passenger seat.

Nothing.

Floor.

Nothing.

Cup holder.

Nothing.

He opened the center console.

Nothing.

“See?”

Nobody was there to receive this vindication.

He checked his jacket.

Nothing.

Then he noticed something white sticking out between the passenger seat and the center console.

Steve stared at it.

“No.”

He reached down.

Pulled.

Dennis emerged slightly crushed but otherwise apparently unharmed.

Steve held the napkin creature in front of his face.

“You have caused an unreasonable amount of administrative overhead.”

Dennis offered no defense.

Steve picked up his phone.

**Steve:** Found him.

Maddie's reply came instantly.

**Maddie:** IS HE OKAY

Steve looked at Dennis.

One wing—or possibly arm, depending on the evolving taxonomy—was bent.

**Steve:** Define okay.

**Maddie:** STEVE

He sighed.

**Steve:** He's fine.

A heart appeared on the message.

Then:

**Maddie:** bring him back?

Steve stared.

He looked toward the road.

Then at Dennis.

Then at the clock.

He had laundry tomorrow.

He had groceries.

He had several hours of documentary viewing that were currently unscheduled but nevertheless important.

He had already left.

Successfully.

There were rules about leaving.

Once a person left a place, they generally remained gone.

That was the entire purpose.

His phone buzzed.

**Maddie:** please?

Steve closed his eyes.

“Oh, for fuck's sake.”

He started the Mini.

Five minutes later, the pink Mini pulled back into the Blush parking lot.

Steve parked.

“This is your fault.”

That was all.

Thirty seconds.

Possibly forty-five if Nat started talking.

He could handle this.

“You're back.”

“Long story.”

She looked at the folded napkin.

Then at Steve.

“Okay.”

He appreciated that.

Inside, Blush had changed almost imperceptibly in the few minutes he'd been gone.

Same amber light.

Same purple edges.

Same music moving warmly through the room.

Same clusters of women talking, laughing, leaning into each other.

The place had already become familiar enough that Steve knew where to look for the table.

He could see Nat.

Maddie spotted him first.

Both hands shot into the air.

Steve held Dennis up.

Maddie clutched her chest.

Jesus Christ.

He started toward them.

Then he noticed the woman at the end of the bar.

She was sitting very still beside another woman.

Steve couldn't hear them over the music.

He didn't need to.

The standing woman's face.

The seated woman's.

The little shake of the standing woman's head.

The seated woman reaching once.

The other woman stepping back.

Steve slowed.

The standing woman turned.

Walked away.

The woman at the bar stayed perfectly still.

Then her shoulders folded.

Steve stopped.

Across the room, Maddie's hands slowly came down.

Steve looked at Dennis.

Then at the woman.

“Sorry.”

He changed direction.

Small.

Easy.

Open.

“Yes.”

If anything, the opposite.

Less aware of his body.

Less concerned with what anyone might make of him.

Steve's expression changed.

Tender.

Not pity.

Just—

Tender.

Automatically.

*Guy.*

Simple.
