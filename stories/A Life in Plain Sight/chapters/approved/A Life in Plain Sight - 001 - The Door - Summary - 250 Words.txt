Steve has a satisfactory life: a good engineering job, a clean apartment, interesting projects, and dinner currently on the stove. His sister Brittney disagrees about the satisfactory part. With her wife Liz providing enthusiastic interference, she insists that he spend an evening at Blush, a lesbian bar.

Steve considers this an obviously flawed experiment. He is a man, the patrons are lesbians, and rejection should settle the argument. Eventually he agrees to one drink, provided Brittney stops trying to repair his social life afterward.

Getting dressed introduces a familiar irritation. Clothes that fit his small frame, narrow waist, and delicate features rarely communicate what he intends. Strangers routinely mistake him for a woman. Tonight, he hopes the facts will speak for themselves.

At Blush, nobody seems to notice the contradiction he has spent the drive preparing to explain. The door staff welcome him, other patrons make room, and a woman compliments his shirt. Steve reaches the bar feeling both relieved and inexplicably annoyed.

Then he overhears five women arguing with the comfortable hostility of people who love one another. Chloe approaches, introduces herself, and offers him a place at their table without pressure. Their banter catches him off guard; so does her beauty.

Steve accepts, insisting that the one-drink limit remains intact. Within moments, Nat is provoking him, Tori is demanding definitions, Maddie has invented a napkin accountant, and Sofia is laughing. Chloe watches as Steve joins their rhythm. For a while, he forgets the door and his planned escape.
