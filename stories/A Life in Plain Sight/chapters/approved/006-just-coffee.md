# Chapter Six

## Just Coffee

At eleven twelve, Steve called Brittney.

She answered on the second ring.

“What's wrong?”

“Nothing's wrong.”

“Are you hurt?”

“No.”

“Car?”

“No.”

“Work?”

“No.”

A pause.

“Then why are you calling me?”

Steve frowned. “I call you.”

“You text me. You call when something's on fire.”

“Nothing is on fire.”

“Okay.”

He could hear movement on her end, then Liz somewhere farther away.

“Who is it?”

“Steve.”

“What happened?”

“Nothing happened.”

Liz got louder. “Is he okay?”

“He says nothing happened.”

“Then why'd he call?”

Steve closed his eyes.

“I can hear both of you.”

“Good,” Brittney said. “Saves time.”

Steve stood in front of his open closet.

This had been a mistake.

He had known it was a mistake before pressing Call.

Unfortunately, he had already changed shirts three times, and continuing without external review had become statistically irresponsible.

“I have a question.”

“Oh my God,” Brittney said.

“What?”

“You called with a question.”

“Yes.”

“You could have Googled it.”

“I did.”

Silence.

Then, very carefully:

“Steven. What is the question?”

He looked at the shirts on his bed.

“What do you wear to coffee?”

Nothing.

“Britt?”

Nothing.

Then a strange choking noise.

“Are you laughing?”

“No.”

“You're absolutely laughing.”

“I am not.”

Liz said, “She's crying.”

“Fuck both of you.”

That made it worse.

Steve waited.

Eventually Brittney recovered enough to breathe.

“Okay. Coffee. Let's do this. What kind of coffee?”

“What do you mean, what kind of coffee?”

“Work coffee? Friend coffee? Catching-up coffee? Date coffee?”

“It's not a date.”

“Oh.”

The single syllable contained enough bullshit to fertilize several acres.

“It isn't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You said ‘oh.’”

“I acknowledged information.”

“You acknowledged it judgmentally.”

“Who's the coffee with?”

Steve didn't answer.

Brittney made a tiny sound.

“Steven.”

“Chloe.”

From farther away:

“HA!”

“Liz!”

“What? I didn't say anything!”

“You yelled ha.”

“I acknowledged information.”

Steve pinched the bridge of his nose.

“I hate this family.”

“No, you don't,” Brittney said cheerfully. “What did Chloe say?”

“She asked if I wanted coffee.”

“And?”

“And I said yes.”

“And?”

“There is no and.”

“Steven.”

He sighed.

“I asked if it was just coffee.”

There was a thump.

“Did you drop the phone?”

“No.”

“Brittney.”

“I put my head on the table.”

Liz was openly laughing now.

“This is why I don't tell you things.”

“You called me!”

“That was apparently an error.”

“Okay. Okay. I'm sorry. What did she say?”

Steve looked at himself in the mirror.

“She said coffee. Her. Me. No hidden agenda.”

“That sounds pretty clear.”

“Yes.”

“So what's the problem?”

“There isn't a problem.”

“Then why are there clothes on your bed?”

Steve looked sharply around his bedroom.

“What the fuck?”

“I know you.”

“You cannot see my bedroom.”

“I don't need to.”

He looked at the bed again.

Four shirts.

Three pairs of pants.

A fifth shirt was hanging over the desk chair because he'd rejected it early and apparently created a separate category for first-round eliminations.

“There aren't that many.”

Brittney started laughing again.

Steve picked up a dark blue polo.

“I'm trying not to give her the wrong idea.”

“What wrong idea?”

He stopped.

There were, annoyingly, several possibilities.

“That I'm treating it like a date.”

“Okay.”

“Or that I'm trying to impress her.”

“Okay.”

“Or that because she expressed interest I'm assuming that means anything beyond what she actually said.”

“Okay.”

“And she's always put together, so I also don't want to show up looking like I don't give a shit.”

“Okay.”

“And—”

Brittney waited.

Steve looked at himself.

“And people already get the wrong idea about me.”

There was no laughter this time.

Brittney's voice changed by half a degree.

“Okay.”

Steve hated that she could do that.

Not turn serious enough to make him regret saying something.

Just meet him where it was.

“I want to look normal.”

“You do look normal.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yeah,” she said. “I do.”

Steve put the polo against himself.

It was fitted.

Too fitted.

He put it down.

Brittney did not ask why.

“What are you wearing now?”

“T-shirt.”

“What kind?”

“Grey.”

“Pants?”

“Shorts.”

“Wear that.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because it's an old T-shirt.”

“Okay. Wear a newer T-shirt.”

Steve stared into the middle distance.

“You're a tremendous help.”

“I'm serious. Jeans. Nice T-shirt. Sneakers. Done.”

“That seems too casual.”

“You're getting coffee.”

“With Chloe.”

There was a pause.

Steve heard himself.

Brittney heard him too.

Neither commented.

“Okay,” she said. “Nice jeans. The dark ones.”

“Yes.”

“And that green shirt.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Steve knew exactly why.

The green shirt fit him extremely well.

That was the problem.

“It doesn't look right.”

“It looks great.”

“It looks—”

He stopped.

“Like what?”

“Never mind.”

Brittney let the silence sit.

Then:

“Blue button-down.”

Steve considered it.

“That might work.”

“Roll the sleeves.”

“Why?”

“Because it looks good.”

“Doesn't that look like I'm trying?”

“Steve, at some point you have to accept that wearing clothing which fits is not a declaration of romantic intent.”

He frowned.

“I never said it was.”

“You called me at eleven twelve on a Sunday to ask permission to wear sleeves.”

“I did not.”

“You're right. You called for technical consultation.”

“Exactly.”

Liz shouted, “Wear the blue shirt!”

“Thank you, Liz.”

“Dark jeans!”

“I heard Brittney.”

“Good shoes!”

“I own good shoes.”

There was a pause.

Liz said, “Debatable.”

Steve hung up.

---

At eleven thirteen, Chloe stood in her bedroom wearing one shoe.

“Too much leg?”

Nat tilted her head.

Tori looked up from her coffee.

Sofia was sitting on the floor beside Chloe's bed.

Maddie was lying across the bed on her stomach with both feet in the air.

Nobody answered.

Chloe's eyes narrowed.

“That was a question.”

Nat said, “Define too much.”

“For coffee.”

“All leg is appropriate for coffee.”

Tori sighed. “That is not guidance.”

“It's excellent guidance.”

“You would wear that to negotiate a hostage release.”

“And I'd get the hostages.”

Sofia pointed at Chloe.

“Turn.”

Chloe turned.

Sofia considered.

“No.”

“No what?”

“Not that.”

“Why?”

“Because you've looked at yourself in the mirror fourteen times.”

“I have not.”

“Fifteen.”

Chloe glared at her.

Sofia smiled.

“Next.”

“This is ridiculous.”

Maddie raised her head.

“Wear pants.”

Everyone looked at her.

“What?”

Nat pointed. “You suggested pants.”

“I own pants.”

Tori looked genuinely surprised. “Do you?”

“Somewhere.”

Chloe kicked off the shoe.

“This isn't a date.”

“Obviously,” Nat said.

“It isn't.”

“That's why we're all here.”

“I did not invite all of you.”

“You invited Sofia.”

“Sofia invited us.”

Sofia looked entirely unrepentant.

“You were spiraling.”

“I was choosing clothes.”

“You sent me six pictures in eight minutes.”

“They were different outfits.”

“Yes.”

“That is how choosing works.”

Tori sipped her coffee.

“You are attempting to solve two contradictory objectives.”

Chloe pointed at her. “Thank you.”

“That wasn't praise.”

“Still.”

Tori put the cup down.

“You want to appear attractive because you are attracted to Steve.”

“Yes.”

“You also don't want to appear as though you're trying to attract Steve because you're concerned he'll interpret that as pressure.”

“Yes.”

“You additionally know from Brittney that if Steve feels scrutinized in relation to his gender presentation, he becomes self-conscious and begins performing masculinity more deliberately.”

“Yes.”

“And therefore you're trying to dress in a manner that communicates interest without appearing to communicate interest.”

Chloe folded her arms.

“Yes.”

Tori nodded.

“That is stupid.”

Nat nearly fell out of the chair.

“Oh my God.”

“Fuck you.”

“No, no, I'm sorry. Victoria Sinclair just called somebody else's plan stupid. I need to mark the date.”

“Your plan was ‘all leg.’”

“Still is.”

Sofia got up.

“Wear what you normally wear.”

Chloe stared at her.

“I normally dress like I'm trying to fuck somebody.”

Silence.

Nat slowly raised one finger.

“She's got us there.”

“I dress like this for groceries.”

“You looked incredible buying toilet paper last week,” Maddie said.

“Thank you.”

“That wasn't the point,” Sofia said.

“I know.”

Sofia went to Chloe's closet.

“Okay. What would you wear if you were meeting me for coffee?”

“You?”

“Yes.”

Chloe considered.

Then pointed.

“That skirt.”

Sofia pulled it out.

“And?”

“The cream top.”

Sofia found it.

Nat looked between them.

“That's still leg.”

“Leg exists,” Tori said.

“THANK YOU.”

“That was not support.”

“Still taking it.”

Sofia handed Chloe the clothes.

“Wear these.”

Chloe looked at them.

They were nice.

Feminine.

Flattering.

Completely normal for her.

Which somehow felt much more dangerous than dressing up.

“What shoes?”

Four women answered at once.

“No.”

Chloe blinked.

Nat pointed at her closet.

“You're on your own.”

“Cowards.”

---

At twelve twenty-three, Steve called Brittney again.

She answered immediately.

“No.”

“I haven't asked anything.”

“No.”

“What does that mean?”

“It means whatever you're about to ask, the answer is no.”

Steve looked at himself in the mirror.

Blue button-down.

Sleeves rolled.

Dark jeans.

Good shoes, despite Liz's slander.

“I look like I'm going to work.”

“You work in polos.”

“I look like I'm going to somebody else's work.”

“Steve.”

“Maybe the T-shirt was better.”

“Steve.”

“What?”

“Are you nervous?”

“No.”

“Okay.”

He frowned.

“You don't believe me.”

“I didn't say that.”

“You said okay.”

“Yes.”

“Judgmentally.”

“Go have coffee.”

“It's not a date.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“Does Chloe know?”

Steve stared at the phone.

“What?”

“Nothing. Have fun.”

She hung up.

Steve looked at himself.

“This family is fucking useless.”

His phone buzzed.

A text from Liz.

**Liz:** Blue shirt.

Steve looked around the room again.

Then typed:

**Steve:** How do you people know things?

**Liz:** Family.

That was not an answer.

Unfortunately, it was also exactly the answer.

---

At one forty-six, Chloe was standing outside the coffee shop when Steve's pink Mini turned into the lot.

She recognized it instantly.

There were not many plausible alternatives.

The car parked.

Steve got out.

Chloe watched him close the door.

Blue shirt.

Sleeves rolled.

Dark jeans.

Shoulders slightly squared.

His walk fractionally more deliberate than last night.

And there it was.

Not dramatic.

Not ridiculous.

If Brittney hadn't told her, Chloe might not have noticed.

But she had.

*When he thinks people are looking at him a certain way, he gets weird.*

Chloe felt the urge to smile.

She didn't.

Not at that.

Steve saw her.

Stopped for perhaps half a second.

Chloe knew exactly what had happened because his eyes had gone briefly to her legs.

Then immediately back to her face.

His shoulders became approximately twelve percent more masculine.

Oh, God.

She was going to die.

“Hey,” she said.

“Hey.”

Steve walked over.

“You look nice.”

He said it carefully.

Not flirtatiously.

Not at all like a man who had changed shirts enough times to require sibling intervention.

Chloe smiled.

“So do you.”

Steve's eyes flickered.

There.

A tiny moment.

Then:

“Thanks.”

She did not say *handsome.*

She did not mention the shirt.

She did not look at his rolled sleeves.

She did not let him know she'd noticed anything.

Brittney had been very clear.

*Don't study him.*

So Chloe opened the door.

“Coffee?”

“Coffee.”

They went inside.

---

Twenty-seven minutes later, Steve had forgotten his posture.

Chloe knew because he'd started drawing on a napkin.

It had happened gradually.

Coffee first.

Normal conversation.

Work.

The universal uselessness of Monday morning status meetings.

A brief argument about whether project managers caused meetings or merely inherited them from a fallen civilization.

Then Chloe asked what Steve did when he wasn't working.

He said cooking.

Cars.

Code.

“Code?” she said.

“Yes.”

“You code at work.”

“Yes.”

“And then you go home and code.”

“Yes.”

“For fun.”

“Yes.”

Chloe took a drink.

“That's upsetting.”

Steve smiled.

“It's not the same thing.”

“How?”

“At work somebody wants something.”

“And at home?”

“I want something.”

Chloe paused.

There was something in that answer she wanted to come back to.

Instead she said, “What do you build?”

“Whatever.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Little things.”

“Such as?”

“Utilities. Automation. Sometimes I get curious about something and then it becomes a problem.”

“Because you're curious?”

“No. Because once I know enough to understand why the existing solution is bad, I have to fix it.”

Chloe smiled.

“There he is.”

Steve frowned. “Who?”

“Nothing.”

He gave her a suspicious look.

She took another drink.

“Best rabbit hole.”

“Best?”

“Or worst.”

“Those are different metrics.”

“Of course they are.”

Steve thought.

“There was one in college.”

Chloe leaned forward.

“Oh?”

“It wasn't really college. It started in college.”

“That sounds promising.”

“It was stupid.”

“Even better.”

Steve looked at her.

She smiled.

He gave in.

“I was awake for about thirty-six hours.”

“Why?”

“Red Bull.”

“That is not why.”

“And Pixy Stix.”

“Steve.”

“There were other contributing factors.”

“Were any of them sleep?”

“No.”

Chloe laughed.

Steve's hands had started moving.

He didn't seem to know.

“I was working on an adaptive reasoning framework.”

“In college.”

“Yes.”

“For class?”

“No.”

“Job?”

“No.”

“Why?”

Steve looked genuinely confused.

“Because I wanted to see if it would work.”

Chloe sat back.

There it was.

The whole fucking man.

“Continue.”

“So I built a basic heuristic framework. It wasn't anything revolutionary. Mostly pattern recognition, adaptive weighting, contextual response—”

“You named it.”

Steve stopped.

“What?”

“You named it.”

“I didn't say that.”

“You absolutely named it.”

“Most projects have names.”

“What was her name?”

Steve stared.

“Why did you say her?”

Chloe smiled.

“Was I wrong?”

He looked away.

“Oh my God.”

“It was an acronym.”

“Of course it was.”

“S.A.R.A.H.”

Chloe put her coffee down.

“Sarah.”

“Synthetic Adaptive Reasoning and Assistance Heuristic.”

She stared at him.

“What?”

“You designed the acronym.”

“Yes.”

“To spell Sarah.”

“That is generally how acronyms work.”

“Who was Sarah?”

Steve stopped.

Chloe's smile widened.

“Steve.”

“My girlfriend.”

“Your girlfriend.”

“At the time.”

“You named your adaptive reasoning whatever after your girlfriend.”

“Heuristic.”

“Your adaptive reasoning heuristic after your girlfriend.”

“Yes.”

“That is adorable.”

“It wasn't adorable.”

“It was incredibly adorable.”

“It was efficient naming.”

“You engineered a backronym for your girlfriend.”

“It was not a backronym.”

“Did you have the acronym first?”

Steve opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Chloe folded her arms.

“Counsel rests.”

“You're not Tori.”

“I've learned things.”

Steve shook his head.

“Anyway, I eventually abandoned it.”

“What happened to Sarah?”

“The project or the person?”

Chloe nearly inhaled coffee.

“Oh, we're going there.”

“No, we're not.”

“What happened to the person?”

Steve looked down at the napkin.

“She cheated on me.”

Chloe's smile disappeared.

“Oh.”

“It was a long time ago.”

“Still sucks.”

“Yeah.”

“With anyone I need to hate?”

Steve hesitated.

That was all Chloe needed.

“There's a name.”

“There doesn't need to be.”

“There is absolutely a name.”

“Chloe.”

“What was his name?”

Steve sighed.

“Chad.”

Chloe stared.

Then covered her mouth.

“Don't.”

“I am so sorry.”

“You're not.”

“I am. Emotionally. But structurally—”

“I know.”

“Chad?”

“Yes.”

“His actual name?”

“Yes.”

“Jesus Christ.”

“I didn't select him.”

That broke her.

Steve tried not to laugh.

Failed.

Chloe wiped under one eye.

“I'm sorry.”

“No, you're not.”

“I'm a little sorry.”

“Thank you.”

“What happened to S.A.R.A.H.?”

“I renamed it.”

Chloe froze.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“You spite-renamed your computer program after your girlfriend cheated on you.”

“It wasn't spite.”

“What did you rename it?”

Steve took a drink.

“Athena.”

Chloe stared at him.

“After the Greek goddess of wisdom.”

“Yes.”

“Not spite.”

“No.”

“Just a completely neutral administrative update.”

“There were branding considerations.”

Chloe laughed so hard the table shook.

Steve waited with the patience of a man being persecuted for entirely reasonable engineering decisions.

When she recovered, she said, “So Athena died in a repository somewhere.”

“No.”

“No?”

“I reused it later.”

Chloe leaned forward again.

“For what?”

“Home automation.”

She blinked.

“Like Google Home?”

“Basically.”

“So you bought a Google Home.”

“No.”

Chloe stared at him.

Steve stared back.

“Steve.”

“What?”

“Why not?”

“I didn't need one.”

“You wanted home automation.”

“Yes.”

“Google Home does home automation.”

“It seemed stupid to buy an entire device for four things.”

Chloe's mouth opened.

Closed.

“You had four smart devices.”

“Yes.”

“And rather than spend—what, fifty dollars?”

“Something like that.”

“You wrote your own Google Home.”

“I didn't write my own Google Home.”

“What did you do?”

“I already had Athena.”

“The adaptive reasoning framework.”

“Yes.”

“Named after your cheating ex-girlfriend's replacement goddess.”

“That is not an accurate technical description.”

“It's an extremely accurate historical description.”

Steve ignored that.

“I just built an interface layer for the devices.”

“Just.”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Not long.”

“Steve.”

“A weekend.”

Chloe put both hands flat on the table.

“You spent an entire weekend building something because fifty dollars was too expensive.”

“It wasn't about the money.”

“You specifically said buying one would be stupid.”

“Because I didn't need additional hardware.”

“You needed an entire weekend.”

“I had the weekend.”

“You also had fifty dollars.”

“That isn't the point.”

Chloe was laughing again.

Steve pointed at her.

“And eventually I added a fifth bulb.”

She stopped.

“A fifth bulb.”

“Yes.”

“Okay.”

“Which exceeded the original configuration.”

Chloe waited.

Steve waited.

She slowly narrowed her eyes.

“Are you presenting the fifth bulb as vindication?”

“It substantially improved the value proposition.”

Chloe dropped her forehead into one hand.

“Oh my God.”

“What?”

“Nothing. Keep going.”

“You're making fun of me.”

“I am deeply interested.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It can be two things.”

Steve tried to look offended.

Unfortunately, he was enjoying himself.

“So you had Athena controlling five light bulbs.”

“Not just light bulbs by then.”

“I'm almost afraid to ask.”

“Thermostat. Television. Locks. Music. A couple other things.”

“And you programmed all of it manually?”

“At first.”

“At first.”

Steve picked up the napkin.

“Specifying every condition got annoying.”

He drew a box.

Then another.

“So I gave it access to state information and added adaptive weighting.”

Chloe watched him.

“Meaning?”

“It could learn routines.”

Another line.

“Like?”

“When I usually got home. Which lights I used. Temperature preferences. Music. Whether it was a weekday. Stuff like that.”

“So it watched what you did.”

“Observed state.”

“Watched.”

“Fine.”

“And learned what you liked.”

“Patterns.”

“Steve.”

“Basically.”

He drew another line.

“The whole point was that I shouldn't have to tell it everything.”

Chloe went still for just a moment.

Not because the sentence frightened her.

It didn't.

It was just—

Very Steve.

Of course he'd build something so he didn't have to ask for what he wanted every time.

She looked at the napkin.

Boxes.

Lines.

Arrows.

His handwriting getting smaller as he ran out of room.

“How did it know when the pattern was wrong?”

Steve looked up.

“What?”

“You said it learned routines. People break routines.”

“Exactly.”

Something lit in his face.

And Chloe knew she'd asked the right question.

“So you don't want simple frequency. That's useless. Context matters.”

He pulled another napkin toward himself.

“If I turn a light on at six every weekday, that doesn't necessarily mean I want the light on at six. It might mean that's when I get home.”

Chloe nodded.

“So arrival is the important event.”

“Maybe.”

“Maybe?”

“Could be sunset. Could be occupancy. Could be another routine correlated with arrival.”

“Okay.”

“So you need competing hypotheses.”

He drew three more boxes.

Chloe leaned closer.

“What if you're sick?”

Steve stopped.

Then looked at her.

“That was actually a problem.”

Chloe smiled.

“Ha.”

“No, that's good.”

He crossed something out.

“No, seriously. Because anomalous behavior shouldn't immediately rewrite the preference model.”

“So how did you fix it?”

“Confidence decay, mostly. Context windows. Exceptions that don't propagate unless repeated.”

Chloe watched him rewrite the diagram.

No defensiveness.

No attempt to explain why her question wasn't technically sophisticated enough.

She'd found a hole.

He liked the hole.

“That’s better,” he said.

Something warm moved through her.

Fuck.

Brittney had warned her.

*If he feels watched, he'll start watching himself.*

So Chloe stopped watching.

She picked up her coffee and joined him in the rabbit hole.

“What about two people?”

Steve looked delighted.

“Exactly.”

“Oh, no.”

“What?”

“You've thought about this.”

“Obviously I've thought about this.”

“Do I want to know how much?”

“No.”

“Thirty-six hours?”

“That was years earlier.”

“Pixy Stix?”

“I have matured.”

“Red Bull?”

“Coffee.”

“Growth.”

“Thank you.”

By the time Steve finished explaining conflicting preference resolution, he had stolen half of Chloe's croissant.

He didn't notice.

Chloe did.

She didn't say anything.

His sleeves had slipped unevenly.

His posture was gone.

One leg was tucked partly beneath the chair.

He was talking with both hands.

The careful man who had walked across the parking lot had disappeared somewhere between Chad and the fifth bulb.

Chloe didn't go looking for anyone underneath him.

She didn't need to.

Steve was right there.

Funny.

Ridiculous.

Kind.

Brilliant.

Very serious about context windows.

And apparently willing to spend an entire weekend avoiding a fifty-dollar purchase.

She liked him.

Not a hypothesis about him.

Not Brittney's maybe.

Him.

That was somehow both simpler and considerably more inconvenient.

Steve finally looked down.

Then at the empty half of her plate.

He stopped.

“Did I eat your croissant?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you stop me?”

“I wanted to see how far you'd go.”

He looked genuinely appalled.

“That's unethical.”

“You seemed happy.”

“That isn't a defense.”

“You ate exactly half.”

Steve looked at the remaining half.

Then at her.

“That's actually interesting.”

“No.”

“If I stopped at half unconsciously—”

“No.”

“—there may be a social boundary component—”

“Steve.”

He smiled.

“Sorry.”

Chloe pushed the remaining half toward him.

He pushed it back.

“No. Absolutely not.”

“You already established precedent.”

“I stole your food.”

“You liberated it from the machinery of breakfast.”

Steve stared at her.

“Maddie.”

“Maddie.”

He laughed.

Chloe watched him for half a second too long.

Steve noticed.

His shoulders shifted.

Just slightly.

There it was.

She looked away first.

Not because she didn't want him to know she was interested.

He knew.

Because she suddenly understood what Brittney had meant.

Give him somewhere he didn't have to check himself.

“So,” she said, taking back her croissant, “does Athena still run?”

Steve nodded.

“Yeah.”

That was it.

Just yeah.

“Still controlling the apartment?”

“More or less.”

“Five bulbs?”

“More than five now.”

“Capitalist excess.”

“Five bulbs is not capitalist excess.”

“You once refused to spend fifty dollars on principle.”

“It wasn't principle.”

“It has become principle in my heart.”

Steve rolled his eyes.

His phone buzzed on the table.

He glanced at it.

Then turned it facedown without reading it.

Chloe noticed that too.

She said nothing.

Steve pointed at the second napkin.

“Okay, but here's the part that's actually interesting.”

Chloe smiled.

“There's more?”

“Obviously.”

Of course there was.

There was always more.

And Steve, who was absolutely not on a date and absolutely not trying to impress her, leaned across the table and began explaining it.
