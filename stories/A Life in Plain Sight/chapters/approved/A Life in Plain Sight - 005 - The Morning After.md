# A Life in Plain Sight
## Chapter 5 — The Morning After

Steve woke to the sound of someone demanding a financial audit.

Not in person, fortunately.

His phone was vibrating against the nightstand, making the little glass of water beside it tremble. He reached for it without opening his eyes.

**Maddie:** DENNIS HAS BEEN ABDUCTED

**Nat:** Good morning to you too.

**Maddie:** HIS OFFICE IS EMPTY

**Tori:** It is eight seventeen on a Sunday.

**Maddie:** CRIME DOES NOT OBSERVE BUSINESS HOURS

Steve opened one eye.

Dennis.

He looked toward the dresser.

There, beside his keys and the receipt from last night's parking garage, stood a folded cocktail napkin. A winged horse in a necktie regarded him with the stern disappointment of an accountant who had discovered irregularities.

Steve sat up.

“Morning, Dennis.”

The horse offered no comment.

“Apparently you've been kidnapped.”

Steve picked up the napkin and examined it.

He had brought Dennis home after all.

At some point during the final round of goodbyes, Maddie must have slipped the drawing into his jacket pocket. He hadn't discovered it until he'd gotten home.

He remembered unfolding it, laughing, and setting it on the dresser.

Then he'd gone to bed.

That much was straightforward.

The rest of Saturday was less so.

He remembered five women at a lesbian bar who had initially regarded him with suspicion, then curiosity, then something considerably warmer.

He remembered Maddie's photographs. Nat's stories. Tori's impossible composure. Sofia's kindness.

He remembered Chloe.

Particularly Chloe.

He remembered a woman crying against his shoulder, a ridiculous custody dispute involving a fictional accountant, and promising to come back Friday.

He remembered being hugged goodbye.

Five times.

He had been counting.

Steve looked at his phone again.

**Chloe:** Good morning, Steve.

**Sofia:** I hope you got some sleep.

**Nat:** Maddie has filed a missing-person report.

**Tori:** She has filed nothing.

**Maddie:** I HAVE EMOTIONAL PAPERWORK

Steve smiled.

He typed.

**Steve:** Dennis is safe. He's with me.

**Maddie:** PROOF OF LIFE

Steve propped the napkin against his coffee mug and took a photograph.

**Steve:** He appears to be reviewing my finances.

**Nat:** My condolences.

**Tori:** Given his qualifications, I would be concerned.

**Maddie:** HE HAS AN MBA

**Steve:** From where?

**Maddie:** PEGASUS BUSINESS SCHOOL

Steve laughed.

He was sitting in bed on a Sunday morning, laughing at his phone.

That was new.

Or perhaps it wasn't new. He laughed at things all the time. Television. Movies. The occasional spectacularly incompetent software implementation.

But this was different.

Somewhere out there were five women who had woken up and thought to include him in their morning.

He hadn't asked them to.

They had simply done it.

Steve looked at Dennis.

“Well,” he said. “I think we made some friends.”

Dennis remained professionally reserved.

“Don't look at me like that. You were there.”

---

Chloe had been awake for almost an hour when Maddie announced that she wanted pancakes.

They were gathered in Nat's living room, a place that had gradually acquired the peculiar distinction of being everyone's preferred Sunday headquarters despite Nat's insistence that she had never volunteered it for that purpose.

Maddie occupied one end of the sofa, barefoot and wrapped in a blanket.

Sofia sat cross-legged on the rug with her coffee.

Tori had taken the armchair nearest the window, wearing an immaculate cream sweater and the expression of a woman who had already made several sensible decisions before breakfast.

Nat was in the kitchen.

Chloe stood at the counter, slicing strawberries.

“I thought you wanted waffles,” Nat called.

“I've evolved.”

“Since when?”

“Eight minutes ago.”

“That's not evolution. That's indecision.”

“Darwin would disagree.”

“Darwin is dead.”

“Convenient.”

Chloe smiled and added another strawberry to the bowl.

It was a pleasant Sunday morning.

The kind they had spent together dozens of times.

There was coffee, breakfast, a half-hearted argument about whether anyone needed to go grocery shopping, and Maddie's increasingly elaborate claims about the responsibilities of fictional accountants.

Nobody mentioned Steve.

Not immediately.

Then Sofia looked up from her phone.

“He sent a picture of Dennis.”

Maddie sat upright.

“HE DID?”

“You've already seen it.”

“I want to see it again.”

Sofia turned the screen.

Maddie studied the photograph with considerable emotion.

“He looks happy.”

“He's a drawing,” Tori said.

“A happy drawing.”

“He looks exactly the same as last night.”

“Some people age gracefully.”

Nat emerged from the kitchen carrying a plate of pancakes.

“I'm beginning to think the napkin was a mistake.”

“No,” Chloe said.

She hadn't meant to answer quite so quickly.

Four faces turned toward her.

Chloe looked down at the strawberries.

“It was sweet,” she said.

Maddie's grin became positively dangerous.

“Oh?”

“Don't.”

“I haven't said anything.”

“You're about to.”

Maddie settled back against the sofa.

“I'm listening.”

Nat set the pancakes on the table.

“Actually,” she said, “so am I.”

Chloe sighed.

It had been such a nice morning.

---

The first thing they remembered was the mozzarella stick.

That was Maddie's fault.

She insisted Steve had performed an act of heroic diplomacy by dividing the final piece of fried cheese.

“He gave Chloe the bigger half,” she said.

“He was being polite,” Chloe replied.

“He was flirting.”

“He was avoiding a fight.”

“Same thing.”

Nat laughed.

“And then he sat there listening to my terrible dating stories as if he genuinely wanted to know what happened.”

“He did,” Sofia said.

“He asked questions,” Nat continued. “Actual questions. Not the kind people ask while waiting for their turn to talk.”

Tori considered that.

“He was remarkably attentive.”

Maddie went quiet.

That alone was enough to make everyone look at her.

“What?” Chloe asked.

Maddie shrugged.

“He looked at my photographs.”

“We know,” Sofia said.

“No. I mean he really looked.”

She pulled the blanket closer.

“He noticed things I didn't know anyone else would notice.”

Nat's expression softened.

“He liked them.”

“He understood them.”

For a moment nobody spoke.

Then Tori set down her coffee.

“I've been trying to decide whether to say something.”

Nat looked over.

“That sounds ominous.”

“It isn't.”

“That's exactly what an ominous person would say.”

Tori gave her a look.

Nat raised both hands.

“Proceed, counselor.”

Tori folded her hands in her lap.

“I liked him.”

Chloe smiled.

“We all liked him.”

“No.”

Tori looked directly at her.

“I mean I liked him.”

The room went quiet.

Tori's composure remained intact, but Chloe could see the effort behind it.

“He's kind,” Tori continued. “He's funny. He's intelligent without needing everyone to know how intelligent he is. He listens. And when that woman needed someone, he went to her without expecting anything in return.”

She paused.

“I found him attractive.”

Maddie's mouth opened.

Tori lifted one finger.

“Do not.”

Maddie closed it.

Tori looked around the room.

“Am I the only one?”

Chloe felt something tighten in her chest.

She could have avoided the question.

She was good at avoiding questions when the answers threatened to complicate something she loved.

But Tori had been brave enough to ask.

“No,” Chloe said.

Everyone looked at her.

Chloe set down her coffee.

“You're not the only one.”

---

Steve's Sunday laundry had begun badly.

He had discovered that the washing machine was already occupied.

By his own clothes.

From Thursday.

He stood staring into the drum.

“Well,” he told Dennis, “this is embarrassing.”

Dennis sat on top of the dryer, supervising.

Steve restarted the load.

His phone buzzed.

**Nat:** Important question.

**Steve:** That sounds threatening.

**Nat:** How many fitted sheets have you successfully folded in your lifetime?

Steve considered lying.

**Steve:** Define successfully.

**Sofia:** Oh, Steve.

**Maddie:** ONE OF US

**Tori:** That is not a measurable answer.

**Steve:** It was folded when I put it away.

**Nat:** Was it rectangular?

**Steve:** In a philosophical sense.

**Chloe:** I think that counts.

**Tori:** It does not.

Steve laughed and carried his laundry basket toward the bedroom.

He liked these women.

That was becoming a remarkably inadequate way of describing it.

He liked their jokes. Their arguments. The way they seemed to know exactly how far they could tease one another.

He liked the fact that they teased him too.

It made him feel included.

And there were things he didn't understand.

References that passed between them so quickly he couldn't catch them. Names, movies, jokes, expressions.

He didn't mind not knowing.

But he wanted to.

Not because he needed to win some conversational contest.

He wanted to understand the world his new friends lived in.

He wanted to know what made them laugh, what they loved, what they remembered.

And perhaps, if he was being particularly ambitious, he wanted to understand why being around them felt so unexpectedly easy.

His phone buzzed again.

**Maddie:** If Steve folds a fitted sheet properly we have to revoke his lesbian card.

**Steve:** I don't have a lesbian card.

**Nat:** That's what you think.

**Tori:** Please do not issue identity documents in the group chat.

**Maddie:** TOO LATE

Steve smiled.

Then he paused.

Lesbian card?

He understood the joke in a general sense, but there was something about the way they used the expression that suggested an entire collection of jokes he hadn't encountered.

He opened a browser.

One search led to another.

And another.

By the time he returned to his laundry, he had discovered several things about lesbian culture, none of which had improved his ability to fold a fitted sheet.

He had also found himself genuinely interested.

That was unexpected.

Not unwelcome.

Just unexpected.

---

“I think we're avoiding the obvious issue,” Nat said.

Chloe knew exactly what she meant.

Unfortunately, so did everyone else.

“He's a man,” Tori said.

“He says he's a man,” Maddie corrected.

“That is generally how we determine what someone is,” Sofia replied.

Maddie looked offended.

“I wasn't arguing.”

“You were about to.”

“I was thinking.”

“Loudly,” Nat said.

Chloe rubbed her forehead.

“He told us he's a man. We respect that.”

“Of course,” Tori said.

“But that doesn't make my attraction disappear.”

Nobody had an answer for that.

Nat leaned against the kitchen counter.

“We're lesbians.”

“Yes,” Chloe said.

“And we're together.”

“Yes.”

“And we're not open.”

Chloe nodded.

There it was.

The part that mattered more than any label.

They had built something together.

Not casually. Not accidentally.

Five women, with five different personalities and histories, had found a way to love one another that worked.

They had rules.

They had promises.

They had a life.

Steve had wandered into that life less than twenty-four hours ago.

And somehow he had already become the subject of a conversation none of them had expected to have.

“I don't want us treating him like a problem,” Sofia said.

“He isn't,” Chloe replied.

“Or like an opportunity.”

“He isn't that either.”

Tori looked at Chloe.

“Then what is he?”

Chloe thought about Steve offering Rachel water.

About the way he'd looked at Maddie's photographs.

About his embarrassment when they'd praised him.

About how easily he'd laughed.

“A friend,” she said.

It was true.

It was also not the entire truth.

---

Steve had settled onto the sofa with a sandwich when the commercial appeared.

He was watching a streaming series he'd already seen twice, mostly because it required very little attention.

Then the music changed.

A young woman with striking red hair appeared on the screen.

The commercial was bright and theatrical, full of color and movement.

Steve looked up.

He liked the sound immediately.

The streaming service identified the artist.

Chappell Roan.

He recognized the name.

He'd encountered it during his earlier browsing.

A lesbian cultural icon.

He let the commercial finish.

Then he reached for his phone.

A few minutes later, he was on YouTube.

“Okay,” he told Dennis. “Let's see what all the fuss is about.”

He listened to one song.

Then another.

The music was fun.

Really fun.

It was theatrical and exuberant, with a kind of unapologetic joy he found infectious.

He caught himself smiling.

Then singing along badly.

Then smiling because he was singing along badly.

Dennis watched from the coffee table.

“Don't judge me.”

YouTube suggested another artist.

Hayley Kiyoko.

Steve clicked.

The song was catchy.

The video was something else.

Women flirting with women. Lingering looks. A kiss that made him raise his eyebrows. A playful, steamy confidence that seemed entirely uninterested in apologizing for itself.

Steve leaned forward.

“Oh.”

He watched.

Then he laughed softly.

“Well, that's rather nice.”

The video ended.

Another began.

He let it play.

He liked the music.

He liked the women.

He liked the way the videos seemed to treat their attraction as something beautiful, exciting, and entirely ordinary.

There was no embarrassed explanation.

No defensive qualification.

Just women wanting women.

And somehow, watching them, Steve felt happy.

Not merely entertained.

Happy.

He couldn't have explained why.

He didn't particularly try.

He had a sandwich to finish and apparently an excellent new playlist to assemble.

---

Brittney arrived carrying a bakery box.

Liz followed with two coffees and the expression of someone who had already been informed that Maddie was responsible for a crisis.

“What's happened?” Liz asked.

“Nothing,” Nat said.

Liz looked around the room.

Five women were sitting together with the suspiciously serious expressions of people who had been discussing something important and had abruptly stopped.

“Right,” Liz said.

Brittney set the box on the table.

“Where's Steve?”

“At home,” Chloe said.

“Good.”

Brittney sat.

“Did he have fun last night?”

Five women exchanged glances.

Liz closed her eyes.

“Oh, for God's sake.”

Maddie sat up.

“What?”

“You've all got that look.”

“What look?”

“The look people get when they've discovered a particularly interesting problem and are about to make it everyone else's problem.”

Tori stiffened.

“We are not doing that.”

“Excellent,” Liz said. “Then this will be a short conversation.”

It was not a short conversation.

Brittney listened while they described Saturday.

She laughed at Dennis.

She smiled at the story about Maddie's photographs.

When Chloe told her about Rachel, Brittney grew quiet.

“That sounds like Steve,” she said.

Liz nodded.

“He notices people.”

“He always has,” Brittney said.

“Sometimes to his own detriment,” Liz added.

Chloe looked at them.

“You've known him a long time.”

“Long enough,” Brittney said.

“Long enough to know he can be spectacularly oblivious,” Liz replied.

“About what?” Maddie asked.

Liz looked at her.

“Practically everything involving himself.”

That earned a laugh.

Then Tori spoke.

“We're attracted to him.”

Brittney stopped smiling.

Liz looked from one woman to another.

“All of you?”

“Not necessarily in the same way,” Sofia said.

“But enough that we need to talk about it,” Chloe added.

Liz put down her coffee.

“Okay.”

There was no outrage in her voice.

That almost made Chloe more nervous.

“Okay?” Nat repeated.

“Okay. You have feelings. Congratulations on being human.”

“It's more complicated than that,” Tori said.

“I know.”

Liz looked at Chloe.

“Do you understand how easily he could get hurt?”

Chloe met her gaze.

“Yes.”

“Do you?”

There was something in Liz's expression that made the question different the second time.

Chloe thought about Steve's surprise when Maddie had admitted wanting him to come back.

How genuinely startled he'd been that his leaving mattered.

“Yes,” she said quietly.

Liz nodded.

“Good. Because if you break his heart, I will become a problem none of you have the administrative infrastructure to handle.”

Maddie blinked.

“That was terrifying.”

“Thank you.”

Tori looked impressed despite herself.

“Very efficiently phrased.”

“I practice.”

Brittney shook her head.

“Liz.”

“What?”

“They're trying to be careful.”

“Then I'm encouraging them.”

“By threatening them?”

“I'm a multifaceted woman.”

---

The discussion lasted through breakfast and into the afternoon.

They talked about attraction.

About what it meant to be lesbians.

About trans women.

About the difference between bodies, identities, and the people someone actually loved.

They disagreed.

They asked questions.

They corrected themselves.

At one point Maddie made an analogy involving a dildo that was so spectacularly ill-advised Tori nearly inhaled her coffee.

“Madeleine.”

“What?”

“That is not a legal argument.”

“I'm not making a legal argument.”

“Thank God.”

“I'm making a philosophical observation.”

“That's worse,” Nat said.

Maddie spread her hands.

“My point is that nobody's orientation is determined by a detachable piece of anatomy.”

Liz considered that.

“An unexpectedly reasonable conclusion reached by a deeply unreasonable route.”

“Thank you.”

“That was not entirely a compliment.”

“I accept it anyway.”

Sofia leaned forward.

“Whatever Steve eventually understands about himself, that's his.”

Brittney nodded.

“Yes.”

“And whatever we feel,” Chloe added, “we don't get to decide for him.”

“Exactly,” Liz said.

Tori folded her hands.

“Then we need to distinguish two questions.”

Nat looked at her.

“Of course you have numbered questions.”

“First, what do we feel?”

“And second?” Chloe asked.

“What are we prepared to do about it?”

That was harder.

Because attraction was not permission.

Because they were five women in a closed relationship.

Because they loved one another.

And because Steve was not a sixth place setting waiting to be filled.

“We can't just decide to include him,” Sofia said.

“No,” Chloe agreed.

“And we can't each make private exceptions,” Tori added.

“No.”

“Then perhaps,” Nat said, “we should stop trying to decide what Steve means to our relationship after knowing him for one evening.”

Maddie looked disappointed.

“But I like him.”

“We all like him.”

“I really like him.”

Nat smiled.

“Then let's be his friends.”

The simplicity of it settled over them.

Chloe thought about Friday.

About seeing him again.

About getting to know him without needing to decide what every feeling meant.

“I can do that,” she said.

Liz watched her carefully.

“Good.”

Then she reached for a pastry.

“Because I meant what I said.”

“We know,” five women answered.

Steve was halfway through putting away the laundry when the next song began.

He almost didn't notice.

He'd left YouTube playing in the living room while he folded towels.

The music had become a pleasant background to an ordinary Sunday.

Then a quieter voice drifted from the speakers.

He stopped.

The song was different from the others.

Less playful.

More intimate.

He walked back into the living room.

On the screen was a young woman whose name he'd already encountered in his browsing.

Marie Ulven.

girl in red.

Steve sat down.

He listened.

There was something in the song that reached past the pleasant excitement of the earlier videos.

A kind of longing.

The vulnerability of wanting someone.

The strange combination of hope and fear that came with imagining a life you wanted but didn't quite know how to reach.

He thought about Saturday.

About entering Blush convinced he wouldn't belong.

About the women who had laughed with him.

About Maddie wanting him to return.

About Chloe's hug.

He thought about how little he'd expected from the evening.

And how much he'd received.

The tears came quietly.

He noticed them when one reached his chin.

“Oh,” he said.

He wiped his cheek.

Then another tear followed.

He wasn't frightened.

He wasn't confused about why the song had affected him.

He wanted what he was hearing.

Not necessarily the exact romance in the song.

Something larger.

The freedom of wanting. The possibility of being wanted. The feeling that there might be people among whom he didn't have to work quite so hard to belong.

He'd spent years assuming that life was supposed to feel more or less as it always had.

Work. Home. Friends when schedules aligned. Pleasant evenings. Familiar routines.

Nothing terrible.

Nothing particularly wrong.

But there had been a kind of distance in it.

As though everyone else had received instructions for a life he was still trying to assemble from the packaging.

Saturday had been different.

And this music—

This music felt different too.

He listened until the song ended.

Then he played it again.

Dennis stood on the coffee table beside him.

Steve picked up the napkin.

“I think,” he told the little horse, “I found something.”

He considered that.

“No. That's not quite right.”

He looked at the screen.

“I think I found some people.”

He sat quietly for another minute.

Then he smiled.

“And the music's pretty good too.”

By late afternoon, the group chat had developed an argument about whether Dennis was entitled to paid vacation.

**Maddie:** HE HAS WORKED ALL WEEKEND

**Tori:** He has existed for approximately twenty hours.

**Nat:** Typical startup culture.

**Sofia:** Has anyone asked Dennis what he wants?

**Steve:** He says he would like a beach holiday.

**Maddie:** I KNEW IT

**Chloe:** Does he have a passport?

**Steve:** He's a Pegasus. I assume international borders are a formality.

**Tori:** That is not how customs works.

**Steve:** I'll let him know.

Steve looked at the napkin.

“Sorry, Dennis. Bureaucracy.”

He was smiling when Chloe sent another message.

**Chloe:** How was your Sunday, Steve?

He looked around the apartment.

The laundry was finished.

The groceries had been put away.

The kitchen was clean.

He had accomplished almost everything he'd intended to do.

And somehow none of those things seemed like the most important part of the day.

**Steve:** Really good, actually.

**Sofia:** Good!

**Nat:** Productive?

**Steve:** Surprisingly.

**Maddie:** DENNIS HELPED

**Steve:** Emotionally, yes.

He hesitated.

Then typed another message.

**Steve:** I found some music I really liked.

**Chloe:** Oh? What kind?

Steve smiled.

**Steve:** I started with Chappell Roan.

There was a pause.

**Maddie:** STEVE

**Nat:** Oh, this should be interesting.

**Steve:** Then YouTube introduced me to Hayley Kiyoko.

**Sofia:** Excellent taste.

**Maddie:** HOW MUCH HAYLEY KIYOKO

Steve considered the question.

**Steve:** Enough that my recommendations may never recover.

**Nat:** Congratulations.

**Tori:** On what, precisely?

**Nat:** Cultural enrichment.

Steve laughed.

Then he looked at the next message he wanted to send.

He wasn't sure how to explain it.

The earlier songs had been fun.

The videos had been beautiful.

But the last one had stayed with him.

He typed slowly.

**Steve:** Then I found girl in red.

The chat went quiet.

Not entirely.

There were typing indicators.

One appeared, vanished, and appeared again.

Steve continued.

**Steve:** One of her songs really got to me.

**Chloe:** In a good way?

He thought about that.

**Steve:** Yeah.

**Steve:** It made me cry, actually.

This time nobody made a joke.

Steve looked at the screen.

He could have changed the subject.

He could have blamed being tired.

He could have made some remark about the dangers of algorithmic recommendations and returned the conversation to Dennis.

Instead, he told them the truth.

**Steve:** I think it was the feeling of wanting a place where you belong.

He paused.

**Steve:** And finding out maybe that place exists.

Across town, five women looked at their phones.

Chloe read the message twice.

Tori put a hand over her mouth.

Nat leaned back against the sofa.

Sofia smiled through suddenly bright eyes.

Maddie, for once, had nothing to say.

Steve sat in his quiet apartment, looking at the conversation.

He thought about the things he'd learned that afternoon.

About the music.

About the women.

About the meaning carried by a question that, until today, would have sounded perfectly ordinary.

He understood the question now.

He understood why someone might ask it.

And he understood, at least a little, why he wanted to.

He began typing.

**Steve:** Do you listen to girl in red?
