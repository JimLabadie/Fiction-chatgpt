# Chapter Three

## Relevant Information

When Steve and Chloe returned to the table, everyone was behaving normally.

This was immediately suspicious.

Nat was drinking.

Tori was looking at her phone.

Sofia was examining her nails.

Maddie was folding a cocktail napkin into something that had either four legs or a catastrophic manufacturing defect.

Nobody said anything.

Steve stopped beside his chair.

“What happened?”

Five faces looked at him.

“Nothing,” Chloe said.

Steve looked at her.

“You weren’t here.”

“No.”

“So you’re not a reliable witness.”

Tori’s phone lowered slightly.

Steve pointed at her.

“You.”

“Me?”

“You look guilty.”

Tori’s eyebrows rose. The expression somehow made the pearls at her throat look judgmental too.

“I am an attorney.”

“Exactly.”

“That is an offensive stereotype.”

“Is it inaccurate?”

“Yes.”

Nat snorted.

Tori turned toward her.

“Do you have something to contribute?”

“Nope.”

“Then don’t.”

Steve looked at Nat.

Nat took another drink, crossed one bare knee over the other, and looked back at him with the serene innocence of a woman who had absolutely committed the crime.

He looked at Sofia.

She smiled beautifully.

Too beautifully.

“Oh, absolutely not.”

Sofia blinked.

“What?”

“You’re doing something too.”

“I am sitting here.”

“That’s what Brittney says.”

“Who’s Brittney?”

“My sister.”

Nat leaned forward.

“You have a sister?”

Steve pulled out his chair.

“I do.”

“Older or younger?”

“Older.”

“How much older?”

“Five years.”

“What does she do?”

“Nurse.”

“Married?”

Steve sat.

“Yes.”

“To?”

He looked at Nat.

“A person.”

“Man? Woman?”

“Nat,” Chloe said.

“What? That’s normal conversation.”

“No,” Tori said, “it is an interrogation wearing a fake mustache.”

Steve laughed.

Nat pointed at Tori.

“That’s rich coming from you.”

“I conduct interviews.”

“You cross-examine people for money.”

“Under rules.”

“You’d be terrifying on a first date.”

Maddie looked up from the napkin.

“She was.”

Tori turned.

“You were not my first date.”

“No, but I was somebody’s.”

There was a pause.

Tori stared at her.

Maddie smiled.

Tori’s expression softened despite an obvious effort to prevent it.

“Technically correct.”

Steve sat back.

There it was again.

That strange little movement inside the group.

Nobody had to explain anything. Nobody seemed particularly concerned about who was sitting beside whom or whose hand had found whose knee or which one of them had just insulted another one in a way that would have started an actual argument in a less secure relationship.

The connections were just there.

Old.

Comfortable.

He liked watching them.

Which was perhaps a weird thing to think about five women he’d met less than an hour ago, but it was true.

They reminded him, disconcertingly, of home.

Not his apartment.

Home home.

Brittney talking over Liz while Liz talked over their mother while somebody's aunt shouted from another room that everyone was wrong.

Arguments that weren't emergencies.

Insults that weren't rejection.

Somebody stealing food off your plate because apparently ownership was a theoretical construct between people who loved you.

Steve understood that language.

Nat returned to him.

“Your sister.”

“Still have one.”

“Married to?”

“My sister-in-law.”

Nat’s eyes narrowed.

Steve smiled.

“Oh, you’re enjoying this now,” Chloe said.

“A little.”

“I knew it.”

“Don’t make this weird.”

“You’re sitting with five strangers answering questions about your sister by technicality.”

“Four strangers.”

The table quieted.

Steve realized he’d said it before he understood what he’d said.

Nat looked around.

“Okay. Who isn’t a stranger?”

Steve took a drink.

“Chloe.”

Chloe’s face changed.

Only a little.

But he saw it.

The corner of her mouth moved. Her eyes did something warmer.

And immediately Steve wished he could retrieve the sentence and inspect it for defects.

“I mean, comparatively.”

“Ah,” Chloe said.

“Yes.”

“Wouldn’t want to make it weird.”

“Exactly.”

“God forbid.”

Steve frowned.

“Why are you saying it like that?”

“Like what?”

“Like—”

He stopped.

Chloe waited.

Steve abandoned the investigation.

He had a more important problem.

Right.

That.

The dance had done several things.

First, it had established that Steve could apparently dance reasonably well despite having accumulated almost no supporting evidence for this claim during the previous thirty-five years.

Second, it had established that Chloe Bennett was very pretty at close range.

This had not technically been new information.

It had, however, become considerably more operational at close range.

Third—

Third was the problem.

Steve set his beer down.

“Actually.”

Five women looked at him.

Steve regretted the word instantly.

There were certain words that should never be spoken to a group.

*Actually* was one.

*Technically* was another.

*We need to talk* could destroy a marriage at twenty paces.

“I should probably clear something up.”

That was worse.

Nat’s glass stopped halfway to her mouth.

Tori put down her phone.

Sofia looked at Steve.

Really looked.

Maddie stopped folding the napkin.

Chloe was the only one who didn’t move.

“Okay,” she said.

Steve looked at her.

This shouldn’t be difficult.

It wasn’t difficult.

He’d done this before.

Hundreds of times, probably.

Restaurants.

Stores.

Work conferences.

The dentist once, which had been particularly confusing because the woman had his fucking chart.

Usually it was irritating.

Sometimes funny.

Occasionally exhausting.

This felt different.

Because normally the correction didn’t matter.

This one might.

Steve rubbed his thumb against the condensation on his glass.

“I think there might be a misunderstanding.”

Nat’s eyes flicked toward Chloe.

Steve saw it.

That made him continue faster.

“Not a bad one. Nobody did anything wrong. I just realized that I may have assumed you knew something, and you may have assumed something else, and now that I’ve noticed that, it would be shitty not to say anything.”

Tori’s head tilted.

Not dramatically.

Just listening.

Steve looked at Chloe again.

“I’m a guy.”

Silence.

Not long.

Maybe a second.

Maybe two.

Long enough for Steve to become intensely aware of the bass line moving underneath the conversation around them, the scrape of a chair somewhere behind him, the cold dampness of the bottle under his fingers.

Chloe blinked.

Once.

“Oh.”

“Yeah.”

Another second.

Steve felt compelled to fill it.

“I know I don’t really—”

He stopped.

Don’t really what?

Look like one?

That sounded pathetic.

And it wasn't as though he didn't know what he looked like. He owned mirrors.

He tried again.

“People get it wrong a lot.”

Nat’s eyebrows went up.

Steve noticed.

Not judgment.

Something else.

He couldn’t identify it.

“So,” he continued, “I just wanted to make sure you knew.”

Chloe looked at him.

“I didn’t.”

There it was.

Steve nodded.

“Okay.”

“You thought I did?”

“I wasn’t sure.”

“When did you realize I might not?”

Steve stared at her.

Chloe waited.

Nat slowly lowered her glass.

Steve could feel the entire table listening.

“While we were dancing.”

Chloe’s mouth twitched.

Steve’s stomach sank.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That’s not nothing.”

“No.”

“So what is it?”

“You realized while we were dancing.”

“Yes.”

“Anything in particular happen?”

Steve looked at her.

She looked completely innocent.

That was bullshit.

“You were flirting with me.”

Nat made a strangled sound.

Chloe turned her head.

“Are you okay?”

Nat covered her mouth.

Tori was looking down.

Sofia had suddenly become very interested in Maddie’s napkin.

Maddie was looking directly at Steve with enormous delighted eyes.

Steve looked around the table.

“Oh, fuck you. What?”

“Nothing,” Nat said through her hand.

“Everybody here is terrible at nothing.”

Chloe looked back at him.

“So you did notice.”

“Eventually.”

“Eventually.”

“I had competing evidence.”

“Such as?”

“You’re a lesbian.”

“Yes.”

“We’re in a lesbian bar.”

“Yes.”

“You have four girlfriends.”

“Yes.”

“And I’m a man.”

Chloe considered him.

“Yes.”

Steve waited.

Chloe waited.

He frowned.

“Those facts seemed relevant.”

“They are.”

“Good.”

“Did you think I was confused?”

“No.”

Chloe’s eyebrow rose.

Steve reconsidered.

“I thought you might have incomplete information.”

Tori made a small sound.

Steve looked at her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Counselor.”

That got her.

Tori smiled despite herself.

“Your phrasing.”

“What about it?”

“Nothing. Continue.”

Steve turned back to Chloe.

“I just didn’t want you thinking I was deliberately letting you believe something that wasn’t true.”

The humor at the table changed.

Not vanished.

Changed.

Sofia stopped pretending to study the napkin.

Tori’s expression softened.

Even Nat went still.

Chloe looked at Steve for several seconds.

“You were worried I’d feel tricked.”

Steve shrugged.

“Wouldn’t you?”

“No.”

“Well, I didn’t know that.”

“No. You didn’t.”

“So.”

“So you told me.”

“Yeah.”

“Thank you.”

Steve exhaled.

He hadn’t realized he’d been holding anything.

“Okay.”

Chloe picked up her drink.

Steve waited.

She drank.

He waited some more.

Chloe looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Oh, now you’re doing it.”

“Doing what?”

“Waiting.”

“For?”

“I don’t know.”

“That’s inefficient.”

Steve stared.

She smiled.

The problem was apparently over.

Except—

No.

It couldn’t be.

Steve looked at the table.

Nat had suddenly developed the facial expression of someone trying to perform long division mentally while under artillery fire.

Tori was more controlled, but not by much.

Sofia looked thoughtful.

Maddie was smiling at him.

Not weirdly.

Well.

Maddie smiled weirdly by default, so that wasn’t useful.

Steve turned back to Chloe.

“That’s it?”

“What else did you want?”

“I didn’t want anything.”

“Then yes.”

“But—”

Chloe waited.

Steve stopped.

He didn’t know what came after *but*.

*But you’re a lesbian.*

She knew that.

*But you were flirting with me.*

She knew that too.

*But I’m a man.*

They had literally just covered it.

He had successfully corrected the data.

What Chloe did with the corrected data was Chloe’s business.

Which was, in fact, the entire reason he’d corrected it.

Fine.

“Okay.”

Chloe smiled.

“Okay.”

Steve picked up his beer.

Nat lasted six seconds.

“So Brittney’s married to a woman.”

Steve closed his eyes.

“Jesus Christ.”

“What? We changed topics.”

“No, you circled back.”

“Is that a yes?”

“Yes.”

“Your sister is a lesbian.”

“Yes.”

“And she sent you here.”

“Yes.”

“To Blush.”

“Yes.”

“A lesbian bar.”

“Nat.”

“What?”

“Do you need me to draw a diagram?”

Maddie brightened.

“I can.”

“No,” four people said.

Maddie looked wounded.

Steve pointed at her.

“I appreciate the offer.”

“Thank you.”

Tori sighed.

Nat was undeterred.

“So why?”

Steve shrugged.

“She thinks I need to get out more.”

“To a lesbian bar.”

“Yes.”

“Specifically.”

“Yes.”

Nat looked at Sofia.

Sofia looked at Nat.

Steve saw that too.

“What?”

Nat turned back.

“Nothing.”

“Stop saying nothing.”

“We don’t know anything.”

“That is demonstrably false. You’ve spent the last half hour trying to know everything.”

Tori covered a smile with her glass.

Nat leaned forward.

“Does Brittney come here?”

“Sometimes.”

“With her wife?”

“Liz. Yeah.”

“Liz.”

“Yes.”

“Does Liz know you’re here?”

“She was sitting at my kitchen table when Brittney sentenced me.”

“Your kitchen?”

“Yes.”

“You cooked?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Steve stared at her.

“Why?”

“I’m curious.”

“You’re always curious.”

“Correct.”

“Chicken.”

“What kind?”

Steve told her.

Nat nodded.

“Good?”

“Very.”

“You’re confident.”

“I fixed the marinade.”

There was a pause.

Chloe looked at him.

“You fixed a recipe?”

“It had too much sugar.”

“You’d never made it.”

“That doesn’t mean the ratio wasn’t wrong.”

Sofia smiled.

“What else do you cook?”

“Whatever.”

“That’s not an answer.”

Steve looked at her.

“Are you interrogating me now?”

“No.”

“She is,” Nat said.

Sofia ignored her.

“Favorite thing to make?”

Steve thought about it.

“That depends.”

“On?”

“Who I’m cooking for.”

Something happened again.

He saw it.

Five women.

Five tiny reactions.

Not the same.

Never the same.

Chloe’s was the easiest now because Steve had finally learned what he was looking at.

Interest.

The realization should have made things less confusing.

It did not.

Sofia's smile softened.

Tori studied him for half a second longer than necessary.

Maddie looked delighted by the entire concept.

Nat—

Nat looked like she'd just found another question.

Steve frowned.

“What?”

Chloe said, “Nothing.”

He pointed at her.

“You’re abusing that word now.”

“I learned from experts.”

Nat raised her glass.

“Thank you.”

Steve shook his head.

Sofia asked, “Okay. Brittney. What’s her favorite?”

“Lasagna.”

“Liz?”

“Pernil.”

Sofia smiled.

“You make pernil for your Puerto Rican sister-in-law?”

Steve looked offended.

“I make *good* pernil for my Puerto Rican sister-in-law.”

Nat slapped the table.

“Okay, I like him again.”

“Again?” Steve said.

“You were briefly under review.”

“When?”

“Recently.”

“For what?”

Nat opened her mouth.

Tori said, “Don’t.”

Nat closed it.

Steve looked between them.

He was missing something.

Again.

He was beginning to suspect this might be a permanent feature of knowing these women.

“Liz taught me,” he said.

“Ah,” Sofia said.

“The first one was apparently a hate crime.”

Sofia laughed.

“Her words?”

“Her aunt’s.”

That got her.

“Oh no.”

“Yeah.”

“What did she say?”

Steve shook his head.

“I don’t speak enough Spanish to reproduce the entire indictment, but I understood my name, *Dios mío*, and something about my mother.”

Sofia was laughing harder now.

“My mother wasn’t even there.”

“That wouldn’t save her.”

“I gathered.”

“You made it again?”

“Obviously.”

Sofia stopped laughing.

Not completely.

Just enough to look at him.

“Obviously?”

Steve shrugged.

“It was bad.”

“So?”

“So I fixed it.”

There it was again.

That look.

Not just Sofia this time.

Tori.

Chloe.

Even Nat.

Steve set down his beer.

“Okay, seriously, what?”

“Nothing,” Chloe said.

“Fuck all of you.”

Maddie smiled.

“You really do sound like us.”

Steve looked at her.

Again.

The same sentence.

This time it didn’t catch strangely.

This time it felt—

Nice.

Which was worse.

He took another drink.

Nat watched him over the rim of her glass.

“So what do you do for fun?”

Steve groaned.

“We’re back to the interview?”

“No. Conversation.”

“You’re terrible at conversation.”

“I am phenomenal at conversation. I get paid to converse.”

“You get paid to manipulate people.”

“That’s Sales.”

“Yes.”

“See? You understand.”

Steve smiled despite himself.

“Code.”

“For fun?”

“Yes.”

Nat looked horrified.

“That is the saddest thing you’ve said tonight.”

“It is not.”

“You voluntarily do your job after work.”

“No. I voluntarily do interesting versions of my job after work.”

“That’s worse.”

“It’s not.”

“What else?”

“Cars.”

Steve caught it.

There.

That one he knew.

“Yes. Cars.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You looked something.”

Chloe’s eyes narrowed almost imperceptibly.

“I believe you.”

“You don’t sound like you believe me.”

“I absolutely believe tiny people can enjoy cars.”
