# The Morning After — Summary (450 words)

Sunday morning, Steve wakes to twenty messages in the newly created Blush Witness Protection group chat. The five women have already incorporated him into their conversational chaos: Maddie has photographed Dennis the napkin accountant with a tiny tie, Nat deliberately baits Steve with incorrect information about his Mini, Tori polices language, Sofia offers warmth, and Chloe’s simple “Morning, Steve” catches Steve’s attention more than he wants to admit. While making breakfast and coffee, Steve realizes the unusual part is not merely the volume of messages but that he has awakened inside an ongoing social conversation. His formerly quiet, scheduled Sunday now contains people who are simply present in his life.

At 9:06, Chloe creates a private chat excluding Steve. What begins as discussion of Chloe’s obvious attraction becomes more complicated when Sofia admits she has noticed Steve too. Tori acknowledges finding Steve attractive because he genuinely listens and incorporates her arguments. Maddie admits she does not yet understand her feelings but knows she missed Steve when he left and valued how seriously he treated her photography. Nat finally concedes that Steve is hot, funny, combative in a way she enjoys, and instinctively kind.

The women confront the apparent contradiction: they are lesbians, while Steve explicitly identifies as a man. They invite Brittney into the conversation carefully. Brittney first asks whether Steve is okay and is quietly moved to learn that he genuinely had fun. When Maddie asks whether Brittney thinks Steve is a woman, Brittney says she has long suspected Steve might be trans, but firmly protects his agency: Steve does not think he is trans; he told them he is a man; therefore they treat him as a man. She warns them not to search so hard for a possible future woman that they stop seeing Steve as he currently understands himself.

Chloe admits Brittney’s suspicion brought her guilty relief because she has never before been attracted to a man. The others acknowledge versions of the same discomfort. Sofia refuses to require Steve to become a woman merely to make her own attraction easier to understand, while Tori insists Steve’s possible future identity cannot become evidence used to resolve their sexuality for them.

Then the second, separate issue finally surfaces: the five-woman polycule is closed. They agree attraction itself violates nothing, but nobody should independently escalate romantically while they determine what their unexpected feelings mean for their existing relationship. Steve remains their friend, not a candidate or problem to solve.

Later, while folding laundry, Steve realizes he finds not only Chloe but all five women attractive. He immediately rationalizes this as simple eyesight: they are hot, he is a man, and they are lesbians together. Expanding the problem set would be irresponsible. The chapter closes with the group teasing his badly folded fitted sheet while Steve recognizes that this constant, ridiculous presence is apparently what Sunday is now.
