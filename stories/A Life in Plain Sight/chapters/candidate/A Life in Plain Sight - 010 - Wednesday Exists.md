# Chapter 010 — Wednesday Exists

At 6:38 Wednesday morning, Steve discovered that the cashmere sweater had become a problem.

It was still hanging where he'd left it.

This was objectively true.

It had not moved during the night. It had not acquired additional properties. It remained a dark blue cashmere crewneck he'd bought almost two years ago because it had been on sale and because cashmere was an objectively superior material when manufacturers didn't fuck it up.

He had worn it three times.

Possibly four.

The problem was that last night he'd held it against himself in the mirror and thought, *Maybe.*

Now it was Wednesday.

Dinner was Thursday.

Which meant this was getting stupid.

“Hey Google, weather.”

Athena provided the forecast.

She did not mention the sweater.

This represented growth.

Steve made coffee.

At 6:51, he texted Brittney.

**Steve:** Hypothetical question.

Three dots appeared immediately.

**Brittney:** No.

Steve stared at the phone.

**Steve:** I haven't asked it yet.

**Brittney:** You texted me before 7am and opened with hypothetical question. Whatever comes next is going to ruin my morning.

**Steve:** What do you wear to a nice dinner if a suit would be too much but you don't want to look like you just came from work?

There was a pause.

Then:

**Brittney:** OH

Steve closed his eyes.

**Steve:** Don't.

**Brittney:** I didn't say anything.

**Steve:** You said OH in capital letters.

**Brittney:** That could mean anything.

**Steve:** It has never meant anything good.

Another pause.

**Brittney:** What kind of restaurant?

Steve realized he didn't know.

Chloe had told him where they were meeting. He had looked up the address, parking, menu, reviews, noise level, whether reservations tended to run late, and three separate photographs of the dining room.

He had somehow not converted any of this into an answer to Brittney's question.

**Steve:** Nice.

**Brittney:** Jesus Christ.

**Steve:** I can send you the website.

**Brittney:** Of course you can.

He sent it.

Thirty-seven seconds later his phone rang.

Steve answered.

“Why are you calling me?”

“Because texting is insufficient for the magnitude of what you've done.”

“I asked what to wear to dinner.”

“You asked what to wear to a date.”

Steve opened the refrigerator.

“I know it's a date.”

Silence.

Steve stopped.

“Britt?”

“I’m here.”

“Why are you being weird?”

“I am not being weird.”

“You stopped talking.”

“I was enjoying something privately.”

“That's ominous.”

From somewhere behind her, Liz yelled, “ASK HIM ABOUT THE SWEATER.”

Steve slowly closed the refrigerator.

“What sweater?”

There was a crash.

Then Brittney, farther from the phone: “You absolute asshole.”

“I thought we were doing clothes!”

“We are doing clothes tomorrow!”

“Thursday is the date!”

“Exactly!”

Steve leaned against the counter.

“Why does Liz know I own a sweater?”

Brittney came back.

“She doesn't.”

“She just yelled—”

“She believes conceptually in sweaters.”

“Brittney.”

“Go to work.”

“You called me.”

“And now I'm hanging up.”

“Wait. What am I wearing?”

“Not a suit.”

“I know.”

“Not a tie.”

“I know.”

“Not a polo.”

“I wasn't going to wear a fucking polo.”

“Good. We're making progress.”

Steve looked toward the bedroom.

“The blue sweater?”

This time Brittney didn't stop talking.

She didn't gasp.

She didn't say *which blue sweater* even though Steve owned more than one.

She just said, “Yeah. That could look really nice.”

Steve looked down at his coffee.

“Yeah?”

“Yeah.”

“With slacks?”

“Dark ones.”

“Shoes?”

“The brown ones you wore to my anniversary dinner.”

Steve thought about it.

“That's not too casual?”

“No.”

“Too soft?”

Brittney was quiet for exactly the right amount of time.

“No.”

Steve nodded, although she couldn't see him.

“Okay.”

“Try it on tonight.”

“I know how clothes work.”

“I have thirty-five years of evidence to the contrary.”

“Fuck you.”

“Love you.”

“Love you too.”

She hung up.

Steve drank his coffee.

Then he went back to the bedroom and looked at the sweater again.

Still just a sweater.

Good.
