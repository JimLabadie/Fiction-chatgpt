# Chapter One
## Cross-Examination

Steve discovered within approximately ninety seconds that Natalie Mercer asked questions the way other people committed burglaries.

She didn't announce anything.

She just got inside.

“So,” Nat said, resting her chin on one hand. “Software engineer.”

Steve took a drink.

“Yes.”

“Where?”

He told her.

“Doing what?”

He told her that too.

Nat nodded thoughtfully.

“How long?”

“Six years.”

“Before that?”

Steve frowned.

“Are you hiring?”

“No.”

“Then why do you need my résumé?”

“I like knowing things.”

Tori didn't look up from her drink.

“She means she likes acquiring information people haven't decided to give her.”

“I asked.”

“You ask in sequences designed to make the next question feel inevitable.”

Nat looked delighted.

“Thank you.”

“That wasn't praise.”

“I accept it anyway.”

Steve glanced between them.

“Is she always like this?”

“Yes,” Chloe said.

“No,” Sofia said at exactly the same time.

Steve looked at Sofia.

She smiled.

“Sometimes she's worse.”

“Okay, wow.”

Nat put a hand to her chest.

Maddie looked up from the cocktail napkin.

“She's being protective.”

The table went quiet.

Nat turned slowly toward her.

“Maddie.”

“What?”

“You don't tell the person you're interrogating that you're interrogating them.”

“Oh.”

Maddie considered this.

“Sorry.”

She looked at Steve.

“She isn't being protective.”

Steve nodded.

“Much better.”

“Thank you.”

Tori pinched the bridge of her nose.

Chloe took a drink.

Sofia was trying not to laugh.

Steve looked at Nat.

“Protective of what?”

“Chloe.”

“Why?”

Nat stared at him.

Steve stared back.

Then Nat looked at Chloe.

Then back at Steve.

“Oh my God.”

“What?”

“You really don't know.”

“Know what?”

Chloe said, “Nat.”

“I haven't done anything.”

“You're about to.”

“You don't know that.”

“I've known you for eleven years.”

“That seems circumstantial.”

Tori finally looked up.

“It is not.”

Steve leaned back slightly.

There was something happening again.

Apparently this table had several simultaneous conversations occurring beneath the audible one, and everyone except him had received documentation.

He didn't like undocumented systems.

“What exactly am I missing?”

Chloe looked at him.

“Nothing.”

Nat made a noise.

Chloe didn't even turn.

“Make another sound.”

Nat pressed her lips together.

Maddie whispered, “She's going to make another sound.”

Nat made another sound.

Chloe closed her eyes.

Steve laughed.

He couldn't help it.

“You people are ridiculous.”

Five faces turned toward him.

Steve realized what he'd said.

“Sorry.”

Tori's eyebrows rose.

Nat looked offended.

Sofia looked delighted.

Maddie said, “Oh, good.”

Steve frowned. “What?”

“Nothing.”

“No. I've learned what ‘nothing’ means at this table.”

“It means something,” Maddie said.

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome.”

Chloe's mouth had curved again.

Steve looked around the table.

“Okay. Fine. Since apparently I'm under investigation, can I at least know the charges?”

Nat sat up.

“I like him.”

“Nat,” Chloe said.

“What? I do.”

“That is not helping.”

“It wasn't supposed to.”

Steve pointed at Nat.

“See, that's honest. I appreciate that.”

Nat pointed back.

“Thank you.”

“Don't encourage her,” Tori said.

“Too late,” Sofia said.

Tori sighed.

Steve grinned.

This was—

Well.

This was actually kind of fun.

Which was irritating.

He had come here to have one drink, establish that Brittney was wrong, and leave with documentary evidence.

Instead he'd somehow become involved in a committee hearing where nobody respected the chair and he wasn't entirely certain who the chair was.

Probably Tori.

Except nobody appeared to listen to Tori.

Possibly Chloe.

Except Nat seemed constitutionally opposed to governance.

Sofia could probably run the entire table if she wanted to, which meant she probably didn't.

Maddie was drawing something again.

Steve leaned toward the napkin.

“What is that?”

“Dennis.”

“The tax accountant?”

Maddie looked pleased.

“You were listening.”

“I have several questions.”

“Don't,” Tori said.

Steve looked at her.

There was genuine warning in her eyes.

He looked back at the napkin.

Dennis now had a briefcase.

And wings.

Steve sat back.

“Understood.”

“Coward,” Maddie said.

“I've known you nine minutes.”

“And already you've abandoned Dennis.”

“I never accepted responsibility for Dennis.”

“Typical.”

Steve looked at Tori.

“You did this.”

Tori's composure cracked.

Just slightly.

“I did nothing.”

“You warned me.”

“I attempted to save you.”

“From the horse?”

“Pegasus,” Maddie said.

“Tax accountant,” Nat added.

Sofia put her head down on Chloe's shoulder and started laughing.

Steve looked at Chloe.

Chloe looked back.

There was something very strange about her eyes.

Not strange.

Focused.

She had been doing that periodically since he'd sat down.

Looking at him like—

No.

He wasn't going to invent things.

Chloe was a lesbian.

Chloe had four girlfriends.

Chloe was being friendly.

Nat was insane.

Those were the available facts.

He took another drink.

“So how do you all know each other?”

“Oh, no,” Nat said. “You don't get to reverse the interrogation.”

“Why not?”

“Because I'm better at it.”

“That remains unproven.”

Nat sat forward.

“Oh, we're doing this?”

“We were apparently already doing this.”

“Fine. Relationship status.”

Steve blinked.

“That escalated.”

“Answer the question.”

“Single.”

“How long?”

“Objection,” Tori said.

Nat looked at her.

“On what grounds?”

“Relevance.”

“Chloe.”

Tori paused.

“Withdrawn.”

Chloe stared at her.

“Tori.”

“What?”

“You're supposed to be the responsible one.”

“I am an officer of the court, not your mother.”

Maddie looked up.

“You'd be a very pretty mother.”

“Thank you, darling.”

Steve glanced between them.

That was the first time he'd seen Tori's entire face change.

Not dramatically.

But enough.

The attorney vanished for a second.

There was just a woman looking at Maddie like Maddie had said something wonderful instead of completely nonsensical.

Then Tori caught Steve watching.

The attorney returned.

“Continue.”

Steve smiled.

Tori narrowed her eyes.

He liked her too.

Shit.

Not like that.

Just—

They were interesting.

All of them.

“What was the question?” he asked.

“How long have you been single?”

Steve shrugged.

“A while.”

Nat waited.

Steve waited.

Nat continued waiting.

“You know,” Steve said, “silence isn't actually a question.”

“Works, though.”

“Not on me.”

“Three years?”

“No.”

“Five?”

“No.”

“Ten?”

Steve took a drink.

Nat's eyes widened.

“Oh, honey.”

“Don't.”

Sofia reached across and smacked Nat lightly on the arm.

“He said don't.”

“I heard him.”

“Then don't.”

Nat stopped.

Immediately.

Steve noticed that.

No argument.

No joke.

Just stopped.

Interesting.

“Sorry,” Nat said.

And she meant it.

That was interesting too.

Steve shrugged.

“It's fine.”

“No,” Nat said. “I pushed.”

“You did.”

“I'll do it again.”

Tori sighed.

“But not about that.”

Steve looked at her.

Nat smiled.

“Deal?”

He considered her.

“Deal.”

Something relaxed.

Not just Nat.

The table.

It was subtle, but Steve felt it.

As if he'd passed some kind of test by telling her to knock it off.

Which made absolutely no sense.

“Okay,” Sofia said. “Our turn.”

Steve looked at her.

“Your turn?”

“You asked how we met.”

“Oh.”

“College,” Chloe said.

“Sorority,” Nat added.

Steve looked at Tori.

“You were in a sorority?”

Tori stared at him.

“What exactly about me makes that surprising?”

“Nothing.”

“That was too fast.”

“I reassessed.”

“You panicked.”

“Those are not mutually exclusive.”

Maddie leaned across the table.

“Tori was terrifying.”

“I was not.”

“You wore pearls.”

“I still wear pearls.”

“Exactly.”

Steve looked at Maddie.

“And you?”

“Tori hated me.”

“I did not hate you.”

“You thought I was unserious.”

“You attended a chapter meeting wearing two different shoes.”

“One was more comfortable.”

“You changed which foot it was on halfway through.”

“The other foot became jealous.”

Steve looked at Tori.

Tori looked at Steve.

There was a long silence.

Finally Steve said, “You married this?”

Maddie gasped.

Tori's mouth fell open.

Nat slapped the table.

Sofia made a noise somewhere between a shriek and a laugh.

And Chloe—

Chloe doubled over.

Steve realized his mistake.

“Wait. Are you married?”

“No,” Tori managed.

“Okay, good. Because that would have been incredibly awkward.”

“You think?”

“I made an inference.”

“You invented a fact.”

“I had supporting evidence.”

“You had no evidence.”

“I had pearls.”

“That is not evidence!”

Maddie was delighted.

“You sound like us.”

Steve stopped.

It was a little sentence.

Probably meaningless.

Maddie was already looking at Dennis again.

But something about it caught.

*You sound like us.*

Steve looked around the table.

Nat was still laughing.

Tori was telling Maddie that footwear jealousy was not a legally cognizable defense.

Sofia had stolen Chloe's drink.

Chloe stole it back without interrupting her conversation.

Nobody seemed concerned that three conversations were happening at once.

Nobody seemed concerned that people were interrupting.

Nobody seemed concerned about much of anything.

Steve knew this.

Not *them*.

This.

The rhythm.

Brittney telling a story while Liz argued with an aunt across the table. Somebody getting up to bring more food nobody had asked for. Three people yelling about something stupid and then immediately coordinating who was driving Grandma home.

Argument without danger.

Insults without injury.

Noise without hostility.

He knew how to do this.

Apparently he had known the entire time.

“You okay?”

Steve looked up.

Sofia.

Not staring.

Not making a production out of it.

Just asking.

“Yeah.”

She held his eyes a moment.

Then smiled.

“Okay.”

And went back to telling Nat she was wrong about something.

No follow-up.

No demand.

Steve liked that.

He liked—

Fuck.

He was liking entirely too many things about this evening.

He checked his beer.

Still the first one.

Technically.

He had not violated the agreement.

Yet.

Chloe stood.

Steve looked up.

She held out her hand.

“Come on.”

He looked at the hand.

Then at her.

“Where?”

“Dance.”

Steve laughed.

Chloe didn't.

“Oh.”

“That's your answer?”

“I don't dance.”

“You have legs.”

“That is an extremely low qualification standard.”

“Blush is inclusive.”

Nat made a choking noise.

Chloe pointed at her without looking.

“Don't.”

Nat raised both hands.

Steve looked toward the dance floor.

Then back at Chloe.

“I really don't.”

“Neither does Nat.”

“Fuck you,” Nat said.

“You jump rhythmically.”

“I dance.”

“You've injured people.”

“One person.”

“Two,” Sofia said.

“The second one was drunk.”

“So were you.”

“That's irrelevant.”

Steve looked at Chloe's hand again.

This was a terrible idea.

He had no idea what he was doing.

She was going to discover this approximately six seconds after they got out there.

Which meant—

Actually.

Fine.

Good.

Useful.

The evening had become dangerously successful.

A little controlled failure might restore equilibrium.

Steve put his hand in hers.

“Fine.”

Chloe smiled.

“Fine.”

She led him away from the table.

Behind them, Nat waited exactly until they were out of conversational range.

Then:

“What the fuck?”

Tori took a slow drink.

Sofia turned in her chair so she could see the dance floor.

Maddie picked up Dennis.

Nat looked around the table.

“Are none of you going to say it?”

“Say what?” Sofia asked.

“Oh, bullshit.”

Tori set down her glass.

“I assume you're about to tell us.”

“Chloe is dancing with a straight girl.”

Tori's eyebrows rose.

“Evidence?”

Nat pointed toward the dance floor.

“That.”

“That establishes neither heterosexuality nor gender.”

“Her name is Steve.”

Tori stared at her.

“And?”

“And it's *Steve*.”

“Women can be named Steve.”

“Name one.”

“Steve.”

Nat looked offended.

“You cannot use the disputed person as your example.”

“I just did.”

Sofia was still watching the dance floor.

“She's cute.”

Nat turned.

“Whose side are you on?”

“There are sides?”

“There are always sides.”

“That's because you make them.”

Maddie looked up.

“I don't think she's straight.”

Nat pointed triumphantly.

“Thank you.”

“I think she's a lighthouse.”

Silence.

Tori closed her eyes.

Nat slowly lowered her hand.

Sofia looked at Maddie.

“A lighthouse.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Maddie shrugged.

“She keeps telling everybody where the rocks are.”

Tori opened her eyes.

For perhaps half a second, something passed across her face.

Then it was gone.

Nat pointed at Maddie.

“We are not discussing maritime infrastructure.”

“You asked.”

“I absolutely did not.”

Sofia laughed.

Then she looked back toward the dance floor.

Chloe had one hand in Steve's.

The other rested lightly at his waist.

And Chloe was leading.

That, by itself, meant nothing.

Chloe liked leading.

Chloe liked knowing what she was doing.

Chloe liked—

Sofia's smile changed.

“Oh.”

Nat caught it instantly.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Fuck you. What?”

Sofia nodded toward the dance floor.

Nat turned.

Tori turned.

Maddie was already watching.

Steve had been right.

He did not know how to dance.

At least not at first.

For the first few measures he was visibly thinking about it.

Watching his feet.

Trying to anticipate Chloe instead of following her.

Chloe said something.

Steve answered.

Chloe said something else.

Steve rolled his eyes.

Then Chloe laughed.

And Steve stopped trying.

It happened almost all at once.

His shoulders loosened.

His attention moved from his feet to Chloe.

She guided him.

He followed.

No contest.

No awkward attempt to take over.

No visible concern about who was supposed to be doing what.

Chloe turned him.

Steve came back laughing.

Nat's mouth opened.

Sofia said, very softly, “Yeah.”

Tori's expression had become unreadable.

Maddie smiled.

“I told you.”

“You said lighthouse,” Nat whispered.

“I know.”

“That doesn't mean anything.”

“It does to me.”

Nat looked back at the dance floor.

Chloe was smiling in a way Nat knew very well.

“Oh, shit.”

Tori looked at her.

“What?”

Nat pointed.

“That's the face.”

“What face?”

“Chloe's face.”

“She has several.”

“Not that one.”

Sofia laughed quietly.

“Oh, that's definitely the face.”

Tori looked again.

Longer this time.

Then:

“Oh.”

Nat slapped the table once.

“THANK YOU.”

“Keep your voice down.”

“Why? We're in a nightclub.”

“Because Chloe will murder you.”

“Worth it.”

Sofia tilted her head.

“She's having fun.”

“That's not the problem.”

“Why is that a problem?”

“Because,” Nat said, with the patience of someone explaining gravity to a child, “Chloe Bennett has never touched a penis recreationally in her entire life.”

Tori nearly inhaled her drink.

Sofia slapped a hand over her mouth.

Maddie frowned.

“You don't know that.”

Nat stared at her.

“What?”

“You said recreationally.”

Tori put her glass down very carefully.

“No.”

Maddie continued.

“There are medical reasons—”

“No.”

“Accidents—”

“No.”

“Maybe somebody handed her one once.”

Sofia collapsed against the table.

Nat looked horrified.

“Who is handing out penises?”

“I don't know. I've never been to medical school.”

Tori was laughing now.

Actually laughing.

Head down, one hand over her eyes.

Nat looked around at all of them.

“You are deliberately avoiding the point.”

Sofia managed, “Your point was Chloe's gold-star status.”

“Thank you.”

“Which is an incredibly stupid thing to care about.”

“I don't care about it!”

“You brought it up.”

“Because it's funny!”

Tori recovered enough to speak.

“Gold-star lesbianism is an absurd and occasionally offensive cultural artifact.”

“Yes.”

“Sexual history does not determine the legitimacy of someone's orientation.”

“Yes.”

“And Chloe's attraction to one person would not require her to surrender her identity.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Nat leaned toward her.

“But it would be fucking hilarious.”

Tori considered this.

“Yes.”

Nat sat back triumphantly.

“Thank you.”

Sofia shook her head.

“You're terrible.”

“You love me.”

“I do.”

“Therefore I'm right.”

“That isn't how love works.”

“It's how Sales works.”

From the dance floor, Chloe turned Steve again.

This time he didn't even hesitate.

He followed her, came back close, and said something that made Chloe throw her head back laughing.

The table went quiet.

Not suspicious.

Not anymore.

Something else.

Sofia saw it first because Sofia usually did.

Chloe wasn't merely flirting.

She liked him.

Already.

Maybe not enormously.

Maybe not importantly.

Maybe this would be one funny night and a story they'd tell later.

But Chloe Bennett was interested.

And the person in her arms looked happier than he'd looked since sitting down.

Sofia watched Steve's face.

There was no calculation there.

No conquest.

No straight guy who'd wandered into a lesbian bar hoping to get lucky.

If anything, Steve seemed faintly bewildered that Chloe continued to be interested in him.

“Nat,” Sofia said.

Nat looked over.

“Ease up when they come back.”

Nat's expression changed.

“I wasn't—”

“Yeah.”

A beat.

Nat nodded.

“Okay.”

Tori glanced at Sofia.

Sofia shrugged.

Maddie folded the napkin with Dennis on it and placed it carefully in the center of the table.

“For evidence.”

Tori looked at it.

“Of what?”

“We'll know later.”

Tori stared at her.

Then, despite herself:

“Fine.”

---

Steve had made an important discovery.

He did not hate dancing.

He hated not knowing what he was supposed to do while dancing.

Those were apparently different things.

Chloe had solved the problem by telling him to stop trying to lead.

“I wasn't trying to lead.”

“You were trying to predict me.”

“That's different.”

“It isn't when you keep stepping where I'm about to step.”

“I was modeling your behavior.”

“You were stepping on my foot.”

“Once.”

“Twice.”

“The second time was statistically related to the first.”

“That sentence means nothing.”

“It means I was already off balance.”

“It means you're bad at dancing.”

“I told you that before we started.”

“And yet.”

She turned him.

Steve followed.

Easily this time.

When he came back, she was smiling.

“See?”

He hated that smile.

Mostly because he liked it.

“Don't.”

“You're dancing.”

“I'm being moved.”

“That's dancing.”

“That's kidnapping with music.”

Chloe laughed.

Steve smiled.

This was ridiculous.

He was dancing with an extraordinarily beautiful lesbian in a lesbian bar after being sent there by his lesbian sister and her lesbian wife because they thought he needed to get out more.

And—

Wait.

Chloe's hand was on his waist.

His hand was in hers.

She had asked him to dance.

Nat had yelled that Chloe was flirting with him.

Steve had dismissed that because Nat appeared to have been assembled in a laboratory to create problems.

But Chloe was looking at him again.

That look.

Focused.

Warm.

Interested.

Oh.

Oh, shit.

Steve missed a step.

Chloe caught him.

“Hey.”

“Sorry.”

“You okay?”

“Yeah.”

“You sure?”

Steve looked at her.

Really looked.

Chloe waited.

No joke this time.

He nodded.

“Yeah.”

She accepted that.

Then the music changed.

Slower, though not slow enough to create a crisis.

Probably.

Chloe didn't let go.

Steve's stomach did something stupid.

That was inconvenient.

Because Chloe was a lesbian.

Which meant—

Well.

There were several possibilities.

None of them were particularly comfortable.

Maybe she knew.

Maybe she didn't.

Maybe she thought—

Steve looked down at himself.

Then back at Chloe.

Oh.

Fuck.

People did.

All the time.

And he'd walked into a lesbian bar.

And Chloe had met him here.

And Chloe was flirting with him.

And he had let her.

Not knowingly.

But now he knew there was at least a possibility.

Which changed things.

Steve felt the evening shift under his feet.

Not catastrophically.

Just enough.

A bad assumption had entered the system.

And now that he'd noticed it, leaving it uncorrected would be a choice.

Chloe smiled.

“You disappeared.”

“What?”

“You went somewhere.”

“Sorry.”

“Don't apologize. Just come back.”

Steve looked at her.

Something about that sentence landed harder than it had any business landing.

So he did.

For the rest of the song.

But as Chloe led him back toward the table, Steve knew he had a problem.

He liked these women.

He liked Chloe.

And if Chloe thought he was someone he wasn't, she deserved to know before either of those things went any further.
