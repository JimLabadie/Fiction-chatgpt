# Chapter One
## The Door

“You’re going to a lesbian bar.”

Steve kept chopping the onion.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Steven.”

“Brittney.”

Across the kitchen table, Liz snorted.

Steve pointed the knife at her.

“Don’t encourage her.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You made a sound.”

“I’m allowed to make sounds in my own house.”

“This is not your house.”

Liz looked around Steve’s kitchen.

“Then why am I eating your food?”

“That’s not how property works.”

“It is in Puerto Rico.”

“You were born in Massachusetts.”

“Racist.”

Steve stared at her.

Liz stared back.

Brittney lasted perhaps three seconds before she started laughing.

Steve returned to the onion.

This was why he had originally suggested dinner at Brittney and Liz’s place. There were tactical advantages to being able to leave.

Unfortunately, he'd also made the mistake of mentioning that he'd been experimenting with a new chicken recipe, which had resulted in Brittney inviting herself over before he'd actually finished the sentence.

Liz had apparently come with the marriage.

Not that he minded.

Mostly.

“Anyway,” Brittney said, “you’re going.”

“I’m not.”

“You need to get out.”

“I am out.”

“You’re chopping an onion.”

“I went to the grocery store.”

“This is exactly what I mean.”

“I also went to Home Depot.”

Brittney looked at Liz.

Liz nodded gravely. “Big day.”

“Oh, fuck both of you.”

Steve scraped the onion into the pan.

The chicken had been marinating since that morning. He'd found the recipe at eleven-thirty the previous night while trying to determine why the garbage collector in a programming language he didn't even use behaved differently under memory pressure on ARM processors.

That investigation had somehow led to a discussion of Korean fried chicken.

Which led to gochujang.

Which led to a cooking blog.

Which led to the realization that the woman writing the blog was using entirely too much sugar in her marinade.

So he'd fixed it.

This was a perfectly normal way to spend a Friday night.

Brittney disagreed.

Brittney was wrong.

“What’s wrong with Blush?” she asked.

“Nothing is wrong with Blush.”

“Then go.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Steve turned around.

“Because it’s a lesbian bar.”

“Yes.”

“And I’m not a lesbian.”

Liz coughed into her beer.

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was a fucking suspicious cough.”

“I swallowed wrong.”

“You weren't drinking.”

Liz picked up her beer and took a deliberate sip.

“There. Better?”

“No.”

Brittney had developed the expression she got when Steve was being particularly difficult.

He knew the expression well.

It was approximately eighty percent affection and twenty percent contemplating fratricide.

Technically sororicide wasn't applicable, despite what apparently half the population of Massachusetts believed on sight.

He had looked it up once.

Not because it bothered him.

Because he'd wanted the correct word.

There wasn't one.

English was stupid.

“Steve,” Brittney said.

“I'm listening.”

“No, you're cooking defensively.”

He looked down.

He was stirring the onions rather harder than necessary.

“That isn't a thing.”

“It is now.”

“They were sticking.”

“They weren't.”

“You're a nurse, not an onion specialist.”

Liz lifted her beer. “She's got you there.”

“Thank you.”

“Still going to Blush.”

Steve closed his eyes.

The onions smelled fantastic.

His sister was an asshole.

These things could both be true.

“Explain to me,” he said, turning the heat down, “why you think sending your thirty-five-year-old brother to a lesbian bar is a productive use of anyone's evening.”

Brittney smiled.

That bothered him immediately.

Not a big smile.

A little one.

A private one.

Steve pointed the wooden spoon at her.

“No.”

“I haven't said anything.”

“I know that face.”

“You know every face I have.”

“Exactly. That's the face where you know something you think I don't know.”

Liz became fascinated by the label on her beer.

Steve looked between them.

“Oh, fuck off. Both of you.”

Brittney laughed.

“I'm serious.”

“So am I.”

“About what?”

“Going out.”

“I go out.”

“You go to work.”

“I go other places.”

“Name three.”

“Home Depot.”

“That was already one.”

“The grocery store.”

“Already one.”

“Brittney's house.”

“I am Brittney.”

“I know. I've known you for thirty-five years.”

“Thirty-five years and you still don't know how lists work.”

“I write software. I know how lists work.”

“Then name three places you voluntarily go where nobody is paying you, selling you something, or related to you.”

Steve opened his mouth.

Nothing useful happened.

Liz took another drink.

He pointed at her without looking.

“Not one fucking word.”

“I am drinking my beer.”

“Judgmentally.”

“You're very sensitive tonight.”

“I am being ambushed in my own kitchen.”

Brittney's grin widened.

Steve turned back to the stove.

This was ridiculous.

His life was fine.

That was the part Brittney seemed constitutionally incapable of accepting.

He had a good job. He made good money. His apartment was clean. He cooked. He had hobbies. He talked to Brittney all the time, whether he wanted to or not.

He had friends.

Probably.

There were people at work.

Some of them had been to his apartment.

One had helped him move a couch.

That counted.

He wasn't unhappy.

He wished people would stop treating *not unhappy* like he'd confessed to a terminal disease.

“I'm fine,” he said.

Brittney didn't answer.

That made him look at her.

She wasn't smiling now.

Not upset.

Just watching him.

And that was worse.

“Don't,” Steve said.

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“No, I wasn't.”

“You were thinking something.”

“I'm usually thinking something.”

“Debatable.”

Liz choked on her beer.

Brittney turned on her wife.

“Excuse me?”

“Nothing, baby.”

“Oh, now you're staying out of it?”

“I've been out of it.”

“You called him a hermit twenty minutes ago.”

“I said he has hermit tendencies.”

“Thank you,” Steve said. “That's completely different.”

Liz pointed at him. “See? He gets me.”

“I regret marrying into this family.”

Steve and Liz answered together.

“No, you don't.”

There was a beat.

Liz looked at him.

Steve looked at Liz.

Brittney looked between them.

Then Liz reached across the counter and bumped her fist against Steve's shoulder.

“Damn right.”

And that was that.

Because that was how things worked.

You could be irritated. You could argue. You could tell somebody they were being an asshole. Brittney could announce that Steve was wasting his life because he'd spent Friday night reading about memory allocation and chicken.

Nobody was leaving.

Nobody ever meant *that*.

Steve checked the chicken.

“Twenty minutes.”

Brittney got up and opened his refrigerator.

“Hey.”

“I want wine.”

“Ask.”

“Can I have wine?”

“No.”

She took the bottle anyway.

“Why do I bother?”

“Because Mom raised you right.”

“Mom raised *you* right too.”

“Five years later. She was tired.”

That got him.

Steve laughed despite himself.

Brittney kissed the top of his head on the way past.

He immediately ducked away.

“Jesus Christ.”

“You love me.”

“Debatable.”

“No, it isn't.”

No.

It wasn't.

---

Dinner should have ended the conversation.

It did not.

Steve had badly underestimated Brittney's commitment to being annoying.

By the time Liz was stealing the last piece of chicken off his plate, Brittney had returned to Blush three times.

“No.”

“You haven't even heard my latest argument.”

“You haven't made an argument. You've issued a directive.”

“Fine. My argument is that you'll have fun.”

“That is speculation.”

“You like women.”

Steve stared at her.

“Yes.”

“Blush has women.”

“Lesbians.”

“Those are women.”

“Thank you, Brittney. I'm glad nursing school covered taxonomy.”

Liz made the mistake of laughing with food in her mouth.

Steve waited while she recovered.

Brittney continued.

“You don't have to hit on anybody.”

“Excellent, because that would be creepy.”

“Why?”

Steve put down his fork.

“Because they're lesbians.”

Brittney looked at him.

Liz looked at him.

Steve looked back.

“What?”

Brittney's mouth twitched.

“Nothing.”

“No. Absolutely not. You don't get to do that.”

“Do what?”

“That.”

“I am sitting here.”

“You're sitting there *meaningfully*.”

Liz muttered, “He's sensitive to sitting.”

“Thank you, Elizabeth.”

“Don't government-name me in front of my wife.”

“Your wife was at your wedding.”

“Still.”

Brittney leaned forward.

“Steve. Go have a drink.”

“At a lesbian bar.”

“Yes.”

“Why that bar?”

“Because I like it.”

“You've been there twice.”

“Three times.”

“Congratulations.”

“And because Liz and I know people who go there.”

That was new.

Steve frowned.

“Who?”

“People.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It is technically an answer.”

“It's semantically useless.”

“Jesus Christ.”

“You married a nurse,” Steve told Liz. “You could have stopped this.”

Liz shrugged. “I like her tits.”

Brittney slapped her arm.

Steve dropped his forehead into his hands.

“For fuck's sake.”

“What?” Liz said. “We're lesbians. Apparently that's relevant tonight.”

“I hate this family.”

“No, you don't,” Brittney and Liz said together.

Steve looked up.

They were both grinning.

Assholes.

He loved them.

Which was the problem with family.

It made murder complicated.

“All right,” he said.

Brittney stopped smiling.

Steve pointed at her.

“One drink.”

Her smile came back.

“One.”

“Sure.”

“Do not ‘sure’ me like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like you're already changing the terms.”

“I haven't changed anything.”

“I go there. I have one drink. I demonstrate conclusively that this is a stupid idea. I come home.”

“Absolutely.”

“And then you stop.”

Brittney tilted her head.

“Stop what?”

“This.”

“Define this.”

“Trying to fix my social life.”

Her expression changed again.

Only a little.

Steve almost missed it.

Almost.

“I'm not trying to fix you,” she said.

He frowned.

“I said my social life.”

“I know.”

Something sat there for half a second.

Then Liz reached over and stole the last roasted potato from Brittney's plate.

Brittney gasped.

“You fucking bitch.”

And the moment was gone.

---

An hour later Steve stood in front of his closet wondering how the hell he'd lost control of his evening.

One drink.

That was all.

This was not a date.

It was not even socializing, technically.

It was field research.

No.

That sounded worse.

Proof of concept.

Also worse.

Fine.

He was going to a bar.

People went to bars.

Men went to bars.

Men definitely went to—

He stopped.

“Don't.”

The empty apartment did not answer.

“Not doing that tonight.”

He pulled out a button-down.

Looked at it.

Put it back.

Too fitted.

He pulled out another.

Too—

He stared at it.

Too what?

It was a shirt.

He put it back.

Black jeans.

Fine.

Dark blue shirt.

Fine.

Boots.

Fine.

He dressed, checked the mirror, and immediately became irritated.

There was nothing wrong with what he was wearing.

Nothing.

The jeans fit.

The shirt fit.

His hair—

His hair was doing that thing.

He ran his fingers through it.

Worse.

Again.

Better.

He stepped back.

A small, pretty woman stared at him from the mirror.

Steve sighed.

“Yeah, yeah.”

This was not news.

At five-six and a hundred and five pounds, Steve had long ago accepted that God, genetics, or some bored cosmic bureaucrat had fucked up the paperwork.

People saw what they saw.

He knew what he was.

He adjusted the collar.

The shirt really was well made.

Single-needle stitching. Good placket. Somebody had actually bothered matching the pattern across the pocket, which shouldn't have been remarkable but apparently basic craftsmanship was now a premium feature.

He caught himself.

“It's a shirt.”

The mirror remained unconvinced.

Steve grabbed his jacket.

---

Blush announced itself in purple.

Purple neon washed across the wet pavement outside, turning the sidewalk into something more cinematic than the neighborhood had any right to be.

Steve sat in his car across the street.

He had been sitting there for seven minutes.

This was not hesitation.

It was reconnaissance.

There was a difference.

People came and went.

Women, mostly.

Unsurprisingly.

Two women walked toward the entrance holding hands. Another three spilled out laughing hard enough that one had to grab the wall. A woman in a leather jacket held the door for someone in a tiny red dress.

Nobody appeared to be conducting lesbian inspections.

That was encouraging.

Or disappointing.

Depending on whether he wanted an excuse to leave.

He checked the time.

He could text Brittney.

*Line too long.*

There wasn't a line.

*Parking sucks.*

He was parked.

*Closed for private event.*

There were literally people walking in.

He could lie.

Brittney would know.

Worse, Liz would know, and Liz would never say anything. She'd just look at him next Sunday.

No.

Fuck that.

Steve got out of the car.

One drink.

He would walk inside, order something, stay a respectable amount of time, discover that—as predicted—he had no particular reason to be there, and report his findings to Brittney.

Then this would be over.

Simple.

The woman at the door glanced up as he approached.

Steve braced himself for—

“Hey.”

He stopped.

“Hey.”

She smiled.

“Have a good night.”

And opened the door.

That was it.

Steve stood there half a second too long.

She raised an eyebrow.

“You going in?”

“Yes.”

“Door works better if you walk through it.”

“Right.”

She was smiling now.

Steve went inside.

Music hit first—not brutally loud, but enough that the room had its own pulse.

Then light.

Warm bodies.

Glass.

Laughter.

Color.

The place was busy without feeling packed, polished without feeling precious. There were women everywhere.

Which, yes.

Lesbian bar.

He had successfully identified the concept.

Steve moved toward the bar.

Nobody stopped him.

Nobody stared.

Nobody asked him what he was doing there.

A woman glanced at him, smiled, then looked away.

Another moved aside so he could pass.

Someone behind him said, “Sorry, babe,” after bumping his shoulder.

Steve reached the bar.

The bartender looked over.

“What can I get you?”

Steve ordered a beer.

She set it down.

He paid.

And just like that, he had fulfilled the terms of the agreement.

He was inside Blush.

He had a drink.

He had conclusively—

Someone laughed behind him.

Not at him.

A big, unrestrained laugh, followed immediately by another woman's voice.

“You cannot possibly believe that.”

“I don't have to believe it. I said it confidently.”

“That is not how facts work.”

“That's how Sales works.”

Steve turned before he could stop himself.

Five women occupied a table a few yards away.

The blonde currently defending Sales looked entirely too pleased with herself.

Across from her, a dark-haired woman in an immaculate jacket had the expression of an attorney contemplating whether homicide could be billed to a client.

Beside her, another woman was drawing something on a cocktail napkin.

Actually—

Steve squinted.

Was that a horse?

No.

A horse with wings?

The woman drawing it looked up suddenly and met his eyes.

Steve looked away.

Shit.

Great.

Excellent.

Now he was the weird guy staring at lesbians in the lesbian bar.

Perfect execution, Steven.

He took a drink.

Behind him, the conversation continued.

“Sales does not create facts.”

“Sales creates *revenue*, Victoria. Revenue buys facts.”

“That sentence is offensive on at least three levels.”

“Only three?”

A different woman spoke.

Lower.

Dry.

“Nat, if you explain capitalism to the attorney again, I'm leaving you here.”

“You can't. We're in love.”

“I'll recover.”

Steve smiled into his beer.

Okay.

That was funny.

“Excuse me.”

He looked up.

A woman stood beside him.

High femme. Dark hair. Beautiful enough that Steve's brain briefly stopped doing anything useful.

She indicated the empty space beside him at the bar.

“Are you using that?”

Steve looked at the stool.

Then at her.

Then back at the stool.

“No.”

“Great.”

She took it.

Steve moved his beer slightly to give her room.

“Thanks.”

“No problem.”

She looked toward the table.

The blonde—Nat, apparently—was now gesturing emphatically with both hands while the attorney looked increasingly offended by the existence of commerce.

The woman beside Steve sighed.

“I leave for thirty seconds.”

Steve glanced over.

“What happened?”

“Sales.”

He laughed.

She looked at him.

Not offended.

Interested.

Steve nodded toward the table.

“I'm a software engineer.”

Understanding dawned instantly.

“Oh.”

“Yeah.”

Her mouth curved.

“My condolences.”

“Thank you.”

“I’m a project manager.”

Steve stared at her.

She stared back.

Neither spoke.

Then Steve slowly picked up his beer.

“So we're enemies.”

“Traditionally.”

“That's unfortunate.”

“Very.”

A beat.

From the table, Nat shouted:

“CHLOE, STOP FLIRTING WITH STRANGERS AND COME DEFEND CAPITALISM.”

Chloe didn't look away from Steve.

“I don't defend capitalism.”

Steve glanced toward Nat.

“Sales does.”

“Sales would sell capitalism a subscription to capitalism.”

Steve laughed before he could stop himself.

Chloe smiled.

Not broadly.

Just enough.

Then she looked at his beer.

“One drink?”

Steve blinked.

“What?”

“You have the posture of somebody serving a sentence.”

He looked down at himself.

“I do not.”

“You absolutely do.”

“I’m standing normally.”

“Very bravely.”

Steve narrowed his eyes.

Chloe took her drink from the bartender.

“First time?”

“Here?”

“No, Steve. On Earth.”

He stared.

She stared back.

Then the corner of her mouth twitched.

Steve laughed.

“Okay. Yes. Here.”

“Thought so.”

“How?”

“You keep looking at the door.”

“I do not.”

Chloe looked toward the door.

Steve followed her gaze automatically.

When he looked back, she was smiling.

“Oh, fuck you.”

“There he is.”

Steve paused.

Something about that landed strangely.

Not badly.

Just—

Comfortably.

As if they'd been arguing longer than four minutes.

Chloe picked up her glass.

“So why Blush?”

“My sister.”

“That sentence requires more words.”

“She thinks I need to get out more.”

“And you disagree.”

“Strongly.”

“Yet here you are.”

“I’m proving her wrong.”

Chloe considered this.

“How?”

Steve opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Looked at his beer.

Chloe waited.

That was annoying.

“I haven't worked out all the metrics yet.”

“Project-management failure.”

“Engineering hasn't delivered requirements.”

“Classic.”

From the table:

“CHLOE.”

Chloe closed her eyes.

“What?”

“You're hoarding the tiny engineer.”

Steve looked at her.

“Tiny?”

Chloe looked him up and down.

It was not subtle.

Steve straightened.

She looked back at his face.

“Would you prefer enormous?”

“Average.”

“No.”

He stared at her.

She took a drink.

Steve felt something dangerous happen.

He liked her.

Not *liked* liked her.

Jesus Christ.

He'd known her for five minutes.

But she was funny.

And gorgeous.

And irritating.

Which, unfortunately, was a combination to which he had historically demonstrated poor resistance.

Chloe nodded toward the table.

“Come sit down.”

Steve looked over.

Five—

No.

Four women now, because Chloe was standing beside him.

Nat was openly watching them.

The attorney was pretending not to.

The artist had abandoned the winged horse and was apparently taking a picture of her drink.

Another woman—beautiful, dark-haired, spectacularly feminine—caught Steve looking and smiled at him with such immediate warmth that he almost smiled back automatically.

Almost.

Then he remembered where he was.

“I don't want to intrude.”

“You won't.”

“You're here with your friends.”

“My girlfriends.”

Steve looked at Chloe.

“All of them?”

“Yes.”

He looked at the table.

Back at Chloe.

“All four?”

“Yes.”

Steve looked at the table again.

Nat waved.

The attorney gave her a look.

The artist waved too, apparently because Nat had.

The fourth woman laughed.

Steve turned back.

“Huh.”

Chloe waited.

“That seems logistically complicated.”

There was a tiny silence.

Then Chloe barked out a laugh.

Not the polished little laugh he'd expected from looking at her.

A real one.

Loud enough that the table noticed.

Steve smiled.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was definitely something.”

“No, it's just—” Chloe shook her head. “That's your question?”

“I have others.”

“I bet you do.”

“I mean, calendars alone—”

“Oh my God.”

“What?”

“You really are an engineer.”

“I told you that.”

“You did.”

Steve looked back at the table.

Four women.

All dating Chloe.

All, presumably, dating—

No.

He wasn't reverse-engineering somebody else's relationship at a bar.

Boundaries.

He had those.

Chloe watched him.

“No pressure.”

And there it was.

Simple.

No coaxing.

No challenge.

Just an invitation.

Steve looked at his beer.

One drink.

He'd promised Brittney one drink.

He hadn't promised to drink it alone.

Technically.

He looked at Chloe.

“Fine.”

Her eyebrow rose.

“Fine?”

“One drink.”

“Your parole terms are very strict.”

“You have no idea.”

Chloe turned toward the table.

Steve followed.

Nat immediately moved a chair with her foot.

Not graciously.

More like she was kicking open a door.

“Tiny Engineer!”

“My name is Steve.”

Nat froze.

The attorney froze.

The artist looked up.

The fourth woman blinked.

Chloe, behind him, made a sound that might have been a cough.

Steve stopped.

“What?”

Nat recovered first.

“Nothing.”

There was something happening.

He didn't know what.

He already disliked it.

The attorney's eyes flicked briefly toward Chloe.

Chloe gave the smallest possible shake of her head.

Steve caught that.

“What?”

“Nothing,” Chloe said.

“That was definitely something.”

The artist leaned toward him.

“Do you believe in ghosts?”

Steve stared at her.

The attorney closed her eyes.

“Maddie.”

“What? We were talking about him.”

“We absolutely were not.”

“We could have been.”

Nat pointed at Maddie. “She's right.”

“She is demonstrably not.”

The fourth woman smiled at Steve.

“I'm Sofia.”

“Steve.”

“I heard.”

“Apparently everybody did.”

“Nat doesn't have an indoor voice.”

“I have an indoor voice.”

Tori looked at her.

“This is your indoor voice.”

Nat considered that.

“Fair.”

Steve sat.

Chloe took the chair beside him.

And somehow, within thirty seconds, Nat was explaining why Sales was the only reason software engineers had jobs, Steve was explaining why this constituted hate speech, Tori was demanding both of them define their terms, Maddie had decided the winged horse was actually a tax accountant named Dennis, and Sofia was laughing hard enough to put her drink down.

Chloe leaned back in her chair.

Watching.

Steve forgot about the door.

Not permanently.

Not meaningfully.

He just—

forgot.

For a while.

And nobody at the table seemed to think he was in the wrong place.
