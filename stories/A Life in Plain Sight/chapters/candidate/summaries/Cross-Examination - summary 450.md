# Cross-Examination - Summary 450

Steve’s attempt to complete his promised single drink at Blush becomes considerably more complicated when he finds himself at a table with Chloe Bennett and her four girlfriends: Natalie Mercer, Tori Sinclair, Sofia Reyes, and Maddie Hart. Within minutes, Nat begins cheerfully interrogating him about his work and personal life, while Steve starts learning the personalities and rhythms of the group. Nat is glamorous, relentless, and nosy; Tori is an elegant attorney whose composure hides deep affection for the others; Sofia is warm and perceptive; Maddie is an artistic eccentric busy inventing a winged tax accountant named Dennis; and Chloe is a striking high-femme project manager whose focused attention keeps catching Steve off guard.

What surprises Steve most is how naturally he understands the group. Their interruptions, affectionate insults, overlapping conversations, boundary-testing, and immediate repairs feel familiar rather than chaotic because his own family operates the same way. When Nat pushes too far about how long Steve has been single, he tells her to stop. She does immediately, apologizes sincerely, and the table moves on without drama. Steve realizes he knows this language: argument without danger, insults without injury, noise without hostility. Maddie’s casual observation that he “sounds like us” lands more deeply than she intends.

Steve is increasingly irritated by another discovery: he is genuinely having fun. Chloe then asks him to dance. He initially agrees partly because he expects his inability to dance to restore the evening to its proper level of failure. Instead, Chloe teaches him to stop predicting her movements and simply follow. Once he does, Steve relaxes, laughs, and discovers he does not hate dancing at all.

Back at the table, the others watch the dance and debate what Steve’s presence means. Nat assumes Chloe is dancing with a straight woman; Tori correctly notes that neither Steve’s gender nor orientation has actually been established. Their joking speculation gradually gives way to something simpler: Chloe clearly likes Steve, and Steve appears happy, unguarded, and completely without predatory intent.

On the dance floor, Steve finally notices Chloe’s attraction. Realizing that she may believe he is a woman, he becomes uncomfortable with benefiting from an assumption she has not knowingly made. Yet Chloe gives him room rather than pressure, and Steve returns to the moment long enough to admit to himself that he is having fun. When they return to the table, he resolves to correct Chloe privately and soon. What began as a one-drink obligation has become friendship, attraction, belonging—and, in Steve’s mind, a data-integrity problem requiring immediate correction. For the first time that evening, his plan to prove Brittney wrong is no longer failing; Steve is beginning to suspect he may not want it to succeed.
