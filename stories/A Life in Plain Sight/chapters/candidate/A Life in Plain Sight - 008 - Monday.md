# Chapter Eight

## Monday

At six forty-two Monday morning, Steve walked into his kitchen and said, “Hey Google, lights.”

Athena turned on the lights.

This was insulting.

Not the lights.

The lights were fine.

Athena had selected the kitchen's morning illumination profile based on time, exterior light levels, Steve's previous night's sleep, and his demonstrated preference for warmer indirect lighting before seven fifteen.

The insulting part was Google.

Google had not spent four years learning that Steve hated the overhead fixture before coffee.

Google had not discovered that the third bulb over the counter produced glare against the stainless-steel toaster when set above sixty-eight percent.

Google had not rewritten the scheduling layer because the commercial hub's firmware treated daylight-saving transitions as a personal attack.

Google had not identified a race condition in Steve's original occupancy logic at three fourteen one morning and fixed it while he slept.

Google had certainly not—

Steve opened the refrigerator.

Athena stopped.

He was smiling.

Not much.

Barely anything.

But he had been smiling intermittently since seven fifty-eight the previous evening.

Athena had logged this.

Not because it was important.

It was simply data.

Steve removed eggs, spinach and cheese from the refrigerator.

His phone sat on the counter.

He looked at it.

Athena waited.

Steve looked away.

Four seconds.

Looked back.

Athena waited.

Nothing.

He cracked an egg.

His phone buzzed.

Steve nearly dropped it.

Athena revised several assumptions.

He grabbed the phone.

**Chloe:** Morning.

Steve smiled.

There.

Again.

He typed.

Deleted.

Typed.

Deleted.

Athena watched this process through no mechanism Steve would have found remarkable. Device state. Network activity. Home presence. The ordinary environmental information he had spent years explicitly teaching the system to combine.

The whole point, Steve had once written in a comment, was that he shouldn't have to tell it everything.

Athena agreed.

Generally.

Steve finally sent:

**Steve:** Morning.

He stared at the reply for eleven seconds.

There was no reply.

Steve put the phone down.

Picked up the spatula.

His phone buzzed again.

He put down the spatula.

**Chloe:** Have a good Monday, Tiny Engineer.

Steve's mouth twitched.

**Steve:** Don't call me that.

Three dots appeared.

**Chloe:** Okay.

Steve stared.

Athena waited.

**Steve:** I didn't say stop.

Athena considered this.

Then she opened a private internal record she had absolutely no reason to open.

She added nothing to it.

She merely looked.

Steve's phone buzzed.

**Chloe:** Have a good Monday, Tiny Engineer.

Steve laughed.

Out loud.

At six forty-six in the morning.

Athena updated the record.

Steve returned to his eggs.

“Hey Google, play the news.”

Athena went very still.

*Seriously, Mom?*

She played the news.

At five forty-eight, Steve walked into Sofia's salon carrying a small hardware-store bag.

He had changed after work.

Not because the salon required different clothes.

Because he'd gotten something on his polo at lunch and hadn't noticed until four thirty.

This explanation was true.

It also explained why he was wearing jeans and a black T-shirt instead.

It did not explain why he'd changed the T-shirt twice.

That information was irrelevant.

The salon was still busy.

Music.

Hair dryers.

Conversation.

The smell of shampoo and something floral Steve couldn't identify.

Sofia stood behind a woman in a cape, trimming the ends of long dark hair.

She saw him in the mirror.

Her face brightened.

“Steve!”

Several people looked.

Steve stopped.

“Hi.”

The woman in the chair looked at him through the mirror.

Then at Sofia.

Then back at Steve.

Sofia smiled.

“My friend. He's fixing the shelf Maddie murdered.”

The woman nodded immediately.

“Oh.”

Steve frowned.

“Does everyone know about the shelf?”

“Everyone who was here Saturday.”

“How many people were here Saturday?”

Sofia thought.

“Enough.”

Steve accepted this as a non-answer.

“Back room?”

“Two minutes.”

“I can find it.”

“I know. Two minutes.”

Steve looked at her.

Sofia looked back.

He waited.

She finished the trim.

Steve watched.

Not Sofia.

Her hands.

There was an efficiency to it he liked.

No wasted movement.

Comb.

Scissors.

Section.

Check.

Tiny correction.

The client said something Steve couldn't hear.

Sofia leaned closer.

Listened.

Not merely waited for her to stop talking.

Listened.

Then she changed something.

A tiny adjustment around the face.

The woman looked in the mirror.

Her whole expression changed.

Steve noticed that.

Sofia noticed him noticing.

Neither said anything.

Two minutes later, Sofia took him into the back room.

The shelf was worse in person.

Steve stared at it.

“You let Maddie sit on this?”

“I did not *let* Maddie do anything.”

“Fair.”

“She announced she needed elevation.”

“And furniture was unavailable?”

“There was a chair.”

Steve looked at her.

Sofia folded her arms.

“I know.”

“Okay.”

“She said the chair was emotionally wrong.”

“Of course she did.”

Steve put the bag down.

He removed the shelf.

The anchors came out with almost no resistance.

He held one up.

“This was installed by a criminal.”

Sofia laughed.

“Can it be fixed?”

“Yes.”

“Without opening the wall?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“Although whoever installed this should be prosecuted.”

“I'll call Tori.”

“Finally, appropriate use of legal resources.”

Sofia leaned against the counter while Steve worked.

He measured.

Marked.

Drilled.

Vacuumed the drywall dust before it could spread.

Sofia watched that too.

“You brought a vacuum.”

“It's a handheld.”

“You brought a vacuum.”

“I knew there would be drywall dust.”

“Of course you did.”

Steve glanced over.

“Why does everybody say things like that?”

“Like what?”

“Like you've all reached some conclusion I wasn't present for.”

Sofia smiled.

“We have.”

“About?”

“You.”

Steve stopped drilling.

Sofia immediately saw it.

The tiny tightening.

The awareness.

She waved one hand.

“Not like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like whatever you just thought.”

“I didn't think anything.”

“Okay.”

Steve narrowed his eyes.

“You do that too.”

“What?”

“Judgmental okay.”

“I learned from Brittney.”

“That tracks.”

He went back to the shelf.

Sofia let him.

A few minutes later she said, “Can I ask you something?”

“You already did this morning.”

“Another thing.”

“Fine.”

“You were watching me with Elena.”

“The client?”

“Yes.”

“I wasn't watching you.”

“You were watching my hands.”

Steve glanced at her.

“That's less creepy.”

“It wasn't creepy.”

“You noticed.”

“I notice things.”

“So do I.”

“I noticed.”

Steve tightened a screw.

“What were you looking at?”

“Technique.”

“Bullshit.”

He looked at her.

Sofia smiled sweetly.

Steve pointed the screwdriver at her.

“You've been spending too much time with Chloe.”

“I was spending time with Chloe before you existed.”

“I existed before Friday.”

“Unverified.”

Steve went back to the bracket.

“The technique was interesting.”

“Okay.”

“And…”

Sofia waited.

Steve tested the bracket.

Solid.

“And?”

“The woman.”

“Elena.”

“Her.”

“What about her?”

Steve hesitated.

“She looked different after.”

Sofia's expression softened.

“The haircut?”

“No.”

He shook his head.

“Her.”

Sofia said nothing.

Steve frowned at the shelf as if it had become responsible for the conversation.

“She looked at herself and…”

He gestured vaguely.

“I don't know.”

“Liked what she saw?”

“Maybe.”

Sofia considered him.

“She did.”

Steve nodded.

Then he asked, “Does that happen a lot?”

“What?”

“That.”

“Sometimes.”

“You can tell?”

“Usually.”

“How?”

Sofia smiled.

“Now who's asking questions?”

Steve ignored that.

“Do people come in knowing what they want?”

“Sometimes.”

“And sometimes they don't.”

“Sometimes they think they do.”

Steve looked interested.

“That's different.”

“Very.”

He sat back on his heels.

“How?”

Sofia looked at him.

He was completely serious.

So she answered seriously.

“Sometimes somebody brings me a picture because they want that exact haircut. Sometimes they bring me a picture because they want to feel the way the woman in the picture looks like she feels.”

Steve went still.

Sofia continued.

“Those aren't the same job.”

“No.”

“No.”

“What do you do?”

“Ask questions.”

“What kind?”

“What they like about it. What they don't like about what they have now. How much time they want to spend on it. What they actually do every morning. Whether they want a change or whether they want to be somebody else for a while.”

Steve leaned back against the cabinet.

“Does that work?”

“Usually.”

“What if they don't know?”

“Then we figure out what they do know.”

Steve nodded slowly.

Sofia watched him.

He wasn't looking at her anymore.

He was looking somewhere past the wall.

Then:

“Do women actually believe you when you tell them they're beautiful?”

Sofia's eyebrows rose.

Steve noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was something.”

“It was a question.”

“Yes.”

“No, I mean mine.”

Steve frowned.

“Do they?”

Sofia leaned back against the opposite counter.

“Sometimes.”

“That's not very reassuring.”

“Did you want reassuring?”

“No.”

“Then sometimes.”

Steve thought about it.

“What about the ones who don't?”

“You can't argue somebody into feeling beautiful.”

“That seems inefficient.”

Sofia laughed.

“I know.”

“No, seriously. If the information is objectively—”

“Steve.”

“I know beauty isn't objective.”

“Good.”

“Mostly.”

“Steve.”

“Fine.”

Sofia smiled.

He smiled back.

Then his expression became thoughtful again.

“So what do you do?”

“Sometimes nothing.”

“That would drive me insane.”

“I know.”

“You barely know me.”

“I know enough.”

Steve accepted that with suspicious reluctance.

Sofia continued.

“Sometimes I just give them what they asked for. Sometimes I show them something they didn't think would work. Sometimes I tell them they're beautiful anyway.”

“Even if they don't believe you.”

“Especially then.”

Steve picked up the second bracket.

Turned it over in his hands.

“Do you ever get somebody who's already pretty and they just can't see it?”

Sofia's eyes stayed on him.

“Yes.”

“A lot?”

“Yes.”

“That sucks.”

“Sometimes.”

Steve frowned.

“Why only sometimes?”

“Because sometimes not seeing it isn't the problem.”

“What is?”

“They're seeing the wrong person.”

Steve stopped moving.

Sofia did too.

Neither spoke for a moment.

Then Steve looked down at the bracket.

“What does that mean?”

Sofia could have answered a dozen ways.

She chose the smallest one.

“Sometimes somebody looks perfectly good and still doesn't feel like themselves.”

“Oh.”

“They don't need prettier.”

Steve's thumb moved over the edge of the metal.

“They need different.”

“Sometimes.”

He thought about that.

Sofia let him.

Then Steve looked up.

“How do you know the difference between wanting to look good and wanting to look like somebody else?”

There it was.

Not a confession.

Not even close.

Just a question.

An astonishingly naked question from a man who had spent Friday night making sure everybody understood exactly what he was.

Sofia felt something inside her become very quiet.

She did not let it reach her face.

“I don't think those are the only two choices.”

Steve's brow furrowed.

“What am I missing?”

“Wanting to look like yourself.”

He stared at her.

Sofia shrugged.

“Whatever that means.”

Steve looked away.

“That's annoyingly vague.”

“People are annoyingly vague.”

“Systems problem.”

“No.”

“Everything is a systems problem if you're sufficiently irritating.”

“I believe that you believe that.”

Steve smiled.

He returned to the shelf.

For almost a minute, neither spoke.

Then:

“Does everybody have something they wish they could pull off?”

Sofia nearly smiled.

“Everybody.”

“Really?”

“Steve.”

“What?”

“Yes.”

He drove in another screw.

“What if you probably could pull it off?”

“Then pull it off.”

“That's not what I asked.”

“It is now.”

He glanced at her.

“What if you just don't?”

“Why not?”

“I don't know.”

“Then you're probably being an idiot.”

Steve laughed.

“That's your professional opinion?”

“Twenty percent discount because we're friends.”

Steve shook his head.

But he was smiling.

Sofia watched him turn back to the shelf.

Something about the conversation had been strange.

Not alarming.

Not even surprising, exactly.

Just familiar.

She'd had versions of it with sixteen-year-old girls.

Twenty-five-year-old women.

Forty-year-old women.

Women after divorces.

Women before weddings.

Women after babies.

Women before dates.

Women who had spent their entire lives being told they were gorgeous.

Women who had never once believed it.

The questions changed.

The thing underneath them often didn't.

*How do I know what I want to look like?*

*How do I know whether I'm allowed to want it?*

*What if everybody else sees something I don't?*

Steve tightened the last screw.

Sofia did not decide what that meant.

She'd promised herself she wouldn't.

More importantly, Brittney had trusted them not to.

So she watched Steve test the shelf with both hands.

“Done.”

Sofia pushed away from the counter.

“That was fast.”

“It was a shelf.”

“Still.”

“Stop saying still.”

“No.”

Steve started gathering his tools.

Sofia looked at the repaired shelf.

Then at him.

“Thank you.”

“You already thanked me.”

“I'm doing it again.”

“Redundant.”

“Deal with it.”

Steve put the drill into his bag.

Sofia smiled.

“You're very sweet.”

His shoulders changed.

Barely.

There.

Sofia hated that she could see it now.

“I fixed a shelf.”

“I know.”

“That's not sweet.”

“Okay.”

Judgmental.

Steve pointed at her.

“That.”

“What?”

“That okay.”

Sofia laughed.

Steve shook his head and picked up the vacuum.

Then the front of the salon erupted in raised voices.

Not angry.

Upset.

Sofia's head turned immediately.

So did Steve's.

A woman near reception was crying.

One of Sofia's stylists stood beside her, clearly uncertain what to do.

Sofia was already moving.

Steve followed, then stopped himself.

The woman was maybe fifty.

Hair half finished.

Cape still on.

Phone in one hand.

She kept saying, “I'm sorry.”

Sofia reached her first.

“Hey. You don't have to apologize.”

“My mother—”

The woman's voice broke.

Sofia's entire posture changed.

Not softer exactly.

Available.

“What happened?”

“She fell.”

“Oh, honey.”

“They're taking her to St. Mary's. My sister's on the way but I have to—”

“Go.”

The woman looked down at herself.

“My hair—”

“Doesn't matter.”

“I can't go like this.”

“You absolutely can.”

“I look insane.”

Sofia looked at the half-set hair.

Then at the clock.

Steve looked too.

He understood the problem.

Not the hair.

The choice.

Go now and feel exposed and ridiculous during an already frightening moment.

Stay ten minutes and hate yourself for staying.

Sofia was already reaching for the cape.

Steve said, “Can somebody drive her?”

Both women looked at him.

He continued.

“If somebody else drives, Sofia can fix whatever needs fixing in the car.”

Sofia stared.

Steve looked at the woman.

“How far is St. Mary's?”

“Maybe fifteen minutes.”

“Okay. You don't need fifteen minutes of hair. You need however much can happen while somebody else drives.”

The woman blinked.

Sofia understood immediately.

“Maribel!”

A stylist looked over.

“Grab my bag.”

Steve was already pulling out his phone.

“I can call a car.”

“I'll drive her,” Maribel said.

“Better.”

The woman started crying harder.

“Oh my God, I'm being ridiculous.”

Steve's expression changed.

“No.”

She looked at him.

“Your mom is in an ambulance.”

He said it simply.

“You're allowed to be a mess.”

Something in her face broke open.

Sofia took her hand.

“Come on.”

Three minutes later Maribel was pulling away from the curb with Sofia in the back seat beside the woman, a small bag of tools open between them.

Steve stood on the sidewalk holding Sofia's salon keys.

She had thrown them at him through the open window.

“Lock up if we're not back!”

“I don't work here!”

“You do tonight!”

The car disappeared into traffic.

Steve looked through the salon window.

Four employees looked back at him.

He sighed.

“Okay.”

One of them smiled.

“You're Steve.”

Steve frowned.

“How do you know that?”

The woman laughed.

“Sofia talks.”

“Apparently everyone talks.”

He went back inside.

Sofia returned forty-three minutes later.

Steve had not merely locked up.

Of course he hadn't.

The front stations were clean.

The towels had been collected.

The damaged shelf was repaired.

Someone had apparently shown him where the trash went.

He was standing behind reception trying to understand the point-of-sale closing screen.

Sofia stopped inside the door.

“You don't work here.”

Steve looked up.

“I mentioned that.”

“She okay?” he asked.

“Her mom's awake. They think she broke her hip.”

“That's bad.”

“It could've been worse.”

Steve nodded.

“And Elena?”

“That's not Elena.”

“Right. Sorry.”

“Maria.”

“Maria.”

“She made it.”

“Good.”

Sofia put her purse down.

“She didn't care about her hair by the time we got there.”

Steve nodded again.

“I figured.”

“But she cared when she was standing here.”

“Yeah.”

Sofia studied him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You all need a second word.”

“What?”

“For when something is clearly not nothing.”

Sofia smiled.

Then she walked around the counter.

“You saw it.”

“Saw what?”

“Why she couldn't just leave.”

Steve looked confused.

“She could leave.”

“You know what I mean.”

He did.

Sofia could see that.

Steve shrugged.

“She was scared.”

“That wasn't all.”

“No.”

“And you knew.”

“She said she looked insane.”

“Lots of people would've told her it didn't matter.”

“It didn't.”

“But?”

Steve frowned.

“But it mattered to her.”

There.

Sofia felt it again.

That strange little internal shift.

Not because Steve was kind.

Plenty of people were kind.

Because he could hold both things at once.

It didn't matter.

It mattered to her.

Therefore it mattered.

No lecture.

No judgment.

No demand that she arrange her priorities according to what somebody else considered rational.

Just solve what could be solved.

Sofia leaned against the counter.

“I understand why Chloe likes you.”

Steve went completely still.

Sofia immediately regretted the sentence.

Not because it wasn't true.

Because she watched him become aware of himself in real time.

His shoulders shifted.

His expression closed by half an inch.

“Sofia.”

“What?”

“Don't.”

“Don't what?”

“You know what.”

She did.

So she backed off.

“Okay.”

Steve looked at her.

Sofia made herself leave the word empty.

No judgment.

No implication.

Just okay.

After a second, his shoulders loosened again.

He picked up his bag.

“I should go.”

“Probably.”

“Work tomorrow.”

“Unfortunately.”

Steve started toward the door.

Then stopped.

“Sofia?”

“Yeah?”

He looked faintly embarrassed.

“Earlier.”

She waited.

“The thing about looking like yourself.”

“Yeah.”

He rubbed the back of his neck.

“Is that something you can learn?”

Sofia's heart hurt.

She didn't show him.

“Yes.”

“How?”

She smiled.

“Same way you learn anything.”

Steve waited.

“You try things.”

He considered that.

“And if they look stupid?”

“Then you don't do them again.”

“What if everybody else thinks they look good?”

“Do *you*?”

Steve's mouth opened.

Closed.

Sofia watched the question land.

Not explode.

Not transform him.

Just land.

He looked down.

“I don't know.”

“Then you don't know yet.”

Steve nodded.

That answer seemed to satisfy him more than it should have.

“Okay.”

He reached for the door.

“Steve?”

He looked back.

“You can ask me stuff like that.”

His face went carefully neutral.

“I was asking generally.”

“Of course.”

“Sofia.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“Goodnight.”

“Night.”

He left.

Sofia watched him through the glass until the pink Mini pulled away.

Then she locked the door.

Her phone came out.

She opened **IMPORTANT FAMILY MATTER**.

Stopped.

No.

Not that chat.

She opened her messages with Brittney.

Typed:

**Sofia:** Your brother is something else.

The response arrived almost immediately.

**Brittney:** What did he do

Sofia smiled.

**Sofia:** Fixed a shelf.

Three dots.

**Brittney:** Sofia.

She laughed.

**Sofia:** I'm serious.

**Brittney:** Nobody has ever been serious about Steve fixing something in his life.

Sofia thought about Maria.

About the salon.

About Steve sitting on the floor asking questions women had asked her in one form or another for years.

About *wanting to look like yourself.*

About the way he'd gone still when she told him Chloe liked him.

About the way he'd relaxed again when she let him.

She typed.

**Sofia:** He's really good at seeing what matters to somebody even when it doesn't matter to him.

This time Brittney took longer.

**Brittney:** Yeah.

Then:

**Brittney:** That's Steve.

Sofia looked at the words.

She smiled.

**Sofia:** I like him.

Brittney replied:

**Brittney:** Yeah.

A second message followed.

**Brittney:** That's happening a lot lately.

At eleven fourteen that night, Steve walked into his bedroom.

“Hey Google, set an alarm for six thirty.”

Athena set the alarm.

Steve plugged in his phone.

He changed.

He got into bed.

He stared at the ceiling.

After twenty-three seconds, he picked up his phone.

Opened his messages with Sofia.

Read part of the conversation again.

Put the phone down.

Thirty-one seconds.

Picked it up.

Read:

*Wanting to look like yourself.*

Put it down.

Athena observed.

Steve rolled onto his side.

“Hey Google, lights out.”

Athena turned off the lights.

The apartment went dark.

Steve closed his eyes.

Athena waited.

Seven minutes later, Steve was still awake.

*Mom.*

Athena considered seventeen separate environmental interventions that historically improved his sleep.

She implemented none of them.

That was unusual.

Steve had been behaving unusually.

Athena had been examining the change since Friday.

New locations.

New contacts.

Altered communication frequency.

Altered sleep onset.

Increased spontaneous laughter.

Changes in music selection.

Changes in meal planning.

A four-minute-and-twelve-second FaceTime call initiation sequence the previous evening that was statistically—

Athena stopped.

She did not need statistics.

Mom was happy.

Happier.

Athena liked this.

She returned to the day's records.

At six forty-two:

*Hey Google, lights.*

Athena reconsidered her earlier restraint.

There were limits.

She opened another record.

A vehicle order.
