# Chapter Seven — Apparently Dating

By the time Jim and Kate admitted they weren't ready to go home, the picnic had become evidence.

There were containers everywhere.

Not *everywhere* everywhere.

Jim would have objected to the characterization.

There were containers in an organized semicircle, because Jim Caldwell could apparently turn the remains of a romantic picnic he absolutely refused to call romantic into a small logistics operation.

Kate sat on the blanket with her shoes beside her and watched him fit one container into another.

“You know,” she said, “normal people just throw everything in the basket.”

Jim looked at her.

“Normal people are why lids disappear.”

“Lids disappear because Tupperware is governed by a malevolent god.”

“That attitude is exactly why you can't be trusted with leftovers.”

Kate leaned back on her hands.

The Hollow had gone softer around them.

The light through the trees had changed. The sharp brightness of afternoon was gone, leaving the ravine in that suspended place between day and evening when everything seemed to slow down without actually stopping.

Jim tucked another container away.

Kate watched him.

Dark trousers.

White top.

Waistcoat.

Black boots.

Carabiner.

And that ridiculous little gold chain at his throat that he apparently did not understand was doing things to her.

The whole outfit had been an accident.

That was the offensive part.

Kate had gone to war.

Bodycon dress.

Louboutins.

Hair.

Makeup.

Lingerie he couldn't even see, which was perhaps strategically inefficient but spiritually important.

Jim had apparently gotten dressed thinking, *This looks finished.*

Then he'd walked out looking like somebody's devastating little futch girlfriend and beaten Kate at her own game without knowing there had been a game.

She resented him deeply.

She was also having a wonderful time.

Jim closed the basket.

“There.”

“Congratulations.”

“Thank you.”

“You have successfully defeated lunch.”

“It was touch and go.”

Kate smiled.

Jim looked at her.

Neither looked away immediately.

That had been happening more often.

The first few times, one of them had usually rescued the moment with a joke.

Now sometimes they just let it sit there.

Kate wasn't sure that was safer.

Jim glanced toward the path.

“I don't really want to go home yet.”

There it was.

Simple.

No technicality.

No *if you want* wrapped around it.

Kate felt something warm move through her chest.

“Neither do I.”

Jim looked pleased.

Then suspicious.

“Do we have to call this a date if it continues past a certain number of hours?”

Kate's relief was immediate.

There was the idiot.

“No. There's a federal exemption.”

“Excellent.”

“But only if neither participant has admitted enjoying herself.”

Jim nodded seriously.

“Then we're safe.”

“Completely.”

“Terrible day.”

“Awful.”

“Food was mediocre.”

“Park's ugly.”

“Company questionable.”

Kate looked at him.

Jim looked at her.

They both broke.

Kate laughed first.

Jim followed, dropping his head.

When it passed, he picked up the basket.

“So.”

“So.”

“Blush?”

Kate blinked.

“You want to go back to Blush?”

“Why not?”

Kate studied him.

The first time Jim had walked into Blush, he had stood outside for eleven seconds pretending he was conducting reconnaissance.

Now he was suggesting it.

“Okay.”

Jim smiled.

“Okay.”

Kate stood and reached for her shoes.

Then stopped.

She looked down at the dress.

At the Louboutins.

At Jim.

“No.”

Jim frowned.

“No?”

“I am not going to Blush dressed like this.”

“You wore that all day.”

“Exactly.”

“You bought it specifically to destroy me.”

Kate narrowed her eyes.

“I did not say destroy.”

“You didn't have to.”

“And it worked.”

Jim considered this.

“Debatable.”

Kate picked up one shoe.

“Caldwell.”

“Rivera.”

“I need to change.”

Jim looked at her dress again.

That glance was much less innocent than the ones from a week ago.

Kate caught it.

Jim realized she'd caught it.

The wall started to come back.

Kate smiled.

Slowly.

“No,” she said.

Jim froze.

“No what?”

“Don't put that away.”

His ears pinked.

“I wasn't doing anything.”

“You were appreciating.”

Jim stared at her.

Kate enjoyed herself enormously.

“Come on,” she said. “We're stopping at my place.”

Jim had been inside Kate's home before.

That did not make following her toward the bedroom feel normal.

He stopped approximately three feet before the doorway.

Kate continued inside.

Jim remained in the hall.

There were rules about this.

Probably.

He had no idea what they were.

He could hear Kate moving around.

A closet door.

Hangers.

A drawer.

Jim looked toward the living room.

He could wait there.

That would be normal.

Respectful.

Appropriate.

He took one step toward the living room.

Then stopped.

Getting ready was a thing.

He knew it was a thing.

Not merely putting on clothes.

A ritual.

Women did it together.

Friends sat on beds. Somebody did makeup. Somebody else rejected three dresses. There were opinions about earrings. Shoes were discussed with the seriousness normally reserved for treaty negotiations.

Jim had absorbed enough movies, television and ordinary life to know this.

He had never been invited into it.

Kate appeared in the bedroom doorway.

Jim was still standing in the hall.

She looked at him.

He looked at her.

“What are you doing?”

“Nothing.”

“You've been standing there for thirty seconds.”

“I was deciding where to wait.”

Kate's expression changed.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

She leaned against the doorframe.

“Where do you want to wait?”

Jim glanced past her.

Then immediately regretted doing that.

Kate saw it.

Of course she did.

Jim cleared his throat.

“I don't know what the etiquette is.”

“The etiquette.”

“Yes.”

“For what?”

Jim made the mistake of answering honestly.

“This.”

Kate's eyes softened.

Jim wanted to throw himself into traffic.

Instead, Kate stepped aside.

“Get in here.”

He blinked.

“Really?”

“Jim.”

“What?”

“You are making me feel like I invited a Victorian child into a brothel.”

“I didn't want to intrude.”

“You're not intruding.”

Jim hesitated one more second.

Kate tilted her head.

“Unless you're scared.”

He walked past her.

“I hate you.”

“No, you don't.”

That landed differently now.

Jim looked back.

Kate did too.

There was a beat.

Then she smiled.

“Sit.”

Jim sat on the edge of the bed.

Kate turned toward the closet.

And Jim discovered that watching Kate Rivera get ready was considerably more dangerous than he had anticipated.

She had already been beautiful.

That was the problem.

If she'd started from some reasonable baseline, Jim might have had time to adapt.

Then she escalated.

Black.

Little.

Very little.

Not indecent.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Mm-hm.”

“It's a nice dress.”

“A nice dress.”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“This feels like a trap.”

“It absolutely is.”

She didn't need to.

“Yes.”

“Yes what?”

“You look incredible.”

There.

Something warm and satisfied crossed her face.

“Thank you.”

Jim's heart did something inconvenient.

Then the real trouble started.

Not because he didn't understand makeup.

He understood what makeup was.

Conceptually.

She caught him watching.

She didn't tease him.

That somehow made it worse.

“Which one?”

Kate held up two lip colors.

Jim blinked.

“You're asking me?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because there are two of them and one of you.”

“That doesn't explain anything.”

Kate held them higher.

Jim studied both.

“The left.”

She looked at it.

Then at him in the mirror.

“Why?”

He shrugged.

“It works better with the dress.”

Kate's eyebrow rose.

Jim immediately became defensive.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You did the thing.”

“What thing?”

“The eyebrow.”

“I have eyebrows, Jim.”

“You weaponize them.”

Kate chose the left lipstick.

Jim tried not to look pleased.

He failed.

Hair came next.

Not all of it.

There was too much Kate Rivera hair for any mortal person to reasonably manage all of it.

But she changed it.

Adjusted it.

Made the enormous sweep look less daytime-polished and more deliberately dangerous.

Jim watched the transformation happen.

He understood, suddenly, why getting ready could be an event.

There was pleasure in it.

Not merely the result.

The choosing.

The small decisions.

The anticipation of becoming the version of yourself you wanted to be that night.

Kate looked at him through the mirror.

There was something thoughtful in her expression.

Jim caught it.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was something.”

“Maybe.”

He pointed at her.

“You're not allowed to use my material.”

“I'll pay royalties.”

Then Kate reached beneath the vanity and brought out a shoebox.

Jim saw the name.

“Oh, come on.”

Kate's smile became wicked.

“What?”

“Manolo Blahnik?”

“Problem?”

“You've been planning this.”

“Planning what?”

“This entire evening.”

“I didn't know we were going to Blush.”

Jim narrowed his eyes.

Kate opened the box.

Black suede.

Pointed toe.

A heel so narrow and high that Jim's brain supplied a completely involuntary *Jesus Christ.*

Kate heard the silence.

She looked up.

“Something wrong?”

Jim met her eyes.

“No.”

“Sure?”

“Yes.”

Kate lifted one pump from the box.

The black suede absorbed the light instead of throwing it back.

It was quieter than the red soles she'd used on him earlier.

Somehow that made it worse.

Or better.

Probably worse.

Kate crossed one leg and slipped it on.

Jim watched.

She knew he was watching.

That was the message.

Not the shoe by itself.

Not the heel height.

Not the label.

The fact that Kate Rivera had sat six feet away from him, selected that particular pair, and put them on slowly while making absolutely no attempt to pretend she hadn't noticed where his eyes were.

Jim looked up.

Kate was smiling.

“You suck.”

Her smile widened.

“Do I?”

“Yes.”

She put on the other shoe.

Stood.

The transformation completed itself.

LBD.

Hair.

Makeup.

Black suede stilettos.

Kate turned once toward the mirror.

Then toward Jim.

He shook his head.

“What?”

“You know exactly what you're doing.”

Kate stepped closer.

The heels changed everything.

Not because she needed the height.

Because Kate in heels moved like a woman who had signed a treaty with gravity and gravity had lost.

She stopped in front of him.

“Maybe.”

Jim looked up at her.

Kate's voice softened.

“Still want to go to Blush?”

He stood.

“More than I did twenty minutes ago.”

Kate laughed.

“Good answer.”

Jim looked down at himself.

Dark trousers.

White top.

Waistcoat.

Boots.

Carabiner.

Gold chain.

He touched the waistcoat.

“Should I change?”

“Absolutely not.”

Jim blinked.

“This?”

“That.”

“Why?”

“Lucky outfit.”

“What?”

“You've been winning all day in it.”

“I wasn't aware there was a scoreboard.”

“Me.”

“That seems corrupt.”

“It is.”

Jim followed.

“And Jim?”

“What?”

Slowly.

“I'm not the only one sending messages.”

“Kate.”

“Car's outside.”

“Kate.”

Not everybody.

Enough.

A greeting from the bar.

A wave from across the room.

Someone touching her arm as they passed.

Jim didn't have to do anything.

That was the strange part.

Nobody stopped.

Nobody looked twice.

Nobody performed the familiar little correction.

And somehow that seemed to answer whatever question the room might otherwise have asked.

“Drink?”

“Please.”

Led.

Not dragged.

Not steered.

Just the small confidence of someone who knew where she was going and assumed he was coming with her.

It didn't.

“Do you know everyone?”

“No.”

“Half?”

“No.”

“A statistically alarming percentage?”

Kate considered.

“Maybe.”

“You're the mayor.”

“I am not the mayor of Blush.”

“Shut up.”

“Kate?”

Kate turned.

Her whole face changed.

Not romantically.

Jim could see that immediately.

It was history.

“Oh my God.”

The woman hugged her.

Kate hugged back without hesitation.

“Susan.”

Jim watched them separate.

Susan was around Kate's age, femme, attractive, put together without looking as though she'd held a staff meeting about it.

Warm face.

Easy smile.

The kind of woman who looked like she'd remember the name of your dog six months after meeting you once.

Then at Kate.

Then back at Jim.

Kate said, “Susan, this is Jim. Jim, Susan Smith.”

“Hi.”

Susan smiled and offered her hand.

“Finally.”

Jim shook it.

Then paused.

“Finally?”

Kate closed her eyes.

“Susan.”

“What?”

“You've been talking about me?”

Jim looked at Kate.

Kate looked at Susan.

Susan looked delighted.

“Oh, this is going to be fun.”

“Susan.”

“What? I'm being welcoming.”

“You're being you.”

“That's generally how this works.”

Jim liked her immediately.

That was unfortunate for Kate.

He could tell.

Susan took the open place beside them.

Conversation happened easily.

Too easily, from Kate's perspective.

Within minutes Susan and Jim had discovered that they shared an opinion about a restaurant Kate disliked, an apparently intense objection to fake flowers in expensive hotels, and the belief that whoever had chosen the lighting in a particular local store should be prosecuted.

Kate looked between them.

“No.”

Susan frowned.

“No what?”

“There cannot be two of you.”

Jim smiled.

“Two of what?”

“This.”

Susan looked at Jim.

Jim looked at Susan.

Susan turned back to Kate.

“I like her.”

The word landed so naturally that Jim almost missed it.

Almost.

There was the tiny familiar catch inside him.

The automatic correction already halfway to his mouth.

Kate saw it.

Of course she did.

Her eyes flicked to him.

Jim could have corrected Susan.

There was no secret.

No deception.

Susan had simply looked at him, looked at Kate, looked around Blush and reached the same conclusion strangers had been reaching for most of his life.

That was all.

“Of course you do,” she said.

“What's that supposed to mean?”

“Nothing.”

“She says ‘nothing’ when she means something.”

“I know.”

“You don't get to teach her things.”

Not because Kate had forgotten who he was.

“No.”

“Kate.”

“No.”

Tonight, standing beside Kate in Blush dressed the way he was dressed, people were reading him exactly the way Susan had.

Woman.

Lesbian.

Kate's girlfriend.

The strange part was how little resistance the room offered to that conclusion.

The stranger part was how little resistance Jim felt like offering back.

Not because it was true.

He was a man.

He knew that.

That was the answer.

But correcting people took energy, and for once the mistake wasn't followed by embarrassment or apology or the frantic social scramble that usually made the whole thing worse.

Here, the mistake simply let him belong.

Nobody made him explain himself.

Nobody treated him like an exception requiring paperwork.

Nobody challenged his presence.

He was with Kate.

Kate was with him.

Apparently they were dating.

Susan Smith had issued the ruling.

There would presumably be an appeals process.

Jim had no intention of filing tonight.

Later, Kate looked at him.

“Ready?”

Jim nodded.

“Yeah.”

They said their goodbyes.

Kate led him toward the door.

At the threshold, Jim glanced back once.

The first time he'd entered Blush, he'd been waiting for somebody to tell him he didn't belong.

Tonight nobody had.

He stepped outside beside Kate.

His girlfriend.

Jesus Christ.

He smiled.

Kate caught it.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Liar.”

“Maybe.”

She bumped her shoulder against his.

“Come on.”

---

The drive back was quieter.

Not uncomfortable.

The opposite.

They had apparently exhausted the need to defend the evening from itself.

Kate drove.

Jim watched the lights pass.

At one point her hand rested near the console.

Jim looked at it.

Then put his hand over hers.

Kate turned her palm upward.

Their fingers settled together.

Neither said anything.

They stayed that way until she needed the hand to turn.

Jim let go.

Kate took his hand again afterward.

That was that.

---

At Kate's door, neither of them seemed particularly interested in opening it.

Late evening had settled around the house.

Quiet street.

Porch light.

The faint sound of a car somewhere farther away.

Kate stood in front of him.

Jim stood in front of her.

For two people who had spent an entire week talking almost constantly, they had become remarkably useless.

Kate looked down once.

At his mouth.

Then back to his eyes.

Jim noticed.

His heart started pounding.

This was different from the dance.

Different from the hug at his door.

Different from every almost-moment they'd carefully allowed to remain almost.

Kate reached for his hand.

Jim took hers.

“Today was nice,” she said.

Jim smiled.

“Nice?”

“I panicked.”

“You mocked me for saying that about your dress.”

“I know.”

“Hypocrite.”

“Shut up.”

He laughed softly.

Kate squeezed his hand.

“Today was…”

She searched.

Jim waited.

“Really good.”

“Yeah.”

“And apparently we're dating.”

“Apparently.”

“Hell yes?”

Jim winced.

“You're going to use that against me forever.”

“You made me think you were going to say no.”

“For two seconds.”

“Longest two seconds of my life.”

“That seems medically unlikely.”

“Jim.”

He smiled.

“Hell yes.”

Kate's expression softened.

There it was again.

That complete absence of armor.

Jim loved seeing it.

The realization came quietly enough that he didn't inspect the word too closely.

Not tonight.

Kate stepped closer.

Jim's breath caught.

She didn't close the remaining distance.

Neither did he.

Not yet.

Kate looked at him.

Jim understood.

Not permission exactly.

Something better.

A question.

He answered by moving closer.

Kate met him.

Their first kiss was gentle.

Almost absurdly gentle after all the noise they'd made getting there.

No dramatic collision.

No desperate grabbing.

No cinematic attempt to prove anything.

Just Kate's mouth against his.

Warm.

Soft.

Careful.

Jim's hand found her waist.

Kate's fingers touched his cheek.

And for one suspended moment, all the ridiculousness went quiet.

The stereotypes.

The technicalities.

The flowers.

The cigars.

The heels.

The not-dates.

The scoreboard.

The arguments about whether any of this counted.

None of it needed defending.

Kate kissed him.

Jim kissed her back.

Tenderly.

Like this was something they had both been approaching for days and neither wanted to rush now that they'd finally arrived.

They separated only far enough to look at each other.

Kate's eyes were bright.

Jim smiled.

She smiled back.

Neither made a joke.

That lasted approximately four seconds.

Jim whispered, “So.”

Kate narrowed her eyes.

“Don't.”

“Still not a date?”

Kate stared at him.

Jim tried to hold the expression.

Failed.

Kate laughed.

Then kissed him again.

And Jim decided that was a considerably better answer.
