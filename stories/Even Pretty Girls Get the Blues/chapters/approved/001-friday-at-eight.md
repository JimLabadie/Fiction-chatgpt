# Chapter One — Friday at Eight

At eight o’clock on Friday night, Blush was still deciding how badly behaved it intended to become.

The bass had already settled into the concrete floor. Purple neon washed the entrance in enough color to make everybody look as though they had arrived with more interesting plans than they probably had. Inside, the room was warm, the air carried dry ice and leather, and the crowd had reached that useful stage between after-work drinks and nightclub proper—busy enough to disappear into, not yet loud enough to require semaphore.

Jim Caldwell stood outside for eleven seconds.

He knew because he counted.

This was not nerves.

It was reconnaissance.

At five foot six and a hundred and five pounds, Jim had spent most of his adult life cultivating the sort of corporate-casual presentation meant to communicate, firmly and without room for discussion, Man Who Has A Dental Plan. It did not work nearly as well as he believed it did.

His face was too fine. His frame was too slight. There was something about the whole arrangement that caused strangers to say “ma’am,” look again, apologize, and then somehow look less convinced by the apology than they had before it.

Jim hated that sequence.

Not in a life-ruining way.

In a standing-at-an-airport-counter-thinking-*for-God’s-sake-not-again* way.

Tonight, he squared his shoulders and walked toward the door.

Clara Bennett looked at him.

Jim produced his ID before she asked.

Clara looked at the ID.

Then at Jim.

Then at the ID again.

Jim’s jaw tightened.

Clara handed it back.

“Evening, Jim.”

He blinked.

That was it.

No double take. No apology. No *sir* delivered with the desperate force of someone trying to repair international relations.

“Evening.”

Clara opened the door.

Jim took two steps, then glanced back.

She was already checking the next arrival.

He frowned and went inside.

At the bar, Kate Rivera saw the entire thing.

Not because she had been waiting for Jim Caldwell. Kate had never seen Jim Caldwell in her life.

She had simply been sitting at her usual end of the bar with a drink, enjoying the first hour of Friday before Blush became crowded enough to turn conversation into an endurance sport.

Kate was good at crowded rooms.

Kate was good at most rooms.

That was, increasingly, the problem.

She managed people for a living. She managed schedules, expectations, meetings, deadlines, other people’s emergencies, and men who began sentences with *Just to play devil’s advocate* as though Satan suffered from inadequate representation.

She could manage a nightclub.

She did not have to, which was one of the reasons she liked Blush.

Here, Jules Taka managed the bar with terrifying efficiency. Sable Ronan moved through the growing room with the easy vigilance of someone who owned every square foot and knew exactly what it was doing. Clara managed the door.

Kate could sit down.

In theory.

In practice, Kate had spent years turning herself into the most polished version of femininity she could construct.

She had stronger features than fashion magazines generally rewarded in women. She had learned early that people sometimes read the bones of her face before they read anything she had deliberately put on top of them.

So Kate had become very deliberate.

Very femme.

Very polished.

Very unmistakable.

And then there was the hair.

The hair was ridiculous.

Kate knew this.

Her hair fell in a thick, extraordinary sweep that had long ago crossed the border between hairstyle and municipal infrastructure. It was the sort of hair strangers remembered, friends threatened to charge rent, and dates inevitably asked to touch before earning anything remotely resembling permission.

Tonight it announced her several seconds before the rest of her could reasonably arrive.

Kate had made peace with that.

Mostly.

She watched the small man at the entrance glance back at Clara with the suspicious expression of someone who had just been denied an argument he had rehearsed in advance.

Interesting.

Jim reached the bar and took the open place two stools away from Kate.

Jules appeared.

“What can I get you?”

Jim looked briefly startled.

Kate hid a smile behind her glass.

There it was again.

Not fear exactly.

Expectation.

As though Jim had entered prepared for the room to challenge his presence and was mildly annoyed that nobody had bothered.

He ordered a drink.

Jules made it.

Jim thanked her.

Then he looked around Blush.

Kate had seen men look around lesbian bars before.

Some looked uncomfortable. Some looked predatory. Some looked as though they had accidentally boarded the wrong train and were waiting for somebody to explain the timetable.

Jim looked like he was taking notes.

Not literally.

His attention caught on a woman passing the bar, dropped immediately to her shoes, followed them for two steps, then rose to the line of her outfit with an expression so thoughtful that Kate nearly laughed.

He caught Kate watching him.

Jim froze.

Kate lifted one eyebrow.

Jim turned back to his drink.

Five seconds passed.

“Those are four inches,” Kate said.

Jim looked at her.

“What?”

“The shoes.”

“I wasn’t—”

“Four inches.”

“I wasn’t staring at her.”

“I didn’t say you were.”

His expression became deeply suspicious.

Kate took a sip.

Jim tried not to look at her hair.

This was considerably less successful than his attempt not to look at the shoes.

Kate waited.

Jim lasted perhaps twelve seconds.

“How long did that take?”

She touched her hair.

“This?”

“No, the federal highway system.”

Kate stared at him.

Jim’s eyes widened.

“Oh God.”

She laughed.

Not politely.

It came out of her before she could stop it, bright and surprised enough that Jules glanced over while polishing a glass.

Jim closed his eyes.

“I’m leaving.”

“No, you’re not.”

“I insulted your hair.”

“You compared it to infrastructure.”

“That was worse.”

“It was considerably better.”

He opened one eye.

Kate smiled at him.

Something changed in his face.

Very small.

But Kate managed people for a living, and she had made a private hobby of noticing the things people tried hardest not to show.

Jim had arrived braced.

That smile unbraced him.

For half a second, the determinedly ordinary little corporate man disappeared.

Not into somebody else.

That wasn’t what Kate saw.

She saw ease.

A softness around his mouth. A quick brightness in his eyes. Something almost pretty becoming openly pretty because he had forgotten, momentarily, to defend it.

Then Jim remembered himself.

His shoulders came back.

Kate’s interest sharpened.

“Well,” she said, “now I have to know your name.”

“Why?”

“So I know who to sue when the Department of Transportation annexes my head.”

He laughed despite himself.

“Jim.”

“Kate.”

“I’m sorry about the hair.”

“No, you aren’t.”

“I am.”

“You’re fascinated by it.”

Jim glanced at it again.

“That can coexist with remorse.”

Kate smiled.

“Oh, I like you.”

Jim nearly dropped his drink.

There.

Again.

Kate had expected embarrassment.

What she got was something more complicated: surprise first, then pleasure, then an almost immediate attempt to bury the pleasure under masculine composure so earnest it bordered on adorable.

Jim cleared his throat.

“You don’t know me.”

“I know you’re rude about civil engineering.”

“That’s fair.”

“And you came into Blush alone on a Friday night looking like you expected someone to issue you a citation.”

“I did not.”

“Jim.”

He looked at her.

“You showed Clara your ID before she asked.”

His mouth opened.

Closed.

Kate leaned an elbow on the bar.

“Who were you expecting to fight?”

“Nobody.”

“Mm-hm.”

“I’m often…”

He stopped.

Kate waited.

Jim looked toward the bar instead of at her.

“Misgendered.”

There was enough practiced irritation in the word to tell Kate it had happened many times.

She did not look him over.

That mattered.

She kept her eyes on his face.

“That gets old.”

Jim glanced at her, apparently surprised by the absence of a follow-up question.

“Yes.”

Kate nodded.

Then she said, “For what it’s worth, Clara wasn’t confused.”

Jim frowned.

“How would you know?”

“I’m here too often.”

“You say that like it’s a qualification.”

“At Blush? It practically comes with continuing-education credits.”

His mouth twitched.

Kate saw him fight the smile.

“Oh, don’t do that.”

“Do what?”

“That.”

“I’m sitting here.”

“You were about to smile and then you put it away.”

Jim stared at her.

Kate tipped her glass toward him.

“Very corporate.”

“You’re dressed corporate.”

“So are you.”

“Yes, but you’re making it look…”

He stopped again.

Kate waited.

Jim looked genuinely trapped.

“Like what?”

“Intentional.”

Kate’s eyebrows rose.

Jim stared into his drink.

“That sounded less weird internally.”

“No, keep going.”

“I would rather not.”

“I would.”

“That seems like your problem.”

Kate laughed again.

This time Jim smiled before he could stop himself.

There she was.

The thought arrived without a name attached to it.

Not *woman*. Not *Emily*. Kate knew nothing about Emily Caldwell. She had no mystical lesbian radar informing her of secret identities, and she had no interest in assigning one to a stranger because his face was pretty and he noticed shoes.

What Kate saw was simpler.

Jim kept doing things naturally and then correcting them.

Looking, then looking away.

Smiling, then suppressing it.

Softening, then squaring his shoulders.

Admiring, then apologizing for having admired.

He was editing himself in real time.

Kate knew something about that.

Her own femininity had been built, at least partly, as an answer. Someone saw masculinity in her face; Kate gave them lipstick, heels, polish, hair enough to require its own evacuation plan.

She had eventually grown to love the answer.

That did not mean she had forgotten the question.

Jim glanced at her again.

“What?”

Kate shook her head.

“Nothing.”

“You’re staring.”

“I’m appreciating.”

His ears went pink.

Kate smiled slowly.

“Oh, that’s useful information.”

“What is?”

“Nothing.”

“That was absolutely something.”

“Maybe.”

Jim narrowed his eyes.

Kate leaned closer, just enough to make him decide whether to retreat.

He didn’t.

Good.

“You know,” she said, “you’re very pretty when you’re annoyed.”

Jim’s entire brain appeared to leave the building.

Kate watched the shutdown happen.

“I’m—”

“Yes?”

“I’m not—”

Kate waited.

Jim tried again.

“I’m a man.”

There was no anger in it.

Only urgency.

A correction he had clearly made before.

Kate’s expression softened, but she did not withdraw the compliment.

“I heard you.”

Jim blinked.

“That’s it?”

“What else were you expecting?”

“I don’t know.”

“Well, when you figure it out, I’ll try to be more disappointing.”

He laughed.

Quietly this time.

Kate sat back.

Jim looked at her for a long moment.

Then, almost against his will, his eyes drifted down.

Kate followed his gaze.

Her shoes.

She looked back up.

Jim looked guilty.

Kate crossed one leg over the other.

“Go ahead.”

“What?”

“You’ve been dying to ask.”

“I have not.”

“Jim.”

He lasted three seconds.

“How high are they?”

Kate grinned.

And this time, when Jim smiled, he forgot to put it away.
