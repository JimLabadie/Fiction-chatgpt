# Chapter Eight — Sanity Check

Kate stayed on the porch until Jim's car disappeared.

This was unnecessary.

She knew it was unnecessary.

Jim was a grown man who had successfully operated a motor vehicle before meeting her and would presumably continue doing so now that they were apparently dating.

Her brain had stopped being useful several minutes ago.

Possibly at the first kiss.

Definitely by the second.

Kate touched two fingers to her mouth.

Then immediately dropped her hand.

“Oh, for God's sake.”

She went inside.

Susan was sitting on her couch with one leg tucked beneath her, holding a glass of wine and looking entirely too comfortable for a woman who did not live there.

She looked up.

Kate closed the door.

Susan looked at her.

Kate took three steps into the room.

Susan kept looking.

“No,” Kate said.

“I haven't said anything.”

“You're saying it with your face.”

Susan took a sip of wine.

Kate narrowed her eyes.

Susan lowered the glass.

“Well?”

“Shut up.”

Susan's smile arrived slowly.

It was unbearable.

Kate kicked off one Manolo and then the other, picked them up by the heels, and carried them toward the living room.

“You kissed her.”

Kate stopped.

There it was.

Not the kiss.

The other thing.

She looked at Susan.

Susan's smile faded.

“What?”

Kate set the shoes down carefully beside the chair.

Then she sat.

Susan waited.

Kate had known Susan long enough to know how rare that was.

Finally Kate said, “Jim is a man.”

Susan blinked.

Once.

Then again.

“What?”

“Jim is a man.”

Susan stared at her.

Kate reached for the wine bottle on the coffee table.

Susan handed her an empty glass without being asked.

Kate poured.

Susan was still staring.

“No.”

Kate looked up.

Susan held up a hand.

“No, I'm not arguing with you. I mean—” She stopped. “Really?”

“Yes.”

“He identifies as a man?”

“Yes.”

“He told you that?”

“Yes.”

“And he knows that everybody tonight thought—”

“Yes.”

Susan leaned back.

“Huh.”

Kate drank.

Susan looked toward the front door as if Jim might still be visible through several walls and half a block of residential street.

Then she looked back at Kate.

“I absolutely thought he was a woman.”

“I know.”

“I called him your girlfriend.”

“I know.”

“To his face.”

“I was there, Susan.”

“And he just smiled at me.”

“He gets it constantly.”

Susan's expression changed.

Not embarrassment exactly.

Something gentler.

“Oh.”

“Yeah.”

“Did I hurt him?”

Kate thought about Jim smiling at Susan. The tiny pause. The familiar calculation she'd seen him make before.

“No. I don't think so.”

“But he noticed.”

“Yes.”

Susan nodded.

That mattered to her.

Kate loved her for that.

Susan picked up her wine again, but didn't drink.

“Okay.”

Kate waited.

“Okay what?”

“I'm recalibrating.”

“That sounds ominous.”

“I'm in marketing. We recalibrate.”

“You make PowerPoints.”

“We make *beautiful* PowerPoints.”

Kate almost smiled.

Susan pointed at her.

“There. That's better.”

“Don't manage me.”

“I'm not managing you. I'm making sure I understand what the hell I just watched.”

Kate's almost-smile disappeared.

Susan saw that too.

“I'm not about to tell you what you are,” she said. “I'm asking whether you're seeing what I'm seeing.”

Kate looked down into her wine.

“That is unfortunately the problem.”

“Okay. Sanity check.”

“Oh, good.”

“Jim says he's a man.”

“Yes.”

“You believe him.”

“Of course I believe him.”

“Good. Jim gets read as a woman all the time.”

“Yes.”

“He knows it happens.”

“Yes.”

“You are not secretly deciding he's a woman because that makes your life easier.”

Kate's head came up.

“No.”

Susan nodded once.

“Good.”

“Susan.”

“Sanity check.”

“I know who gets to decide who Jim is.”

“I know you do.”

Kate looked away.

Susan's voice softened.

“But?”

Kate hated that Susan knew where to put one syllable.

She took another drink.

“But I've wondered.”

Susan didn't move.

Kate continued before she could lose her nerve.

“I've wondered if he's trans.”

“Okay.”

“I have reasons.”

“I assumed you did.”

“And none of those reasons give me the right to decide that for him.”

“No.”

“He may never be trans. He may be exactly what he says he is right now for the rest of his life.”

“Yep.”

Kate rubbed her thumb along the stem of the glass.

“And I don't know if I'm seeing something real or if I'm seeing something I desperately want to be real because it makes everything easier for me.”

Susan's expression sharpened.

“There it is.”

Kate laughed once without humor.

“Yeah.”

Susan set down her glass.

“Because you're a lesbian.”

“Gold star.”

“Show-off.”

Kate gave her a flat look.

“What? Mandy would be fascinated to learn that apparently my entire romantic history has been erased.”

Kate gave her a flat look.

“You talked about Mandy for twenty minutes before we left Blush.”

“Because she sent me a picture of the lamp.”

“It was an ugly lamp.”

“It was a hideous lamp. I love her. I can still recognize crimes against lighting.”

Kate finally laughed.

It escaped before she could stop it.

Susan smiled, but only for a second.

Then she leaned forward.

“So this is the first man you've ever been attracted to.”

Kate opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Susan waited.

“I don't know.”

“That was not the answer I expected.”

“Neither was he.”

“Fair.”

Kate stared into her glass.

“If this felt like being attracted to a man, I think I could deal with it.”

Susan's eyebrows rose.

“I would lose my mind. I would call you. You would be unbearable. Eventually I would figure out whether I needed a new word for myself or whether Jim was just Jim.”

“Very mature.”

“Thank you.”

“That isn't what's happening.”

“No.”

Kate looked at her.

“No?”

Susan's gaze held hers.

“No.”

The room got very quiet.

Kate put her glass down.

Susan continued carefully.

“I have known you since freshman year, when you thought frosted eye shadow was a civil right.”

“It was 2008.”

“It was a crime scene.”

“You participated.”

“I was young and vulnerable.”

“You owned six colors.”

“Seven. But this isn't about my courage under fire.”

Kate folded her arms.

Susan smiled faintly.

“I know what you look like when you like a woman.”

Kate didn't answer.

“I know what you look like when you're flirting. I know what you look like when you're trying not to flirt. I know what you look like when you've decided you're absolutely not going to fall for somebody and are therefore approximately forty-eight hours from falling for her.”

“Him.”

Susan nodded immediately.

“Him.”

That helped more than Kate wanted it to.

Susan went on.

“And tonight? Watching you with Jim did not look like Kate Rivera discovering men.”

Kate closed her eyes.

“Thank you. That's so much worse.”

“I know.”

“No, you don't.”

“I watched you dance with him.”

Kate opened her eyes.

Susan pointed at her.

“That face. That exact face.”

“What face?”

“The one that says you would like to file a complaint against reality.”

“I would.”

“Denied.”

“Fuck you.”

“Also denied.”

Kate reached for her wine again.

Susan let her have the sip.

Then, “Is the penis the problem?”

Kate choked.

Susan waited politely while she coughed.

“You cannot just say that.”

“I just did.”

“Jesus Christ.”

“We are two lesbians discussing your boyfriend. Subtlety has already packed a bag and left town.”

Kate covered her face.

Susan was enjoying herself now.

Not cruelly.

Which somehow made it worse.

Kate lowered her hands.

“I don't know.”

“Okay.”

“I've never—”

“I know.”

“I mean, obviously.”

“Gold star.”

“Stop saying it like I won a spelling bee.”

Susan considered.

“You did have excellent penmanship.”

Kate threw a couch pillow at her.

Susan caught it.

“Violence. Very heterosexual.”

“I'm going to kill you.”

“Lesbian again. We're back.”

Kate stared at her.

Susan grinned.

Then her expression settled.

“Seriously.”

Kate exhaled.

“The mechanics aren't what is making me panic.”

“That's what I thought.”

“How could you possibly know that?”

“Because lesbians have encountered penis-shaped technology before, Kate.”

Kate stared.

Susan lifted one shoulder.

“A strap-on does not make lesbian sex straight.”

“A strap-on is not a person who tells you he's a man.”

“Correct.”

Susan said it instantly.

No joke.

No argument.

Kate paused.

Susan nodded.

“That matters. A lot. I'm not pretending it doesn't.”

“Okay.”

“My point is just that I don't think you're lying awake terrified by the geometry.”

Despite herself, Kate snorted.

“The geometry?”

“I work in marketing, not medicine.”

“You're a vice president.”

“Exactly. I delegate anatomy.”

Kate laughed again.

This one stayed.

Susan waited until it faded.

Then she said, “You're scared because Jim is a man.”

“Yes.”

“And you're attracted to him.”

“Yes.”

“And being attracted to him feels…”

Kate looked toward the door.

She could still feel his hand at her waist.

His mouth.

The ridiculous softness of that first kiss after a week of the two of them turning everything into a joke because apparently sincerity required protective equipment.

“Familiar,” she said.

Susan didn't answer.

Kate swallowed.

“That's the part I can't explain.”

“You don't have to tonight.”

“But what does that say about me?”

“Probably several things. We can make a spreadsheet.”

“Susan.”

“Fine. No spreadsheet.”

Kate leaned back.

Susan watched her.

“I don't think you need to solve your entire sexual orientation before breakfast.”

“Very generous.”

“I am an exceptional friend.”

“You stayed in my house while I was outside kissing someone.”

“I gave you privacy.”

“You were ten feet away.”

“There was a wall.”

Kate shook her head.

Susan smiled.

Then Kate's phone buzzed on the table.

Both women looked at it.

Kate did not reach for it.

Susan looked at Kate.

Kate looked at Susan.

The phone buzzed again.

“Oh my God,” Susan whispered.

“Don't.”

“Is that him?”

“Don't.”

“Look at your face.”

“I hate you.”

“No, you don't.”

Kate grabbed the phone.

Jim.

Home safe.

A second message sat beneath it.

Also I have been informed by reliable sources that apparently I have a girlfriend now. She seems bossy.

Kate smiled.

She couldn't help it.

Susan made a tiny strangled sound.

Kate's head snapped up.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You made a noise.”

“I witnessed something private.”

“You read my face.”

“It was in seventy-two-point font.”

Kate typed.

Your reliable sources are correct. Your girlfriend is extremely bossy. Go to bed.

Three dots appeared almost immediately.

Yes ma'am.

Kate stared at the screen.

Susan stared at Kate.

Kate put the phone facedown.

“No.”

“I still haven't said anything.”

“You're about to.”

“I am.”

“Susan.”

Her friend leaned forward.

The teasing was gone now.

Not the warmth.

Never that.

But the bullshit had left the room.

“I believe you about Jim.”

Kate went still.

“I believe that he's a man because that's what he says he is. I believe you when you say you're not going to decide something else for him. And I believe you when you say this does not feel to you like suddenly discovering that you're into men.”

Kate's throat tightened.

Susan continued.

“I also believe my own eyes.”

“That sounds dangerous.”

“Usually.”

“Susan—”

“I saw you tonight.”

Kate looked away.

“No. Look at me.”

She did.

“I saw him too. I saw the way he looks at you. I saw the way you look at him. I saw you two hold hands like you'd been trying not to do it for a month.”

“It has been a week.”

Susan blinked.

“A week?”

Kate nodded.

Susan stared at her.

Then she picked up her wine and took a long drink.

“Well, that's deeply lesbian.”

Kate laughed so hard she had to put her glass down.

Susan waited.

Kate wiped under one eye.

“Fuck you.”

“I'm serious. A week?”

“We weren't dating.”

“You were dating so hard it apparently bent time.”

Kate started laughing again.

Susan smiled at her.

And there it was.

The old ease.

Freshman year.

Bad eye shadow.

A thousand conversations after midnight.

The person Kate could tell the ugliest, strangest version of a thought to and trust that Susan would not confuse the thought with the person having it.

Susan was quiet for a moment.

Then she said, “Do you remember The Incident?”

Kate stared at her.

“I was there.”

“I know. I have witnesses.”

“We agreed never to use it as evidence.”

“We agreed never to tell the story in public.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It is tonight.”

Kate groaned.

Susan leaned forward.

“Think about it. You and me. Two lesbians. Best friends. We loved each other. Everybody with functioning eyes thought it made sense.”

“It did make sense.”

“Exactly. On paper, we were practically mandatory.”

Kate's mouth twitched despite herself.

Susan continued.

“And then we kissed.”

Kate closed her eyes.

“The most platonic romantic kiss in the history of mankind.”

“We broke apart laughing.”

“You laughed first.”

“You snorted.”

“I did not snort.”

“You absolutely snorted.”

Kate pointed at her.

“This is why the story is classified.”

Susan smiled, but her eyes stayed serious.

“My point is, every category said you and I should have worked.”

Kate's smile faded.

“And we didn't.”

“Not for one second.”

“No.”

Susan nodded toward the front door.

“But I watched you with Jim tonight.”

Kate went still.

“That's what I'm trying to tell you. The labels matter. They describe something real. But they didn't manufacture chemistry between you and me just because we checked the right boxes.”

Kate looked toward the door.

“And they don't explain him.”

“No,” Susan said. “They don't.”

Susan reached across the space between them and took Kate's hand.

“You can figure out the labels later.”

Kate squeezed her fingers.

“And Jim gets to figure out Jim.”

“Absolutely.”

“And if I'm rationalizing—”

“I'll tell you.”

Kate looked at her.

Susan's mouth twitched.

“Ruthlessly.”

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome.”

They sat quietly for a moment.

Then Susan said, “Now I'm going to say the other thing.”

Kate groaned.

“What other thing?”

“The thing you are somehow missing while performing advanced lesbian metaphysics on my couch.”

“I don't like that sentence.”

Susan studied her.

“Do you love him?”

Kate stopped breathing.

Susan didn't fill the silence.

Kate looked down at their joined hands.

“I don't know.”

It came out too quickly.

Susan's eyebrows rose.

Kate glared at her.

“I don't.”

“Okay.”

“That was not convincing.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You did it with your eyebrows.”

Susan squeezed her hand.

“For what it's worth, I watched him tonight too. I can't tell you what's inside his head. But the way that man looks at you?”

Kate's eyes burned.

Susan's mouth softened.

“If he's not falling for you, he is doing a spectacular impression.”

“Susan.”

“You don't have to answer me tonight. You definitely don't have to tell him tomorrow. I'm not scheduling a wedding.”

“Thank God.”

“Mandy would kill me if I scheduled a wedding without her.”

Kate blinked.

“Mandy knows about this?”

“Mandy knows there is a woman—”

Susan stopped.

Kate raised an eyebrow.

Susan corrected herself.

“—a person who has turned my normally terrifyingly competent best friend into this.”

“This what?”

Susan gestured at all of her.

Kate looked down at herself.

She was still in the little black dress.

Barefoot now.

Hair beginning to loosen.

Lipstick slightly less perfect than when she'd left the house.

Her Manolos sat abandoned beside the chair.

Her phone was facedown on the coffee table because apparently a two-word text from Jim could now destabilize municipal infrastructure.

“Oh,” Kate said.

“Yeah.”

Kate picked up her wine.

Susan picked up hers.

They touched glasses.

Kate drank.

Then she looked at her oldest friend.

“What did you tell Mandy?”

Susan's smile became suspicious.

“That is a much longer conversation.”

“Susan.”

“And I have been instructed to bring both of you to dinner.”

Kate narrowed her eyes.

“Both of us.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because Mandy has questions.”

“She hasn't met Jim.”

“Correct.”

“She hasn't met me with Jim.”

“Also correct.”

“And she has questions.”

Susan smiled into her wine.

“You're going to like her.”

“I already like Mandy.”

“You're going to like her less after dinner.”

Kate leaned back.

“This sounds like a threat.”

“It is an invitation.”

“Those are different things.”

“Not in our sorority.”

Kate laughed.

Then she glanced at her phone again.

Susan saw.

Of course she saw.

She had been seeing Kate for half her life.

This time she didn't tease her.

She just smiled.

Kate picked up the phone.

There was one more message.

Goodnight, girlfriend.

Kate read it twice.

Then once more because dignity was already dead.

She typed back.

Goodnight, boyfriend.

She hesitated.

Then added:

Hell yes.

She sent it.

Across from her, Susan quietly raised her glass.

Kate looked at her.

“Not one word.”

Susan drank.

Kate smiled at the phone anyway.
