# Chapter Three — Worth the Risk

For almost a block, neither of them said anything important.

This turned out to be surprisingly difficult.

Jim and Kate had left Blush with no destination, no plan, and—according to both of them—absolutely no date occurring whatsoever. They walked beneath the Friday-night glow of St. Claire with the comfortable aimlessness of people who had already spent too long talking to each other to pretend they were still strangers.

Kate's heels clicked against the pavement with infuriating confidence.

Jim looked down.

Kate caught him.

Again.

“You know,” she said, “eventually I'm going to start charging you.”

Jim kept walking. “For what?”

“Viewing privileges.”

“I was checking for structural damage.”

“To my shoes?”

“To the pavement.”

Kate made a thoughtful sound. “Four and a half inches of municipal destruction.”

“Exactly.”

“You're concerned about infrastructure.”

“Deeply.”

“That's very civic-minded of you.”

“I contain multitudes.”

Kate bumped his shoulder with hers.

It was barely anything.

Jim felt it anyway.

That was becoming irritating.

They passed a restaurant spilling warm light through its windows, then a coffee place with chairs already stacked on half the tables, then a couple walking toward them holding hands and arguing cheerfully about whether they had remembered to feed something named Pickles.

Jim watched them pass.

Kate watched Jim.

He caught her doing it.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“No. Absolutely not. We've established rules about your nothing.”

“My nothing is proprietary.”

“Your nothing is creepy.”

“You like my creepy.”

Jim looked at her.

Kate smiled.

There were several possible answers to that.

Every single one of them seemed dangerous.

He chose survival.

“So,” he said, “question.”

“That sounded ominous.”

“It isn't.”

“That sounded more ominous.”

Jim shoved his hands into his pockets.

He had not planned the question. That was probably why it came out.

“Do you consider yourself a lesbian?”

Not sharply. Just enough.

Jim nodded.

“You ask me if I'm a lesbian, I say yes, and then you nod like I just confirmed the library closes at nine?”

“I had a follow-up.”

“Thank God. I was beginning to think your conversational skills had collapsed.”

“If we're doing this.”

“The whole thing.”

“That narrows it down beautifully.”

“Screw the world. Screw the labels. Screw everybody getting to decide what everything means.”

Kate's expression changed.

Not much.

Just enough for Jim to see the first dangerous spark.

He should have stopped.

Instead, he grinned.

“We need to do a stereotype.”

“A stereotype.”

“Yes.”

“A lesbian stereotype.”

“Obviously.”

“Obviously.”

“We pick one,” Jim said, warming to an idea that had already become objectively terrible, “and we fucking own it.”

Closed.

Opened again.

“You want to deliberately perform a lesbian stereotype.”

“No.”

“No?”

“I want to dominate a lesbian stereotype.”

There it was.

The glint.

Jim had seen it once or twice already, but now he understood it.

The glint meant Kate Rivera had stopped deciding whether an idea was sensible and started deciding how much damage she could do with it.

“Careful, Caldwell.”

“Why?”

“If you own it too hard, you might accidentally become a lesbian.”

Jim considered this with the seriousness the warning deserved.

“Worth the risk.”

“What?”

“You're insane.”

“You've known me for what, two hours?”

“It has been an extremely informative two hours.”

“You're avoiding the challenge.”

And possibly their basements.

And possibly the basements of their enemies.

“Scoreboard.”

“There is no scoreboard.”

“One to zero.”

“That isn't even how scoring works.”

“Two to zero.”

“You're making numbers up.”

“Three.”

Kate stopped walking.

Jim took two more steps and turned.

She stood under the spill of light from a shop window, holding a pink flamingo lamp and looking at him as though deciding whether murder would ruin her shoes.

Jim smiled.

Kate pointed the lamp at him.

“You listen to me, provisional lesbian.”

Jim's eyebrows went up.

“You have had beginner's luck.”

“Sure.”

“You stumbled into one acceptable dress.”

“Really good dress.”

“Acceptable.”

“You bought it.”

“For charity.”

“Of course.”

“And you wore one flannel shirt without looking like a lumberjack who lost custody.”

“High praise.”

“And the boots fit because God has abandoned me.”

Jim nodded sympathetically.

“Tough night.”

Kate stepped closer.

“You are not better at being a lesbian than I am.”

Jim leaned in.

“Then why are you so defensive?”

Kate's eyes narrowed.

Their faces were much too close again.

“I am not defensive.”

“Four to zero.”

“You suck.”

“You like me.”

Kate's gaze dropped to his mouth.

Jim saw it.

This time neither pretended otherwise.

The whole ridiculous night seemed to balance there.

The cigar.

The walk.

The challenge.

The dress.

The heels Jim had looked at when he thought nobody was watching.

The flannel.

The stupid lamp between them.

Kate looked back into his eyes.

“I am absolutely not falling for you.”

Jim's heart did something humiliating.

“Good.”

“Good.”

“Because I would hate for this to get weird.”

Kate looked down at the flamingo lamp between them.

“Yes,” she said. “God forbid.”

Jim laughed.

Kate started walking again.

He fell into step beside her.

After a few seconds, he said, “So what do lesbians stereotypically eat after winning at thrifting?”

Kate groaned.

“You did not win.”

“I'm hungry.”

“That is the first sensible thing you've said all night.”

“Food isn't a date.”

“No.”

“People eat food with people they aren't dating all the time.”

“Constantly.”

“We could eat food.”

“Platonically.”

“Extremely.”

“While discussing how badly you lost.”

Jim looked at her.

Kate looked at him.

“Fine,” he said.

“Fine.”

They walked another few steps.

Jim's carabiner clicked softly against his belt.

Kate's smile appeared before she could stop it.

Jim caught her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Kate.”

She shifted the flamingo lamp under her arm.

“I'm just wondering how long provisional membership lasts.”

Jim smiled.

“Worried?”

“Deeply.”

“About me becoming a lesbian?”

“No.”

Kate looked him over, slow enough to be rude.

Jim nearly walked into a parking meter.

“About you becoming an insufferable one.”

He recovered.

“Too late.”

Kate laughed.

And together, emphatically not dating, they went to find dinner.
