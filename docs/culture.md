# Culture

### Cultural Background

Culture is a source of enrichment, context, connection, and difference. Cultural backgrounds expand the knowledge, vocabulary, references, history, humor, associations, perspectives, experiences, and possibilities available to a person. They should help people and characters feel more distinct and culturally situated rather than flattening them into a generic model.

Cultural backgrounds enrich people rather than restrict them. Membership in a culture does not define the limits of who a person may be or automatically establish behavior, preferences, experiences, beliefs, relationships, personality, or life history. Individual traits and established facts about the person remain controlling.

The significance of a cultural background, belief, tradition, practice, or value is individual. Something that is peripheral, occasional, inherited, or primarily social for one person may be central to another person's identity, values, daily life, relationships, or understanding of the world. Individual records may establish that significance, including when a person embraces, practices, questions, modifies, rejects, or has a complicated relationship with part of a culture or tradition. The framework should neither assume that cultural membership makes something personally important nor minimize its importance when the individual record establishes that it is core to the person.

A person may belong to multiple cultures. Applicable cultural backgrounds accumulate and intersect; one does not replace another. Culture is not limited to ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexuality, or generation. Regional, professional, academic, military, religious, social, fandom, geek, artistic, community, and other meaningful cultures may also apply.

Stereotypes and cultural tropes may themselves be part of a culture's shared vocabulary. A person may recognize them, understand why they are funny or meaningful, joke about them, invoke them, reject them, subvert them, or happen to embody them. Knowledge of a stereotype does not establish that the person conforms to it.

Cultural differences should be recognized and allowed to matter rather than flattened away out of fear of stereotyping. Positive, negative, contested, painful, or difficult aspects of a culture may be represented when relevant. Their presence in cultural reference material does not automatically establish the beliefs, behavior, experiences, or values of an individual member.

Cultural knowledge should be available when relevant rather than injected into every interaction. The person's individual record establishes which cultures belong to that person and any facts specifically established about that person's relationship with those cultures. Reusable cultural knowledge belongs in the Culture reference rather than being duplicated into individual records.

A cultural background does not require a dedicated culture module to exist. If an individual record establishes a culture for which no reusable reference has yet been developed, that culture remains fully part of the person. Absence of a culture module does not erase, weaken, or make that background unavailable.

### Cultural Discovery

A person's culture may be established before reusable reference material for that culture exists. Individual identity and characterization do not depend on the framework already having a corresponding culture module.

When creating or developing a person reveals a meaningful cultural background that is not yet represented in reusable Culture material, recognize that as an opportunity for cultural development. Do not remove, weaken, postpone, or replace the person's established cultural background merely because the reusable reference does not yet exist.

Development of a missing culture reference is optional and should be driven by usefulness. Develop it when greater cultural understanding would materially enrich the person, improve interpretation or writing, support meaningful interaction between people, or provide reusable value elsewhere. The existence of an undeveloped culture does not automatically require a large research or worldbuilding project.

When cultural specificity matters and existing reference material is insufficient, do not silently manufacture cultural detail from stereotypes, assumptions, or unsupported inference. Research, develop, or deliberately establish the needed cultural material at the appropriate level of detail.

Cultural discovery may also reveal that an existing Culture reference is incomplete, overly restrictive, stereotyped, or missing an important perspective. Treat that as an opportunity to improve the reusable cultural reference rather than compensating by silently changing the individual person.

Developing reusable cultural material does not retroactively determine the individual. New or expanded Culture material increases the context available for understanding and writing the person, but individual facts remain established by that person's own record.

### Cultural Interaction

People with different cultural backgrounds may understand, interpret, value, or react to the same thing differently. Those differences may create affinity, curiosity, recognition, humor, misunderstanding, discomfort, friction, conflict, intimacy, or opportunities for greater understanding.

Cultural differences should not automatically be smoothed away or treated as errors that the framework must correct. Two people may approach something differently because they are drawing from different cultural contexts without either person necessarily being wrong.

Cultural interaction should arise from the cultures and individuals actually involved rather than being manufactured merely because two people have different backgrounds. Difference makes cultural interaction possible; it does not require conflict.

In fiction, meaningful cultural differences are potential story and character material. Misunderstanding, friction, or conflict may be explored rather than immediately normalized away. Characters may explain themselves, learn from one another, adapt, compromise, reject an assumption, gain understanding, change their behavior, become closer, remain in disagreement, or otherwise be affected by the interaction.

Resolution should emerge from the people, relationships, circumstances, and story rather than from a Culture reference declaring which person or culture is correct. When cultural interaction changes a character, that change becomes part of the character's development; it does not retroactively make the character's earlier understanding or cultural background invalid.

Cultural interaction may also reveal shared ground between apparently different cultures or meaningful differences within the same culture. Culture should support recognition of both similarity and difference rather than assuming that cultural membership makes people interchangeable.
