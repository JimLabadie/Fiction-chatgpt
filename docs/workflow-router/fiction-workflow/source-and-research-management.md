# Source & Research Management

### Scope & Activation

Use for fiction supporting files, research, provenance, legacy repositories, migrations, duplicate generations, archives, source-document placement, and movement between storage systems.

### Source Model

* **Fiction Docs GitBook** — governing durable story/world/shared-framework material.
* **Emily Google** — preferred supporting fiction environment for source files, research, drafts, exports, and operational material that should not become governing canon merely by storage.
* **Migration Workspace** — temporary reconciliation/provenance workspace; not a governing fiction source.
* GitHub, Jim/Primary Google, ChatGPT Library, old Projects, chats, and other legacy surfaces may contain important evidence but are not automatically current authority.

### Boundaries

Do not blindly copy a legacy folder into the new system. Do not delete or demote a legacy source until provenance, authority, migration completeness, and recovery needs are understood. Source ownership and conversational identity are distinct; global privacy/account rules still apply.

### Migration Procedure

1. Inventory without changing anything.
2. Classify each item as workflow/instruction, governing fiction content, source/reference, draft/history, duplicate, or unresolved.
3. Map provenance and likely authority; flag conflicts instead of resolving silently.
4. Design the destination from actual material rather than reproducing legacy folder structure by default.
5. Migrate one bounded capability/story/world slice first.
6. Verify retrieval, editing, persistence, and fresh-chat recovery.
7. Migrate incrementally.
8. Decide archive/legacy treatment only after verification.

### Verification

For every move or durable write, verify destination, source/account, resulting content, and whether the original intentionally remains. A copy is not a completed migration until authority and recovery behavior are clear.
