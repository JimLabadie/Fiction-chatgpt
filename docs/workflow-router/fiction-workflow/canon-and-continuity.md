# Canon & Continuity

### Scope & Activation

Use when determining what governs, reconciling contradictions, checking continuity/timelines, recovering lost material, distinguishing approved from proposed content, or deciding whether a change becomes canon.

### Authority Rules

* Live governing GitBook records outrank memory, chat summaries, stale duplicates, historical archives, and recovery evidence.
* A title such as “authoritative” is evidence, but authority must still be consistent with the live structure and current governing records.
* Approved manuscript text, current-state records, bibles, development notes, and historical material may have different roles; do not flatten them into one authority class.

### Boundaries

Never silently reconcile conflicting facts. Never promote a proposal, brainstorm, recovered fragment, or historical draft into canon without approval. Preserve source provenance when recovering material.

### Procedure

1. Retrieve all materially relevant governing records.
2. Identify the conflict or continuity question precisely.
3. Separate established canon, approved text, current operational state, proposals, historical evidence, and unknowns.
4. Present or apply the smallest resolution consistent with the user's approval.
5. Update every governing record that must remain synchronized; do not edit unrelated history merely to make it look tidy.
6. Verify all writes by read-back and report unresolved conflicts explicitly.

### Interaction

Use Story Development or Worldbuilding for the domain content itself. Use Source & Research Management when evidence comes from legacy files, GitHub, Drive, Library, or migration sources.
